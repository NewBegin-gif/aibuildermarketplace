What AI features actually cost — the credit is where the price hides

Most software you already pay for now has an AI feature, and it is rarely priced like the rest of the product. Our catalog holds pricing research on 479 tools. For 19 of them the research states a concrete AI cost condition: what the meter counts, how much is included, when it resets and what happens when it runs out. Those 19 are below, each with its source.

Content last changed 27 September 2026.

Short answer: the subscription is often not the whole bill. AI features are commonly metered in credits, capped per tier, sold as an add-on, or reserved for a higher plan — and the conversion from "credit" to the thing you wanted is the number vendors publish least often.

Four questions before you pay

  1. What do I pay for? A seat, a credit, a minute, a session — find the unit the AI is metered in.
  2. How much is included? The allowance per month, per seat or per account, and on which plan.
  3. When does it reset? "400 credits" and "400 credits a month" are different products.
  4. What happens when it runs out? A top-up price, an overage rate, a hard stop or a sales call.

Then convert to your own unit of work (a finished deck, a minute of usable video, a researched lead) and check whether retries count. The records below answer these four questions where the vendor does, and say so where it doesn't.

One subscription, several meters

Seat pricing multiplies predictably. AI features broke that, because the vendor's own cost is per generation, so the bill splits into meters that run at different speeds.

Our illustration. The four patterns below are the ones that recur in our research.

1. Credits, where a retry can count as a use

ElevenLabs charges per generation request, and a regeneration is only free while the content and settings are unchanged. Lindy.ai publishes its own ranges: 2–250 credits for an everyday ask, 1,000–2,500 for a big build. Retries can raise usage, depending on what the meter counts — so estimate attempts, not finished outputs.

2. Caps per tier, which decide your ceiling

Here the AI is included but rationed. Descript runs two meters under a per-editor price: media hours and monthly AI credits. Gamma gives 400 credits once at signup, then 1,000 a month on Plus and 4,000 on Pro. Fireflies.ai keeps Pro on the same 20 AI credits as its free plan. The tier you need can be set by usage rather than headcount.

3. AI as an add-on, priced apart from the plan

Freshchat and Freshservice both sell Freddy AI Copilot at $29 an agent a month on top of the seat — on Freshservice even on Enterprise, whose card says "Freddy AI included" about a different product. SEO Space sells its agent layer as a second subscription. Wati bills AI co-pilot credits as an add-on, with Meta's fees on top. If the AI is why you're buying, check whether it is inside the number you're comparing.

4. Nothing published

Pingtree's pricing page, read on 27 September 2026, lists usage in leads, pings, clicks and storage and presents Copilot, its AI builder, as the self-serve way to build — but states no AI usage limit or credit. That is not a verdict on the product. It means the AI part cannot be budgeted from the website: ask before you commit.

Frequently asked questions

Why do AI tools charge in credits instead of a flat fee?

A model call has a real cost to the vendor every time it runs, and that cost varies with what you ask for. Credits pass the variability on to you. The useful version tells you what one credit buys; the less useful version gives you a balance without the conversion.

Do retries cost credits?

Often, but it depends on what the meter counts. ElevenLabs charges per generation request, and a regeneration is only free while the content and settings are unchanged. Lindy.ai publishes ranges per task rather than a fixed price. Check what counts as one use before you estimate how many you need.

How do I compare two tools when both price in credits?

Convert both to your own unit of work: cost per finished deck, per minute of video, per researched lead. That means finding out what one unit consumes. Where a vendor publishes it, comparison is arithmetic: Ngram states from 10 credits a second of premium AI generation, Gamma 1-3 credits a card. Where they don't, ask, and treat the missing answer as part of the price.

Are free AI allowances worth anything?

Some are a working budget and some are a sample. Gamma gives 400 credits once at signup; Miro's free plan carries 10 AI credits a month. Whether an allowance renews monthly or is granted once changes what the free plan is for.
