What AI features actually cost — the credit is where the price hides

Every tool you already pay for has grown an AI feature, and almost none of them are priced like the rest of the product. We went back through our own pricing research on 307 tools and pulled out every place where the AI is billed separately. 21 of them had something specific enough to quote. Those quotes are below, word for word from our dossiers.

Short answer: the subscription is rarely the whole bill. AI features are usually metered in credits, capped per tier, sold as an add-on, or gated to a plan above the one you were comparing — and the conversion rate from "credit" to "thing you actually wanted" is the number vendors are least likely to publish.

One subscription, several meters

When software was software, the price was per seat and you could multiply. AI features broke that, because the vendor's own cost is per generation. So the bill splits into meters that run at different speeds, and the one on the pricing page is usually the slowest.

Our illustration. The four patterns below are what we actually found in our research — not a framework we invented and then went looking for.

1. Credits, where a retake counts as a use

This is the big one, and the trap inside it is not the price per credit — it's that nobody produces anything on the first attempt. Our ElevenLabs dossier says it as plainly as we can: credits are the meter and retakes are the tax. The same shape shows up in agent tools, where one instruction is not one credit: Lindy.ai burns 5–10+ credits on a research-heavy run, and Emergent 10–20+ on a complex change. If you budget by finished outputs, you will be wrong by whatever your redo rate is.

2. Caps per tier, which decide your ceiling

Here the AI is included, but rationed, and the ration is often what actually separates the tiers. Descript runs two meters underneath a per-editor price: media hours and monthly AI credits. Gamma gives 400 one-time credits free, then 1,000 a month on Plus and 4,000 on Pro. Miro's free plan carries 10 AI credits a month. None of that is hidden — but it means the tier you need is set by your usage, not your headcount.

3. AI as an add-on, priced apart from the plan

Aircall notes that AI features increasingly live in add-ons or higher tiers. Later lists AI credits among its add-ons. Wati meters automation triggers and AI credits separately, with Meta's per-conversation fees landing on top. The pattern to watch: if the AI is the reason you're buying, check whether it is inside the number you're comparing.

4. Nothing published at all

The most uncomfortable one. Pingtree is the clearest example we found: its entire homepage is built around Copilot, its prompt-driven AI agent, while the pricing page is written purely in infrastructure units — leads, requests, clicks, storage, API calls — and says nothing about AI usage limits or which tier gates which Copilot capability. That is not a criticism of the product. It is a warning that you cannot budget it from the website.

How to budget it without guessing

Four questions, in order. They take about ten minutes per tool and they are the ones that would have saved us money.

  • What is one unit of my work worth in credits? One finished deck, one minute of usable video, one enriched contact. If the vendor publishes the conversion, this is arithmetic. If not, ask them — and note that having to ask is itself information.
  • What is my redo rate? Multiply by it. Two or three is normal for anything creative.
  • Does the allowance renew or is it a one-off? "400 credits" and "400 credits a month" are different products.
  • Is the AI in the tier I'm comparing, or one above? This is the single most common reason a quote comes in higher than expected.

And one thing not to do: don't pick the tool with the most generous-sounding credit allowance. Allowances are denominated in each vendor's own unit, so they aren't comparable until you convert them. A big number in a cheap currency is still a small budget.

Frequently asked questions

Why do AI tools charge in credits instead of a flat fee?

Because the underlying cost is per-generation, not per-user. A model call costs the vendor real money every time it runs, so flat pricing would lose money on heavy users. Credits pass that variability on to you. The honest version of this would be a clear conversion rate; the common version is a number with no stated exchange rate.

What is the most expensive mistake when budgeting for AI features?

Budgeting the sticker price and forgetting the retake. Nobody generates once. As our ElevenLabs dossier puts it, retakes are the tax — regenerate a paragraph three times and you pay three times. Estimate your real number of attempts, not your number of finished outputs.

How do I compare two tools when both price in credits?

Convert both to your own unit of work: cost per finished deck, per minute of video, per enriched contact. That means finding out what one unit consumes, which vendors often bury. Where a vendor publishes it — Ngram states roughly 50 credits per export and about 10 per second of AI video — comparison is easy. Where they don't, treat the silence as a risk, not a detail.

Are free AI tiers worth anything?

Some are a genuine working budget and some are a demo. Gamma's free tier is 400 one-time AI credits, which is an evaluation. Miro's free plan includes 10 AI credits a month. Read whether the allowance renews monthly or is granted once — that single word changes what the free tier is for.
