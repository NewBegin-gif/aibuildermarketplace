Customer.io

Is Customer.io worth it?

Customer.io is genuinely best for product-led companies whose messaging is triggered by what people do in the product rather than by a list — that is what this is built for and it is genuinely better at it than list-first tools. Skip it if you send newsletters and campaigns to a list — that is a different job and much cheaper elsewhere; also skip it if your profile count is unpredictable, since profiles are the meter.

From the Customer.io review — hand-researched, sources on the page.
How much does Customer.io cost?

(2026-08-01, read on their own pricing page) Essentials starts at $100 a month billed monthly, scaled by profiles. Premium starts at $1,000 a month billed yearly. Enterprise is quote-only. Overages are published, which is unusual and useful: additional people and object profiles $0.009 each on Essentials, additional 1,000 emails $0.12, additional AI credits $10 per 100,000. Essentials includes 2 workspaces; Premium and Enterprise unlimited. Early-stage companies that have raised under $10M can get twelve months free through their startup programme. Always verify the live price.

From the Customer.io review — hand-researched, sources on the page.
What is the catch with Customer.io?

The gap between the two published tiers is the thing to plan around: $100 to $1,000 a month is ten times, and there is nothing in between. What pushes you across is not a feature you choose but your profile count growing, so the honest question at signup is what your list looks like in a year rather than today. Credit where due — the overage rates are printed, at $0.009 a profile and $0.12 per thousand emails, which most tools in this category will not tell you until the invoice. Use those numbers: growing into overage on Essentials is often cheaper than the jump to Premium, and you can work out exactly where the crossover is.

A 7-day free trial runs without a credit card. Those are the annual-billing rates. Month to month the same tiers read $59, $79 and $99 per user (both columns read in a live browser session on 2026-07-24), so the 'up to 35% off' the toggle advertises is real but front-loaded — you pay a year up front to get it, and the discount shrinks as you go up the range: about 34 percent on Basic, 25 on Pro, 20 on Advanced. From the Dripify review — hand-researched, sources on the page.
What is the catch with Dripify?

Understand what you are buying before the price matters: LinkedIn prohibits this entire product category. Its User Agreement bans using 'bots or other unauthorized automated methods' to 'send or redirect messages', and its prohibited-software page says members using such tools 'risk having their accounts restricted or shut down'. There is no carve-out for cloud tools or low volumes, and Dripify is not an outlier here — every competitor sits on the same side of that line. Dripify's own documentation is candid about the consequence, warning that LinkedIn 'may issue warnings, temporary restrictions, or even permanent bans', and it publishes a recovery procedure for exactly that scenario. Two things follow. First, the advertised ceiling is not a working speed: the app allows up to 75 connection requests a day, while Dripify's own setup guide tells beginners to run 10 to 15, and brand-new accounts 2 to 5, building up 10 to 20 percent a week. Anyone buying on the headline number is buying capacity they are told not to use. Second, Activity Control — the feature Dripify names as its best protection, which adjusts your pace automatically — sits on the $79 Advanced tier. On Basic and Pro, safe pacing is your own manual responsibility. And when a vendor writes that a feature 'makes sure your LinkedIn account won't get flagged', read that as a claim about a third party's enforcement decisions that no vendor can actually guarantee.

Is Gamma worth it?

Gamma is genuinely best for anyone who wants to turn a prompt or notes into a good-looking presentation, doc or webpage draft in minutes. Skip it if you need pixel-perfect, brand-controlled decks — you'll spend the saved time re-polishing the AI output.
