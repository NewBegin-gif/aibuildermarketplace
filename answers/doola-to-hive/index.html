Is doola worth it?

doola is genuinely best for founders (often non-US) who want US LLC/C-Corp formation, EIN and compliance handled in one place without the paperwork. Skip it if you're a US resident comfortable filing formation yourself — the state fee plus a cheap registered agent costs far less than the bundle.

Starter about $297/yr (LLC or C-Corp formation, EIN, registered agent, US address); Pulse bookkeeping ~$300/yr; Tax and Compliance ~$1,999/yr; Business-in-a-Box ~$2,999/yr — plus state filing fees on top ($50-500 by state) (2026). Always verify the live price.

From the doola review — hand-researched, sources on the page.
What is the catch with doola?

Formation is the cheap part; the recurring bookkeeping and tax tiers are where the real cost sits, and state fees are extra on top of doola's price. Convenient especially for non-US founders, but a local accountant may be cheaper once you're established.

Dripify

Is Dripify worth it?

Dripify is genuinely best for sales teams that already run LinkedIn outreach, understand that automation breaches LinkedIn's terms, and accept that risk with their eyes open — the cloud architecture means sequences run with your browser closed and no extension injected into your session. Skip it if anyone whose LinkedIn account is not expendable, since a restriction can be permanent and no tool can promise otherwise; or anyone tempted by the 75-a-day ceiling, because the vendor's own advice is roughly a fifth of that.

From the Dripify review — hand-researched, sources on the page.
How much does Dripify cost?

(2026-07-24, read on their own pricing page) Four tiers, priced per user: Basic $39, Pro $59 and Advanced $79 per user per month billed annually, plus an Enterprise tier that is quote-only. A 7-day free trial runs without a credit card. Those are the annual-billing rates. Month to month the same tiers read $59, $79 and $99 per user (both columns read in a live browser session on 2026-07-24), so the 'up to 35% off' the toggle advertises is real but front-loaded — you pay a year up front to get it, and the discount shrinks as you go up the range: about 34 percent on Basic, 25 on Pro, 20 on Advanced. What each tier buys is unusually consequential here: Basic gets you one drip campaign and what the page calls 'limited daily quotas'; unlimited campaigns and full quotas start at Pro; Advanced adds multi-team management and the safety feature Dripify itself recommends. Every tier includes 100 email-finder credits a month. Always verify the live price.

From the Dripify review — hand-researched, sources on the page.
What is the catch with Dripify?

Understand what you are buying before the price matters: LinkedIn prohibits this entire product category. Its User Agreement bans using 'bots or other unauthorized automated methods' to 'send or redirect messages', and its prohibited-software page says members using such tools 'risk having their accounts restricted or shut down'. There is no carve-out for cloud tools or low volumes, and Dripify is not an outlier here — every competitor sits on the same side of that line. Dripify's own documentation is candid about the consequence, warning that LinkedIn 'may issue warnings, temporary restrictions, or even permanent bans', and it publishes a recovery procedure for exactly that scenario. Two things follow. First, the advertised ceiling is not a working speed: the app allows up to 75 connection requests a day, while Dripify's own setup guide tells beginners to run 10 to 15, and brand-new accounts 2 to 5, building up 10 to 20 percent a week. Anyone buying on the headline number is buying capacity they are told not to use. Second, Activity Control — the feature Dripify names as its best protection, which adjusts your pace automatically — sits on the $79 Advanced tier. On Basic and Pro, safe pacing is your own manual responsibility. And when a vendor writes that a feature 'makes sure your LinkedIn account won't get flagged', read that as a claim about a third party's enforcement decisions that no vendor can actually guarantee.
