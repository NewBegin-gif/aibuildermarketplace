Healthie

Is Healthie worth it?

Healthie is genuinely best for growing group practices (10+ clinicians) in health, wellness and nutrition that need multi-provider scheduling, telehealth and a real API to automate operations. Skip it if you're a solo practitioner or tiny team who wants the simplest, cheapest setup — SimplePractice is easier and lighter for that.

From the Healthie review — hand-researched, sources on the page.
How much does Healthie cost?

(re-verified 2026-08-17 on their own pricing page; yearly billing saves 10%) The entry tier is far lower than most summaries suggest: Core is $19.99/mo billed monthly or $18 billed yearly, Essentials $49.99 or $45, Plus $129.99 or $115, and Group $149.99+ or $135+ per month. Enterprise scales above that. Two costs sit outside the tier and matter for a practice: additional team members are $50 per clinician per month, and the ClaimMD billing integration starts at $30 a month. There is a 14-day free trial, and full API access sits above the standard group tier. Always verify the live price.

From the Healthie review — hand-researched, sources on the page.
What is the catch with Healthie?

Two honest limits: it's pricier than alternatives with thin mid-tier options, and several users report slow customer service (sometimes a week or more to reply). It really only pays off at scale — solo or tiny practices get more value, more simply, elsewhere.

What is the catch with HubSpot?

Three numbers on this pricing page are not what they look like, and they compound. The first is the $7. It is the number in large black type on the Starter card, and the $20 beside it in small grey type is the list price. The banner above the table explains why: up to 65% off Starter, new customers only, for a limited time. So $7 is an introductory rate and $20 is what the seat costs afterwards — a 186% step for a system that by then holds your contact database, which is the single most painful kind of software to leave. The second is the $800. Professional carries a required one-time onboarding fee of $3,000, disclosed in a footnote under the card rather than in the price. Year one on Professional is therefore $800 × 12 + $3,000 = $12,600 if you pay annually, or $890 × 12 + $3,000 = $13,680 month to month. The onboarding adds roughly a third to the first year, and no amount of shopping around removes it — it is mandatory. Enterprise does the same thing at $7,000 on top of $43,200 a year. The third is the seat count, because it is not the meter that usually bites. Professional includes 3 Core Seats and 2,000 marketing contacts. For a marketing platform, contacts are the axis that grows on its own: your list gets bigger whether or not you hire anyone, and the contact tier moves the bill without anyone deciding to upgrade. Price your next two years on the list you expect to have, not the team you have now. One more thing worth knowing before you start on Free. The free tier is genuinely usable — two users, 1,000 contacts, no expiry — and that is exactly how the ladder is designed to work. The step from Starter to Professional is not incremental: at list prices a three-seat team goes from $60 a month to $890 plus $3,000 once, which is roughly fifteen times the monthly cost. Whatever you build on Free, build it knowing where the next rung is.

What is the catch with Hubstaff?

The seat definition is the whole story and they state it plainly, which deserves credit: you pay for every seat you have created, not every person actually being tracked. So an account that grew with your headcount and never got tidied up is billing you for people who left. Audit your seat list before renewal, not after. The second thing is that features on the lower tiers are add-ons rather than inclusions — Insights, Tasks, Silent app and extra screenshots at $2.50 a seat a month, Locations at $3.33 — so the plan you priced and the plan that does what you watched in the demo may differ by ten dollars a seat. And be clear with yourself about why you are buying it: screenshots and activity levels change how a team feels about their work, and that cost does not appear on any pricing page.
