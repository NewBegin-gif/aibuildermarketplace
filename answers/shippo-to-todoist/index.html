Shippo

Is Shippo worth it?

Shippo is genuinely best for e-commerce sellers shipping across multiple carriers who want rate comparison, label printing and tracking in one place with genuine rate discounts. Skip it if you ship a handful of packages monthly through one carrier (their native tools are fine), or you're big enough for direct carrier contracts to beat aggregated rates.

From the Shippo review — hand-researched, sources on the page.
How much does Shippo cost?

(re-verified 2026-08-11, read on their own pricing page) App plans. Starter is free: up to 30 labels a month, discounted USPS, UPS, FedEx and DHL Express rates, 1 user login, and 5¢ per label if you connect your own carrier account. Pro is listed as "Starts at $17/mo", with an annual option quoted at $205/year (a 10% saving); it covers 1-200 labels a month, makes your own carrier accounts free, and adds branded tracking pages, chat and phone support and up to 5 user logins. The comparison table puts Pro at up to 10,000 shipments a month with $0.08 per label beyond that. Premier is contact-us: unlimited shipments, 15+ user logins and a dedicated account manager. Non-US address validation is billed separately at 9¢ per validation on Starter, 8¢ on Pro and 6¢ on Premier. API calls not tied to a Shippo label are billed at API Starter rates. Always verify the live price.

From the Shippo review — hand-researched, sources on the page.
What is the catch with Shippo?

The pricing is honest but layered. The free tier stops at 30 labels a month, and its 5¢ per label on your own carrier accounts quietly adds up. Pro looks like a flat $17 a month until you read the comparison table: it covers up to 10,000 shipments and then bills $0.08 for every label beyond that, so a good sales month adds a variable line to a subscription that reads as fixed. Address validation outside the US is metered too, at 9¢, 8¢ or 6¢ depending on plan. The real money is elsewhere anyway — in the carrier rate discounts, which vary by lane and weight. Run your five most common shipments through it against your current rates; that comparison decides, not the subscription price.

Signal House

Is Signal House worth it?

Signal House is genuinely best for teams sending real SMS volume in the US who already know what an A2P 10DLC campaign is and want a cheaper per-segment rate than Twilio’s list without giving up published pricing. (2026-08-07, read on their own pricing page) Usage-based, no platform subscription. What is the catch with Signal House?

Two things are missing from a page that is otherwise unusually specific. The first is voice. The product is sold as an SMS and Voice API and the pricing page publishes no voice rate at all — nor short code pricing. If the voice half is why you are looking, you cannot budget it from public information and will have to ask. The second is the arithmetic behind the Twilio comparison, which is the whole pitch. Take the entry rate with no minimum. Signal House is $0.0065 a segment against Twilio’s $0.0083, which reads as a 22% saving. But carrier fees are a pass-through and identical either way: add T-Mobile’s $0.0045 to both and you get $0.0110 against $0.0128 — about 14%, not 22%. Numbers narrow it further, $1.00 against $1.15. So the saving is real and it is worth having at volume, but it is a discount on the platform margin rather than on your bill, and the headline does not make that distinction. On a million segments a month that difference is roughly $1,800 a month against Twilio’s list — worth switching for, provided the voice side does not turn out to be where the money goes. Worth noting on the other side: Signal House prints its A2P 10DLC registration fees on the pricing page, and Twilio’s equivalent page says only that fees apply and points elsewhere. Being able to see the whole bill before you sign is not nothing.

Skip it if you depend on marketplace discovery to sell tickets (Eventbrite's traffic is the fee you're paying for), or you run two events a year at tiny volume.

How much does TicketTailor cost?

No subscription (2026): pay-as-you-sell about $0.85/ticket (+VAT), or prepaid credits down to ~$0.30/ticket; free events are genuinely free for most organizers (under ~5k tickets/yr); charities get 50% off forever. Stripe/PayPal/Square processing fees apply separately. Always verify the live price.

What is the catch with TicketTailor?

The fee honesty is the product — flat cents per ticket instead of percentage skims makes it dramatically cheaper than the big platforms on higher-priced tickets (a $0.30-0.85 fee on a $50 ticket vs a percentage is no contest). The trade: you bring your own audience. There's no discovery marketplace pushing your event, so if you rely on a platform's browsing traffic to sell out, the cheap fees won't fill seats.

Is Todoist worth it?

Todoist is genuinely best for individuals and small teams who want a fast, elegant task manager that gets out of the way. Skip it if you need full project management with timelines, workloads and dependencies — this is a to-do app, not a PM suite.

How much does Todoist cost?

Free (up to 5 projects, no reminders); Pro about $5/mo (annual, ~$7 monthly), Business about $8/user/mo (annual, ~$10 monthly) (2026). Always verify the live price.

What is the catch with Todoist?

The free plan's 5-project cap and missing reminders push most serious users to Pro quickly — but Pro is genuinely cheap. It's a personal/team task manager, not a full project platform, so don't expect Gantt-level PM.
