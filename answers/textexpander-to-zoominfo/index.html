TextExpander

Is TextExpander worth it?

TextExpander is genuinely best for support, clinical-admin and recruiting teams that need everyone sending identical approved wording — the shared library, permissions, email-domain sign-up, auto-subscribe and SSO are what the paid tiers actually buy, and the SOC 2 and HIPAA-with-BAA posture the vendor advertises is the one thing no free expander offers a compliance-bound team. Skip it if you are one person on a Mac with a couple of dozen fixed snippets — macOS Text Replacement is built in and free, Espanso is free and open source across all three desktop platforms, and a subscription only starts earning its keep when you need fill-in fields, scripting, or a second person editing the same library.

From the TextExpander review — hand-researched, sources on the page.
How much does TextExpander cost?

(vendor pricing page, read 21 Jul 2026; USD per user/month billed annually, excluding tax) Individual $3.33 ($39.96/yr), Business $8.33 ($99.96/yr), Growth $10.83 ($129.96/yr), Enterprise quoted. There is no free tier — the only free route is a 30-day trial with no credit card. Individual is a genuine single-user plan; the organisation plans start at a minimum of two seats (stated on the vendor's own upgrade documentation). Two things the tier table does not advertise loudly: snippet activity history — which is what lets you track changes and restore deleted snippets — is capped at 7 days on Individual, Business AND Growth, and is unlimited only on Enterprise; and the vendor's own pricing note says buyers in VAT/GST countries including the EU, UK and Australia may see an estimated 20% increase on the listed USD price. We are not quoting monthly per-user prices: the live page publishes the annual figures, and the monthly numbers circulating on comparison sites could not be confirmed at source. Always verify the live price.

From the TextExpander review — hand-researched, sources on the page.
What is the catch with TextExpander?

On a Mac the operating system already does the basic job free — Apple's built-in Text Replacement syncs static snippets across your Apple devices, though Apple's own documentation notes it is not available in every app — and Espanso covers the power-user case free and open source on Mac, Windows and Linux. On Windows there is no built-in equivalent (contrary to a widely repeated claim, PowerToys has no text-expansion utility), so the case for paying is genuinely stronger there. Do not buy it for mobile: the vendor's own mobile page is an early-access signup form for Android and iOS betas, not a shipping product, even though the surrounding copy is written in the present tense. Add the 7-day activity history that applies even on the $10.83 Growth tier — where SSO is the only published addition over Business — and the estimated 20% uplift for EU, UK and Australian buyers, and for one person with a few dozen fixed snippets the honest answer is usually don't.
