Toggl

Is Toggl worth it?

Toggl is genuinely best for freelancers, consultants and agencies that bill by the hour and want frictionless tracking with budgets, rates and profitability per project. Skip it if you need surveillance-style monitoring (screenshots, activity scoring — deliberately not Toggl's philosophy), or free-tier tracking already covers you.

From the Toggl review — hand-researched, sources on the page.
How much does Toggl cost?

(re-verified 2026-08-17 on their own pricing page) Free for a limited number of users; Starter $9 per licence a month; Premium $14 per licence a month — but read the asterisk, because that $14 applies to your first year of Premium annual and then renews at $18 per licence a month. Enterprise is quoted. The renewal step is the thing to budget: a ten-person team on Premium goes from $1,680 in year one to $2,160 in year two, and it is stated on the page rather than hidden. Always verify the live price.

From the Toggl review — hand-researched, sources on the page.
What is the catch with Toggl?

Toggl's honesty problem is the good kind: the free tier is so capable that many teams never need to pay — the paid line is drawn exactly at 'do you bill clients' (budgets, rates, profitability). If you do, Starter earns itself fast; if you track time for personal discipline, resist the upgrade. Time tracking's real cost everywhere is adoption: the tool is frictionless, but the habit still has to be built.
