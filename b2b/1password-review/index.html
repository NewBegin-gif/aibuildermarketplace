What 1Password does

Password manager and security platform for teams: store and share passwords, passkeys and secrets with autofill, breach monitoring and developer secrets management — enterprise-grade vaults with simple admin controls.

The honest knock

It's the polished, category-leading pick, and it's priced like it — free and cheaper managers exist, so you're paying for the UX, ecosystem and business controls, not raw functionality. For a business, that's usually worth it.

What buyers report about 1Password

Recurring themes from G2 and Trustpilot, read in August 2026. We have not run 1Password ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Ease of use and autofill reliability across devices are what reviewers mention first.
  • • Support responsiveness is repeatedly singled out on Trustpilot, which is unusual for a consumer subscription.
What they complain about
  • • No permanent free tier, and reviewers who only want basic password storage say the subscription is hard to justify against free alternatives.
  • • The master password plus Secret Key model confuses new users, and losing either makes recovery genuinely hard. That is a security design choice, not a bug — but know it before you commit.
  • • Apple autofill conflicts and occasional fill errors.
  • • A bug that stops you clearing a date of birth in identity items has been reported since 2022 and is still open.

Where the ratings disagree. 4.6 on G2 against 4.2 on Trustpilot — a narrow gap, which is itself a signal. Tools with billing problems show a much wider one.

When the paid plan is worth it

1Password has no free tier: after the 14-day trial every plan is paid, so the real question is which paid tier, not free-versus-paid. For one person, Individual at $2.99 USD/month on annual billing covers unlimited items across every platform, and moving up buys nothing. Families at $4.49 USD/month starts paying for itself the moment a second person needs a vault: two Individual seats cost more than one Families plan, which covers up to 5 members. Business at $8.99 USD per user, per month earns its premium only when you need admin controls, user provisioning and shared team vaults, and it bundles a free Families plan for each user's personal use. It does not pay off for a solo user or a couple who only want autofill. What no upgrade fixes: account recovery. If you forget your account password and lose your Secret Key, no tier lets 1Password restore your data.

How to actually use 1Password

First real value comes from installing the browser extension, not just the desktop app, and this is the step new users most often skip. Your account password alone does not fill logins: the extension autofills each saved site password and captures newly created logins into a vault, so install it first and then let it capture and fill credentials. Two more snags recur. Importing from another manager means exporting from the old tool, then importing through the 1Password desktop app. And the Secret Key, 34 letters and numbers stored in your Emergency Kit, cannot be recovered or reset by 1Password, so anyone who breezes past saving it can get permanently locked out. Save the Emergency Kit before storing anything you cannot afford to lose. Distilled from vendor documentation and independent walkthroughs

Frequently asked questions

Does 1Password cost $48 a year or $36?

Both numbers are on 1Password's own pages, and they describe different billing terms. The FAQ says pricing "can be as little as $48 USD per year for an individual": that is the month-to-month rate totalled, $3.99 x 12 = $47.88. The annual plan is $2.99 a month, which is $35.88 for the year. The same pattern runs through the family figure: $5.99 monthly is the $72 a year the FAQ quotes, while the annual plan is $4.49. All four rates read on 11 August 2026.

Is there a free version?

No. After the 14-day trial every plan is paid — the plan tables list no free tier (checked 11 August 2026). That makes the real question which paid tier, not free versus paid. For one person, Individual at $2.99 a month on annual billing already covers unlimited items across every platform, and moving up buys nothing you would use alone.

What is the cheapest way to cover more than one person?

Families, from the second person on. Two Individual seats cost $5.98 a month on annual billing; Families is $4.49 and covers up to five members. For a business the line sits elsewhere: Teams Starter Pack is $24.95 a month flat for up to 10 members paid annually, and Business is $8.99 per user per month paid annually, which passes the Starter Pack once you are past three users — what you buy there is admin control and provisioning, not more storage. All figures read 11 August 2026.

Is 1Password secure and reliable enough to depend on?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.

Who should not use 1Password?

Skip it if you're an individual on a budget — a free or cheaper manager covers the basics of secure storage. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
