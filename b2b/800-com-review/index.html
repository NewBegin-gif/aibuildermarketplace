800.com Review (2026)

Communication & VoiceResearched assessment · by Daniel HaketUpdated 2026-07-24

Communication tools win on reliability and coverage — every missed call or message is a customer who quietly moves on. So where does 800.com actually fit?

The honest bottom line. Worth it for businesses that want a professional toll-free or vanity number with call tracking. Skip it once you need a full unified-comms suite, or you already have a phone system and just need occasional forwarding.

What 800.com does

Toll-free and vanity phone numbers for business, with call tracking, routing and analytics — plus 800 AI Agents (launched 2026) that answer calls, book appointments and filter spam when nobody picks up.

Pricing (2026)

(2026) Three plans, ~15% off annual: Personal $23/mo (toll-free + local + fax number, 1,000 minutes), Unlimited $59/mo, Pro $117/mo (5 numbers). No activation fees, 30-day money-back guarantee. In July 2026 800.com announced an added Complete plan at $99/mo on annual billing (excl. taxes and fees) bundling every feature including its new AI Agents — vendor-stated, as the public pricing page is bot-protected and we could not re-verify it ourselves. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

Correction after re-checking: (re-verified 2026-07-23 against the live plans page) 800.com's plans page lists four tiers: Startup, Small Business, Complete and a quote-only Enterprise. Yearly rates are shown by default at $19, $49 and $99 per month, each against a struck-through month-to-month rate of $23, $59 and $119, with the yearly option badged SAVE 15%. Older pricing that names other tiers is out of date — the current plans page carries only these four names.

The honest knock

It does one thing — toll-free and vanity numbers with call tracking and routing — cleanly, but it's a phone-number layer, not a full business-comms platform, so you won't get team chat, video or a contact center here. The Personal plan's 1,000-minute cap matters for higher call volume, and the Unlimited step-up is a notable jump. For a business that mainly wants a professional toll-free presence and call analytics, it's straightforward and fairly priced. The 2026 AI Agents (auto-answering, appointment booking, spam filtering) are the newest piece and sit in the bundled Complete plan, so treat them as young rather than proven.

When the paid plan is worth it

800.com has no free tier, so the only decision is which paid plan. The plans page shows yearly rates by default: $19, $49 and $99 per month for Startup, Small Business and Complete, each set against a struck-through month-to-month rate of $23, $59 and $119 and badged SAVE 15%. Enterprise is quote-only. Startup covers 1,000 minutes, 1 business number, 1 user and 120 texts, with additional minutes at $0.06/min. That is enough if the number is a single forwarding line for one person. Small Business gives you 3 numbers, 3 users, 3 extensions, 500 texts and $0.04/min. Its minutes entry reads Unlimited, followed by the footnote "*Subject to our Fair Usage Policy". Read that before you buy on it. The Fair Usage Policy publishes no numeric limits at all and lets 800.com throttle or limit traffic, require a plan change or usage-based fees, temporarily suspend services, or terminate accounts and reclaim phone numbers. The numbers sit in the terms instead, under a Reasonable Use Policy: 800.com may disconnect or suspend service if you consistently exceed 1,000 minutes per seat per month, or 1 concurrent call at any time per extension. Buy this tier for the extra numbers, the extra users and the cheaper per-minute rate, not for uncapped calling. Complete does not extend that. The plan comparison table lists 1,000 minutes for Complete, not unlimited, at $0.04/min. What the extra money buys is 5 numbers, unlimited users and extensions, 1,000 texts and 500 AI Agent minutes. If you are on Small Business mainly for the unlimited entry, moving up gives it away. Watch AI usage too: AI Agent minutes beyond the included 500 run $0.60/min, against $0.04/min for voice. No tier removes the $100 per-number port-out fee. And no tier settles the contradiction inside 800.com's own terms, where a 30 Day Money Back Guarantee — itself conditional on not accumulating excessive usage — sits in the same document as an all-caps clause stating that monthly, biannual and annual subscription payments are non-refundable and will not be prorated regardless of usage.

How to actually use 800.com

800.com's plans page states that it does not charge activation fees and that no long-term contracts or commitments are required. Taxes and fees are charged on all plans. How fast you go live depends on the number type, and 800.com's own pages disagree. The plans-page FAQ says random numbers are activated within 1-2 hours and that vanity numbers can take 1-5 days; a second answer on the same page says the ordering and setup process for vanity numbers may take 3 to 5 days; the get-started FAQ says "Although some vanity numbers can take up to 3-5 days, most orders are activated within a few hours"; the toll-free page says vanity toll-free numbers take up to 2-3 days. Most orders are quick, then — but plan for up to five days on a vanity number, and do not put it on printed material until it is live. Budget for texting as a separate line item. SMS registration is listed at $19 one-time plus $2-$10 per month on all three plans, on top of the included texts. Additional texts are listed at $0.06 on Startup and $0.04 on Small Business and Complete; the comparison table labels that column "/min", which appears to be an error for per text, so check it against your first invoice. We have not run 800.com's SMS setup ourselves, and none of the vendor pages above documents a business or number verification step, so treat approval timing as unknown rather than quick. Plan your exit before you commit. Porting a number in takes approximately 2 weeks. Porting out later costs $100 per number. An account that remains inactive for over 180 days may be terminated, and once an account has been terminated for longer than 120 days you lose the number — worth knowing before you build a brand around a vanity string. The terms do describe a refund on an annual account closed within 4 months of processing the subscription, less the amount owed for the months where services were available and any applicable overage charges, but the same document also states in all caps that annual subscription payments are non-refundable and will not be prorated, so do not plan around getting money back. Support runs 9am-6pm Eastern Time, Monday to Friday. Distilled from vendor pages only, all fetched 2026-07-23: 800.com's plans page (prices, plan comparison table, FAQ), get-started FAQ, toll-free landing page, terms, and Fair Usage Policy. No third-party reviews, no hands-on use.

Best for: businesses that want a professional toll-free or vanity number with call tracking, routing and analytics to look bigger and measure inbound calls.
Who should skip it: if you need a full unified-comms suite (video, chat, contact center), or you already have a phone system and just need occasional forwarding.

The natural comparison is Grasshopper or UniTel Voice — close virtual-number peers — compare included minutes and whether you need vanity numbers specifically. Decide by which one fits the job above, not by the louder brand.
