Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 27 Sep 2026

Read on adwisely.com/pricing; every figure below stood unchanged from our previous reading on 2 August 2026. Both plans charge 10% of the ad spend Adwisely manages, with a monthly minimum that acts as a floor rather than a fee on top: $49 on Self-Serve and $249 on Concierge (managed). At $5,000 of monthly spend the Self-Serve fee is therefore $500, not $549, and total monthly Adwisely fees are capped at $3,250 (re-read 15 September 2026). 7-day free trial, no setup fee. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

It is cheap at low spend, but at higher budgets it's a real percentage cut of your marketing dollars until the cap — so calculate the effective cost at your actual spend, not the $49 sticker. It's built for e-commerce retargeting/prospecting on Meta and Google specifically, so it's not a general campaign manager. For stores that want largely hands-off retargeting, it removes real work.

What Adwisely does

Automated retargeting and prospecting ads on Meta and Google for Shopify, WooCommerce and BigCommerce stores.

What buyers report about Adwisely

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Adwisely ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Real-time Shopify sync pauses ads on out-of-stock SKUs automatically, which is money most stores quietly waste. 4.1 to 4.3 across 386 Shopify App Store reviews.
  • • It runs targeting and bidding end to end rather than half-automating and handing back.
What they complain about
  • • The bill is a flat fee plus a percentage of ad spend, so it scales with the budget you give it. Model it at your real ad spend rather than the entry figure — the tier you need depends on what you run through it.
  • • Google and Meta only. No wider channel coverage.
  • • Granular per-ad control and approval workflows are not there — if you need to sign off on creative before it runs, this is the wrong shape of tool.

When the paid plan is worth it

There is no free tier, so the real split is Self-Serve at a "$49" minimum monthly fee against Concierge at "$249 USD". Everything else is identical: both charge "10% of your ad spend", both stop at the same ceiling ("Your total monthly fee will never exceed $3,250"), and both start with the same "7-Day Free Trial". The higher tier buys people, not features. The pricing page advertises "10 hours of hands-on optimization every month", and the Ad Expert help article explains the shape: one expert per platform, Meta and Google, each giving "at least 5 hours per month to your account", plus a setup review "Within 24 hours of your campaign going live".

Concierge earns its keep if you would otherwise pay someone to watch both channels daily, and if your spend is large enough that the 10% fee dwarfs the gap between the two minimums. On a modest budget you are mostly funding a floor you do not consume. Upgrading fixes none of the structural items: not the eligibility bar of "100+ online orders in the last 90 days" or "$10,000+ in monthly online revenue", not the 10% cut of spend, and not the cap.

Common Adwisely setup mistakes

Where Adwisely setups actually go wrong, and what it costs you when they do. Same sources as the setup notes above — not from a walkthrough we ran ourselves.

Where it stalls is access.

  • You share Meta assets at named permission levels — ad account at "Partial access → Manage campaigns", catalog and pixel at "Full control" — and then wait, because "Within 24 hours we will check if everything is okay with the asset you shared".
  • On Google the usual failure is the account you signed in with: it needs "Admin-level access" in Merchant Center, and it must be the same Google account across Adwisely, Google Ads and GMC.
  • Onboarding also breaks on a Google-side limit — "Google Ads allows up to five manager accounts per account" — until you remove one.
  • Budgets gate too: Meta Retargeting needs "200+ orders recorded within the past 90 days" and "$20 / day"; the Meta AI campaign wants "$30 / day".
  • Adwisely says its learning phase runs "14 to 30 days" — their claim, not our timing.

The natural comparison is Managing Meta/Google Ads directly, or hiring a freelancer — DIY or freelance routes — Adwisely automates the busywork; compare its 10% fee against a specialist's flat retainer at your spend.

Frequently asked questions

When does the Adwisely free trial start, and how long is it?

Seven days, and the clock starts at your first campaign, not at install. The help article on cost (read 27 September 2026) says the trial begins when you create your first Meta or Google campaign and ends seven days later whether that campaign is running or paused, with one trial per Shopify domain. The trial waives Adwisely's own fee only: the ad spend itself is billed by Meta and Google as usual. Adwisely's llms.txt file still says 14 days; the pricing page and help centre both say 7.

How does Adwisely bill the 10% fee during the month?

In pieces. Per the same help article (read 27 September 2026), the plan minimum of $49 or $249 is charged at the start of the month and the 10% usage fee is settled at the end, but each time the accumulated fee reaches $1,000 an interim $1,000 charge is taken, so a high-spend store sees several charges in one month. The total still follows the rule: 10% of spend, never below the minimum and never above $3,250.

At what ad spend does Concierge cost the same as Self-Serve?

Around $2,500 a month. Both plans charge 10% of managed spend with only the minimum differing, so once 10% of your spend passes $249 the bills are identical and Concierge's two Ad Experts cost nothing extra; Adwisely's help article says the same. Below that the gap is up to $200 a month. The fee counts combined Google and Facebook spend per store.

What should I weigh before buying Growth & Revenue software?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Small teams get the most out of this category when one clear problem is already costing real hours or revenue; buying ahead of that just adds cost and another login. Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.
