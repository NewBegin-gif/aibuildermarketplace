Growth & RevenueResearched assessment · by Daniel HaketUpdated 2026-09-21

Cost scales linearly with clients — great at 5, pricey at 50+. Throughout this review we weigh it against Databox.

Best for: marketing agencies that need white-label client reports from 85+ integrations — automated, on-brand and hands-off.
Who should skip it: if you're a solo operator with a couple of data sources, or you want rock-solid single-dashboard KPI tracking — Databox or a lighter tool may fit better.

Frequently asked questions

What do agencies actually say about AgencyAnalytics on Reddit?

"agency analytics reddit" is the single biggest search that reaches this page, so it deserves a straight answer: we do not summarise Reddit threads here, because we cannot verify who wrote them or when. What we can point at is the vendor conceding the most common complaint in its own documentation. AgencyAnalytics' help centre calls integration outages "by far the most common reason for missing data", and its troubleshooting doc warns that similarly named metrics are calculated differently between platforms - its "Clicks" pulls the "Total Clicks" metric from Facebook Ads. If connector reliability is what you are trying to research, that is the vendor answering in writing.

What does AgencyAnalytics cost for a real client roster?

One published rate: $20 USD per client per month, billed annually, with unlimited staff and client users (read on the vendor pricing page 11 September 2026). Twenty-four clients is $480 a month at that rate; from 25 clients up the vendor stops publishing a price and asks you to contact sales for a custom plan. Price the add-ons on top of that: Rank Tracker at $41.67 a month per 500 keywords and AI Tracker at $20.83 a month per 250 credits, both billed annually. The currency selector also offers CAD, AUD, NZD, GBP and EUR.

Is there a month-to-month price?

Yes: $25 USD per client per month. The plan card only shows "Save 20% annually" beside the $20 annual rate and never prints the monthly one, but the structured data on that same page publishes an AggregateOffer with lowPrice 20 and highPrice 25 - and $20 divided by 0.8 is exactly $25, so the two agree. What the page does spell out is what you trade: monthly plans carry no commitment and can be cancelled any time, while the discounted annual plan requires a one-year commitment. The 14-day trial needs no credit card, and there is a 30-day money-back guarantee (all read 11 September 2026).

What should I weigh before buying Growth & Revenue software?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Small teams get the most out of this category when one clear problem is already costing real hours or revenue; buying ahead of that just adds cost and another login. Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.
