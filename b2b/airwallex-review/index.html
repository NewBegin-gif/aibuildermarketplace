What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

What the page does not settle is everything around it. The Grow plan's platform fee 'varies with team size' with no table, so the $12 a user is not the whole subscription -- though their table does say the first ten Spend users are free, which is the part the headline rate hides in your favour. Chargeback and dispute fees are not priced anywhere, only mentioned as a feature. And the page's own disclaimer says per-transaction fees apply to certain transactions with the detail in a separate fee schedule.

So: use the published FX rate to compare against your bank, then ask for the fee schedule and the platform fee before you move money.

When the paid plan is worth it

Explore is genuinely free at $0 per user per month, and for most small teams it is the right answer — the paid tiers buy controls, not capability. Grow is $12 per active Spend user per month -- the first ten are free and the rate runs to 250 users -- plus a platform fee that varies with team size; Accelerate is quote-only. Read on their own pricing page on 28 August 2026: the monthly fee is charged per business legal entity, once, however many accounts you hold under it.

The money is in the transaction fees, not the subscription, so price it there. FX conversion is 0.5% above the interbank rate on major currencies and 1% on everything else. Local transfers to 120+ countries are free; SWIFT is $15–$25 per transfer. Accepting card payments costs 2.80% + $0.30 domestic and 4.30% + $0.30 international, with subscription billing at 0.50% per successful card transaction. That FX markup is the reason to look at Airwallex at all.

Against a traditional bank charging 2–3% on conversion, 0.5% pays for itself on the first sizeable transfer — and the free Explore tier means there is no subscription to justify before it does.

How to actually use Airwallex

Open on Explore first. It costs nothing, it is the same rails as the paid tiers, and the fee schedule that determines whether Airwallex saves you money applies identically. Upgrade when you need approval workflows and spend controls, not before. Set up local receiving accounts in the currencies your customers actually pay in, because that is what avoids the conversion fee entirely. Getting paid USD into a USD account costs nothing to convert; getting paid USD into a EUR account costs you 0.5% before anything else happens.

Route transfers locally rather than by SWIFT wherever the corridor allows it. Local is free to 120+ countries and SWIFT is $15–$25 — on a $500 supplier payment that is the difference between free and 5%. Check the corridor before you send, not after.

Frequently asked questions

Can I work out what the Grow plan will cost me?

Not from the page, and that is worth knowing before you plan around it. Read on 19 September 2026, the US pricing page puts Grow at $12 per user a month and then adds, in the same card, plus platform fee based on team size — a fee it never prints. The fee table fills in the seat side: up to 10 Spend users are free and the $12 rate runs to 250 Spend users. It also adds a dimension most buyers miss, because the charge is per legal entity: up to three entities are included and each one after that is $1,000. Explore stays at $0 per user a month and Accelerate is quote-only. So the honest summary is that two of the three plans have a published price and none of them let you total your own bill. Ask for the platform fee in writing at your headcount before you compare this with your bank.

Is the yield on my USD balance a rate I can count on?

No, and Airwallex's own footnote is the reason. The three plan cards showed 3.22%, 3.37% and 3.49% on USD when we read them on 19 September 2026, the same three figures as on 5 September — but the footnote under them had moved to say the indicative rate is the 7-day annualised historical rate of the underlying funds as of 18 September 2026, net of fees. It is a restamped historical number, not a promised rate; when we first recorded these in August they were 3.21%, 3.36% and 3.48%. The same footnote says what you are buying: a money market fund through a broker-dealer, where you could lose money, the $1.00 share value is sought rather than guaranteed, there is no FDIC cover, and SIPC protects securities customers up to $500,000 including $250,000 for cash. Treat it as an investment decision, not a plan feature.

What does it cost to take money in and move it out?

This is where the published numbers are, and they are unusually complete. Read on 19 September 2026: card acceptance is 2.80% + $0.30 on domestic cards and 4.30% + $0.30 on international ones, local payment methods are $0.30 plus the method's own fee, and recurring subscription billing adds 0.50% per successful card transaction while invoicing itself is free. On the way out, FX is 0.5% above the interbank rate for major currencies and 1% for the rest, local transfers to 120+ countries are free, and SWIFT costs $15 to $25 per transfer on SHA and OUR terms. One cost is missing: the US page lists dispute management as a feature with no fee beside it. Only the Canadian schedule prices it — 15 CAD per chargeback request and 0.30 CAD per refund request, both before whatever the card network adds. Ask what your entity is charged.

What should I weigh before buying Finance software?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.
