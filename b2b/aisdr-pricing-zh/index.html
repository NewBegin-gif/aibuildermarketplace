AiSDR Pricing Guide 2026: The Complete Breakdown for B2B Founders

If you've spent any time researching AI sales development tools, you've probably run into AiSDR — the platform that promises to replace (or supplement) a human SDR with an AI agent that researches leads, writes personalized outreach, manages replies, and books meetings on autopilot. The pitch is compelling: a full-time AI rep for less than half the cost of a junior human hire. But does the pricing actually hold up once you factor in add-ons, data credits, and the realistic volume you'll need to hit pipeline targets?

This guide breaks down AiSDR's 2026 pricing structure in plain terms, what you actually get at each tier, how the ROI math works for early-stage and growth-stage B2B teams, and which alternatives deserve a look before you commit a card number.

Overview: What AiSDR Actually Does

AiSDR positions itself as an "AI Sales Development Representative" — not just an email sequencer, but an autonomous agent that handles the full top-of-funnel motion: lead sourcing, enrichment, personalized copywriting, multichannel outreach (email + LinkedIn), reply handling, objection responses, and meeting scheduling. It's built for B2B teams that want outbound volume without scaling headcount linearly. The core differentiator versus tools like Instantly, Smartlead, or Apollo's sequencer is the conversational AI layer. Instead of firing off static sequences, AiSDR reads inbound replies (interested, not now, wrong contact, out of office) and generates contextual responses in your brand voice — theoretically freeing your actual human reps to only touch qualified conversations.

The catch, as with most AI-first GTM tools, is that the marketing implies "set it and forget it," while the real-world usage requires ongoing prompt tuning, list quality management, and deliverability oversight. It's not a fire-and-forget hire — it's a force multiplier that still needs a human steering it.

Key Features

  • AI-generated personalized outreach: Uses LinkedIn activity, company news, funding signals, and job titles to generate emails that don't read like templated spam — at least in the first few sends.
  • Multichannel sequencing: Combines email and LinkedIn touches into a single sequence, which matters because email-only outbound in 2026 is converting at roughly half the rate it did three years ago.
  • Autonomous reply handling: The AI classifies replies and responds to common objections ("not interested," "send more info," "wrong person") without human intervention, escalating only qualified leads to your calendar.
  • Built-in lead database and enrichment: Includes access to a contact database (similar in scope to Apollo or ZoomInfo, though smaller), so you're not required to bring your own list — though bring-your-own-list is supported and often produces better results.
  • Meeting booking automation: Directly integrates with calendar tools to book meetings without a human touching the thread.
  • CRM sync: Native integrations with HubSpot and Salesforce, plus Zapier/Make for everything else.
  • Deliverability tooling: Domain warm-up, sending limits, and inbox rotation to protect sender reputation — critical since most churn in this category comes from burned domains, not bad copy.

Pricing Breakdown (2026)

AiSDR's pricing has consolidated into three primary tiers, priced per seat/workspace with usage-based components layered on top. Here's the realistic breakdown based on current published rates and typical add-on costs:

  • Starter — ~$900/month: Includes one AI SDR agent, up to roughly 1,500–2,000 contacted leads per month, email-only outreach, basic CRM sync, and standard support. This tier is aimed at solo founders or very early-stage teams testing outbound before committing further.
  • Growth — ~$1,700–$2,200/month: Adds LinkedIn outreach, higher sending volume (roughly 4,000–6,000 contacts/month), reply automation with more nuanced objection handling, and priority support. This is the tier most funded startups actually land on.
  • Scale/Enterprise — custom, typically $3,500+/month: Multiple AI agent seats, dedicated deliverability infrastructure, custom integrations, SLA-backed support, and volume-based lead database credits. Priced per negotiation, usually requiring a minimum annual commitment.

On top of the base subscription, expect these real additional costs that sales pages don't always headline:

  • Sending domains and mailboxes: Budget $10–$15 per inbox per month, and you'll typically need 5–10 inboxes to hit meaningful volume without tanking deliverability — that's an extra $50–$150/month minimum.
  • Data/enrichment credits: If you exceed the included lead database quota, additional contact credits run roughly $0.10–$0.25 per verified contact, which adds up fast at scale (5,000 extra contacts = $500–$1,250/month).
  • LinkedIn automation add-on: Not always bundled at the Starter tier; adding it later can cost an extra $200–$400/month depending on seat count.
  • Onboarding/setup: Some plans include a paid onboarding package ($500–$1,500 one-time) for ICP definition, sequence build, and deliverability configuration — technically optional, but skipping it usually means a slower ramp and worse initial results.

Realistically, a Growth-tier customer running a proper outbound motion should budget $2,200–$3,000/month all-in, not the headline $1,700–$2,200 subscription price alone. That's a meaningful gap founders need to model before comparing this to headcount cost.

Pros & Cons

Pros:

  • Meaningfully cheaper than a full-time human SDR when you factor in salary, commission, benefits, and ramp time (a US-based junior SDR typically costs $70K–$90K OTE fully loaded).
  • No ramp time in the traditional sense — the AI is "productive" from day one, though sequence and voice tuning still takes 2–4 weeks to dial in.
  • Scales instantly — doubling volume doesn't require hiring or training, just upgrading a tier or adding contact credits.
  • Reply handling reduces the "dead lead" problem where a rep never follows up on a lukewarm response.
  • Multichannel approach (email + LinkedIn) outperforms single-channel tools on reply rates in most published benchmarks.

Cons:

  • Total cost of ownership is materially higher than the advertised subscription price once inboxes, data credits, and add-ons are included.
  • AI-generated personalization quality degrades over long sequences — the first two or three touches are genuinely good; touches 4+ start to feel generic.
  • Deliverability is still your responsibility. AiSDR provides tooling, but a poorly warmed domain will tank results regardless of AI quality.
  • Built-in contact database is smaller and less accurate than dedicated data providers like ZoomInfo or Clay — plan to supplement with your own enrichment for anything beyond generic SaaS/tech personas.
  • No AI tool fully replaces a closer — it books meetings, but conversion from meeting to pipeline still depends entirely on your AE or founder-led sales process.
  • Enterprise-tier pricing requires annual commitment, which is a real risk if your ICP or messaging isn't validated yet.

ROI for B2B Founders: The Actual Math

Let's run real numbers instead of vague "10x your pipeline" claims.

Scenario 1 — Early-stage founder-led sales (Starter tier, $900/month + ~$100 in inbox costs = ~$1,000/month all-in):

  • 1,800 contacts/month at a realistic 1–2% positive reply rate (industry benchmark for cold B2B outbound in 2026) = 18–36 qualified conversations.
  • At a typical 20–30% meeting-to-conversation conversion, that's 4–10 booked meetings/month.
  • If your average deal size is $12,000 ACV and your close rate from booked meeting is 15%, that's roughly 0.6–1.5 closed deals/month, or $7,200–$18,000 in new ARR against a $1,000 spend.
  • That's a 7x–18x return in month one alone — though realistically the first 30–45 days are spent tuning, so treat month one as ramp and measure from month two onward.

Scenario 2 — Growth-stage team replacing a planned SDR hire (Growth tier, ~$2,600/month all-in):

  • 5,000 contacts/month, same 1–2% positive reply benchmark = 50–100 qualified conversations.
  • 10–25 booked meetings/month at the same conversion assumptions.
  • Compare this to a human SDR: $75K/year fully loaded = $6,250/month, typically producing 8–15 booked meetings/month once ramped (3+ months in).
  • AiSDR at $2,600/month is producing comparable or higher meeting volume at roughly 40% of the fully-loaded cost of a human SDR, with zero ramp-up delay.

The honest caveat: these numbers assume decent list quality, a validated ICP, and messaging that's already been tested somewhere (even manually). If your ICP is still fuzzy, no AI tool — AiSDR included — will save you from testing against the wrong audience. The tool amplifies a working motion; it doesn't invent one.

Best Alternatives to Consider

AiSDR isn't the only option, and depending on your stage, one of these might fit better:

  • Apollo.io ($49–$149/user/month): Cheaper, more mature data layer, solid sequencing — but far less sophisticated AI reply handling. Better if you want a human-driven motion with AI assistance rather than an autonomous agent.
  • Clay + a sequencer (Clay starts at $149/month, plus $30–$100/month for a sending tool): More technical to set up, but gives you granular control over enrichment and personalization logic via workflows. Better for teams with a technical operator who wants full customization rather than a black-box AI.
  • Artisan AI (~$1,500–$3,000/month): AiSDR's closest direct competitor, similarly positioned as an "AI BDR." Comparable pricing, marginally stronger brand recognition, but similar deliverability and data-quality tradeoffs.
  • 11x.ai (custom pricing, typically $2,000+/month): Similar autonomous-agent positioning, generally seen as more enterprise-focused with heavier onboarding requirements.
  • Human SDR + Instantly/Smartlead stack (~$6,000–$8,000/month fully loaded): Still the right call if your sales motion requires deep qualification, complex discovery, or enterprise-level relationship building that AI reply-handling can't yet replicate.

If your ACV is under $20K and your sales cycle is short and transactional, AiSDR-style AI agents make strong economic sense. If you're selling six-figure enterprise contracts with multi-stakeholder buying committees, treat AI SDR tools as a top-of-funnel volume play feeding a human-led mid-funnel — not a replacement for the whole motion.

Verdict

AiSDR's 2026 pricing is fair, not cheap. The headline subscription undersells the real cost once you add inboxes, data credits, and onboarding — budget 30–40% above the sticker price for an honest comparison. But even at the fully-loaded price, it comes in well below the cost of a human SDR, with faster time-to-first-meeting and none of the hiring, training, or turnover risk. The tool is best suited to B2B founders and RevOps leads with a validated ICP and messaging who want to scale outbound volume without scaling headcount. It's a poor fit for teams still figuring out who their buyer is — no amount of AI personalization fixes a targeting problem. For most seed-to-Series B B2B companies selling mid-market or SMB deals, the math works: run the Starter tier for 60 days to validate reply and meeting-booking rates against your specific ICP, then scale into Growth once you have real conversion data instead of vendor benchmarks.
