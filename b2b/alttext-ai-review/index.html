What AltText.ai does

Bulk AI alt-text generation for image-heavy sites: crawls your pages or plugs into your CMS (WordPress, Shopify, WooCommerce, Magento, Contentful, DatoCMS) and writes the alt attributes back automatically, in 130+ languages. Credit-metered — 25 free credits to try, then from $5/mo for 100 images.

Pricing (2026)

(verified 21 Jul 2026) Credit-metered — one credit = one image. Free trial: 25 credits, no credit card required. Monthly: Bronze $5/mo (100 credits/mo, 5¢ per image), Silver $19/mo (500), Gold $59/mo (2,000), Titanium $119/mo (5,000), Platinum $229/mo (10,000). Annual buys the same yearly credit volume at a discount the page labels 17-31% off: $49/yr (1,200 credits), $189/yr (6,000), $489/yr (24,000), $1,179/yr (60,000), $2,199/yr (120,000). One-time credit packs are 50 credits for $3 with no subscription; beyond 120,000 credits a year is custom enterprise pricing. Credits roll over and never expire, you can cancel anytime, and every feature ships on every plan. Two multipliers change the real per-image cost: advanced formats (AVIF, HEIC, HEIF, JP2, SVG, TIFF) bill 2 credits and are paid-plans-only, and each additional translation language costs 1 more credit per image. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The headline ‘5¢ per image’ only holds for a plain JPG in one language. AVIF, HEIC, SVG and TIFF bill double — precisely the formats a performance-tuned image pipeline outputs — and every extra language adds another credit per image, so by the published rules one AVIF product shot in five languages is six credits, not one. Those advanced formats are also locked out of the 25-credit free trial, so the one thing you cannot measure before paying is your own burn rate. Credits are spent on generation rather than on acceptance: regenerate a line you didn’t like and you pay again, and AltText.ai states plainly that used credits are not normally refundable. The category caveat an honest review owes you: models describe what is visibly in a photo well, but alt text is a judgement call — whether an image is decorative or informative, and what your brand actually calls the thing in it, is context the model doesn’t have. Bulk generation is a far better starting point than the empty alt attributes on most sites, but treat the output as a draft to spot-check. ‘ADA compliance’ here means the field gets filled, not a legal guarantee that what’s in it is right.

When the paid plan is worth it

The paid split here is unusual in one specific way: the pricing page lists the same integrations and features on every plan, so moving up a tier buys credit volume and a lower unit rate and nothing else. Bronze is $5/mo for 100 credits (5c per image), Silver $19/mo for 500, Gold $59/mo for 2,000 (3c). The step to Gold only pays back near real volume — at roughly 300 images a month you are paying about $40 extra for a rate you never reach, and Silver is the fit. Watch the one-time pack: 50 credits for $3 works out at 6c an image, above the cheapest subscription, so pay-as-you-go is the most expensive rate on the page rather than the cautious one. Annual discounts are uneven too — the page labels Bronze $49/yr as 19% off but Gold $489/yr as 31%, so "go annual" is worth much more at some tiers than others. What upgrading does not buy: a better model, or any review or approval step. A Platinum line reads like a Bronze one, and someone still has to read it.

How to actually use AltText.ai

Fastest route to something real: sign up for the 25 free credits (no card), and run the free scanner at alttext.ai/crawl-analyzer against your site first so you know how many images actually lack alt text before you pick a plan. On WordPress, install the Alt Text AI connector plugin (20,000+ active installs, PHP 7.4+ and WordPress 4.7+), paste the API key from your account's API Keys page, then run Bulk Generate against images with no alt text. Elsewhere the web UI takes uploads or URLs, plus CSV, Dropbox, Cloudinary and page-scrape imports. Where people stall: local, staging and password-protected sites. The docs call it critical to uncheck "This site is reachable over the public internet" in Technical Settings, otherwise their servers cannot fetch your images and nothing happens. Second, letting WordPress regenerate missing metadata during a bulk run — the docs warn this causes failures and timeouts on shared hosting. Third, images under 50x50 pixels or over 16MB are rejected. If bulk runs die midway, WP-CLI batch size is settable from 1 to 50, default 10. Distilled from vendor docs (alttext.ai/docs, the WordPress integration page and the support FAQ), plus the public WordPress.org plugin listing for install counts and system requirements

Best for: image-heavy sites that need alt text written back into the CMS automatically — e-commerce catalogues, publishers and long-running blogs on WordPress, Shopify, WooCommerce, Magento, BigCommerce, Contentful, DatoCMS or Storyblok — where the native integrations, page crawler, CSV import/export and developer API let you clear thousands of missing alt attributes without touching them by hand.
Who should skip it: if you have a few dozen images: write those yourself for free and you will describe them better than any model, because you know why each image is on the page — or your visuals are diagram-, product- or brand-specific enough that every generated line needs rewriting anyway, in which case the credits buy you a draft you were going to redo.

The natural comparison is accessiBe — accessiBe is an accessibility overlay — a script that patches issues in the visitor’s browser at runtime. AltText.ai writes real alt text into your CMS, so it lives in your source, ships to every visitor and is what search crawlers actually read. Pick the overlay for broad WCAG patching across a site you cannot easily edit; pick AltText.ai when you specifically want durable alt text in the page itself. Decide by which one fits the job above, not by the louder brand.
