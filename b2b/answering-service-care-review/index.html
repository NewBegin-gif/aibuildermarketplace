What Answering Service Care does

US-based live answering with real people, 24/7 and bilingual English/Spanish, with call transfers, SMS and email relays, a client portal and app on every plan. Monthly plans run $40 Starter, $179 Small Business (100 minutes), $329 Pro (250) and $639 Enterprise (500), around 10% off if you pay a year upfront. Read the small print first: Starter includes zero minutes, there is a $50 start-up fee mentioned only in the body text, and the explanation below the table quotes three prices that do not match the table above it (read 12 Aug 2026).

Pricing (2026)

(2026-08-12, read off their own pricing page, both billing toggles) Four plans, priced on included minutes. Billed monthly: Starter $40 with **zero** minutes included and $1.75 per minute after that; Small Business $179 for 100 minutes, then $1.70; Pro $329 for 250 minutes, then $1.60; Enterprise $639 for 500 minutes, then $1.35. Paying a year upfront takes about 10% off the monthly rate: $161, $296 and $575 for the three larger plans. There is also a $50 start-up fee, which appears only in the prose underneath the table and not on the plan cards. Every plan carries the same feature set — 24/7 cover, bilingual English/Spanish operators, call transfers, SMS and email relays, custom greetings and hold music, a client portal, usage reporting and the mobile app — so you are buying minutes, not capability, and there is no long-term contract. One caution about the page itself: the explanatory text below the table contradicts the table three times, quoting Pro at $319, Enterprise at $599 and Starter overage at $1.65. We have used the figures from the pricing table, which is the one the buttons act on. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Do the arithmetic before you pick a tier, because their own page does not. Starter buys the line and nothing else: at $40 plus $1.75 a minute, it stays the cheapest option only up to roughly 79 minutes a month — past that the $179 Small Business plan costs less. The same crossover logic repeats: Small Business gives way to Pro at about 188 minutes, and Pro gives way to Enterprise at about 444. Note too that Small Business is the one tier where an included minute ($1.79 each, $179 for 100) costs more than an overage minute ($1.70), so there is no saving in buying that bundle for its own sake. And the second thing to price in is the $50 start-up fee, which is easy to miss where they have put it.

Best for: a small business that loses money when a call goes to voicemail — trades, clinics, law and property firms — and wants real people answering around the clock without hiring a receptionist. It suits you especially if your call volume is genuinely low and predictable, because the plans are priced in minutes and nothing is gated behind a higher tier.
Who should skip it: if your callers mostly want things a human does not need to do — order status, opening hours, a password reset — because you are paying per minute for a person to read them out. Skip it too if your volume is high and variable: at 500-plus minutes you are past the published ladder and into a custom quote, and per-minute billing makes a busy month expensive in a way a flat-rate provider would not.
