What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The 50%-off banner sits at the top of the page and rewrites every figure on it. So the $39.50 that catches your eye is a three-month rate against a $79 list price. That is a 100% increase waiting in month four, on software that holds your books and is therefore painful to leave. The second thing to pin down is users: Lite includes two, and for a nonprofit with a bookkeeper, a treasurer and a director that is already tight, so establish the per-user cost before you commit.

What Aplos does

Fund accounting built for nonprofits and faith-based organisations, tracking restricted and unrestricted money separately. Lite is $79/month for 2 users, then $129 and $229. A 50%-off-for-three-months promotion was running in August 2026, so check what month four costs.

What buyers report about Aplos

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Aplos ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Reviewers who are not accountants say the fund structure is the reason they stay: restricted and unrestricted money stays separated without them having to invent a workaround, and volunteer treasurers describe getting productive without an accounting background.
  • • Donor-side work is the other consistent positive — contribution tracking and year-end giving statements come up repeatedly, alongside the training videos and onboarding material.
  • • Being cloud-hosted matters more here than in most categories, because reviewers describe boards and treasurers in different places working from the same books.
What they complain about
  • • Support consistency is the loudest complaint. A January 2026 two-star reviewer describes being unable to reach customer service at all; others report waits of around two weeks for follow-up and difficulty getting complex cases resolved rather than acknowledged.
  • • Several accounting mechanics are described as missing rather than awkward: contributions not posting at general-ledger level, no default expense coding per vendor, returned (NSF) cheques requiring duplicate entry, and no payroll of its own.
  • • Bank feeds draw reports of mismatches between what Aplos shows and what the bank statement says, which is exactly the reconciliation work the software is bought to remove.
  • • Price increases after signing are a recurring theme, including reviewers who say the subscription doubled without new features arriving, and features moving up into higher tiers.

Where the ratings disagree. The pricing complaints on Capterra are not about the entry price — they are about what happened afterwards. That is worth holding against a promotion that discounts the first three months: the reviewers describing a doubled subscription were already inside. Ask what the renewal price is, in writing, before the discount period sets your expectation.

When the paid plan is worth it

Start with the number you will actually pay in month four, not the one on the page today. When we read it on 15 August 2026 a promotion took 50% off the first three months with code SUMMER26, showing Lite at $39.50 against a list price of $79. That is a real saving of about $118 across a quarter, but it changes nothing about the ongoing cost, and a budget built on $39.50 breaks in the fourth month.

The published tiers are $79, $129 and $229 a month. The one hard limit worth checking before you commit is seats: Lite includes two users and so does Core, so a third person does not mean the next tier up, it means Advanced at $229. The trial is 15 days and takes no credit card, which is long enough to run a real month-end close -- do that rather than clicking around, because a fund accounting tool reveals itself at reconciliation, not at setup.

How to actually use Aplos

Aplos publishes a six-step quick-start order, and it pays to follow it literally because one step is awkward to undo. Import your people first -- donors and vendors from a spreadsheet -- then edit the chart of accounts you are given and set starting balances on your asset and liability accounts. Aplos is explicit that those balances should be the bank balances as of the first transaction you enter, so they belong in place before you record anything, not after.

Then set the annual budget, and create your purposes in the Donations section: purposes are what contribution statements are built from, and linking each one to its accounts is what makes donation deposits post into the bookkeeping as well. Inviting the rest of the team is the last step, and seats decide how far you get -- both Lite and Core include two users. The 15-day trial takes no card, which is enough to carry one real month-end close.

Distilled from Aplos's own Quick Start Guide for Accounting and Finances in its support centre (help.aplos.com/hc/en-us/articles/30708075177997-Quick-Start-Guide-for-Accounting-and-Finances), read 19 August 2026, together with the plan cards and seat counts on aplos.com/pricing, read the same day.

The natural comparison is QuickBooks — the comparison every nonprofit treasurer ends up making, and it turns on one question rather than price. QuickBooks is cheaper and more widely known, but it tracks classes, not funds — so restricted and unrestricted money live in the same pot with a label on top. If your auditor or your grant terms require true fund separation, that difference is the whole decision and the price gap is secondary.

What should I weigh before buying Finance & Accounting software?

Ask your accountant before you buy; it is the cheapest step in this whole decision. What matters is whether it exports in the format they already work in, whether the ledger mapping survives that export, and whether they can access it directly. A tool your accountant has to re-key by hand costs more than it saves.
