What Apollo does

Sales intelligence and engagement platform with a large B2B contact database. Find ideal-fit leads, enrich them and run multichannel outreach from one place.

Pricing (2026)

A free plan (limited credits, ~250 emails/day fair use); paid Basic about $49, Professional $79, Organization $119 per user/mo billed annually — higher monthly (2026). Credit-based limits mean the effective cost often runs higher. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The credit and export caps are the catch — real usage on enrichment and dialing often runs well above the sticker, and data accuracy is hit-or-miss, so verify before you outreach at scale.

When the paid plan is worth it

The free tier is a look, not a workflow: 900 credits a year, 2 sequences, and on non-paying plans Apollo only lets you connect Gmail mailboxes — that mailbox restriction, not the credit count, is usually what forces the upgrade. Basic at $49/user/month billed annually (30,000 credits/year) buys unlimited sequences and CRM integrations, and that is the jump most small teams actually need. Professional at $79 (48,000 credits/year) only pays back if you dial or run several mailboxes; it adds the US dialer, 5 mailboxes and AI research. Organization at $119 carries a three-seat minimum, so it starts around $357/month in total — you are buying SSO, the international dialer and 15 mailboxes, not better data. What upgrading does not fix: even Apollo's Unlimited plans are capped, at the lesser of dollars-paid divided by $0.025 or 1 million credits per account per year, and no tier takes domain and mailbox deliverability off your desk.

How to actually use Apollo

Apollo's own route is short: log in, install the Chrome extension, connect a mailbox, build a filtered list, then launch a sequence. Every paid plan includes a live 1:1 onboarding session at no extra cost — typically 30 to 60 minutes, with the invitation arriving within your first 30 days. Apollo's own claim is that most teams send their first sequence in that single session. Teams of 1-20 get the Essential track; 20+ get a Professional track that adds CRM and enrichment setup. Two things stall people. On a non-paying plan you can only connect Gmail mailboxes, so Outlook users hit a wall at the mailbox step. And Apollo's deliverability page describes domain warm-up as automated but publishes no timeframe, so you are guessing at when a new sending domain is safe — firing the first sequence too early is the common self-inflicted wound. An independent blog walkthrough (rajanarya.com) budgets roughly two days to set up and a first campaign within a week, and advises testing on a small list before exporting hundreds. Distilled from Apollo's own onboarding page (apollo.io/onboarding), the FAQ text on apollo.io/pricing and Apollo's deliverability page, plus one independent blog walkthrough (rajanarya.com); Apollo's knowledge base blocks automated access.
