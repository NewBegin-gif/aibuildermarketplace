What buyers report about Apollo

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Apollo ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • One tool instead of three — reviewers who previously paid for a database, a sequencer and an enrichment tool separately rate the consolidation as the main reason they stay.
  • • Breadth of the contact database, and that you can go from a search to a sequence without an export step.
What they complain about
  • • Data accuracy outside North America. US contact accuracy runs around 88%; reviewers report bounce rates of 15–25% on large exported lists, including on addresses marked verified.
  • • Credits do not roll over, and reviewers describe extra charges appearing in later months with no refund.
  • • Slow ticket response, mentioned on both G2 and Trustpilot.
  • • Interface lag once lists get very large.

Where the ratings disagree. Apollo scores about 4.7 on G2 across roughly 9,600 reviews, but around 2.9 on Trustpilot across about 1,000. That gap is worth understanding rather than averaging: G2 reviews are mostly written by people mid-contract who like the product, while Trustpilot collects the billing disputes, account suspensions and cancellation attempts. Read the G2 reviews for what the tool does and the Trustpilot ones for what happens when you want to leave.

When the paid plan is worth it

The free tier is a look, not a workflow: 900 credits a year, 2 sequences, and on non-paying plans Apollo only lets you connect Gmail mailboxes — that mailbox restriction, not the credit count, is usually what forces the upgrade. Basic at $49/user/month billed annually (30,000 credits/year) buys unlimited sequences and CRM integrations, and that is the jump most small teams actually need. Professional at $79 (48,000 credits/year) only pays back if you dial or run several mailboxes; it adds the US dialer, 5 mailboxes and AI research.

Organization at $119 carries a three-seat minimum, so it starts around $357/month in total — you are buying SSO, the international dialer and 15 mailboxes, not better data. What upgrading does not fix: even Apollo's Unlimited plans are capped, at the lesser of dollars-paid divided by $0.025 or 1 million credits per account per year, and no tier takes domain and mailbox deliverability off your desk.

How to actually use Apollo

Apollo's own route is short: log in, install the Chrome extension, connect a mailbox, build a filtered list, then launch a sequence. Every paid plan includes a live 1:1 onboarding session at no extra cost — typically 30 to 60 minutes, with the invitation arriving within your first 30 days. Apollo's own claim is that most teams send their first sequence in that single session. Teams of 1-20 get the Essential track; 20+ get a Professional track that adds CRM and enrichment setup.

Two things stall people. On a non-paying plan you can only connect Gmail mailboxes, so Outlook users hit a wall at the mailbox step. And Apollo's deliverability page describes domain warm-up as automated but publishes no timeframe, so you are guessing at when a new sending domain is safe — firing the first sequence too early is the common self-inflicted wound. An independent blog walkthrough (rajanarya.com) budgets roughly two days to set up and a first campaign within a week, and advises testing on a small list before exporting hundreds.

Frequently asked questions

What does Apollo cost per seat, and what do the credits mean?

Read from the pricing table on 11 August 2026: Free $0 with 900 credits per seat per year, Basic $49 with 30,000, Professional $79 with 48,000, and Organization $119 with 72,000 — all per seat per month billed annually, and the credits granted upfront on the paid tiers. Organization carries a three-seat minimum, so it starts around $357 a month before you add anyone. Even Apollo's Unlimited plans are capped, at the lesser of dollars-paid divided by $0.025 or 1 million credits per account per year.

Why do price comparison sites quote Apollo differently?

Because Apollo's plan table is drawn in the browser: fetch the page and no prices come back. What a machine does get is Apollo's own schema.org block on the same URL, and on 11 August 2026 that listed Free $0, Basic $49 and Professional $99 as monthly offers, with an Enterprise "contact for pricing" and no Organization tier at all — a different plan set from the one on screen. If a comparison page or an AI assistant quotes you Apollo prices, check them against the live table yourself.

What is the real limit on the free plan?

Not the credit count. The free tier gives 900 credits a year and 2 sequences, but the binding restriction is that non-paying plans can only connect Gmail mailboxes — Outlook users hit a wall at the mailbox step, and that is usually what forces the upgrade rather than running out of credits.

What should I weigh before buying Growth & Revenue software?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Small teams get the most out of this category when one clear problem is already costing real hours or revenue; buying ahead of that just adds cost and another login. Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.
