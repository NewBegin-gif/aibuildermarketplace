What Aspire is

Business account, cards, expense and payables for modern companies.

  • Multi-currency account — receive, hold and send in several currencies.
  • Corporate cards — physical and virtual cards with controls and cashback.
  • Expense management — capture, categorize and approve spend in one place.
  • Payables & invoicing — pay suppliers and bill customers internationally.

Where it genuinely helps

Consolidation for global startups. Aspire pulls account, cards, expenses and bill pay into one platform, which removes the usual patchwork of a bank plus three SaaS tools. For founders operating across borders — particularly in Southeast Asia — that integration and speed are the real draw versus a traditional business bank.

Pros & cons

  • Multi-currency support suited to cross-border businesses
  • Spend controls and cashback on cards
  • Faster onboarding and workflows than a traditional bank
  • Strong fit for startups and SMBs in APAC

Watch-outs

  • Availability and features vary by region — check yours
  • It's a finance platform, not a bank — understand the distinction
  • FX and fees still deserve a real comparison for your flows

Who it's for

Best for startups and SMBs — especially cross-border or APAC-based — that want their account, cards and spend management in one modern platform rather than a bank plus separate tools. If you need a full-service bank relationship or your region isn't well supported, weigh that first.

Why switch from a traditional business bank

As an ex-banker I'll be fair to banks: they're right when you need a deep, full-service relationship or strong local protections. But for a modern, cross-border startup, a traditional bank often means slow onboarding, clunky multi-currency handling, and a separate stack of expense and payments tools. Aspire's case is integration and speed — account, cards, expenses and payables in one place. Weigh that against the bank relationship and protections you actually need.

How hard is it to switch?

Low — and you don't have to go all-in. Opening Aspire doesn't require closing your bank, so you can run it alongside and route one currency or workflow through it first. Test a single month of real transactions, compare the landed costs and the time saved, and expand only if the numbers hold up.
