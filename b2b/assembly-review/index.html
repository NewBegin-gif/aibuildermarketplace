What Assembly does

AI-powered client portal for professional service firms: CRM, messaging, invoices, proposals, contracts and e-signatures under your own brand.

Pricing (2026)

(2026) A free plan, then a Recognition plan around $2–$3 per member/month (cheaper annually), scaling to Engagement, Culture Suite and custom Digital-HQ tiers. Rewards (gift-card redemptions) are funded separately from the per-seat fee. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

Correction after re-checking: The dossier price line no longer matches the vendor page. Assembly now publishes a single named plan — Empower at "$3 per user / month (billed yearly)" — plus a custom-priced Enterprise tier; the Recognition / Engagement / Culture Suite / Digital-HQ ladder is gone, no monthly (non-annual) rate is shown, and no free tier appears on the pricing page at all, although the help centre still references a free plan for sending gift cards. Both plan buttons lead to a demo booking rather than self-serve signup. Rewards remain funded separately from the per-seat fee. (re-verified 26 Jul 2026)

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The per-member price is genuinely low, but two honest notes. Recognition is only useful if leadership actually drives the habit — buy the software without a culture push and it goes quiet fast, which is a people problem no tool fixes. And the reward payouts (gift cards) are a real cost on top of the per-seat fee, so budget the points you'll fund, not just the licenses. As a cheap, broad engagement/recognition and workflow hub, though, it's strong value.

What buyers report about Assembly

Recurring themes from G2 and Software Advice, read in August 2026. We have not run Assembly ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Peer-to-peer alongside manager-to-employee recognition gives genuine 360-degree feedback rather than top-down only. Around 4.7 out of 5.
  • • The rewards catalogue is what employees actually engage with, and that is what makes a recognition programme survive its first quarter.
What they complain about
  • • The rewards merchant range is the recurring request — reviewers want more choice than the current catalogue offers.
  • • Rewards come as MasterCard; reviewers ask for virtual Visa as an option too. Worth checking against what your team can actually use locally.

When the paid plan is worth it

Assembly no longer publishes a tier ladder. The pricing page shows Empower at "$3 per user / month (billed yearly)" and an Enterprise tier with custom pricing; both buttons lead to a demo booking, not a checkout. A free plan still exists inside the product — the gift-card help article opens by telling free-plan accounts the page does not apply to them — but it reads as a gifting layer rather than a recognition layer: the recognition-settings article says only admins on paid Assembly accounts can turn on core values. Paying makes sense once you want values-tagged recognition, points and budget controls and analytics instead of just pushing gift cards out. What upgrading does not solve: rewards are funded separately from seats, and Assembly's own billing article says it does not offer invoice billing to any new customers, so you prefund a balance or get charged per transaction. Single sign-on is gated by plan too — the SAML article restricts it to Business and Enterprise plans, tier names that no longer appear on the pricing page, and warns that downgrading loses the connection.

How to actually use Assembly

Setup runs from the admin panel: at app.joinassembly.com click the Admin icon in the lower-left corner of the left nav, then work through Integrations, Recognition settings and Rewards. The route is short; the friction sits mostly outside the app. Microsoft Teams needs a Global Admin to install the chat app — if anyone else installs it, the app is visible only to that person. Slack asks you to allow Assembly access to your workspace. Core values, the setting that makes recognition mean something, is limited to paid accounts. Distilled from Built on Assembly's own documentation, all read this session: the Rewards collection on help.joinassembly.com (Account balance & how to add balance; Invoices & Monthly Billing, which states "We do not offer invoice billing to any new customers."; Overview: rewards billing and transactions; How to set up gift card rewards; Amazon Rewards in Assembly), the SAML Authentication + SSO article in the same help centre, the mirrored vendor articles on joinassembly.com/articles for configuring recognition settings and for installing the MS Teams and Slack chat apps, plus the pricing page. No independent walkthrough was found. Searches surfaced only Assembly's own blog and press releases, review-directory listings (Capterra, G2, Gartner) and comparison pages — none of which is an independent hands-on write-up, and the directory ratings are not used here as evidence. Every timing and fee figure above is what the vendor documents, not something measured on our side.

Common Assembly setup mistakes

Where Assembly setups actually go wrong, and what it costs you when they do. Same sources as the setup notes above — not from a walkthrough we ran ourselves.

The step teams underestimate is money: rewards run off a prepaid account balance, and Assembly's help centre puts bank transfer (ACH) at "3-6 business days" and wire at "2-10 business days", with credit card listed as instant but carrying "a 3.5% processing fee charged by Stripe" for customers onboarded on or after February 1, 2026 — their documented timing and terms, not our measurement.

  • Fund the balance before you announce the launch, not after.
  • Amazon rewards are limited to the United States and Canada, and require configuring Assembly as a purchasing system inside Amazon Business first.

Frequently asked questions

Is Assembly secure and reliable enough to depend on?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.

Who should not use Assembly?

Skip it if leadership won't actively champion recognition (the tool alone won't create the habit), or you need a deep, dedicated HRIS rather than an engagement layer. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
