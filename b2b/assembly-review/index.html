What Assembly does

Branded client portal for agencies and professional service firms: messaging, invoices, proposals, contracts and e-signatures in one place. Two meters run at once — Starter stops at 50 clients on $29/month billed yearly, and each extra internal user costs $29 on Professional and $59 on Advanced.

Pricing (2026)

(re-verified 2026-08-15, read on their own pricing page with both billing tabs open) Four tiers on yearly billing: Starter $29/month, Professional $99/month, Advanced $499/month and Enterprise from $2,000/month. On monthly billing the same four cost $49, $139, $599 and $2,400. Two of those moved since our 7 August read: Starter and Professional came down from $39 and $149, while Advanced went up from $399. Starter covers 1 internal user, 50 clients and 100 automation tasks a month; Professional 3 internal users then $29 for each extra, 500 clients and 1,000 automation tasks; Advanced 5 internal users then $59 each, with unlimited clients and automations, full white-labelling and HIPAA with a BAA; Enterprise adds custom SSO and a sandbox workspace. Billed monthly, those extra internal users cost $39 and $79 instead. Every tier includes the CRM, client portal, messaging, invoices, proposals and contracts, tasks, file-sharing and intake forms. No free plan is offered; the trial runs 14 days and takes no card. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Two meters run at once and they move at different speeds. Clients is the one that bites first: Starter stops at 50, and the step to 500 costs $70 more a month. Internal users is the other, and it gets worse as you climb — an extra person is $29 on Professional and $59 on Advanced, so the more expensive plan makes each additional colleague more expensive too, which is the opposite of what a tier ladder usually does. The last step is the steepest in the category: $499 to a $2,000 minimum, and what that buys is SSO and security controls rather than more capacity.

When the paid plan is worth it

Starter is a real plan rather than a demo: $29/month on yearly billing carries the CRM, the branded portal, messaging, invoices, proposals, contracts, tasks and forms in full. What it does not carry is room — 1 internal user, 50 clients and 100 automation tasks a month — so what pushes you up a tier is almost always a count, not a missing feature. Professional at $99 is where the portal stops looking like Assembly's and starts looking like yours: a custom domain for the portal, a custom email domain, the API plus Zapier and Make, custom apps and app-visibility settings. Advanced at $499 is a compliance tier rather than a capacity one — full white-labelling, an audit log, client access permissions, HIPAA with a BAA, and MFA you can enforce on everyone. Two things paying more does not fix. Extra internal users are billed on top at $29 each on Professional and $59 each on Advanced, so the pricier plan charges more per colleague, which is the opposite of how a tier ladder usually works. And the last step is $499 to a $2,000 minimum, where capacity is already unlimited: Enterprise buys custom SSO, a sandbox workspace and security controls the pricing page itself still marks coming soon.

How to actually use Assembly

Assembly's own documentation puts two setup steps in an order that matters, and your plan decides whether you can follow it. A custom domain is a Professional feature ($99/month), and the domain guide advises setting one up before you invite clients — otherwise, in its words, they will need to log in again. A firm that starts on Starter and moves the portal to portal.yourfirm.com later pays for that ordering with a second round of client logins. Connecting it is Settings > Domains, a CNAME added at your registrar, then 5 minutes to 24 hours of waiting; the docs single out Cloudflare, where the proxy — the orange cloud — has to be off. Getting clients in runs three ways: one at a time, a CSV bulk import, or direct sign-up. The import is where the documented limits bite — First name, Last name and Email are required (or a Full name field that has to contain both), 200 rows per CSV file, and a comma anywhere in a field value makes the import fail. The last step asks for Import only or Import and invite, and clients left in Not invited status get no email notifications at all, which the docs suggest using on purpose while you upload files and assign forms first. Automations are thinner than the plan limits suggest. The feature ships with three templates — assign a contract or a form when a client first logs in, and upload files or folders when a client or company is created — plus a build-from-scratch option, with more promised over time. Assembly Studio, the AI app builder, is documented as private beta. Every limit and timing above is what the vendor documents, not something we measured. Distilled from Assembly's own pricing page, re-read 15 August 2026 with both the yearly and monthly billing tabs open, plus their documentation at assembly.com/docs — the custom-domains, inviting-clients and automations guides and the Studio welcome page — read 7 August 2026. No independent hands-on walkthrough was found: searches returned vendor material, review-directory listings and comparison pages, and directory ratings are not used as evidence here.

Best for: agencies and professional services firms who want one branded place for clients to message, sign, pay and share files instead of four tools and an email thread.
Who should skip it: if you have a handful of clients and already use a shared drive and an invoicing tool, because the value here is the single branded portal and that only matters once clients get confused about where things live.

The natural comparison is Copilot or HoneyBook — the direct client-portal peers — compare on client count and cost per extra internal user, not on the feature list. Decide by which one fits the job above, not by the louder brand.

Frequently asked questions

Is Assembly secure and reliable enough to depend on?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.

Who should not use Assembly?

Skip it if you have a handful of clients and already use a shared drive and an invoicing tool, because the value here is the single branded portal and that only matters once clients get confused about where things live. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
