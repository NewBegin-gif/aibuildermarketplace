Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 17 Aug 2026

Assembly publishes exactly one price: Empower at $3 per user per month, billed yearly, with an Enterprise tier quoted on request for advanced security, custom integrations and tailored implementation. There is no monthly/yearly toggle and no other published tier. Empower covers milestone celebrations, announcements, awards and challenges, the global reward catalogue, peer-to-peer recognition, points and budget controls, the Dora Hub AI insights and advanced people analytics.

Note that the reward points themselves are funded by you on top of the seat price, so budget the catalogue separately from the licence. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Clients is the one that bites first: Starter stops at 50, and the step to 500 costs $70 more a month. Internal users is the other, and it gets worse as you climb. An extra person is $29 on Professional and $59 on Advanced, so the more expensive plan makes each additional colleague more expensive too — the opposite of what a tier ladder usually does. The last step is the steepest in the category: $499 to a $2,000 minimum, and what that buys is SSO and security controls rather than more capacity.

What Assembly does

Branded client portal for agencies and professional service firms: messaging, invoices, proposals, contracts and e-signatures in one place. Two meters run at once — Starter stops at 50 clients on $29/month billed yearly, and each extra internal user costs $29 on Professional and $59 on Advanced.

What buyers report about Assembly

What they praise

When the paid plan is worth it

Starter is a real plan rather than a demo: $29/month on yearly billing carries the CRM, the branded portal, messaging, invoices, proposals, contracts, tasks and forms in full. What it does not carry is room — 1 internal user, 50 clients and 100 automation tasks a month — so what pushes you up a tier is almost always a count, not a missing feature. Professional at $99 is where the portal stops looking like Assembly's and starts looking like yours: a custom domain for the portal, a custom email domain, the API plus Zapier and Make, custom apps and app-visibility settings.

Advanced at $499 is a compliance tier rather than a capacity one — full white-labelling, an audit log, client access permissions, HIPAA with a BAA, and MFA you can enforce on everyone. Two things paying more does not fix. Extra internal users are billed on top at $29 each on Professional and $59 each on Advanced, so the pricier plan charges more per colleague, which is the opposite of how a tier ladder usually works.

And the last step is $499 to a $2,000 minimum, where capacity is already unlimited: Enterprise buys custom SSO, a sandbox workspace and security controls the pricing page itself still marks coming soon.

How to actually use Assembly

Assembly's own documentation puts two setup steps in an order that matters, and your plan decides whether you can follow it. A custom domain is a Professional feature ($99/month), and the domain guide advises setting one up before you invite clients — otherwise, in its words, they will need to log in again. A firm that starts on Starter and moves the portal to portal.yourfirm.com later pays for that ordering with a second round of client logins.

Connecting it is Settings > Domains, a CNAME added at your registrar, then 5 minutes to 24 hours of waiting; the docs single out Cloudflare, where the proxy — the orange cloud — has to be off. Getting clients in runs three ways: one at a time, a CSV bulk import, or direct sign-up. The import is where the documented limits bite — First name, Last name and Email are required (or a Full name field that has to contain both), 200 rows per CSV file, and a comma anywhere in a field value makes the import fail.

The last step asks for Import only or Import and invite, and clients left in Not invited status get no email notifications at all, which the docs suggest using on purpose while you upload files and assign forms first. Automations are thinner than the plan limits suggest. The feature ships with three templates — assign a contract or a form when a client first logs in, and upload files or folders when a client or company is created — plus a build-from-scratch option, with more promised over time.

Assembly Studio, the AI app builder, is documented as private beta. Every limit and timing above is what the vendor documents, not something we measured.

Distilled from Assembly's own pricing page, re-read 15 August 2026 with both the yearly and monthly billing tabs open, plus their documentation at assembly.com/docs — the custom-domains, inviting-clients and automations guides and the Studio welcome page — read 7 August 2026. No independent hands-on walkthrough was found: searches returned vendor material, review-directory listings and comparison pages, and directory ratings are not used as evidence here.

The natural comparison is Copilot or HoneyBook — the direct client-portal peers — compare on client count and cost per extra internal user, not on the feature list.

Frequently asked questions

What does Assembly cost per user?

$3 per user per month, billed yearly, for the Empower plan — the only price Assembly publishes, read on their own pricing page on 17 August 2026 and unchanged in our July and August captures. Enterprise is quoted on request. Remember the reward points come out of your own budget on top of that.

Is Assembly a good fit for a remote-first company?

That is close to the exact question people type before landing here, so here is the honest split. Peer-to-peer recognition, milestones, announcements and a global reward catalogue are the core, and reviewers at remote-first companies describe adoption sticking. The thing to check before you buy is integration: on Capterra a reviewer explains they rarely log in precisely because it does not connect to the other tools their company lives in. A recognition tool people have to remember to visit is a recognition tool people forget.

What are the known weak spots?

Reporting and customisation are the two that recur in 2026 reviews, named even by people who otherwise rate it well. Image and file sharing is also reported to lose resolution or crop, and one reviewer found how many points someone received was unclear in the interface until a later release. None of these are dealbreakers for recognition itself; all of them matter if you plan to report on it.

What should I weigh before buying Business Operations software?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.
