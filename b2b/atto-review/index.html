Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 5 Sep 2026

Per user per month, billed annually: Starter $5.65, Plus $7.95 (job codes, breaks and notes), Advanced $10.75 (job costing), Expert $16.55 (talk to sales). Payroll processing is an add-on at $5.95 per payroll user a month plus a $39.95 monthly base fee. No base price and no setup fee on the plans themselves, unlimited jobs and locations, and a free sign-up to start.

History: our earlier read put Starter at about $6.95 and the top tier at $19.95; the current ladder is lower on every rung. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What buyers report about Atto

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Atto ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Field teams get their hours in without confusion, and reviewers name the absence of pay disputes as the concrete result.
  • • From $6.95 per user per month, cheaper per head than most of the category.
What they complain about
  • • You are billed per user even for a manager who only views hours and never tracks their own. Reviewers use the word greedy about it — count your seats accordingly.
  • • GPS location tracking is unreliable unless an iPhone is present and unlocked, which is a real limitation for the job it is sold for.
  • • The app freezes while generating reports.
  • • Support is email-only and reviewers want it faster. There is no free plan.

When the paid plan is worth it

Atto has no free tier: after the trial you pick from a paid ladder, and the gate that matters is GPS. Starter, $6.95 per user/month billed monthly or $5.95 billed annually, covers clock-in/out, job details and overtime calculations but not real-time GPS tracking. That starts on Plus, $9.95 monthly or $7.95 annually. If you came to Atto for GPS-verified timesheets, which is the reason most field crews look at it, Starter is not the product you had in mind and Plus is the honest entry price.

Advanced at $12.95 monthly adds the tablet Kiosk, mileage tracking and job-specific pay rates. Expert at $19.95 monthly, $16.55 annually, is mostly service — priority support, personalized onboarding — plus API access, so it only pays back if you actually need to push data somewhere Atto does not already export to. Upgrading fixes none of the things that break rollouts: every phone still needs continuous location permission, and the crew still has to accept being located. Annual billing saves 20% on every tier.

How to actually use Atto

Sign up at dashboard.attotime.com, fill in name, company, industry and password, and the 14-day free trial starts at signup with no card. The documented order is: invite the team from Settings > Invite New Members, by email or by sharing the account link. Then set company settings for location tracking, workweek and overtime rules, breaks and mileage, and assign Admin, Manager or Employee roles and pay rates under Settings > Manage Team.

Atto says you can invite first and tune the rest later, so a crew can be clocking in the same day.

Common Atto setup mistakes

Where Atto setups actually go wrong, and what it costs you when they do. Same sources as the setup notes above — not from a walkthrough we ran ourselves.

The friction is on the phone, not the dashboard.

  • Atto's clock-in troubleshooting lists the permissions each phone needs: on iOS, Always or While Using the App with precise location on; on Android, Allow all the time or while-in-use.
  • Which one depends on whether your account runs continuous or clock-in-only tracking.
  • Miss it and managers just see Location Permissions OFF in Team Activity.
  • Other quiet blockers: an account can require a Job Code before clock-in, and job-site boundaries will refuse a clock-in from outside the perimeter.

Frequently asked questions

What should I weigh before buying Operations & Workflow software?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.
