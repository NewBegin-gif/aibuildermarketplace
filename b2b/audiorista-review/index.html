Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 15 Aug 2026

(2026-08-15, read on their own pricing page) Demo is $29/mo and Lite $240/mo for iOS and Android apps. Business is $400/mo per app, plus a tiered variable fee from $0.80 per monthly active user. Enterprise sits on that same $400 per app with further fees based on services, features and commitment, and the plan card quotes an Enterprise floor of $5,000/mo. 30-day free trial.

Demo carries all Lite features except publishing to the App Store and Google Play, and monetization is disabled on it — a preview tier rather than a product. Paid app sales, RevenueCat in-app subscriptions and Zapier webshop sync start at Lite; the web app, Stripe web payments and push-notification campaigns start at Business. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The Business tier adds a per-active-user fee from $0.80 on top of $400 per app, so growth literally raises the bill — and Apple and Google's own store commissions still apply to in-app sales on top of everything.

When the paid plan is worth it

The $29 Demo tier is a sandbox rather than a product: Audiorista's own comparison table says monetization is disabled there, statistics are off, and you cannot publish to the App Store or Google Play. The first tier that ships anything is Lite at $240/mo, covering up to 100 titles, up to 200 included active monthly users, one admin and simple stats — workable for a single creator with a fixed catalog.

It stops paying back as soon as you pass those limits, because the next step is Business at $400 per app per month plus a tiered variable fee starting from $0.8 per monthly active user, capped at 10,000 catalog items; Enterprise starts from $5,000/mo. Business is also where the web app, Stripe web payments, push-notification campaigns, multiple admins and automated RSS syncing live — on Demo and Lite the pricing table still marks push notifications as coming soon.

Upgrading does not remove Apple's and Google's commission on in-app subscriptions, does not supply the developer accounts (you bring those), and does not cap the per-user fee: growth raises the bill.

How to actually use Audiorista

Sign up, choose a plan, and a Project Wizard sets the foundation — template, theme, layout, language, integrations, app name — then hands you a Launch Checklist in the dashboard that runs from branding and first titles through to requesting iOS and Android builds. You preview on a real phone through TestFlight or Firebase App Tester, logging in with your Audiorista credentials.

Common Audiorista setup mistakes

Where Audiorista setups actually go wrong, and what it costs you when they do. Same sources as the setup notes above — not from a walkthrough we ran ourselves.

The friction sits downstream.

  • Uploads are transcoded to encrypted HLS before they play; Audiorista's stated baseline is a 5 minute wait, longer at peaks (their number, not our timing).
  • Publishing is yours to own: you supply the Apple Developer and Google Play accounts, generate an iOS distribution certificate and an Android keystore, and upload both to Audiorista.
  • Apple organisation enrolment needs a D-U-N-S number, and Audiorista tells you to allow up to 5 business days for it.
  • On Demo and Lite, RSS import is manual and one-time; automated syncing is Business-only and support has to switch it on.
  • The 30-day Lite trial requires a credit card.

The natural comparison is Uscreen — Uscreen is the video-first heavyweight for creator apps; Audiorista is audio-native — leaner for podcasts and audiobooks, with per-user fees appearing at the Business tier.
