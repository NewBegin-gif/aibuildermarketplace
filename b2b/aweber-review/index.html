Best for

small businesses and creators who want reliable, simple email marketing with a genuinely usable free tier.

Skip it if

you need advanced, behaviour-driven automation — newer tools go deeper for the money.

What AWeber does

Email marketing and automation for small businesses and creators: drag-and-drop newsletters, prompt-built landing pages, autoresponders and sign-up forms, AI writing help, and since August 2026 a ChatGPT plugin and Claude connector that read a whole automation back with its engagement numbers.

What buyers report about AWeber

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run AWeber ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise

  • • It permits affiliate content in emails, with consent and within guidelines — which several competitors do not. If that is your model, it narrows the field fast.
  • • 4.3 out of 5 across 176 Trustpilot reviews (read 24 August 2026), with the interface repeatedly called easy to learn.

  • • Advanced segmentation is limited — if you segment deeply, check it against your list first.
  • • Mobile rendering is not always optimised.

When the paid plan is worth it

AWeber still has a free tier, but it is no longer shown on the pricing page; you reach it through the separate free signup page, which documents "Up to 500 subscribers" and "3,000 emails per month". Those are the only two allowance figures that page prints, and neither is expressed as a multiple of your list. The paid cards, by contrast, promise sends of 10x your subscriber maximum on Lite and 12x on Plus.

Lite ($12.49/month billed annually, $15.00 month-to-month at 500 subscribers) holds you to 1 email list, 3 landing pages, 3 email automations, 3 users and 1 custom segment. Plus ($19.99/month annually, $30.00 monthly at 500) removes those caps and cuts the ecommerce transaction fee from 1.0% to 0.6%. Behavioural automation, webpage tracking and the free migration service are marked as excluded from Lite in AWeber's own comparison table, so if automation is the reason you are paying, Lite is the wrong stop.

Upgrading does not decouple price from list size: at 25,000 subscribers the month-to-month cost is $210.00 for Lite and $250.00 for Plus.

How to actually use AWeber

Signup collects your company name, website, industry and a postal address (AWeber notes this is required by CAN-SPAM), then asks whether you have subscribers to migrate. The real work is under Domains & Addresses: add a sending address and click the link in AWeber's verification email, then select it as your From address under List Settings. The docs warn against a generic address such as name@gmail.com. Next, authenticate the domain — CNAME records for DKIM plus a TXT record for DMARC.

AWeber offers automatic setup for over 40 domain providers; otherwise you paste records into your DNS by hand and, per the docs, allow up to 48 hours for the records to propagate. This step bites later: AWeber re-checks sender authentication at send time, and a broadcast whose domain no longer validates is returned to Draft status. Second snag if you are migrating: imports run through a permission check and can be pulled for manual review.

AWeber says such imports are typically reviewed within 1 business day (their claim, not our timing) — until then nothing sends.

Frequently asked questions

Is AWeber's free plan still available?

Yes, but not from the pricing page, which now leads with a 14-day trial. The free tier lives on AWeber's separate free-signup page and documents two limits: up to 500 subscribers and 3,000 emails per month. Both were still there on 1 October 2026. Neither figure is expressed as a multiple of your list, unlike the paid cards, which promise sends of 10x your subscriber maximum on Lite and 12x on Plus.

What will AWeber cost as the list grows?

Price tracks subscriber count on every paid tier. At 500 subscribers Lite is $12.49 and Plus $19.99 per month billed annually; the published grid runs up to $1,050.00 a month, and at 25,000 subscribers the month-to-month cost is $210.00 for Lite and $250.00 for Plus. Upgrading does not decouple the price from the list; it removes caps. Lite holds you to 1 list, 3 landing pages, 3 automations, 3 users and 1 custom segment, and AWeber's own comparison table excludes behavioural automation, webpage tracking and the free migration service from Lite.

What is the deliverability setup nobody warns you about?

Domain authentication, and it bites twice. You add CNAME records for DKIM and a TXT record for DMARC — AWeber automates this for over 40 domain providers, otherwise you paste them by hand and the docs say to allow up to 48 hours for propagation. The part that catches people later: AWeber re-checks sender authentication at send time, and a broadcast whose domain no longer validates is returned to Draft status. If you are migrating a list, the import also goes through a permission check that AWeber says is typically reviewed within 1 business day — their claim, not our timing — and nothing sends until it clears.

What do AWeber's AI tools actually cover?

Four of them are named on AWeber's own pricing page, read on 9 September 2026: an AI Page Builder for landing pages, an AI Signup Form Builder, an AI Writing Assistant for email copy and an AI Subject Line Assistant. They are presented as part of the product rather than as a separate paid add-on, and they sit alongside the ChatGPT plugin and Claude connector AWeber shipped in August 2026. What we have not tested is output quality — we have not run these ourselves, so treat this as a list of what exists, not a verdict on how good it is. If you are choosing between AWeber and a cheaper sender on price alone, this is the part of the bundle that does not show up in a price comparison.

What should I weigh before buying Growth & Revenue software?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Small teams get the most out of this category when one clear problem is already costing real hours or revenue; buying ahead of that just adds cost and another login. Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.
