When the paid plan is worth it

AWeber still has a free tier, but it is no longer shown on the pricing page; you reach it through the separate free signup page, which documents "Up to 500 subscribers" and "3,000 emails per month". Those are the only two allowance figures that page prints, and neither is expressed as a multiple of your list, unlike the paid cards, which promise sends of 10x your subscriber maximum on Lite and 12x on Plus. Lite ($12.49/month billed annually, $15.00 month-to-month at 500 subscribers) holds you to 1 email list, 3 landing pages, 3 email automations, 3 users and 1 custom segment. Plus ($19.99/month annually, $30.00 monthly at 500) removes those caps and cuts the ecommerce transaction fee from 1.0% to 0.6%. Behavioural automation, webpage tracking and the free migration service are marked as excluded from Lite in AWeber's own comparison table, so if automation is the reason you are paying, Lite is the wrong stop. Upgrading does not decouple price from list size: at 25,000 subscribers the month-to-month cost is $210.00 for Lite and $250.00 for Plus.

How to actually use AWeber

Signup collects your company name, website, industry and a postal address (AWeber notes this is required by CAN-SPAM), then asks whether you have subscribers to migrate. The real work is under Domains & Addresses: add a sending address and click the link in AWeber's verification email, then select it as your From address under List Settings. The docs warn against a generic address such as name@gmail.com. Next, authenticate the domain — CNAME records for DKIM plus a TXT record for DMARC. AWeber offers automatic setup for over 40 domain providers; otherwise you paste records into your DNS by hand and, per the docs, allow up to 48 hours for the records to propagate. This step bites later: AWeber re-checks sender authentication at send time, and a broadcast whose domain no longer validates is returned to Draft status. Second snag if you are migrating: imports run through a permission check and can be pulled for manual review. AWeber says such imports are typically reviewed within 1 business day (their claim, not our timing) — until then nothing sends. Frequently asked questions

Is AWeber's free plan still available?

Yes, but not from the pricing page, which now leads with a 14-day trial. The free tier lives on AWeber's separate free-signup page and documents two limits: up to 500 subscribers and 3,000 emails per month. Both were still there on 11 August 2026. Neither figure is expressed as a multiple of your list, unlike the paid cards, which promise sends of 10x your subscriber maximum on Lite and 12x on Plus.

What will AWeber cost as the list grows?

Price tracks subscriber count on every paid tier. At 500 subscribers Lite is $12.49 and Plus $19.99 per month billed annually; the published grid runs up to $1,050.00 a month, and at 25,000 subscribers the month-to-month cost is $210.00 for Lite and $250.00 for Plus. Upgrading does not decouple the price from the list; it removes caps. Lite holds you to 1 list, 3 landing pages, 3 automations, 3 users and 1 custom segment, and AWeber's own comparison table excludes behavioural automation, webpage tracking and the free migration service from Lite.

What is the deliverability setup nobody warns you about?

Domain authentication, and it bites twice. You add CNAME records for DKIM and a TXT record for DMARC — AWeber automates this for over 40 domain providers, otherwise you paste them by hand and the docs say to allow up to 48 hours for propagation. The part that catches people later: AWeber re-checks sender authentication at send time, and a broadcast whose domain no longer validates is returned to Draft status. If you are migrating a list, the import also goes through a permission check that AWeber says is typically reviewed within 1 business day — their claim, not our timing — and nothing sends until it clears.

Is AWeber worth it for a small team?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Small teams get the most out of this category when one clear problem is already costing real hours or revenue; buying ahead of that just adds cost and another login. Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.

Who should not use AWeber?

Skip it if you need advanced, behaviour-driven automation — newer tools go deeper for the money. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
