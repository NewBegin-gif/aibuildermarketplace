What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Auto-publishing 30 AI articles a month and building $800+ in backlinks monthly is exactly the scaled-content-and-links pattern Google's spam policies (Helpful Content, link-spam) are written to catch. It can lift metrics short-term; on a brand you're building for years, it's a real durability risk. The 90-day money-back guarantee is a genuine plus that offsets some of that.

What BabyLoveGrowth does

AI SEO/GEO content automation — auto-publishes optimized articles, builds backlinks and runs keyword research, aimed at fast hands-off organic growth for lean teams.

When the paid plan is worth it

There is no free tier — only a 3-day trial — so the real decision is Grow versus Scale, and both are priced per website and billed yearly. Grow at €75 a month (€900 a year, list €247) already carries the full engine: up to 30 auto-published articles a month, $800 worth of backlinks built monthly, the GEO audit and CMS publishing. Scale at €224 a month (€2,691 a year, list €599) adds no articles and no extra backlink budget.

Its own list reads everything in Grow plus a dedicated SEO/GEO specialist capped at 2 hours per month, 3 additional content languages and beta access. You are buying a strategist and multilingual reach, not more output, so it only pays back if you genuinely publish in several markets. Running multiple sites is where the discounts sit: the docs list 10% off for 2-4 websites, rising to 20% at 15+. Upgrading fixes none of the friction: article styling stays your theme's problem, new sites still wait 15 to 20 days for first backlink placements, and the docs state there are no refunds after the trial ends. Read that against the 90-day traffic guarantee on the pricing page.

How to actually use BabyLoveGrowth

Onboarding wants your website URL, a business description, target audience, a brand colour hex, a URL whose writing style should be copied, and your sitemap URL for internal linking. You then approve a 30-day content plan and connect a CMS under Settings > Integrations. The vendor advertises "Live in 4 minutes" — their claim, not our timing. The publishing hop is where people stall. WordPress is a 6-step wizard: install the official plugin, activate it, paste an Integration Key from the WordPress sidebar, then publish a test article; the card reads "Awaiting test" until that first publish succeeds.

No CMS? Blog Hosting puts the blog on blog.yoursite.com behind one CNAME record, and the docs warn propagation can take 1-2 hours and that a Cloudflare-proxied record must be flipped from orange cloud to grey or the blog will not connect. Backlinks lag longest: the docs say new sites typically wait 15 to 20 days for first placements, well past the 3-day trial.
