What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Auto-publishing 30 to 120 AI articles a month while swapping backlinks with 4,000 other member sites is exactly the scaled-content-and-reciprocal-links pattern Google's spam policies are written to catch, and the vendor's own FAQ describes the exchange in those terms: your site places links for others as theirs place links for you. It can lift metrics short-term; on a brand you're building for years, it's a real durability risk. The 90-day money-back guarantee used to offset some of that, and we can no longer say it does: the plan cards promise it, and the FAQ on the same page says no refunds are given after the three-day trial ends. Ask for those terms in writing before you pay for a year.

When the paid plan is worth it

There is no free tier — only a 3-day trial — so the real decision is Grow versus Scale, with the billing toggle deciding a quarter of the bill. Grow is €99 a month, or €75 if you commit to a year; Scale is €399, or €291 yearly. What changed since our last reading is what Scale is: it now carries 120 branded articles a month against 30 on Grow, $1,600+ of backlinks against $800+, 50 tracked AI prompts against 10, and unlimited seats against one.

Divide those out and the monthly ladder costs €3.30 per article on Grow and €3.33 on Scale, so the step up buys volume, not a discount. The two things you cannot get on Grow at any volume are the dedicated 1-on-1 SEO/GEO specialist and the monthly report with a human; the engine, the languages, the publishing integrations and the site audit are identical on both. Scale therefore pays back only if you genuinely publish at four times the rate, or if that specialist is why you are buying.

And read the refund terms before choosing the yearly column: the cards promise 90 days money back, the FAQ below them says refunds stop with the trial.

How to actually use BabyLoveGrowth

Onboarding wants your website URL, a business description, target audience, a brand colour hex, a URL whose writing style should be copied, and your sitemap URL for internal linking. You then approve a 30-day content plan and connect a CMS under Settings > Integrations. The vendor advertises "Live in 4 minutes" — their claim, not our timing. The publishing hop is where people stall. WordPress is a 6-step wizard: install the official plugin, activate it, paste an Integration Key from the WordPress sidebar, then publish a test article; the card reads "Awaiting test" until that first publish succeeds.

No CMS? Blog Hosting puts the blog on blog.yoursite.com behind one CNAME record, and the docs warn propagation can take 1-2 hours and that a Cloudflare-proxied record must be flipped from orange cloud to grey or the blog will not connect. Backlinks lag longest: the docs say new sites typically wait 15 to 20 days for first placements, well past the 3-day trial.

Frequently asked questions

Is the 90-day money-back guarantee real, when the FAQ says there are no refunds?

Both sentences are on the pricing page, and they contradict each other. The plan cards and the banner beneath them promise "90-day protection: see your organic visibility improve, or we refund you in full", repeated as "90-day money-back guarantee" under each plan. The FAQ at the bottom of that same page says the opposite: "We offer a 3-day free trial period to ensure our service meets your needs... due to the costs associated with external APIs and AI services, we cannot offer refunds after the trial period ends." A neighbouring answer confirms the trial ends on day three and you are charged for the plan you selected. So the only refund window the FAQ acknowledges is those three days. Get the 90-day terms in writing before you pay, and ask specifically what counts as organic visibility not improving and who measures it — that is the whole difference between a year you can walk back and one you cannot.

Why does the same plan show €99 in one place and €75 in another?

Because the page has a Monthly/Yearly toggle, and the comparison table below it does not follow that toggle. Read on 19 September 2026: the page loads on the monthly setting at €99 a month for Grow and €399 for Scale. Click "Pay yearly · save 25%" and the cards switch to €75 and €291 a month, labelled "Billed yearly". The struck-through €247 and €599 stay put in both settings, and the page prints no annual total anywhere. The long feature table further down keeps printing "Grow €99/mo" and "Scale €399/mo" even while the cards above it show the yearly prices, so a screenshot of that table is always the monthly ladder. One more thing if you are outside Europe: read from a European connection the page prices in euros, while the structured data embedded in the same page carries the figures 99 and 399 as USD.

What does Scale actually buy over Grow?

Four times the output, at almost exactly the same price per article. Grow is 30 branded articles a month, 10 AI prompts tracked daily across ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini, $800+ a month of backlinks, one seat and email support. Scale is 120 articles across four languages each with its own market keyword research, 50 prompts tracked, $1,600+ a month of higher-DR backlinks, unlimited seats, priority same-day support, a dedicated 1-on-1 SEO/GEO specialist and monthly reporting with a human. Divide it out and the monthly ladder is €3.30 per article on Grow against €3.33 on Scale, so the tier is not a volume discount — it is a bigger meter plus the two things Grow has no version of at all, the specialist and the human report. Everything else in the table is identical on both: same engine, same 50-plus languages, same publishing integrations, same audit.

The backlinks come from an exchange — what does that mean for my own site?

It means your site becomes a publisher for other members, not only a beneficiary. The vendor's own FAQ describes it plainly: you join a network of 4,000+ niche-relevant sites where members automatically exchange backlinks, they place your links inside real articles on relevant sites in your industry, and your site does the same for others. Reciprocal placement at that scale is the pattern Google's link spam policy names directly, and it is a different question from the AI-content one the vendor does answer on that page — their reply there is that Google penalises low quality rather than AI as such, with a link to Google's 2023 statement, which says nothing about link exchanges. Adult, gambling and illegal niches are excluded from the network. If the links are the reason you are buying, price the plan as if they were switched off.
