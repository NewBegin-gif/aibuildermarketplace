What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Some large retailers and brand registries — notably certain Amazon Brand Registry and big-box requirements — increasingly ask for a GS1-issued prefix tied to your own company, and a third-party barcode (legitimate and globally unique as it is) may not satisfy that specific gatekeeping. For most marketplaces and independent retail it's completely fine and far cheaper; just confirm your channel's exact requirement first.

What Barcodes Talk does

Authentic UPC and EAN barcodes for retail and Amazon listings. Buy GS1-sourced barcodes for your products as a one-time purchase, without an annual GS1 membership.

When the paid plan is worth it

There is no free tier and no subscription — the only ladder is quantity. The buy page runs many more tiers than those quoted here: "1 Barcodes - $5.00", "10 Barcodes - $15.00 ($1.50 ea)", "100 Barcodes - $45.00 ($0.45 ea)", "1,000 Barcodes - $180.00 ($0.18 ea)" and "100,000 Barcodes - $3,500.00 ($0.035 ea)". The unit price falls fastest at the bottom, so stepping off single codes into a small block is the one move that pays for itself the moment you expect more than a handful of SKUs.

It keeps falling above that, but the rungs tighten near the top: $0.048 each at 25,000 against $0.035 at 100,000. Because the codes carry no renewal fee and, per their help centre, do not expire, buying headroom is cheap — but coming back later costs nothing either, so there is no lock-in argument for a bigger block. What more spend does not buy is a GS1 company prefix in your own name.

Their Works for Amazon Guarantee carves out Brand Registry sellers, where GTINs must be rented from GS1, and also excludes books, restricted major brands, misconfigured bundles and category-rule breaches. No quantity tier changes that.

How to actually use Barcodes Talk

Checkout is the whole setup: choose a quantity, pay once, and the package downloads immediately — "UPC & EAN formats · High-res images (EPS & JPEG) · Certificate of GTIN Assignment". Nothing is provisioned for you afterwards. The part that catches people is what "registering" actually means. Their help centre says there is no universal barcode database: you match each number to a product in your own system, then submit the product data separately to every store, marketplace and distributor.

A purchase does auto-create a listing in Bar Codes Talk's own BCT GEPIR — their registry, not GS1's. So a GS1 lookup still surfaces the original pre-2002 prefix holder rather than your company, and that mismatch is what triggers Amazon's "not GS1" messages. Their documented fix is an Amazon case with the certificate plus product photos, "typically takes 1-3 business days", and they say Amazon can take "up to 72 hours to recognize new GS1 registrations" (their claim, not our timing). Brand Registry sellers are excluded outright, so plan the channel before you buy.

Frequently asked questions

How many barcodes do I actually need?

One for every uniquely sellable version of a product, which is almost always more than people expect. The vendor puts it as a worked example: a product sold in three colours and four sizes needs twelve separate barcodes, and the same logic applies to flavours, scents and pack sizes. Their free Barcode Quantity Calculator takes your line-up and returns a count without an account. Get this right before you pay, because the ladder rewards buying a block: a single code is $5, but 25 are $25 ($1.00 each) and 100 are $45 ($0.45 each). Overshooting is cheap and the codes carry no renewal fee, so a small cushion for next year's SKUs costs cents. Undershooting only means a second order, which is allowed and never conflicts with the first — just less efficient.

Will these work on Amazon and in shops?

On Amazon, yes for listing purposes, and the vendor backs it with a Works for Amazon Guarantee: if Amazon will not accept a listing, their team reviews the case and works with you to resolve it. Amazon requires a valid UPC or EAN to create a catalogue listing, and these are real GS1-originated GTINs. Note that the FNSKU Amazon assigns for FBA tracking is a separate thing Amazon issues, not something you buy here; their free tool prints those labels. Retail is where you check first. The page claims acceptance by 99% of retailers and admits the remainder in its own FAQ: a small number of retailers specifically require barcodes rented directly from GS1. That group includes certain brand-registry and big-box requirements. Confirm your specific channel's rule in writing before you buy from anyone.

Is this really cheaper than going to GS1 directly?

On their numbers, by a wide margin — but those numbers are one seller describing its competitor, so treat them as a starting point rather than a quote. Their comparison table puts a single barcode at $5.00 one-off against "$250+ initial + $50/year" at GS1, with annual renewals of "$50 – $2,100/year" depending on how many codes you hold, and $0 forever on their own side. The structural difference is real and not disputed: GS1 licenses a company prefix you keep paying for, and a pre-2002 broker code is sold outright with no renewal. Price your own case at GS1's published rates before deciding, because the gap narrows if you need only a handful of codes for a single year, and it closes entirely if your retailer insists on a prefix in your own name.

Whose company will a GS1 lookup show for my barcode?

Not yours, and this is the thing to understand before buying. The vendor states plainly that its inventory originates from GS1-USA prefixes registered before 2002, which it says means they are owned outright rather than licensed, made possible by the 2002 UCC class-action settlement. Those prefixes were issued to whoever held them then, so a GS1 registry lookup surfaces that original holder rather than your business; a purchase here creates a listing in Bar Codes Talk's own BCT GEPIR registry instead. For most marketplaces and independent retail nobody ever performs that lookup. Where it bites is brand-registry and big-box onboarding that checks the prefix against your legal entity. Registering is your own work either way: the FAQ is explicit that you assign each code to a product in your own records, then submit product data separately to every store, marketplace and distributor you sell through.

What should I weigh before buying Operations & Workflow software?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.
