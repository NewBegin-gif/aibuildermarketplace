Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 10 Sep 2026

Read on reallygoodemails.com/pricing; the four tiers and both billing rates stood unchanged from our readings on 21 July and 2 August 2026. Sold as RGE Studio since the 14 May 2026 rebrand. Starter free — up to 10 designs, 6 exports/mo, landing-page builder, custom subdomain, 20GB/mo image traffic, unlimited users but no roles or permissions. Professional $25/mo billed yearly ($30/mo monthly) — unlimited designs and folders, unlimited reusable blocks, 2 editing users plus 4 view-only, AI writing assistant.

Business $134/mo billed yearly ($160/mo monthly) — 2 editing users plus 10 view-only, 2 workspaces, approval workflows, advanced brand controls, QA assistant. Enterprise: custom, quoted by sales. The free trial is 15 days on the Business plan. The embeddable Beefree SDK is a separate product on its own price list (developers.beefree.io) and is not covered by these numbers. Extra editing seats are billed on top: free on Starter, $15 a month or $150 a year on Professional, and $35 a month or $350 a year on Business (help-centre article "Multi-user support and management in RGE Studio", read 10 September 2026). Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Professional and Business both cap at two full editing seats, so a "mid-sized team" plan at $134/mo annual still buys two people who can actually build. Everyone else lands in view-only slots — four on Professional, ten on Business — or an Enterprise quote. The free Starter is the odd one out: unlimited users, but with no roles or permissions at all. Exports are metered at six a month, rather than the unlimited free tier its old reputation suggests.

And the naming is a live tripwire. Since 14 May 2026 the builder is RGE Studio at reallygoodemails.com, while Beefree remains the company name and the name of the separate developer SDK. So check which product a plan or invoice actually refers to. It is purely a creation environment that never sends, so it always sits on top of your ESP. Worth it when the ESP's native editor is the bottleneck — agencies, multi-brand teams, ESP migrations — and duplicated effort when it isn't.

What Beefree does

RGE Studio (formerly Beefree — renamed 14 May 2026): drag-and-drop email and landing-page design. Build responsive, on-brand emails fast and export clean HTML to any major ESP, no coding required. Beefree remains the company name and the name of its separate developer SDK; the builder itself now lives under reallygoodemails.com.

What buyers report about Beefree

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Beefree ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • You design here and export to whichever ESP you use — Mailchimp, Klaviyo, HubSpot, ActiveCampaign and 20+ others. That decoupling is the reason to use it.
  • • Reusable content blocks, live collaboration and a large template library, with a genuine free tier.
What they complain about
  • • The generated HTML does not reliably stay under Gmail's 102 KB clipping limit, particularly on image-heavy templates or emails with many blocks. Check your send size before a big campaign.
  • • The free Starter tier caps at 10 designs and 6 exports a month, which is a demo rather than a working plan.
  • • $15 Freelancer, $25 Professional per user, $30 Team per user, $134 Business — the collaboration features that justify it sit on the higher tiers.

When the paid plan is worth it

The pricing page lists Starter at $0, Professional at $30/mo ($25 billed yearly), Business at $160/mo ($134 billed yearly) and Enterprise on request. Starter caps you at 10 designs, 3 custom templates and 6 exports a month, and stamps a "Designed with RGE Studio" footer on every design. So Professional pays back quickly for anyone shipping more than a few emails a month. The steepest jump is Professional to Business, $30 to $160, and it buys co-editing, a custom domain on published pages, four simultaneous review requests per workspace, 10 HTML imports a month instead of 4, and 1 TB of CDN traffic instead of 250 GB.

That is mostly an approval-and-governance purchase; the builder is the same, with one exception low in the table — multilingual designs run one primary language plus three additional on Professional and plus twenty-four on Business, so more than four languages pushes you up the ladder by translation, not workflow. Upgrading does not buy seats: both paid tiers include 2, and extras cost $15 a month on Professional and $35 on Business ($150 and $350 a year).

Nor does any tier send email — RGE Studio builds and exports, so an ESP stays a separate bill.

How to actually use Beefree

The vendor docs describe a short path: log in, land on the Projects page, click Create new. Then choose a blank email, a template from a catalog of 1,600+ templates, a page, or a folder — folders prompt Starter users to upgrade. After saving, the details page is where you send a test or export via connector, raw HTML, ZIP or PDF. Starting a Professional or Business trial is a six-step sequence from the Subscription page and needs no payment details.

Common Beefree setup mistakes

Where Beefree setups actually go wrong, and what it costs you when they do. Same sources as the setup notes above — not from a walkthrough we ran ourselves.

The friction comes later.

  • Publishing pages on your own domain is Business-and-up.
  • It needs a CNAME pointing to bdn.beefree.io and, if you run a CAA record, an entry allowing letsencrypt.org.
  • After that the domain sits pending with no timeline the docs will commit to, and unconfirmed requests are deleted after 15 days.
  • Migration is rationed too: 4 HTML imports a month on Professional, 10 on Business, 1 MB per file, with background images, dividers and menus unsupported.
  • And Unlayer JSON conversion is a manual support-ticket service.

The natural comparison is Stripo or your ESP's own editor — Stripo is the head-on builder rival — compare template depth, export connectors into YOUR sending stack, and how many real editing seats each plan actually gives you.
