What Beehiiv does

The newsletter platform built for growth. Advanced analytics, monetization tools, and seamless integrations. Scale from 0 to millions of subscribers like Morning Brew.

Pricing (2026)

Subscriber-based pricing and you keep 100% of paid subscriptions — no platform fee, unlike Substack's 10%. Cost grows gradually and transparently with your list size (2026). Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Next to Substack it's more to learn. Substack's built-in discovery network and dead-simple setup are easier if you're starting from zero with no audience and don't care about automation or SEO. Beehiiv's depth is the point, but it's a steeper start.

What buyers report about Beehiiv

Recurring themes from G2, Capterra, TrustRadius and Product Hunt, read in August 2026. We have not run Beehiiv ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Monetisation is built in rather than bolted on — ads, referrals and paid subscriptions in the same product. Around 4.3 across the platforms.
  • • The editor and the growth tooling are what reviewers say they would miss.
What they complain about
  • • The jump from free to paid is abrupt: Launch is free to 2,500 subscribers, then Scale is $43/month — and you cross that line the moment you want to monetise, whichever comes first.
  • • Cost at scale is the complaint on every single platform. Around 5,000 subscribers puts you near $78/month, Max is $96.
  • • The learning curve on the website builder and workflows is steeper than the marketing suggests.
  • • Integrations are thin against Mailchimp's ecosystem, and support responsiveness slips exactly when something is urgent.

When the paid plan is worth it

beehiiv's Launch plan is $0 per month and covers up to 2,500 subscribers, and it already includes custom domains and unlimited email sends, so the free tier is a real publishing tier rather than a demo. At the pricing page's default setting of 1,000 subscribers with annual billing selected, Scale is $517 billed annually ($43 per month) and Max is $1,151 billed annually ($96 per month); those per-month figures are the annual-billing rates, not the month-to-month price. The biggest jump in the table is Launch to Scale, because that is where monetization moves: the Ad Network, paid recommendations and a 0% take rate on paid subscriptions are all Scale-and-above, and the help center states the Ad Network is available to anyone on the Scale plan or above who is actively sending. Scale to Max is a narrower step: removing beehiiv branding, up to 10 publications, unlimited seats, priority support and a sponsorship storefront. Upgrading does not solve list size: both Scale and Max stop at 100,000 subscribers, above which beehiiv requires Enterprise. It also does not solve deliverability or domain setup, which is the same DNS work on every tier.

How to actually use Beehiiv

beehiiv's own platform walkthrough sets the order: create and confirm the account, then set up the workspace and publication; each post moves through 5 tabs in the Post Builder — Compose, Audience, Email, Web and Review. Distilled from Vendor material only: beehiiv's pricing page plus help-center articles on plan types and tier pricing, custom domains, DMARC, the Substack migration, the platform walkthrough, the free trial and the Ad Network; we found no independent walkthrough worth citing.

Common Beehiiv setup mistakes

Where Beehiiv setups actually go wrong, and what it costs you when they do. Same sources as the setup notes above — not from a walkthrough we ran ourselves.

The friction is not in that list.

  • A custom domain means adding 12 DNS records, and beehiiv says verification can take up to 72 hours and warns against removing or re-adding those records while it runs; Cloudflare users must set each record to DNS Only, since proxied records cannot be detected.
  • A valid DMARC record has been mandatory for custom domains since February 2024, and DNS changes can take 24-72 hours to fully propagate.
  • Migration is the other slow part: beehiiv's Substack guide puts content migration at 5-10 minutes and the Stripe paid-subscriber transfer at 5-15 minutes, but recommends excluding subscribers with an activity rating of 0 for the first 2-4 weeks of sending.
  • The free trial sits on a Launch account with temporary higher-tier access, and locks the Ad Network, paid subscriptions, community and additional users; no trial length is stated.
