What BidX does

Amazon-advertising automation from German vendor BidX GmbH: bid and budget automation, campaign creation, dayparting and target-ACOS rules for Sponsored Products, Brands and Display, with Amazon DSP and Marketing Cloud reporting and organic-plus-paid share-of-voice tracking in the same dashboard.

Pricing (2026)

(2026-08-06, read in a live browser on their own pricing page, EUR view) Amazon PPC is sold in three tiers and every one of them is a monthly fee plus an unspecified percentage of connected ad spend: Self Service Platform €495/month, Managed Platform €1,995/month and Managed Service €4,995/month. The tiers differ mainly in how much of a human you get — one monthly strategy call on Self Service, up to two calls and up to 10h of assistance a month on Managed Platform, up to four calls and up to 32h on Managed Service. Amazon DSP is priced separately on the same page: Self Service €495/month and Managed Service €995/month, again plus a percentage of ad spend. Amazon Marketing Cloud is sold as reporting: €295 for the default report, custom reports quote-only. The Share of Voice add-on covers the first 100 keywords free and charges $250 a month per additional 500 keywords. All prices are net with VAT added on top, and the page states that plans are based on an annual commitment paid monthly; the FAQ adds that new customers can take a paid three-month proof-of-concept period first. Walmart Managed Service, high ad-spend volumes and large agencies are contact-sales. Sellers under €10,000 a month in ad spend are pointed to a separate product, BidX Essential, whose own landing page at get.bidx.io/essentials lists $195/month for up to $10K in monthly ad spend — a flat fee with no percentage attached. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The number that decides your bill is the one number the page does not print. Every Amazon PPC and DSP tier ends with “+ % of connected ad spend per month”, and that percentage appears nowhere — not on the tier cards, not in the footnotes, not in the FAQ. So €495 is a floor, not a price, and nobody spending real money on Amazon ads can work out their monthly cost from this page. Ask for the percentage in writing, and ask whether it is charged on all connected spend or only on what BidX manages, before you get as far as a contract. The second thing to price is the commitment. The page states plans run on an annual commitment paid monthly, and the FAQ confirms it: the commitment is annual, and the only shorter path on offer is a paid three-month proof of concept. There is no free trial. That makes the cheapest visible door €5,940 a year net of VAT before a cent of the percentage — a very different proposition from the €495 the eye lands on. Two smaller inconsistencies worth noticing, because they tell you how carefully the price list is maintained. On a page priced in euros, the one add-on with a hard number is quoted in dollars ($250 per extra 500 keywords), so ask which currency you are actually billed in. And BidX Essential — the $195/month product they route smaller sellers to — lives on a completely separate landing page carrying a 2024 copyright line and a different phone number; it is not listed among the tiers on the pricing page itself, so confirm it still exists at that price before you plan around it.

Best for: brands and agencies already spending well past €10,000 a month on Amazon ads who want bid, budget and campaign automation, Amazon DSP and AMC reporting in one platform with a named growth manager attached — and who value a German vendor with EU invoicing, SEPA and direct debit, and support in English and German.
Who should skip it: if your ad spend is under roughly $10K a month, because BidX itself routes you to a different product at that size; you want to try before you commit, since there is no free trial and the commitment is annual with only a paid three-month proof of concept as a shorter path; you need your total cost on paper before a sales call, because the percentage of ad spend is unpublished; or you advertise mainly outside Amazon and Walmart.

The natural comparison is Teikametrics — the closest peer we cover, and the comparison is unusually clean because both charge a fee plus a percentage of ad spend — the difference is how much of that they will tell you up front. Teikametrics publishes both halves: $149/month billed annually (or $179 monthly) under $10K of ad spend, and 3% of ad spend above $10K on the Advanced tier. BidX publishes the monthly fee and withholds the percentage on every tier. At the small end the gap is stark too: Teikametrics' entry plan is self-serve at $149, while BidX's answer under $10K is the separate Essential product at $195/month. Model both at your own real monthly spend rather than at the headline, and get the BidX percentage in writing before you compare — without it the two are not comparable at all.
