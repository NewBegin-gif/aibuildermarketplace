What Bill.com does

Accounts-payable and receivable automation for finance teams with real approval chains. Priced per user from about $45/month, with transaction fees stacked on top — roughly $0.49 ACH, $1.99 cheque, $9.99 international wire, 2.9% card.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Per-user pricing plus per-transaction fees means the real cost depends on team size and payment volume — it's built for finance teams with genuine AP workflows and is overkill if you pay a handful of bills a month.

What buyers report about Bill.com

Recurring themes from G2, TrustRadius, Gartner Peer Insights, the Apple App Store and GetApp, read in August 2026 (App Store reviews from September 2025 through 30 July 2026; the GetApp listing is marked last updated July 2026). We have not run Bill.com ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Approval workflows and centralised payments genuinely save finance teams time — 4.3 on G2 across more than 700 reviews.
  • • Scales with the business without needing to be replaced, which reviewers name as the reason they stay.
  • • GetApp reviewers in operations and firm-administration roles name reliable, on-time payment processing and the fact that AP, AR, invoicing and reporting sit in one place, and one App Store reviewer in October 2025 reports a suggested fix actually shipping within days.
What they complain about
  • • Support is the dominant complaint: hard to reach, and slow to resolve once you do.
  • • Syncing with QuickBooks Online, Sage Intacct and NetSuite is reported as troublesome often enough to plan for — test your own integration during the trial, not after.
  • • International payment capability is limited relative to the domestic product.
  • • Held and delayed payments are the dominant App Store complaint and they run right up to July 2026: money described as held hostage, a five-day payment that took sixteen business days, and funds arriving on a virtual card rather than as the expected bank transfer.
  • • Getting verified is its own ordeal. A March 2026 reviewer calls the verification process invasive and exhausting with daily phone calls, and several describe getting stuck creating or linking companies just to receive a single payment.
  • • Support has no phone line in these accounts, and reviewers on both platforms describe tickets closed without resolution. GetApp reviewers put it plainly: once your problem falls outside the help articles you are at the mercy of whoever picks up.
  • • Fee surprises come up too, including a debit-card payment triggering a charge reviewers considered unreasonable, and needing a subscription at all in order to be paid by other companies.

Where the ratings disagree. The App Store is where a different person complains. G2, TrustRadius and Gartner are written by the AP and AR administrators who chose BILL; the app reviews are largely from people on the receiving end of a payment, who did not choose it and just want their money. Their complaints — verification, held funds, an unexpected virtual card — are about being paid through BILL rather than paying with it. If you are evaluating it, that is the experience you are handing your own suppliers.

When the paid plan is worth it

BILL's AP & AR line runs Essentials at $49 user/month, Team at $65 user/month, Corporate at $89 user/month, and Enterprise at custom pricing. The largest step in that table is from Team to Corporate. Essentials connects to accounting software only through manual CSV import/export and gives you 6 standard user roles; Team adds automatic 2-way sync with QuickBooks Online, Pro, Premier and Xero; Corporate folds in procurement, which is an add-on on Essentials and Team, and lower-cost approver-only seats. Upgrading pays off when most of the people you are adding only approve, or when you need purchase requests and purchase orders. What it does not solve is your ledger: automatic 2-way sync with QuickBooks Enterprise, Oracle NetSuite, Sage Intacct, Microsoft Dynamics, Acumatica and Rillet, plus API access, single sign-on, dual control and multi-entity accounting, all sit in Enterprise. No tier changes the per-payment fees either; they stack on every plan, at $0.59 for ACH, $1.99 for a mailed check, $19.99 for an international USD wire and 2.9% when you fund by card. The separate Spend & Expense product is listed at $0 user/month.

How to actually use Bill.com

BILL's own docs route a new account through bill.com/signup, where you select Accounts Payable & Receivable, set a username and password, then sign in and add a bank account in the web app; production billing begins after a 30-day trial. Frequently asked questions

BILL vs Ramp for accounts payable — which syncs better with QuickBooks and NetSuite?

We have not run both, so take this as what each vendor documents rather than a bench test. BILL is an AP and AR product sold per user — $49, $65 and $89 a month for Essentials, Team and Corporate — with two-way sync to accounting systems as its core pitch and per-transaction fees on top. Ramp leads with corporate cards and spend management and gives its software away against interchange, so the comparison is not like for like: you are weighing a paid AP workflow against a card-funded one. The question worth asking both in writing is which direction the sync runs for bills, vendors and payment status, and what happens to a record that is edited on the accounting side after it has synced.

I received a check from bill.com — is it legitimate?

Most likely yes, and here is why it looks odd. BILL is the platform thousands of companies use to pay their suppliers, so a check arriving with bill.com on it usually means your customer paid you through it rather than that BILL owes you anything. A mailed check is a standard $1.99 payment method on their fee schedule. Verify it the ordinary way — match it to an invoice you actually issued and to a customer you actually have, and contact that customer directly using a number you already had, not one printed on the letter. Buyers do report on the app stores that payments arrive in a different form than expected, including on a virtual card rather than as a bank transfer, so an unexpected format is not by itself a red flag.

What does BILL cost beyond the per-user price?

The fees are the part that scales with your business rather than your headcount. On top of $49, $65 or $89 per user per month sit $0.59 for an ACH payment, $1.99 for a mailed check, $19.99 for an international USD wire (an FX wire is free), 1.0% for an instant payment with a $9.99 minimum and $100 cap, and $11.99 for Pay Faster ACH. Expedited checks run $24.99, $19.99 and $14.99 for overnight, two-day and three-day. Paying by card costs 2.9%, and if you are the one being paid by card you pay 2.9% too. Read on their pricing page on 15 August 2026, and unchanged in our captures since July.

Is Bill.com safe to run my finances through?

The bar here is higher than features. Check who actually holds the money and under what licence, whether client funds are segregated, what audit trail and permission controls you get, and how support behaves when something goes wrong mid-transaction. Anything touching payments should be able to answer those in writing; if it cannot, that is your answer.

Who should not use Bill.com?

Skip it if you pay only a few bills a month — your accounting software or a free tool like Melio's base plan covers it without per-seat fees. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
