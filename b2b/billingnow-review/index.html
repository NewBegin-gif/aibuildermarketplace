What BillingNow does

Invoicing and expense tracking with AI — unlimited invoices, expenses, clients and users, at $9.99 a month or $99.90 a year, made in Geneva.

Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 20 Aug 2026

Two plans, both carrying the identical feature list with no limits: $9.99 a month, or $99.90 a year which the page itself works out to $8.33 a month and labels 'Save 17%'. Both include unlimited invoices, expenses, client relations, users, estimates and credit notes, plus payment tracking, tax rates, PDF export and the AI assistant. There are no per-user fees and no feature-gated tier.

The trial is 3 days and the page states three separate times that no credit card is required to start it. SOC 2 Type II certified with European-standard compliant invoices. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The honest watch-out is the trial length rather than its terms: three days is short for migrating billing into a new tool, and the practical constraint is that there is no import path. The help centre documents CSV and Excel export but no import, so an existing client list has to be retyped — which is more than three days of work for anyone with a real book. It is also a young product from a small Geneva maker, so weigh ecosystem depth and longevity before you move your billing history in.

When the paid plan is worth it

There is only one product here and both plans carry the identical feature list, so this is not an upgrade decision — it is a billing-frequency one. Read on their own site on 20 August 2026: $9.99 a month, or $99.90 a year, which the page itself works out to $8.33 a month and labels a 17% saving. In cash that is a difference of $19.98 over a year. Small enough that if you have any doubt about staying, pay monthly; the annual discount is not large enough to be worth locking in a tool you are unsure about.

The number that actually matters is the one that is missing: there are no per-user fees, and users are unlimited on both plans. That is the whole argument for this product against conventional invoicing tools, and it compounds with team size. One person pays $9.99 a head; a five-person team pays $2.00 a head; a ten-person team pays $1.00. Unlimited invoices, expenses, client relations, estimates and credit notes sit on both plans too, so there is no volume ceiling to grow into either.

What to weigh instead of tiers is migration. The trial is three days, and the constraint is not the clock but the on-ramp: the help centre documents CSV and Excel EXPORT but no import, so an existing client list has to be retyped by hand. For a freelancer with a dozen clients that is an evening; for anyone with a real book it is more than three days of work, and it should happen before the trial starts rather than during it.

Bank accounts and tax rates also live in Organization Settings rather than in the signup wizard, so IBAN and tax-rate setup is a separate trip after onboarding finishes. One mismatch to check against your own needs before paying for a year: the homepage advertises Stripe-powered payment links, but the Payments article documents only manually recorded payments. If getting paid inside the tool rather than merely invoicing from it is the reason you are buying, confirm that specific flow works in the three days you have.

How to actually use BillingNow

You sign up with an email address and sign in either through Continue with Google or by clicking a magic link emailed to you with the subject Sign in to BillingNow; the docs say you never need a password. First login forces a two-step company wizard: step one takes organization name, tax ID, address, default currency and timezone; step two takes a logo (PNG or JPG, max 2MB), an invoice model, a primary colour and standard text before and after the line items.
