What Birch does

Ad automation for performance marketers (formerly Revealbot): rule-based optimization, automated scaling and creative insights across Meta, Google, TikTok and Snapchat — run and optimize ad campaigns on autopilot.

Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 2 Aug 2026

(2026, formerly Revealbot) Essential about $49/mo and Pro about $99/mo, both with a $10k monthly ad-spend ceiling; overages meter (~0.49% Essential, ~1% Pro of excess spend, monthly billing only); Enterprise custom with no cap. 14-day free trial, two months free on annual. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The tidy $49/$99 tiers assume you stay under a $10k monthly ceiling, and past it you either pay spend-based overages or move to custom Enterprise — so a scaling advertiser's real cost rises with their budget. It's also a power tool: rule-based automation across Meta, Google, TikTok and Snapchat rewards advertisers who know what to automate, and can amplify mistakes if your rules are wrong. For hands-on performance marketers, it's a serious time-saver.

What they praise

  • • Every plan includes unlimited team members, API access and Slack reports.
What they complain about
  • • Price is the main complaint — $69/month up to $10K ad spend, rising to $299 at $500K. Reviewers say it gets steep fast at real spend, and one calls the structure anti-agency.
  • • The interface is not friendly for anyone newer to paid media, and reviewers describe it as time-consuming to learn.
  • • You are buying rules-based automation on top of Meta — if you would not write those rules yourself, the tool will not write them for you.

When the paid plan is worth it

Birch's real split is not free versus paid but reporting versus automation. Essential starts at $49 per month at up to $10,000 monthly ad spend and gives you Workspaces, Post boosting, Reports and Slack alerts; automated rules and strategies, Explorer, Launcher, Stage and custom metrics all sit on Pro, which starts at $99 per month. If rule-based optimisation is why you are looking at Birch, Essential is not a cheaper version of the product.

Price then tracks spend: Pro runs $99, $249, $499, $799, $1,299 and $1,799 against limits of $10,000, $30,000, $75,000, $150,000, $300,000 and $500,000. The sharpest step sits at the top of Essential, which stops at $499 and a $150,000 limit, so crossing into the $300,000 band means Pro at $1,299. Moving up a tier buys headroom, not features: the tiers differ only in spend limit and overage rate, and upgrading does not erase overages already accrued, so exceeding a $249 plan by $5,000 still adds 0.83 percent, or $41.50, to the next bill.

Hub's server-side tracking is priced separately by event volume on top of the plan: free to 10,000 events a month, then $10 at 500,000.

How to actually use Birch

You sign up with an email, no card required, and get a 14-day trial; then, under Settings and Platforms, you authorise Meta, Google, TikTok or Snapchat and assign each ad account to a Workspace, since only assigned accounts count as connected and are billed. Birch's own course runs to 17 videos across four chapters, with setup a single lesson in the Getting started chapter, and its help center's Getting started collection runs to 32 articles.

Common Birch setup mistakes

Where Birch setups actually go wrong, and what it costs you when they do. Same sources as the setup notes above — not from a walkthrough we ran ourselves.

The friction is on the platform side.

  • Birch lists only Meta assets where you are an admin of both the Business Manager and the ad account.
  • Their troubleshooting article blames missing accounts on permissions, on delays after admin rights are granted, and on Meta glitches — re-authorising or toggling admin rights off and on is the fix.
  • A Workspace takes unlimited ad accounts, but each ad account can sit in only one Workspace.
  • Unlinking is destructive: rules become drafts, and reports, custom metrics and launch templates are deleted permanently.
  • Server-side tracking needs a CNAME to signals.birch.events, with propagation up to 48 hours.

The natural comparison is Madgicx or the native ad managers — comparable ad-automation platforms — Birch's edge is granular rule automation; compare on spend-based pricing at your budget.
