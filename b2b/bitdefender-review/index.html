Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 25 Sep 2026

Read on their own product pages, 6 September 2026 and again, unchanged, 25 September 2026, where a configurator prices each tier by device count and term. At 10 devices for one year the list prices are GravityZone Small Business Security $324.99, Business Security $384.99 and Business Security Premium $879.99 — $32.50, $38.50 and $88.00 per device per year. A 30% discount is applied by default on all three, bringing them to $227.49, $269.49 and $615.99.

Small Business Security at 5 devices for one year is $169.99 list, $118.99 discounted. Add-ons are priced separately: at 10 devices, Web Access and Device Control $41.99 and Network Attack Defense and Risk Management $41.99 on Small Business Security, Setup & Go $224.99, and Essential: Tips & Best Practices $54.99 a seat. Enterprise Security, Defense XDR and MDR are contact-sales. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

The honest knock

Add-ons are billed separately too — web access and device control, network attack defense and risk management, and setup each carry their own line. Protection scores are consistently strong, so the real trade-off is cost, not efficacy.

What Bitdefender does

GravityZone endpoint protection for small business, managed from one console. Add-ons and setup are priced separately.

What buyers report about Bitdefender

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in September 2026. We have not run Bitdefender ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Centralised management is what reviewers name first: one console, one portal, global policies, scripted installs and updates pushed to endpoints wherever they sit. A UK engineer running a global fleet describes managing antivirus, policies and updates for every device from a single portal that depends on nothing but a web connection.
  • • Threat protection is described as holding across quite different attack vectors — email phishing, malicious attachments and infected USB sticks — and reviewers running it for clients say it has caught real threats rather than merely idling in the tray. TrustRadius's own synthesis of recent reviews lists proactive blocking and disinfection as a consistent strength.
  • • Low footprint on the endpoint comes up repeatedly, several times as an explicit contrast with a previous product: one 120-endpoint shop that moved off another vendor calls GravityZone far less intrusive and resource-hungry than what it replaced.
  • • The modular build is treated as a buying reason rather than an upsell: reviewers describe bolting EDR and later MDR onto the same console as their needs grew, with help integrating each module.
What they complain about
  • • Console navigation is the most consistent complaint, and it is not a beginner's problem — the people raising it are IT owners and MSPs. Reviewers describe drilling into an event as harder than expected, ask for the homepage graphics to be clickable for troubleshooting, and repeatedly list 'GUI navigation' and 'ease of use' as what should improve.
  • • Support responsiveness is the second recurring complaint, named by reviewers who otherwise rate the product 8 and 9 out of 10 — the pattern is that the product is liked and the queue is not.
  • • Granularity is a real limit for admins: reviewers want finer control over settings and over what goes into an installation package, specifically which action is taken when a possible threat is detected.
  • • The worst-case story on TrustRadius is concrete and worth reading before you deploy: a small translation firm rated it 1 out of 10 after files were quarantined or deleted without a prompt and restoring them did not work. Their conclusion is a warning about fit, not just a bad day — they say the product suits you only if you know your file exceptions up front.
  • • Two reviewers separately name the commercial side rather than the software: one wishes the billing portal were easier to understand, and uninstalling is described as difficult.

Where the ratings disagree. The two platforms disagree about nothing, which is itself the useful finding — they disagree about emphasis. TrustRadius's aggregate is 8 out of 10 across 190 reviews and ratings. SourceForge has only 2 user reviews, so its averages carry no weight as a score, but the shape of those sub-scores matches the written complaints exactly: overall 4.5, features 4.5 and design 4.5, against ease 3.5 and support 3.5. Two independent review sets, one of them tiny, put the weakness in the same two places — the console and the support queue — and not in the protection. Read that as the practical trade: what you buy works, what you live with is the interface and the wait.

When the paid plan is worth it

Bitdefender's business line runs GravityZone Small Business Security, then GravityZone Business Security, then Business Security Premium, with Enterprise Security, Defense XDR and MDR above them on a contact-sales basis. The first three are priced openly by a configurator, and reading it settles the upgrade question arithmetically (read 6 September 2026). At 10 devices for a year the list prices are $324.99, $384.99 and $879.99 — $32.50, $38.50 and $88.00 per device per year.

A 30% discount sits on all three by default, taking them to $227.49, $269.49 and $615.99, so what you pay at signup is 70% of list and renewal is the number to plan for, not the one on the button. The step from the first tier to the second buys breadth: Network Attack Defense, endpoint risk management and web and content control. On Small Business Security those same capabilities are sold as add-ons, at $41.99 each for 10 devices.

Which makes the comparison easy and slightly absurd: $324.99 plus both add-ons is $408.97, against $384.99 for Business Security, which lists network attack defense, risk management and web content control among its modules. Its page (re-read 25 September 2026) does not name device control, so confirm that one before counting it. If you find yourself wanting either add-on, you have already overpaid for the cheaper tier. Premium is the tier that costs more than twice Business Security, and what it buys is investigation — attack forensics and visualisation, sandbox analysis.

That is worth $88 a device only if someone is going to read the output. What none of it solves is the console. Bitdefender's own onboarding path runs through GravityZone Control Center — build an installation package, choose modules, push it to endpoints, then apply policies — and paying for a higher tier adds modules to that same workflow rather than simplifying it. Budget the setup time at every tier, or budget the $224.99 Setup & Go add-on instead.

How to actually use Bitdefender

The vendor's onboarding guide splits the work into three stages: first steps, initial setup, and security monitoring. Concretely you validate the licence key and set up Control Center, build an installation package under Network > Installation Packages and pick modules there, run or push that package onto endpoints, then apply policies and run a scan. The list is short; the friction is not. Bitdefender's docs require you to uninstall — not merely disable — any existing anti-malware first, and note that Windows Defender and Windows Firewall are switched off automatically during install.

Remote push is where time goes. Target Windows machines need Administrative Share and Network Share enabled, File and Printer Sharing on TCP 139 and 445 plus UDP 137 and 138, ports 8443 and 7074 reachable, and UAC set to Never Notify unless you use the built-in domain administrator account. On macOS the user must approve Bitdefender's extensions by hand or some features do not work. Bitdefender sells a one-hour Setup & Go session that includes sample deployment on up to 5 endpoints — a fair signal of where buyers stall.

Frequently asked questions

Does the 30% discount come back when the licence renews?

The page does not say. Read 25 September 2026, all three GravityZone configurators apply 30% off by default, and the Business Security FAQ says an online purchase is a recurring subscription that renews automatically unless you switch automatic renewal off. Nowhere does it state the renewal price. So budget on the list price — $384.99 a year for Business Security at 10 devices rather than the $269.49 you pay at signup — and ask the store or a partner what the renewal will be before buying.

Is a two- or three-year licence cheaper per year?

Yes, a little. For Business Security at 10 devices (configurator, read 25 September 2026) one year is $269.49 after the default discount, two years $454.99 and three years $643.99, which works out at about $227 and $215 a year. List prices are $384.99, $649.99 and $919.99. The saving is roughly a fifth per year over three years, paid entirely up front, and the discount note still applies: the renewal price after the term is not published.

How many devices can I buy online?

From 1 to 100 devices; the configurator's footnote (read 25 September 2026) counts laptops, desktops and file servers. The licensing FAQ says you can add extra licences at any time, and that for more endpoints you go through an official Bitdefender partner in your region. An online subscription starts on the purchase date, and a free trial is available first, but you have to buy before the trial period ends to keep protection running.

What should I weigh before buying IT & Productivity software?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.
