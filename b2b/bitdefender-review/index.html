Bitdefender Review (2026)

What Bitdefender does

GravityZone endpoint protection for small business, managed from one console. Roughly $57–96 per device per year at list on an annual plan.

When the paid plan is worth it

Bitdefender's business line runs GravityZone Small Business Security, then GravityZone Business Security, then Business Security Premium, with Enterprise Security, Defense XDR and MDR above them on a contact-sales basis. The first three are priced openly by a configurator, and reading it settles the upgrade question arithmetically (read 6 September 2026). At 10 devices for a year the list prices are $324.99, $384.99 and $879.99 — $32.50, $38.50 and $88.00 per device per year.

A 30% discount sits on all three by default, taking them to $227.49, $269.49 and $615.99, so what you pay at signup is 70% of list and renewal is the number to plan for, not the one on the button. The step from the first tier to the second buys breadth: Network Attack Defense, Web Access Control, Device Control and endpoint risk management. On Small Business Security those same capabilities are sold as add-ons, at $41.99 each for 10 devices.

Which makes the comparison easy and slightly absurd: $324.99 plus both add-ons is $408.97, against $384.99 for Business Security, which includes them and more. If you find yourself wanting either add-on, you have already overpaid for the cheaper tier. Premium is the tier that costs more than twice Business Security, and what it buys is investigation — attack forensics and visualisation, sandbox analysis. That is worth $88 a device only if someone is going to read the output.

What none of it solves is the console. Bitdefender's own onboarding path runs through GravityZone Control Center — build an installation package, choose modules, push it to endpoints, then apply policies — and paying for a higher tier adds modules to that same workflow rather than simplifying it. Budget the setup time at every tier, or budget the $224.99 Setup & Go add-on instead.

How to actually use Bitdefender

The vendor's onboarding guide splits the work into three stages: first steps, initial setup, and security monitoring. Concretely you validate the licence key and set up Control Center, build an installation package under Network > Installation Packages and pick modules there, run or push that package onto endpoints, then apply policies and run a scan. The list is short; the friction is not. Bitdefender's docs require you to uninstall — not merely disable — any existing anti-malware first, and note that Windows Defender and Windows Firewall are switched off automatically during install.

Remote push is where time goes. Target Windows machines need Administrative Share and Network Share enabled, File and Printer Sharing on TCP 139 and 445 plus UDP 137 and 138, ports 8443 and 7074 reachable, and UAC set to Never Notify unless you use the built-in domain administrator account. On macOS the user must approve Bitdefender's extensions by hand or some features do not work. Bitdefender sells a one-hour Setup & Go session that includes sample deployment on up to 5 endpoints — a fair signal of where buyers stall.

Distilled from Tier prices, the default 30% discount, the device and term controls and the add-on prices read on 6 September 2026 in the configurator on bitdefender.com's own GravityZone Small Business Security, Business Security and Business Security Premium pages, at their default 10 devices for one year and, for the smallest tier, also at 5 devices. Per-device rates and the add-on-versus-upgrade comparison are our own arithmetic on those figures. The module lists and the Control Center onboarding sequence come from Bitdefender's own product and GravityZone documentation pages; no independent walkthrough was available to cite. The previous '$57-$96 per device per year' figure in this review carried no source and is not what the configurator shows.

The natural comparison is Microsoft Defender for Business or ESET — built-in or rival endpoint suites. Weigh it against Norton on the job you actually need done.
