Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 1 Sep 2026

The meter is credits, and every tier carries the same 100+ data sources. Free is $0 with 200 credits, no card required, capped at 20 action columns per grid. Growth is $349 a month billed monthly for 15,000 credits, with unlimited actions and inbound integrations like RB2B, Factors and Vector. Booster is $799 for 50,000 credits and adds 50 action columns per grid, curated scraping scripts, a private Slack channel and Salesforce integration.

Enterprise is quote-only but publishes a floor most vendors hide: annual billing at $15,000 and above, adding SOC 2 and SSO, priority support and custom credit volumes. Annual billing takes 10% off. The number to watch is the step from Free to Growth: 200 credits to 15,000 is a seventy-five-fold jump with nothing in between, so a small team that outgrows the free tier has no gentle next rung -- the bill goes from nothing to $4,188 a year in one move. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

The honest knock

What to check during that trial is not the feature list but your own burn rate: 200 credits go quickly once a waterfall tries three sources per record, and the next rung is $349. Work out what one enriched record actually costs you in credits, multiply by your real monthly volume, and compare that against what you pay your current vendors combined. That is the only comparison that settles it, and Bitscale gives you the free tier to run it.

What Bitscale does

GTM data layer that sits on top of your CRM: enrich records through waterfalls across 100+ B2B sources, track buying signals and run AI agents against the result. Free tier at 200 credits; paid from $349 a month for 15,000.

When the paid plan is worth it

The plan price is not the meter -- credits are, and the tiers buy them in bulk. Growth is $349 for 15,000 credits, Booster $799 for 50,000. Per credit that is roughly 2.3 cents against 1.6 cents, so Booster is about 30% cheaper per unit and the upgrade pays for itself the moment you would otherwise buy credits twice. The feature steps matter less than that arithmetic, with one exception worth knowing before you choose: Salesforce integration sits on Booster, not on Growth.

If your pipeline lives in Salesforce, the decision is made for you at $799 rather than $349, and that is the single most expensive line in this pricing table for anyone who assumed a CRM sync came with the mid-tier.

How to actually use Bitscale

Start on the free tier and do not build anything yet. Take fifty real records from your CRM, run one waterfall over them, and count the credits it consumed -- that number, multiplied by your monthly volume, is your actual bill and it is the only figure that matters. Then check whether your CRM is on the tier you were planning to buy: Salesforce sync arrives at Booster, not Growth. Only after both of those would we connect anything to production data.
