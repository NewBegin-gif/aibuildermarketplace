Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 8 Sep 2026

The meter is credits. Free is $0 with 200 credits, no card required, capped at 20 action columns per grid and 50,000 rows. Growth is $349 a month for 15,000 credits, 30 action columns and 100,000 rows, with a two-way HubSpot integration and inbound integrations like RB2B, Factors and Vector. Booster is $799 for 50,000 credits and 50 action columns, adding curated scraping scripts, a private Slack channel and Salesforce integration.

Enterprise is quote-only but publishes a floor most vendors hide: annual billing at $15,000 and above, adding SOC 2 and SSO, priority support, role-based team access and Snowflake pipelines. Annual billing takes 10% off. Two lines in the plan table change the arithmetic and are easy to miss. Credits roll over up to three times the plan allowance on Growth and Booster, and not at all on Free, so a quiet month is banked rather than lost.

And a credit is spent only when a data provider returns a valid result -- the pricing FAQ says failed lookups, formula columns and CRM syncs are always free -- so a waterfall that finds nothing costs nothing. The page also contradicts itself twice: the comparison table gives all four tiers 100+ data sources while the Free card says 10+ B2B data sources, and the Growth and Booster cards both promise Unlimited Actions while the same page's table caps action columns per grid at 30 and 50.

The step from Free to Growth is still the one to plan for: 200 credits to 15,000 with nothing in between, so the bill goes from nothing to $4,188 a year in one move. Monthly company and people search is metered separately at 2,000 on Free, 15,000 on Growth and 50,000 on Booster. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

What to check during that trial is not the feature list but your own burn rate: 200 credits go quickly once a waterfall tries three sources per record, and the next rung is $349. Work out what one enriched record actually costs you in credits, multiply by your real monthly volume, and compare that against what you pay your current vendors combined. That is the only comparison that settles it, and Bitscale gives you the free tier to run it.

What Bitscale does

GTM data layer that sits on top of your CRM: enrich records through waterfalls across 100+ B2B sources, track buying signals and run AI agents against the result. Free tier at 200 credits; paid from $349 a month for 15,000.

When the paid plan is worth it

The plan price is not the meter -- credits are, and the tiers buy them in bulk. Growth is $349 for 15,000 credits, Booster $799 for 50,000. Per credit that is roughly 2.3 cents against 1.6 cents, so Booster is about 30% cheaper per unit and the upgrade pays for itself the moment you would otherwise buy credits twice. Two things soften that meter, and both arrive with the first paid plan: credits roll over up to three times your plan allowance on Growth and Booster but not on Free, and a credit is spent only when a provider returns a valid result, so failed lookups, formula columns and CRM syncs cost nothing.

Paying does not only buy volume, it stops you paying for misses and for quiet months. What upgrading does not fix is the shape of the ladder. There is nothing between 200 credits and 15,000, so a team that outgrows the free tier has one move and it costs $4,188 a year, and no tier makes the enrichment itself cheaper. One feature step decides this before any of that arithmetic does: Salesforce integration sits on Booster, not on Growth.

How to actually use Bitscale

Start on the free tier and do not build anything yet. Take fifty real records from your CRM, run one waterfall over them, and count the credits it consumed -- that number, multiplied by your monthly volume, is your actual bill and it is the only figure that matters. Count it honestly, because the meter is narrower than it looks: credits are charged only when a provider returns a valid result, so a waterfall that tries three sources and finds nothing costs you nothing, and formula columns and CRM syncs are free too.

Then check whether your CRM is on the tier you were planning to buy, since Salesforce sync arrives at Booster and not at Growth. Only after both of those would we connect anything to production data. If you do go paid, note that unused credits roll over up to three times your plan allowance on Growth and Booster, so a slow month is banked rather than burned -- which makes the 10% annual discount easier to judge than it first looks.
