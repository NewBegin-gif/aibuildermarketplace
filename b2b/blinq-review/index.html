What Blinq does

Digital business cards for modern networking. Share your contact details instantly via QR or link, and capture leads straight into your CRM.

Pricing (2026)

(2026) Free plan (2 cards, unlimited QR sharing); Premium ~$7.33/mo (5 cards, branded QR, export); Business ~$4.99/user/mo annual ($6.99 monthly, 5-user minimum) with CRM sync and templates; Enterprise custom. A lead-capture add-on for events starts around $199/mo. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The core product is honestly cheap — the price jump hides in the event lead-capture add-on (~$199/mo), which is where teams that live on trade shows actually get value. And a truth about all digital cards: the wow factor works precisely because adoption is still low; the durable value is the CRM sync (contacts flowing automatically into your pipeline), so judge Business tier by that integration, not by the tap-to-share trick.

What buyers report about Blinq

Recurring themes from Capterra, GetApp, SourceForge and app-store reviews, read in August 2026. We have not run Blinq ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • The free tier is genuinely usable — unlimited sharing and no Blinq branding on your card, which most competitors charge for.
  • • Setup takes minutes and cards take real branding, links and media. Paid tiers are $4.50 and $7.50 per user per month on annual billing.
What they complain about
  • • The friction is on the receiving end, not yours. Recipients unfamiliar with digital cards — and Android users in particular — find saving your contact confusing. If your audience is not tech-forward, test it on one of them first.
  • • Importing contacts is clunky.
  • • Reviewers who want more than the basics find the useful extras sit behind the paid tiers.

When the paid plan is worth it

Blinq's free tier gives you two digital business cards at $0, which covers a solo user who only needs to hand over contact details. Premium at $9.99 per month, or $7.33 per month billed annually, raises that to five cards and adds branded QR codes, custom design and the AI Notetaker — worth it if your card is a client-facing asset that has to match your brand, and not otherwise. The genuinely large jump is Premium to Business, at $6.99 per card per month billed monthly or $4.99 billed annually, with a minimum of five team cards. That is where the admin platform, user management and admin-controlled email signatures sit, so Business is priced for the person who has to keep a team's cards consistent, not for the person carrying one. Enterprise is custom-priced and is the only tier with enforced SSO and SCIM provisioning. Upgrading does not fix control of the rollout: the help centre states that only the workspace owner can set up and manage integrations, and central signature sync is documented for Google Workspace only. Premium offers a 7-day free trial, Business a 30-day one.

How to actually use Blinq

The Business admin onboarding guide lays out a four-step checklist, in order: create a Team Template with your logo, colours and field permissions; add team members by email, shareable link, bulk CSV, SSO or SCIM; create Team Cards from the template and send activation emails; then connect your CRM under Settings > Integrations. Setup is short; the friction comes afterwards. Activation links expire — the help centre states the link in the claim email is only valid for 7 days, so any batch you send and forget has to be resent card by card. Enforced SSO is not self-serve: Blinq asks you to email Support a list of domains with an example address for each, then approves them, and you may be required to validate ownership via DNS verification. Central email signature sync needs Google Workspace Super Admin access, and Google advises allowing up to 24 hours to install; no equivalent Microsoft 365 deployment is documented. Template images must be cropped to 16:9, and templates cannot be built from the mobile apps. Best for: teams and field-sales orgs that want consistent, branded digital cards with contacts syncing straight into the CRM — and events teams via the capture add-on.
Who should skip it: if you're an individual (the free tier honestly covers you), or your networking volume doesn't justify per-user pricing.

The natural comparison is HiHello or Mobilo — the digital-card peer set — compare CRM integrations and whether you need hardware (NFC cards) versus pure app. Decide by which one fits the job above, not by the louder brand.

Frequently asked questions

Is Blinq secure and reliable enough to depend on?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.

Who should not use Blinq?

Skip it if you're an individual (the free tier honestly covers you), or your networking volume doesn't justify per-user pricing. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
