Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 20 Aug 2026

No subscription, no activation cost and no minimum commitment: you pay per ride or rental taken, with consolidated monthly billing, spend limits and policy controls included. One line the marketing does not carry, but Bolt's own support pages do: Bolt charges a service fee on every trip made with the business payment method, billed at the start of the following month. On credit card it is deducted automatically and does not appear on the individual trip invoice — it arrives as a separate service fee statement on the 3rd of each month; on postpaid it sits on the monthly invoice.

The amount is not published. Still commitment-free to pilot, but ‘you only pay for the rides’ is not the whole bill. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

Correction after re-checking: (re-verified 2026-07-23) Bolt Business is not sold as a subscription: signup asks only how rides are paid for, by credit card or postpaid. Bolt's business page states there are no activation costs and no minimum commitment, so what you pay is what your team rides. Postpaid is not granted on the spot: Bolt reviews the application and its team contacts you to agree a credit limit. Invoices and statements go to the invoice email you register, monthly.

The honest knock

Pricing is dynamic like all ride-hailing; the business layer adds control and receipts, not fixed corporate fares, so budget-predictability comes from your spend limits, not from locked prices.

What Bolt Business does

Business travel and mobility management. Give your team expense-tracked rides and travel with centralized billing and reporting.

When the paid plan is worth it

There is no paid tier here. Bolt for Business has no subscription, no activation cost and no minimum commitment; the only structural choice at signup is how rides get paid for, credit card or postpaid. Postpaid reads like the upgrade, and what it is not is the useful part. It is a credit decision rather than a plan: you send sales your company registration and tax ID, and the account activates only once Bolt approves it -- the same review applies if you switch over from card.

You get one monthly invoice and a credit limit, and the limit bites: reach it and nobody can order on the business method until you top up or it is raised. Two unpaid invoices and the business payment method is switched off at month end. What neither option removes is the service fee. Bolt charges one on every trip taken with the business payment method, billed at the start of the following month.

On postpaid it sits on the invoice; on credit card it is deducted automatically, never appears on the trip invoice, arriving as a separate service fee statement on the 3rd. No account type touches the ride price, which stays dynamic.

How to actually use Bolt Business

Check coverage first: Bolt lists the countries and cities it serves on its own site, and third-party city counts do not agree with Bolt's own, so check that list for your team's locations rather than a headline number. Registration asks for your company's legal name, country of registration, legal invoice address, registration code (SIREN in France), VAT code if VAT-liable, and an invoice email that must be unique, since the same address cannot serve two business accounts.

Bolt rejects addresses ending in .care, .app, .bio, .agency, .law or .team, accepts only Latin letters and digits, and warns that copy-pasting the address can produce an error. Choose credit card or postpaid billing; only postpaid is reviewed, after which Bolt's team contacts you to confirm the application and discuss your credit limit. Adding staff is the usual snag: enter the exact phone number the employee's Bolt account is connected to.

If their account uses a personal number and you want a company number, they create a new account on it, or you add the personal number instead. For bulk onboarding, use the dashboard's downloadable spreadsheet template.
