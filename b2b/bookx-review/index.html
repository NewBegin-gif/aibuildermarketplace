What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

One service and one team member means a salon with two stylists, or a studio running two class types, is already on Starter — so treat $9.99 a month as the real entry price, not zero. From there the ladder is gated on locations and team members rather than on how much you actually book: five, ten and twenty at $9.99, $19.99 and $39.99. A busy single-location business therefore pays the same as a quiet one, which is generous, while a small chain climbs the ladder for reasons that have nothing to do with volume.

Above twenty locations or team members the published table stops and you have to ask. And the whole thing only exists inside Shopify — the calendar rides on your storefront, checkout and POS — so there is nothing here for a business that takes bookings anywhere else.

The free plan is a demo, and the meter is staff, not bookings

Every tier carries unlimited bookings, the free one included, so nothing here meters what you sell. What the ladder gates is locations and team members, and that makes the free plan a demonstration rather than a plan: one service and one team member means a salon with two stylists, or a studio running two class types, is already on Starter. Treat $9.99 a month as the entry price, not zero.

Above that the steps are small and specific. Deposits and multiple payment options are Growth at $19.99, not Starter — Starter carries book-now-pay-later and partial payments, which is a different thing. Shopify Flow, POS and seasonal pricing are Growth too. Two-way Google Calendar sync per team member, Klaviyo and webhooks are Business at $39.99. That is not a number being withheld behind a sales call, it is a ceiling with nothing above it — so if you schedule across more sites or a bigger roster than that, settle the arrangement before you build on it.

How to actually use BookX

Setup is guided. Install, click Get Started, and pick one of eight service templates — regular, full-day or multi-day, each in a pay-at-checkout and a pay-later variant, plus a custom route and a service without a product. Name the service, link your products to it, set the booking slots, then optionally set lead time and capacity. Finally enable the BookX app embed in the Theme Editor and preview the widget; you can skip that and return to it.

Before you take a real booking, do the three checks the vendor files under Mandatory: disable the dynamic checkout button, disable the accelerated checkout buttons on the cart page and cart drawer, and enable cart checkout validation. Express checkout otherwise routes buyers around the slot logic. One trap the free tier sets: a service is tied to a product, so one service means one product. Merchants who attached several classes on Free have reported being locked out with no explanation why.

Frequently asked questions

What should I weigh before buying Operations & Workflow software?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.
