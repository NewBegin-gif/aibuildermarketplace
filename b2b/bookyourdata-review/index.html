What Bookyourdata does

B2B email lists and verified contact data on demand. Build targeted prospect lists with verified business emails and pay only for the contacts you need.

Pricing (2026)

Pay-as-you-go, no subscription (2026): from $99 one-time for 250 contacts (~$0.40 each), dropping toward $0.05-0.08 per contact above 10k credits. Credits never expire; 97% accuracy guarantee with refunds on bad data; 10 free credits monthly to test. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The model is honest — no subscription, credits keep, bad data refunded — so the real scrutiny belongs on usage: bought lists are only as good as your targeting and your sending infrastructure, and cold-emailing purchased contacts has legal boundaries that vary by country (GDPR territory especially). The data being accurate doesn't make the campaign compliant; that part is yours.

What buyers report about Bookyourdata

Recurring themes from G2, Trustpilot and Gartner Peer Insights, read in August 2026. We have not run Bookyourdata ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Data accuracy is the single most-mentioned positive on G2 (55 mentions), and it sits at 4.9 out of 5 across 395 reviews.
  • • Pay-as-you-go with credits that do not expire — unusual in this category, and reviewers name it directly.
What they complain about
  • • Accuracy depends heavily on batch size. Independent users report 2–3% bounce on pulls of 250–500 contacts, but 25–40% on batches over 10,000 — well outside the advertised 97% guarantee. Test at the volume you actually intend to buy.
  • • Company-level data is weaker than contact-level data; "inaccurate company information" recurs.
  • • Filtering is fiddly — 16 combined mentions of filtering difficulty point at a UI problem rather than a data one.
  • • Nine separate G2 reviewers call the pricing expensive.

Where the ratings disagree. 4.9 on G2 against 3.8 on Trustpilot, and the Trustpilot complaints are specifically about data going stale at volume. That matches the batch-size pattern above rather than contradicting it.

When the paid plan is worth it

Bookyourdata has no paid tiers in the usual sense: the help centre states it is not a subscription-based service and runs a pay-as-you-go credit model, with no contract and no costs beyond the credit price. The split that matters is free versus paid, and small versus large purchase. A new account gets 10 free credits at registration with no credit card, but they apply only to self-service Prospector search; the BeSpoke built-to-spec list service is excluded, so they tell you nothing about custom sourcing. One credit unlocks one contact's complete profile, verified email and direct phone included, across 100+ fields, so buying more credits buys more rows rather than more features. Volume is the only lever: the vendor states the per-contact rate drops as the credit volume bought in one transaction rises, so splitting a budget across several small purchases costs more than one larger one. Upgrading does not buy better data. The 97% deliverability guarantee (their claim, not our measurement) covers every credit, and the remedy is credits returned to the balance, not money back. Credits do not expire. The help centre publishes no pack sizes or per-credit rates; the pricing page was unreachable in this session.

How to actually use Bookyourdata

The vendor's Getting Started collection runs to six articles. You register, which credits the account with 10 free credits, then open Prospector and build a search from the filter menu on the left, which Bookyourdata says supports 100+ firmographic and contact-level filters. Saving and loading filters, and bulk including or excluding companies by pasting names or domains one per line, are separate documented steps. Downloading is six steps: log in, filter, click Download, enter the number of leads, choose an Email Verification Preference, and export a .csv. Common Bookyourdata setup mistakes

Where Bookyourdata setups actually go wrong, and what it costs you when they do. Same sources as the setup notes above — not from a walkthrough we ran ourselves.

The friction sits at that last choice.

  • Verification runs at the moment of download, and the two export filters are Deliverable Only and Deliverable & Risky; on risky addresses the docs say do not mail where your provider enforces a bounce threshold of 5% or less, and mail slowly on a dedicated IP.
  • Suppression is manual: exclusions are pasted line by line, with no documented file upload.
  • And although the docs describe credits being returned for inaccurate records, they never say how you file that claim or by when.
