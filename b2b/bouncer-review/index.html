What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Not charging for unknowns and duplicates is genuinely fair — many rivals do. The honest framing for any verifier: it removes invalid addresses, which protects deliverability, but it can't make a cold list warm or a purchased list legitimate. Verify before every big send (lists decay measurably per month), and treat 'accept-all' domain results as the gamble they are, not as verified.

What they praise

  • • Individual reviewers put non-urgent support replies at 24-48 hours; Bouncer says it offers premium email and chat support.
  • • Costs add up on large lists - $60 for 10,000 credits and $750 for 250,000 on pay-as-you-go (prices re-verified August 2026).

When the paid plan is worth it

Bouncer has no free tier beyond the 100 free credits on signup, and verification is sold as credits in two ways: one-off pay-as-you-go blocks, or the monthly-subscription mode in the same pricing calculator, which Bouncer states is discounted against pay-as-you-go. The lever is therefore how much volume you commit to and whether you commit monthly. Published pay-as-you-go prices are $8 for 1,000 credits, which is also the minimum purchase, then $35 for 5,000, $60 for 10,000, $250 for 50,000, $400 for 100,000, $750 for 250,000, $1,250 for 500,000 and $2,000 for 1,000,000.

The per-credit rate improves fastest across the lower and middle tiers and the curve flattens after 250,000, so the largest blocks repay only at sustained volume; Bouncer states credits never expire, which limits the downside of over-buying. Buying a bigger block changes nothing else. It does not raise throughput, which the docs put at 100-200k addresses per hour by default and adjustable only by asking Bouncer. It does not shorten the 15-minute retry window that makes small and large jobs take comparable time.

The $25, $125 and $250 per month Deliverability Kit tiers and the Shield subscriptions that start at $2 per month for 1,000 real-time checks are separate products - inbox placement monitoring and signup-form verification, not list cleaning.

How to actually use Bouncer

Bouncer's documented route is short. Create an account at app.usebouncer.com, which comes with 100 free credits. From there you either upload your list as a CSV file, the format the FAQ names, or take the API path. For the API, open API in the left-hand menu, press Generate API Key, and pass it as an x-api-key header or as the password in Basic auth. A sandbox lets you test the integration free of charge.

Common Bouncer setup mistakes

Where Bouncer setups actually go wrong, and what it costs you when they do. Same sources as the setup notes above — not from a walkthrough we ran ourselves.

The friction sits elsewhere.

  • Signing up with a private Gmail or Hotmail address is not accepted unless you email Bouncer first and explain who you are, so a human gate can precede your first verification.
  • Results are not instant either: retries run for up to 15 minutes, which is why small and large jobs take a similar time.
  • Batches process sequentially, so a 100,000-email batch delays a 1,000-email batch queued behind it, and Batch/Sync caps a request at 10,000 emails and caches results for 24 hours, after which the same address is charged again.
  • The dashboard itself is behind a customer login.
Best for: email marketers and outbound teams that clean lists regularly and want accurate verification with fair credit mechanics (no expiry, no double-charging).
Who should skip it: if you send rarely to a stable opt-in list (occasional pay-as-you-go beats any subscription), or your problem is content/reputation rather than list hygiene.

The natural comparison is ZeroBounce or NeverBounce — the verification peer set — accuracy claims are near-identical; compare per-1,000 rates at your volume and the unknown/duplicate billing policy. Weigh it against ZeroBounce on the job you actually need done.
