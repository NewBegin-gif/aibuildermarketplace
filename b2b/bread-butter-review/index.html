Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 20 Sep 2026

Two published plans. Free at $0: unlimited website traffic, 200 stored lead profiles, 30 days of attribution retention, your existing web forms, lead capture and content gating, social logins with your own branding, server-side journey tracking, CSV, Google Sheets and Mailchimp export, Slack alerts, unlimited team members, one-day email response. AI Enabled Pro at $49 a month per website: 5,000 lead profiles, 10-year retention, 3,000 credits a month, three peripheral sub-domains, one-hour email response, plus attribution reporting, journey and event tracking, custom views, real-time email validation, Focused Score AI, deep profile enrichment, Nurture AI messages, the Focused Index, and CRM, Zapier and webhook sync.

One add-on: Scale Credits at $89 a month per 10,000 credits, stackable. Profiles beyond 5,000 cost 2 credits each; Intelligence Brief syncs cost 5 credits each; the Intelligence Stream needs an enterprise licence. Pro comes with a 30-day trial, no credit card. The pricing page publishes no annual rate and no enterprise price. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

Correction after re-checking: (2026-09-20) The pricing page has been restructured since our 17 August reading and two published figures are gone. The +$49 a month block that added 5,000 lead profiles no longer exists: profiles above the included 5,000 are now charged at 2 credits each from the same pool as everything else, leaving Scale Credits at +$89 per 10,000 as the only add-on. And the vendor no longer rates Pro's 3,000 credits at 10 to 50 deep profile researches a month; the page now prices enrichment at an average of 10 to 150 credits per profile research, which puts the same allowance between 20 and 300 researches before storage overage or Brief syncs take their share. LeadScore AI is now called Focused Score AI. The Enterprise & Agency card is no longer on the pricing page.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

On privacy, refined twice in open dialogue with the vendor (16 Jul 2026, verified against their docs). The anonymous journey IS tracked from the first visit, server-side via their site plugin. So ad-blockers and Safari’s ITP don’t strip it the way they kill client-side scripts — the vendor estimates 30-50% of high-value traffic is lost to client-side blocking. What stays dormant until someone submits an email is identity resolution — the ‘hand-raise’ that connects the captured history to a verified person.

You still owe normal GDPR care on enriched data, and the real trade-off is simply this: no IP-based guessing at which company an anonymous visitor works for.

What Bread & Butter does

Lead-intelligence platform with server-side tracking: captures the full visitor journey from the first visit, then enriches, scores and nurtures the moment someone submits an email — verified identities instead of IP-based company guesses.

What buyers report about Bread & Butter

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Bread & Butter ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • It sits between analytics and CRM and closes the attribution loop from first touch to closed-won, with every won deal sharpening the ideal-customer profile.
  • • Free Insights tier, then $29 and $49 a month per website, with a fully functional 30-day trial and no card required.
What they complain about
  • • Pricing is per website. An agency or a multi-brand business multiplies that figure rather than sharing one subscription.
  • • Visitor identification depends on matching against professional signals — the same limitation every tool in this category has, and remote or consumer-ISP traffic is where it thins out.
  • • Three Clutch reviews is the extent of the independent record. There is not enough here to lean on; use the trial.

When the paid plan is worth it

The free tier accepts unlimited traffic but stores only 200 lead profiles with 30 days of retention, and it withholds the whole intelligence layer: no Focused Score AI, no deep profile enrichment, no Nurture AI, no attribution reporting, no journey and event tracking, and no CRM, Zapier or webhook sync. It still captures and exports leads — forms, content gating, branded social logins, server-side tracking, CSV, Google Sheets, Mailchimp and Slack are all on the free card — so the upgrade question is not whether you can collect leads, it is whether you want them scored, enriched and routed.

Pro at $49 a month per website switches that on and raises you to 5,000 profiles with 10 years of retention. The limit that bites first is not profiles, it is credits. Pro ships 3,000 a month, and the same pool pays for enrichment at an average of 10 to 150 credits per profile research, 2 credits per stored profile above 5,000, and 5 credits per Intelligence Brief pushed into Slack, a CRM, Zapier or a webhook.

So on a busy form you are rationing which leads get researched, and more depth costs $89 a month per 10,000-credit block. What upgrading does not solve is scope: Pro is priced per website and covers one domain plus three peripheral sub-domains, so a second site is another $49, and the pricing page no longer publishes an agency or enterprise rate at all. Read on breadbutter.io/pricing, 20 September 2026.

How to actually use Bread & Butter

On WordPress the route is shortest: install the plugin from Plugins, Add New, activate it, then finish the connect step with email or Google, which the docs put at under 30 seconds. Nothing else is mandatory, because the plugin watches your existing WPForms, Gravity Forms or Contact Form 7 without configuration. Off WordPress, copy the script from Settings, App and Domains into your head tag and register each subdomain separately; Pro covers 3 peripheral subdomains.

First real value comes from submitting one of your own forms and finding the scored profile in the dashboard. The documented failure point is caching: the vendor calls clearing your caching plugin or server cache the most common issue. After that come testing while logged in as an admin, which may not track, and installing through both the plugin and Google Tag Manager at the same time. Set UTMs on campaigns before you judge the scoring, since the documentation treats UTM data as the context the predictive score relies on.

Frequently asked questions

What does the free plan actually give me?

More than a traffic counter, and more than it used to. Read on breadbutter.io/pricing on 20 September 2026, Free carries unlimited website traffic, 200 stored lead profiles, 30 days of attribution retention, support for the web forms you already run, lead capture and content gating, social logins via Google, LinkedIn, Apple and a dozen more with your own logo rather than theirs, server-side journey tracking that ad-blockers and Safari's ITP do not strip, export to CSV, Google Sheets and Mailchimp, Slack alerts when a lead converts, unlimited team members and a one-day email response target. What it withholds is the intelligence and the plumbing: no attribution reporting, no journey and event tracking, no custom views, no real-time email validation, no Focused Score AI, no deep enrichment, no Nurture AI, no Focused Index, no CRM, Zapier or webhook sync, and no peripheral sub-domains. In one line: free captures and exports leads, it does not score, enrich or route them.

Pro is $49 a month. What can push the bill above that?

One add-on, and a credit pool that several features drink from. Read on 20 September 2026: Pro includes 5,000 lead profiles, 10-year retention and 3,000 credits a month, and the only block you can buy is Scale Credits at $89 a month per 10,000, stackable. The separate $49 block for extra profiles that this page carried in August is gone — profiles above 5,000 now cost 2 credits each out of the same pool. So size the pool, not the plan. The vendor prices deep profile enrichment at an average of 10 to 150 credits per profile research, which puts 3,000 credits somewhere between 20 and 300 researches; every Intelligence Brief pushed into Slack, a CRM, Zapier or a webhook costs 5 credits on top; and storage overage comes out of the same number. The full real-time session history, which they call the Intelligence Stream, is not sold by credit at all and needs an enterprise licence.

I run three websites. What does that cost?

Three subscriptions, unless two of them are sub-domains. Pro is $49 a month per website and includes three peripheral sub-domains — the vendor's own examples are a help centre or a web app — so documentation at docs.yourdomain.com is covered while a second brand on its own domain is not. Three separate domains is $147 a month before any credit block. Worth knowing before you plan around it: as read on 20 September 2026 the pricing page shows only two cards, Free and Pro, with no annual rate and no agency or enterprise price; the only enterprise mention left on the page is the licence required for the Intelligence Stream integration. A multi-site arrangement is therefore a sales conversation rather than a published number. The Pro trial is 30 days and takes no credit card.

Will Bread & Butter integrate with my existing stack?

Yes. It is built to sit on top of your existing stack rather than replace it. It installs through a lightweight, server-side site plugin, so ad-blockers and Safari ITP do not strip the tracking, and its Automation and Integrations panel then pushes enriched, scored lead activity into the tools you already run, triggered by rules such as a lead crossing a score threshold or an email being verified. Google Analytics (GA4) is a documented native integration, and there is an in-app integrations library plus conversion tools; check that library for your specific CRM and marketing platforms before you commit.
