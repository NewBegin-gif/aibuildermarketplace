What Breezy HR does

Recruiting and applicant tracking made simple. Post jobs to 50+ boards, manage candidates on a visual pipeline, and automate scheduling and screening.

Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 17 Aug 2026

What buyers report about Breezy HR

Recurring themes from G2, Capterra and TrustRadius, read in August 2026. We have not run Breezy HR ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Ease of use and quick setup are the dominant themes — 4.4 on G2 across 683 reviews, 4.5 on Capterra across roughly 1,400.
  • • Moving candidates visually through the pipeline and collaborative hiring are what reviewers say they would miss.
  • • On TrustRadius the reason people stay is plumbing rather than features: it pulls applicants in from Indeed and the major boards and from your own site into one place, and loading candidates and importing their CVs is described as effortless.
What they complain about
  • • Feature gating is steep. Interview scorecards, eSignatures and multiple pipelines need Growth at $273/month; HRIS integrations and offer management need Business at $439. The free Bootstrap tier and $157 Startup plan are narrower than they look.
  • • Monthly billing costs meaningfully more than annual — $189, $329 and $529 against $157, $273 and $439.
  • • Resume parsing glitches and limited advanced reporting come up regularly.
  • • A 2025 case documents a customer paying a renewal through Breezy's own portal and then being threatened with service interruption over a higher-priced contract. Breezy acknowledged their contracts and invoices do not sync. Keep your own record of what you agreed.
  • • The gap named in late 2025 is the AI-adjacent part: candidate matching does not tell you whether someone fits on skills, and CV parsing is weaker than the rest of the product. If you were hoping to shortlist automatically, that is the piece to test.
  • • An older reviewer reports the app crashing and slow responses from technical support — worth checking against current experience, since that account dates from 2022.

When the paid plan is worth it

Bootstrap is free and keeps unlimited users, but the pricing page caps it at "1 Active Pool or Position" and says Bootstrap plans give unlimited access only to candidates added within the last 30 days. That 30-day window, more than the single opening, is what usually forces an upgrade: older applicants stop being reachable. Startup at $157/mo annually ($189 month-to-month) lifts both limits, which is the entire case for teams that hire more than one role at a time. Growth at $273/mo adds scorecards, interview guides and automated reference checking; Business at $439/mo ($529 monthly) adds custom roles and permissions, HRIS integrations and offer management. The biggest step in the paid table is Growth to Business, and it buys governance, not reach. Upgrading does not solve everything: SMS ($41/mo), Onboard ($49/mo) and Breezy Intelligence credits (from $30 per 100,000) remain paid add-ons at every tier, while API access, advanced reporting and SSL enforcement on custom domains sit behind the quote-only Custom Pro plan. Annual billing "includes 2 months free!"

Common Breezy HR setup mistakes

Where Breezy HR setups actually go wrong, and what it costs you when they do. Same sources as the setup notes above — not from a walkthrough we ran ourselves.

The friction sits downstream.

  • Job posting guidelines require a full company name, City, State and Country, and a description of at least 700 characters, and only paid positions can reach job boards.
  • Breezy approves postings "usually within one business day", boards can still decline, and the docs say that if nothing appears within 24 hours of approval the ad likely missed a board's criteria.
  • A custom careers domain is paid-plans-only, needs a CNAME, and is not self-serve: you email support to have the subdomain attached, and SSL enforcement is a Custom Pro item.
  • Imports cap at 10 MB per CSV and 50 MB per resume batch, need text-based files rather than scanned PDFs, require manual column mapping, and positions close at 90,000 candidates.

Frequently asked questions

What does Breezy HR cost?

The free Bootstrap plan covers one open position with unlimited users, permanently. Paid runs $157, $273 and $439 a month billed annually ($189, $329 and $529 monthly) for Startup, Growth and Business, with Pro quoted. Read on 17 August 2026.

Why does 'unlimited users' matter here?

Because most applicant tracking is sold per recruiter seat, so involving hiring managers costs money. Breezy includes unlimited users and unlimited open positions on every paid tier, which changes the comparison for a small company where five people interview but nobody recruits full-time.

Is onboarding included?

No — it is a separate product starting at $49 a month, providing automated role-based workflows that sync with the ATS. If your reason for buying is the handover from offer to first day, budget that alongside the hiring plan rather than assuming it is bundled.

Does Breezy HR replace my current setup or sit on top of it?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.

Who should not use Breezy HR?

Skip it if you hire once or twice a year — the free single-position plan is plenty, and the paid jump only pays off with concurrent open roles. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
