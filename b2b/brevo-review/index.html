Brevo Review (2026)

Growth & RevenueResearched assessment · by Daniel HaketUpdated 2026-07-29

Growth tools only earn their keep if they move a real number — leads, conversions or revenue — not just activity. So where does Brevo actually fit? Most people land here while weighing it against Mailchimp, so that is the comparison this review keeps coming back to: does it do the job you actually need, without charging you for the parts you don't?

The honest bottom line. Worth it for businesses with large contact lists who email selectively and want to pay by send volume, not list size. Skip it once you email your whole list daily — the per-send model can flip from cheap to costly at high frequency.

What Brevo does

Email marketing, SMS and CRM in one platform. Design campaigns, automate follow-ups and manage contacts so you can nurture leads and customers from a single hub.

Pricing (2026)

Free forever (300 emails/day, unlimited contacts); paid Starter from about $9/mo and Business from $18/mo, scaling by monthly send volume rather than contact count (2026). Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Charging by sends rather than contacts is genuinely cheaper for big lists you email infrequently — but the free daily cap and add-on fees mean high-frequency senders should model their real send volume first.

When the paid plan is worth it

Brevo's own API documentation names five account types, Free, Starter, Standard, Professional and Enterprise, and the rate-limit table shows exactly where those tiers separate. The general rate limits, including 1,000 requests per second on the transactional email endpoint, are listed as available to all five, so a free account is not throttled below a paid one on raw sending throughput. The first real gate is Professional: the advanced rate limits, 2,000 requests per second on email and 600 requests per hour on the SMTP endpoints instead of 300, are available only to Professional and Enterprise accounts. The widest jump in the tables is Enterprise, where extended limits reach 6,000 requests per second and the quota table moves active automation workflows from a general 50 to 500 and stored contacts from 5M to 900M. Upgrading does not solve deliverability, domain authentication or list hygiene, since the DNS and sender setup is identical on every tier. If your constraint is monthly send volume rather than throughput, campaign count or workflow count, the documented tier differences buy you nothing you would notice.

How to actually use Brevo

Brevo's quickstart is short. You generate an API key from the Brevo account, optionally whitelist your IP address, then send a first request to the v3 API with the api-key header. That is 3 steps, and a 200 response carrying your account details means you are through. The friction sits elsewhere. Domain authentication is a separate flow with 3 options, DNS records, hosting a file, or sending an email, and the DNS route needs 2 TXT records, a DKIM record and a Brevo code; a value mismatch between them returns HTTP 400. The docs state no propagation or waiting time, so budget for whatever your DNS host takes. SMTP senders configure port 587 or 2525 unencrypted, or 465 with SSL or TLS, and should note that the relay does not support batch sending and that scheduled delivery is in public beta with a delay of up to 5 minutes. The API key screen itself sits behind a customer login.
