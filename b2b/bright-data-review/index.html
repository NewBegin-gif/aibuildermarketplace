What Bright Data does

The web data platform trusted by enterprises. Collect public web data at scale with proxies, ready-made datasets and scraping APIs — fuel for your market research and AI projects.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

This is enterprise-grade web data infrastructure at enterprise-grade prices — the per-GB meter looks small until you multiply by real scraping volume, and the committed tiers only pay off if your usage is steady. The bigger honest note is compliance: Bright Data is strict about KYC and use-cases (a feature, not a bug), and whatever you collect still has to respect the target sites' terms and applicable law. Budget legal thought alongside the gigabytes.

When the paid plan is worth it

Bright Data's clearest paid/free split is on the API products. The Web Scraper API, Web Unlocker and SERP API each publish a free tier of 5K records or requests per month with no credit card, then Pay as you go, then Scale, then a custom-priced Enterprise tier. The biggest jump in the table is from Pay as you go to Scale. Pay as you go is $1.5/1K with no commitment; Scale is $499 /month, bundles 384,000 records and drops the overage rate to $1.3/1K. That commitment only repays itself if your steady monthly volume is high enough that the included allowance beats what $499 would buy at the pay-as-you-go rate, and below that the no-commitment tier is simply cheaper. Proxies follow the same shape: residential Pay As You Go shows $4.00 / GB against a struck-through $8, and the $2.50/GB tier needs a $1,999 monthly commitment. What spending more does not fix is access. Residential proxies stay gated behind a human-reviewed KYC check at every tier, and the docs state approval is never automatic, instant or self-serve.

How to actually use Bright Data

The vendor's Web Unlocker quickstart is short: 6 steps in the control panel — sign in, open the Create an API page, choose Web Unlocker API, name it, add a payment method (free trial users only), and finalize creation. The zone then exposes an API key and zone name on its Overview tab, which you send to the api.brightdata.com/request endpoint. The docs warn the API key is shown on screen only, so it has to be copied and stored straight away. The real friction sits elsewhere. Residential proxies are not self-serve: the docs limit access to verified companies that pass a human-reviewed KYC check, require a corporate email domain (a personal Gmail or Outlook account cannot be approved), require funds already on the account balance, and state KYC cannot be started from Playground or Limited Trial mode. Submission needs a company registration document plus business and use-case detail, status arrives within 48 hours, and it is tracked under Account Settings > Profile. ISP proxies, datacenter proxies and Web Unlocker skip KYC entirely. Distilled from Vendor material only: Bright Data's own pricing pages plus the Web Unlocker quickstart, the send-your-first-request page and the residential network-access and FAQ docs on docs.brightdata.com; we found no independent walkthrough worth citing, since the setup guides that surfaced were on competing proxy vendors' and comparison sites.

Best for: data teams and enterprises with real, ongoing web-data needs (pricing intelligence, market research) that justify committed volume and pass use-case vetting.
Who should skip it: if you need occasional small scrapes (Browse.ai-class tools or datasets are far cheaper), or your use-case wouldn't survive a compliance review.

The natural comparison is Oxylabs or Smartproxy — the premium proxy/data peers — compare effective per-GB at your committed volume and the quality on your specific target sites. Decide by which one fits the job above, not by the louder brand.

Frequently asked questions

Is the "50% OFF" on Bright Data's pricing a real discount?

Treat the discounted number as the price. Every capture we hold of that page — 7 July, 2 August and 11 August 2026 — shows the same struck-through list price beside the same discounted one, on residential proxies and on the Scraper APIs alike. A sale that is present in every observation is not an event you need to hurry for.

What does Bright Data cost to actually start?

Less than the headline suggests on the free tier and more than it suggests on proxies. New accounts get 5,000 credits a month, which Bright Data values at about $7.50, shared across Unlocker, SERP, Web Scraper and Scraper Studio; credits renew on the 1st, do not roll over, and stop hard. Proxies are not in that free tier. And the "from $2.5/GB" on the residential card is the $1,999-a-month tier: with no commitment you pay $4.00/GB against a struck-through $8 (read 11 August 2026).

Can I just sign up and use residential proxies?

No, and this is the step that catches people rather than the price. The vendor docs restrict residential access to verified companies that pass a human-reviewed KYC check: a corporate email domain is required (a personal Gmail or Outlook account cannot be approved), funds must already be on the account, and KYC cannot be started from Playground or Limited Trial mode. Submission needs a company registration document plus business and use-case detail, and status arrives within 48 hours. ISP proxies, datacenter proxies and Web Unlocker skip KYC entirely.

Is Bright Data secure and reliable enough to depend on?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.

Who should not use Bright Data?

Skip it if you need occasional small scrapes (Browse.ai-class tools or datasets are far cheaper), or your use-case wouldn't survive a compliance review. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
