What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Credits burn faster than expected: row counts, screenshots and 'premium' sites all multiply the meter, so monitor a big site daily and do the math first. And scrapers break when sites redesign — robots need maintenance, which is your time even when the tool is no-code. Also check the terms of the sites you monitor; 'technically possible' and 'permitted' aren't the same thing.

What buyers report about Browse.ai

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026 (Chrome Web Store reviews dated June 2023 to February 2026). We have not run Browse.ai ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • You can have a working scraper without code in minutes — ease of setup and support are what the positive reviews are about. 4.7 on G2.
  • • There is a real free plan: 50 credits a month across 2 sites.
  • • Extension users describe it as genuinely point-and-click no-code scraping, used for things like enriching LinkedIn data (June 2024), and one reviewer runs a daily email and spreadsheet update from monitored sites that works even on very old-fashioned pages (June 2023).
What they complain about
  • • When a target site redesigns, your robot breaks and needs retraining. This is the top recurring complaint and the hidden running cost of the whole category.
  • • JavaScript-heavy sites cause timeouts and failures.
  • • Credit-based pricing with no good way to estimate a job's cost before running it — reviewers describe watching credits drain and hitting overages mid-task.
  • • Anyone expecting a set-and-forget data feed is the group that ends up frustrated.
  • • Concrete breakage rather than general grumbling: one reviewer reports Skyscanner simply blocking it (February 2024), and another describes the studio breaking when used with his own cookies and login, with recordings failing to upload (June 2024).
  • • Reliability of the robot itself is questioned by a July 2023 reviewer who says it worked properly about one time in ten and that the capture is not automatic — you still have to tell it what to pull.
  • • Support gaps are visible in public: a July 2023 reviewer left for a competitor calling it overpriced and underwhelming, and the developer replied openly that technical support for free accounts had been paused while the user base grew. Later reviews suggest that improved but did not fully close.

Where the ratings disagree. The extension listing is harsher than the G2 and Capterra picture, and the reason is who writes there: people using the free tier through the browser, not teams who bought a plan and got onboarding. Read it as a good preview of what the free tier feels like unaided — which is exactly the tier most people try first.

When the paid plan is worth it

The free tier gives 50 credits a month on 2 websites, enough to prove a robot works but not to run one on a schedule. Personal costs $19 per month billed annually for 12,000 credits a year and 5 websites; month to month it is $48 for 2,000 credits a month. Professional is $69 per month billed annually for 60,000 credits a year and 10 websites; month to month it is $87 for 5,000 credits a month.

Personal to Professional is the step most buyers will actually reach: five times the credits for 3.6 times the annual price. The biggest jump is Professional to Premium, starting at $500 per month billed annually for 600,000+ credits a year, so it only earns its keep at real volume or when the managed onboarding is the point. Upgrading does not fix compatibility. The vendor's own help pages say custom CAPTCHAs are not solved, that neither MFA nor 2FA is supported (though their own login guide contradicts this), and that heavily bot-protected sites may still fail.

No tier changes that. Credit burn follows job shape too: a 100-row list plus its 100 detail pages costs 110 credits on any plan.

How to actually use Browse.ai

The documented route is short. From the dashboard you click Build New Robot, choose extraction or monitoring, enter the Origin URL, and click Start Training Robot. Robot Studio loads the live page and you point and click to capture data 'From a list' or as 'Just text', or take screenshots. You then set pagination, label columns, finish recording and approve the sample. Browse.ai says this takes under 10 minutes (their claim, not our measurement).

Distilled from Vendor material only: the Browse.ai pricing page plus help-center articles on building a first robot, Robot Studio training, credit calculation, scraping behind a login, cookie pop-up failures, robots that stop working and site compatibility; we found no independent walkthrough worth citing.

Common Browse.ai setup mistakes

Where Browse.ai setups actually go wrong, and what it costs you when they do. Same sources as the setup notes above — not from a walkthrough we ran ourselves.

The friction comes afterwards.

  • For login-protected pages you must already be signed in in your own browser before handing over session cookies, and the docs recommend refreshing them monthly and after any password change.
  • Cookie consent banners are a documented breakage cause, because the robot replays your training clicks and the banner varies by region or A/B test.
  • Layout changes break robots too; the docs say to contact support if retraining is needed more than once a month.
  • The docs also contradict themselves on 2FA.

The natural comparison is Octoparse or Apify — close scraping platforms — Apify is more developer-oriented; compare credit math on YOUR row volumes and site list.

Frequently asked questions

What does Browse.ai actually cost to run a robot on a schedule?

The free tier is a proof of concept rather than a plan: 50 credits a month across 2 websites, which shows a robot works but will not keep one running. Personal is $19 a month billed annually, or $48 month to month, for 5 websites. Professional is $69 annually or $87 monthly for 10 websites. Read on their pricing page on 15 August 2026. The figure to model is credits rather than seats: a 100-row list plus its 100 detail pages costs 110 credits, whatever plan you are on.

Has Browse.ai changed its prices recently?

Not by a character. Our monthly captures of browse.ai/pricing from July and August 2026 are byte-for-byte identical, the same 18,413 characters, and reading the page again on 15 August returns the same figures. If you priced it a month ago, that quote stands.

Does a bigger plan fix robots that keep breaking?

No, and this is the thing to weigh before upgrading. Browse.ai's own help pages say custom CAPTCHAs are not solved and that heavily bot-protected sites may still fail, and no tier changes either. Cookie banners and layout changes break robots on every plan; what you buy with a bigger plan is credits, websites and support speed, not compatibility. If a site needs retraining more than once a month, the vendor's own advice is to contact support rather than to pay more.

What should I weigh before buying IT & Productivity software?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.
