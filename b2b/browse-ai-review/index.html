What Browse.ai does

No-code web scraping and data extraction. Turn any website into an automated data pipeline or spreadsheet automatically.

Pricing (2026)

Credit-metered (2026): Free ~50 credits/mo (2 sites); Personal about $19/mo annual (12,000 credits/yr, 5 sites); Professional ~$69/mo annual (60,000 credits, 10 sites); Premium custom (600k+ credits, managed). A credit ≈ 10 extracted rows; premium sites cost more credits. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

Correction after re-checking: Premium is no longer listed as purely custom pricing: the pricing page now shows "Starting at $500" per month billed annually for 600,000+ credits per year (re-verified 2026-07-29).

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Two honest realities of no-code scraping: credits burn faster than expected (row counts, screenshots and 'premium' sites all multiply the meter — monitor a big site daily and do the math first), and scrapers break when sites redesign — robots need maintenance, which is your time even when the tool is no-code. Also check the terms of the sites you monitor; 'technically possible' and 'permitted' aren't the same thing.

Recurring themes from G2, Capterra and Trustpilot, read in August 2026. We have not run Browse.ai ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise

  • • There is a real free plan: 50 credits a month across 2 sites.
What they complain about
  • • When a target site redesigns, your robot breaks and needs retraining. This is the top recurring complaint and the hidden running cost of the whole category.
  • • JavaScript-heavy sites cause timeouts and failures.
  • • Credit-based pricing with no good way to estimate a job's cost before running it — reviewers describe watching credits drain and hitting overages mid-task.

Where the ratings disagree. 4.7 on G2 against 2.7 on Trustpilot across 14 reviews. The Trustpilot sample is small, so weigh it lightly — but the complaints in it are billing and reliability, which is the same pattern as everything else metered by credits.

When the paid plan is worth it

The free tier gives 50 credits a month on 2 websites, enough to prove a robot works but not to run one on a schedule. Personal costs $19 per month billed annually for 12,000 credits a year and 5 websites; month to month it is $48 for 2,000 credits a month. Professional is $69 per month billed annually for 60,000 credits a year and 10 websites; month to month it is $87 for 5,000 credits a month. Personal to Professional is the step most buyers will actually reach: five times the credits for 3.6 times the annual price. The biggest jump is Professional to Premium, starting at $500 per month billed annually for 600,000+ credits a year, so it only earns its keep at real volume or when the managed onboarding is the point. Upgrading does not fix compatibility. The vendor's own help pages say custom CAPTCHAs are not solved, that neither MFA nor 2FA is supported (though their own login guide contradicts this), and that heavily bot-protected sites may still fail. No tier changes that. Credit burn follows job shape too: a 100-row list plus its 100 detail pages costs 110 credits on any plan.

How to actually use Browse.ai

The documented route is short. From the dashboard you click Build New Robot, choose extraction or monitoring, enter the Origin URL, and click Start Training Robot. Robot Studio loads the live page and you point and click to capture data 'From a list' or as 'Just text', or take screenshots. You then set pagination, label columns, finish recording and approve the sample. Browse.ai says this takes under 10 minutes (their claim, not our measurement). The friction comes afterwards. For login-protected pages you must already be signed in in your own browser before handing over session cookies, and the docs recommend refreshing them monthly and after any password change. Cookie consent banners are a documented breakage cause, because the robot replays your training clicks and the banner varies by region or A/B test. Layout changes break robots too; the docs say to contact support if retraining is needed more than once a month. The docs also contradict themselves on 2FA. Distilled from Vendor material only: the Browse.ai pricing page plus help-center articles on building a first robot, Robot Studio training, credit calculation, scraping behind a login, cookie pop-up failures, robots that stop working and site compatibility; we found no independent walkthrough worth citing.

Best for: ops, research and growth teams that want to monitor competitors, prices or listings and pipe structured web data into sheets without writing scrapers.
Who should skip it: if you need one-off extractions (free tools or a VA are cheaper), or mission-critical pipelines where you'd rather own the code than rent the robot.

The natural comparison is Octoparse or Apify — close scraping platforms — Apify is more developer-oriented; compare credit math on YOUR row volumes and site list. Decide by which one fits the job above, not by the louder brand.

Frequently asked questions

Is Browse.ai secure and reliable enough to depend on?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.

Who should not use Browse.ai?

Skip it if you need one-off extractions (free tools or a VA are cheaper), or mission-critical pipelines where you'd rather own the code than rent the robot. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
