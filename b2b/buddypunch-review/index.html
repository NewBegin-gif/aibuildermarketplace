BuddyPunch Review (2026)

Operations & WorkflowResearched assessment · by Daniel HaketUpdated 2026-08-01

The best operations tools remove repetitive work quietly — the win is fewer dropped balls and less manual oversight, not more dashboards. So where does BuddyPunch actually fit?

The honest bottom line. Worth it for small-to-mid teams that need straightforward employee time tracking with GPS and payroll-ready exports. Skip it once you're a team of two or three — the fixed base fee makes the per-head cost sting until you scale up.

What BuddyPunch does

Time clock and employee time tracking for hourly teams. Track hours, breaks, PTO and overtime with GPS and photo punch-in, and export payroll-ready reports.

Pricing (2026)

Per-user plus a base fee: Starter about $5.49/user/mo, Pro $6.99, Enterprise $11.99, each with a roughly $19/mo base fee (2026). GPS and scheduling are add-ons on lower tiers and a payroll add-on costs extra. 14-day trial, no card. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

Correction after re-checking: Buddy Punch's help center now lists Starter at $4.99/user/mo annual ($5.99 monthly), Pro at $6.99 annual ($7.99 monthly) and Advanced/Enterprise at $12.99 annual ($13.99 monthly) on a $19.00 base fee, so our Starter $5.49 and Enterprise $11.99 figures are out of date, and our Pro figure of $6.99 now applies only to annual billing (monthly Pro is $7.99) (re-verified 2026-07-29).

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

That flat $19 base fee makes it pricey for very small teams — at three or four users the base fee dominates the bill, so it's better value once you're past a handful of employees.

When the paid plan is worth it

Buddy Punch's help center documents three tiers, all carrying the same $19.00 monthly base fee: Starter at $4.99/user/month on annual billing or $5.99/user/month monthly, Pro at $6.99 annual or $7.99 monthly, and the top tier (headed Advanced, priced as Enterprise) at $12.99 annual or $13.99 monthly. The biggest jump is Pro to Advanced, where the per-user rate nearly doubles and what it buys is Real-Time GPS at no extra cost, dedicated enterprise support and SSO via Okta, OneLogin and SAML 2.0. API access is already included in Pro, so that step only pays for itself if you need enterprise SSO or the dedicated support line: Real-Time GPS is a $2 per user/month add-on on Pro. The cheaper Starter-to-Pro step unlocks QR code, PIN and kiosk punching, webcam photos on punches, API access and scheduling at no additional cost; scheduling alone is a $1 per user/month add-on on Starter, so a team that only wants shifts can stay on Starter. Upgrading does not change billing mechanics: users added beyond your initial count are billed prorated, with no refunds or credits when the count drops. Payroll is a separate add-on at $8 per user/month plus a $49 monthly base.

How to actually use BuddyPunch

The vendor's quick start guide reduces first value to three major steps: add employees, start tracking time, then run reports for payroll. Employees are added individually from the Employees section, or in bulk from a CSV template carrying username, first name, last name, email address, password and timezone, with administrators running the bulk upload. Usernames are shared across Buddy Punch, so a name another customer already uses will not import. The welcome-email checkbox on the bulk import is unchecked by default, so a technically clean import can leave staff never told they have logins. Anything payroll-related is a separate onboarding of five sections (terms of service, organization details, payment setup, tax setup, filing authorizations); linking the bank through Plaid verifies immediately, but typing account details by hand triggers a $0 prenote test transaction and may bring a request for documents proving account ownership. The docs tie completing it to getting approved for 2-day or 3-day payroll more quickly. Shared-device punching by QR code or PIN/kiosk is not in Starter, so a breakroom-tablet setup needs Pro from the start. Frequently asked questions

Does BuddyPunch replace my current setup or sit on top of it?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.

Who should not use BuddyPunch?

Skip it if you're a team of two or three — the fixed base fee makes the per-head cost sting until you scale up. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
