By Daan Haket · Tested hands-on · written with an AI assistant · who does what

That flat $19 base fee makes it pricey for very small teams — at three or four users the base fee dominates the bill, so it's better value once you're past a handful of employees.

Best for

small-to-mid teams that need straightforward employee time tracking with GPS and payroll-ready exports.

Skip it if

you're a team of two or three — the fixed base fee makes the per-head cost sting until you scale up.

When the paid plan is worth it

Buddy Punch's help center documents three tiers, all carrying the same $19.00 monthly base fee. Starter is $4.99/user/month on annual billing or $5.99/user/month monthly, Pro $6.99 annual or $7.99 monthly, and the top tier (headed Advanced, priced as Enterprise) $12.99 annual or $13.99 monthly. The biggest jump is Pro to Advanced, where the per-user rate nearly doubles and what it buys is Real-Time GPS at no extra cost, dedicated enterprise support and SSO via Okta, OneLogin and SAML 2.0.

API access is already included in Pro, so that step only pays for itself if you need enterprise SSO or the dedicated support line: Real-Time GPS is a $2 per user/month add-on on Pro. The cheaper Starter-to-Pro step unlocks QR code, PIN and kiosk punching, webcam photos on punches, API access and scheduling at no additional cost; scheduling alone is a $1 per user/month add-on on Starter, so a team that only wants shifts can stay on Starter.

Upgrading does not change billing mechanics: users added beyond your initial count are billed prorated, with no refunds or credits when the count drops. Payroll is a separate add-on at $8 per user/month plus a $49 monthly base. Set against two rivals in our files, the base fee decides the small-team comparison. Deputy's Lite plan is $5 per user a month on annual billing and $5.50 monthly, with a $30 minimum per invoice on monthly plans (read 19 September 2026); Connecteam's Small Business Plan is free for up to 10 users (read 22 October 2026).

For five people on monthly billing, Buddy Punch Starter is $48.95 a month, Deputy Lite reaches its $30 minimum, and Connecteam costs nothing. The gap narrows as headcount grows, because the $19 base fee is spread over more people.

What we found running BuddyPunch ourselves

Hands-on · 2026-10-29

The first findings are at the top of this page. The rest:

  • Each punch shows the IP address of whoever entered it, here the manager.
  • The standard export ('Export to Excel' on the payroll export) is actually a CSV, with employee ID, names and hours: 1.500 and 2.000, which match our test shifts. It has no notes, emails or start and end times. The employee profile export is part of a paid add-on ($2 per user a month).
  • The employee import takes eight fixed columns (username, names, email and password required). It has no notes field and no update option, so you cannot change records by importing again.
  • API access is only in the Advanced plan ($12.99 per employee a month plus $19), not in the Pro plan the trial runs on.

How to actually use BuddyPunch

The vendor's quick start guide reduces first value to three major steps: add employees, start tracking time, then run reports for payroll. Employees are added individually from the Employees section, or in bulk from a CSV template carrying username, first name, last name, email address, password and timezone, with administrators running the bulk upload. Usernames are shared across Buddy Punch, so a name another customer already uses will not import.

The welcome-email checkbox on the bulk import is unchecked by default, so a technically clean import can leave staff never told they have logins. Anything payroll-related is a separate onboarding of five sections: terms of service, organization details, payment setup, tax setup and filing authorizations. Linking the bank through Plaid verifies immediately, but typing account details by hand triggers a $0 prenote test transaction and may bring a request for documents proving account ownership.

The docs tie completing it to getting approved for 2-day or 3-day payroll more quickly. Shared-device punching by QR code or PIN/kiosk is not in Starter, so a breakroom-tablet setup needs Pro from the start. Check the import route with support before planning a large roll-out. The roles-and-permissions article (updated 24 December 2025) marks bulk upload of employees as something neither admins nor managers can do in the app and asks you to contact Buddy Punch support, who will run the bulk upload for you.

Before go-live, an admin should also set the account-wide rules only admins control: automatic punch-out based on hours worked or on the scheduled shift, GPS tracking during shifts, webcam photos on punches, and whether managers may see pay rates. Changing an employee's overtime rule later does not recalculate past pay periods on its own; the same article says support has to do that.

Frequently asked questions

Can you export your data from BuddyPunch?

Exit check 2026-10-29; route excerpt: Time Cards > Export > Payroll Export > Export to Excel; came along (excerpts): Both Employee Id values and Unicode names survive …; lost (excerpts): The standard hours CSV omits employee free text, hire date, email and shift start/end timestamps; re-import: not tested.

What does Buddy Punch actually cost a team of three or five?

More per head than the sticker suggests, because of the base fee. Read 24 September 2026 on buddypunch.com/pricing, every plan adds a $19 monthly base fee to the per-user rate, on monthly and annual billing alike. On Starter paid monthly ($5.99 a user) three people come to $36.97 a month, about $12.32 each; five people come to $48.95, about $9.79 each. On annual Starter ($4.99) five people are $43.95 a month. Pro at $7.99 monthly puts five people at $58.95. The base fee is the same on every tier, so it weighs heaviest on the smallest teams and fades as headcount grows.

Do managers, admins and seasonal staff each need a paid seat?

Managers do, administrators and inactive staff do not. The FAQ, read 24 September 2026, names three user types: employees and managers are paid and count towards your total, administrators are free and unlimited. Only active employees count; terminated or seasonal staff can stay in the system as inactive. The catch is the seat count itself: a plan is bought for a set number of employees, you can swap people in and out within that number, and going above it is charged pro rata for the rest of the billing cycle. So a summer peak costs extra seats for the remainder of the month or year, not just for the weeks you used them.

What if Buddy Punch does not work out after we pay?

The window is short on monthly and longer on annual. Per the refund FAQ, read 24 September 2026, a full, non-prorated refund is available if you cancel within 72 hours of signing up for monthly billing or within 30 days for annual billing. Subscriptions renew automatically until an administrator cancels under Settings, Billing, Cancel my Account. Once you are on annual you cannot move to monthly until the term ends, while monthly to annual is allowed any time. Cancelling also deletes your data and access to prior records, so export time reports first. The 14-day trial needs no card, so test during the trial rather than relying on the refund.

Can the account owner track their own hours on an admin login?

No. Buddy Punch's roles-and-permissions article (updated 24 December 2025) says administrators are free accounts that do not count towards billing, and as a result cannot punch in or out, track breaks or track time off. An owner who also works shifts needs a second, paid employee or manager login for their own time. Billing is admin-only: managers cannot open the billing page, change the plan or its frequency, see invoices, pause or cancel the account.

Can employees see their own time cards and other people's schedules?

Their own, yes; other people's only if you allow it. The same article says employees can export the Time Card report for their own hours and by default see only their own schedule, availability and time off; an admin can open up the schedule, availability or time off of colleagues. Labour costs stay hidden from employees and managers unless allowed, and an admin decides whether managers may see pay rates for the staff assigned to them.

Does Buddy Punch have an API and an AI connector?

Yes. The public API launched as v1 on 8 April 2026 with scoped API keys. Since 7 May 2026 rate limits depend on the plan: 60 requests a minute on a trial or unpaid account, 600 on paid plans below Enterprise and 1,000 on Enterprise, and a trial account may hold one active API key. The Claude connector (help article dated 18 August 2026) needs an administrator on Pro, Advanced or Enterprise, connects at api2.buddypunch.com/mcp and lets you grant read or write access area by area, so a read-only trial is possible. The API changelog added employee settings on 22 September, projected time-off balances on 13 October and time-off balances in the connector on 16 October 2026.

Can Buddy Punch webhooks notify my system when someone punches in?

Not at the moment. Since 16 October 2026 the API rejects subscriptions to punch, employee, shift, time card and other core events with an invalid_event_type error, because Buddy Punch was accepting them without ever sending them. The events it does send cover tasks, onboarding and offboarding, punch questions and daily sign-offs, and only where those features are switched on for your account. A failing endpoint is disabled after 10 failed deliveries in a row, so an integration that needs live punches has to poll the API.

What happens if a Buddy Punch card payment fails?

The pricing FAQ (read 22 October 2026) says the account email is notified after each failed payment and the charge is retried up to 4 times within the next month; if none succeeds the account is cancelled, and a cancelled account loses access to prior records and has its data deleted. Keep the card current, and export time reports regularly if the account runs on a personal card.
