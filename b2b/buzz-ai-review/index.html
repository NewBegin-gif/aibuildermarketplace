When the paid plan is worth it

The limit that bites first is credits, not features. Core includes 500 credits per seat per month and AI Employee 5,000 (read 5 September 2026), and that single pool pays for data enrichment, AI functions and dialer calls alike. At 1 credit per enriched email, a seat that prospects a few hundred fresh contacts a month will drain the balance before it gets much use out of the AI, and "Run out of credits" is one of the documented reasons a campaign step stalls.

If you work lists you already own and run social sequences without enrichment, Core at $79 per seat (September 2026; it was $125 in August) is enough, and the AI Employee tier is wasted money. It buys 5,000 credits plus AI targeting and messaging, which only repays you if you enrich and dial at volume. What upgrading does not fix at any tier: Buzz drives your own social account, so InMails still need a separate Sales Navigator subscription, and the platform's own caps still apply, including a 1,900-character ceiling on an InMail. Cancelling also requires 31 days of written notice regardless of plan.

How to actually use Buzz.ai

Start with the free seat, which needs no card, then connect a sending account before anything else. Buzz has you add the account manually: choose a geographic location (optionally your own proxy), set timezone and working hours, then enter the account email and password. Since April 2025 two-step verification is required for Sales Navigator accounts, so keep your phone to hand, because you get prompted for the code inside the workspace.

Sending then ramps: Warming Up raises daily actions from your minimum toward your maximum, and clicking Confirm restarts that ramp. Click "+ Create New Campaign", pick Simple, Smart or Magic, name it, paste the post or event you want to target, then add steps such as Send Connection Request and Send Message and set the delays between them. Where it goes wrong in practice: delays are a floor, not a promise, and the documentation warns that a one-hour delay does not guarantee the step runs in one hour.

Steps also stall quietly on an invalid email, a disconnected email or CRM integration, or an empty credit balance.

Frequently asked questions

What does Buzz.ai cost?

Core is $79 per seat a month with 500 credits and AI Employee $695 per seat a month with 5,000 credits, both billed monthly with a $0 setup fee footnoted 'waived on prepaid annual'. Enterprise is custom with 25,000 credits, five seats and a $2,500 setup fee; the white-label plans ($1,000 a month for 20 seats, or $250 per seat with a five-seat minimum, both billed annually) carry the same $2,500 fee. Read on 5 September 2026. In August the same page said Core $125 (launch offer $75 for three months, 500 credits) and AI Employee $795 (5,000 credits).

Is the $795 tier about to get more expensive?

It went the other way. In August 2026 Buzz.ai's own page said the AI Employee plan 'moves to $1,000/mo soon' and invited you to lock in $795. By 1 September the plan read $695 a month. The $2,500 setup fee we then saw on 'one plan' sits, on the 5 September page, on Enterprise and the white-label plans, not on AI Employee — but the $0 fee on the Teams plans is marked 'waived on prepaid annual', so ask what a monthly contract is charged before you compare first-year totals.

What happens after the three-month launch price?

The launch offer is gone from the page. In August 2026 Core was $75 for three months and then $125; since 1 September the page simply lists Core at $79 a month. If you signed up on the launch offer, check what your fourth month bills at rather than assuming either number.

What should I weigh before buying Sales & CRM software?

Most tools in this category plug in rather than replace. Check which direction the data flows and whether the sync is genuinely two-way, because a one-way push creates a second source of truth that your reps will quietly stop trusting. If it does aim to replace your CRM, price the migration honestly: history, custom fields and reporting rarely move across cleanly.
