What CallRail does

Call tracking and attribution — which campaigns, keywords and ads produce phone calls and form leads. Plans run about $50–195/month, but included minutes, numbers and SMS are modest and overages meter at roughly $0.05 a local minute. It is an attribution layer, not a phone system or CRM.

Pricing (2026)

(2026) Four plans about $50–$195/mo: Call Tracking $50, +Conversation Intelligence or +Form Tracking $100, Complete $195. Prices assume tight limits (~250 local minutes, 5 numbers, 25 SMS); overages meter (~$0.05/local minute, ~$3 per extra number). 14-day trial, no free plan. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The headline price is a floor, not a ceiling — the included minutes, numbers and SMS are modest, so an active campaign quickly hits metered overages that meaningfully lift the bill. It's also specifically an attribution tool: it proves which marketing drives calls and leads, but it isn't a phone system or CRM, so you add it alongside those, not instead of them.

What buyers report about CallRail

Recurring themes from G2, GetApp and Trustpilot, read in August 2026. We have not run CallRail ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Call attribution that marketers actually trust, and easy to run — 4.5 on G2 across 1,600+ reviews, with several reviewers saying they would pay more.
  • • A 14-day trial on every tier.
What they complain about

  • • Tier-2 support non-responsiveness surfaced independently in separate reviews.

When the paid plan is worth it

CallRail has no free plan, so the real choice is the entry tier versus the intelligence tiers. Lead Tracking starts at $50/mo on annual billing, or $55 month-to-month, and includes 5 local numbers, 250 local minutes and 25 text messages. Users are unlimited, so the bill scales with call volume rather than headcount — and volume is what squeezes first. Past 250 minutes each extra local minute is $0.06, while the next bucket up, 750 minutes, is $68/mo. Upgrade for volume before you upgrade for features. The feature tiers — Lead Conversion at $150/mo for Premium Conversation Intelligence, Lead Conversion Complete at $195/mo adding form tracking — only repay themselves if someone will actually read the call summaries and sentiment scoring and shift ad spend because of them. What no tier fixes is the metered model: numbers, minutes, texts, transcription and analysis minutes are billed on top at every price point, and the 25-message text allowance is identical on the $50 plan and on the $265 one.

How to actually use CallRail

Every account starts with a 14-day free trial and no credit card. CallRail documents the first pass as under 10 minutes: create a tracking number, install the JavaScript snippet, set up notifications, place a test call, then open the activity dashboard. Paste the snippet on every page right before the closing </body> tag, then dial your tracking number from a phone other than the destination number — if the destination rings, the routing works. The snippet is where it goes wrong. Dynamic number insertion only swaps a number that matches your swap target, so span tags splitting the digits, stylised dashes, or a script nested inside an iframe or noscript element will fail silently, and caching plugins such as WP Rocket can strip the snippet altogether. The slower traps are scheduling ones: texting is blocked during the trial and needs A2P 10DLC registration that can take up to 30 days, and porting an existing number in runs 4-6 weeks. Distilled from CallRail help center: getting-started, JavaScript snippet, swap target formatting, caching plugins, 10DLC registration and number porting articles

Is CallRail worth it for a small team?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.
