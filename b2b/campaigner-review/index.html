Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 5 Sep 2026

Campaigner prices by contact count and bills per account, not per user: a slider sets the tier and there is no separate monthly/annual toggle. Two self-serve plans: Essentials from $14 a month at 1,000 contacts ($38 at 5,000, $158 at 25,000, $409 at 100,000) and Advanced from $35 a month at 1,000 contacts ($70 at 5,000, $258 at 25,000, $649 at 100,000), plus a quote-only Custom tier.

Essentials excludes automation workflows and conversion tracking but includes the REST API, webhooks and SFTP; landing pages, sub-accounts and white-label are Custom only. SMS/MMS is not bundled into any plan: pay-as-you-go from $0.009 per message, sold through sales. Reputation Defender is an add-on at 20% of plan cost. The 30-day trial is full-feature but takes a card and auto-charges when it ends.

The honest knock

Automation workflows and conversion tracking only arrive on Advanced (from $35 a month at 1,000 contacts) — Essentials is a sending tool with an API, not a marketing-automation platform — and the trial auto-converts to paid if you do not actively cancel, which tells you something about the retention strategy. Contact-based pricing also means dormant subscribers bill like active ones; prune first.

What Campaigner does

Email and SMS marketing for mid-market senders with larger lists. The useful features start high — automation and API only arrive on Essential at about $179/month, while Starter at $59 is a sending tool rather than an automation platform. Contact-based pricing bills dormant subscribers too.

What buyers report about Campaigner

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Campaigner ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Segmentation and automation are deeper than the price suggests, and reviewers running complex campaigns rate the workflow builder.
  • • $14/month at 1,000 contacts on Essentials is competitive on paper.
What they complain about
  • • Cancellation requires a phone call plus 30 days' notice. Reviewers describe still paying for a month after the service stopped working for them.
  • • The 30-day trial takes a card and auto-converts. Reviewers report being charged for over a year on a trial they never used.
  • • SMS works only for US and Canadian numbers as of early 2026, and this is not stated prominently at signup. For a European team that is a dealbreaker discovered after paying.
  • • No free version, and below 500 contacts reviewers say it is simply the wrong tool.

When the paid plan is worth it

There is no free tier — only a 30-day trial that takes a card. The real decision is Essentials versus Advanced, and pricing scales by contact count, not per user: $14 versus $35 a month at 1,000 contacts, $158 versus $258 at 25,000, $409 versus $649 at 100,000. The limit that bites first is automation. Essentials has no workflows at all — no time-based, rules-based or conditional triggers, no delay nodes — no conversion tracking, and no way to remove the Campaigner footer branding.

Stay on Essentials if you broadcast a scheduled newsletter to one list: you still get the drag-and-drop editor, A/B and multivariate testing, dynamic segments, the REST API, webhooks and SFTP. Advanced pays for itself the moment you want one triggered sequence, or want to attribute revenue to a send. Upgrading does not fix the ceiling above it. Landing pages, sub-accounts, SSO and white-label stay quote-only on the Custom tier. The media library is capped at 50MB on every plan, Reputation Defender adds 20% of plan cost at any tier, and SMS stays pay-as-you-go behind a sales conversation.

How to actually use Campaigner

Start the 30-day trial — full-feature, but it takes a card and charges automatically when the trial ends unless you cancel first. Fix the sending identity before anything else. Campaigner's knowledge base carries separate articles for validating a business From domain instead of a Gmail or Yahoo address, and for SPF, DKIM and DMARC records. Its deliverability page describes authentication as something its team walks through with you. So treat DNS as a task with lead time, not a checkbox. Then run the import wizard, build in the drag-and-drop editor, and schedule.

Common Campaigner setup mistakes

Where Campaigner setups actually go wrong, and what it costs you when they do. Same sources as the setup notes above — not from a walkthrough we ran ourselves.

The trap is the first big send.

  • The anti-spam policy states that campaigns are screened automatically and held until a postmaster reviews and clears them.
  • The flags it lists include campaign size, the billing plan chosen, and how little time passed between account creation and scheduling — the exact profile of a new account.
  • Campaigner's own remedy is to let support help with the first large list upload and to test-send to a few addresses first.
  • Purchased or rented lists are refused outright.
