What Capsule does

Deliberately simple CRM for small businesses: contacts, pipeline and tasks. Free for 250 contacts and two users, then about $21/user/month on Starter. The simplicity is the product — advanced automation, deep reporting and integrations thin out, and a scaling sales org can outgrow it.

Pricing (2026)

Correction after re-checking: (2026) Free tier (250 contacts, 2 users); billed monthly: Starter $21/user/mo, Growth $38, Advanced $60, Ultimate $75; billed annually: Starter $18, Growth $36, Advanced $54, Ultimate $72. 14-day trial, no card. (re-verified 2026-07-31)

The honest knock

Capsule's simplicity is the product: it deliberately does less than the CRM giants, which is why small teams actually use it. The honest flip side — advanced automation, deep reporting and integrations thin out at the top tiers, and a scaling sales org can outgrow it. Buy it to finally have a CRM the team fills in; plan a migration path if you're heading toward enterprise process complexity.

What buyers report about Capsule

Recurring themes from G2 and Capterra, read in August 2026. We have not run Capsule ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Ease of use is the most repeated compliment across roughly 886 verified reviews, at 4.5 to 4.7. Onboarding is fast and the interface stays out of the way.
  • • The drag-and-drop pipeline is used by non-technical staff without training, and the pricing is fair below about 15 users. Free for 2 users, paid from $18.
What they complain about
  • • There is no phone support and no live chat on any tier. If that matters to you, it matters before you buy, not after.
  • • Support response times draw complaints on the lower tiers.
  • • Reporting handles the basics but frustrates anyone wanting custom dashboards.
  • • Advanced customisation is limited. Ultimate runs to about $72 per user.

When the paid plan is worth it

The free plan is a working two-person CRM rather than a demo: 250 contacts, 1 sales pipeline and 5 custom fields. It stops being viable once the team passes the 2-user maximum or the list outgrows 250 records; the cheapest paid tier is Starter, US$21 per user per month billed monthly, US$18 billed annually. The more consequential line is Starter to Growth (US$38 monthly, US$36 annual). Workflow automations, multiple sales pipelines, project boards, team and access controls, reporting dashboards and the AI enrichment and summary tools all sit at Growth and above. Starter itself adds 30,000 contacts, email templates, a shared mailbox, basic reporting, premium integrations, goals, custom activity types and the AI Pipeline Generator. Advanced (US$60 monthly, US$54 annual) mostly raises ceilings, 120,000 contacts, 50 sales pipelines and 50 project boards, so it repays only organisations genuinely running parallel processes. Ultimate (US$75 monthly, US$72 annual) is presented as being for larger teams with 5+ users and adds 240,000 contacts, a dedicated account manager, custom training and ongoing priority support. What no tier changes: every seat costs the same regardless of how lightly it is used, and marketing automation sits outside the product as a separate paid add-on.

How to actually use Capsule

Capsule starts empty, and the import screen is where the shaping happens. Import accepts CSV or Excel; a row carrying a first or last name becomes a Person, while a row with only the Organisation column filled builds the company record. Custom fields need not exist beforehand, since an admin can create most of them at the column-matching stage, within a 250-character limit for text fields. A DataTag does have to be set up under Account Settings > Tags and DataTags first. Duplicate rows can be imported as new, ignored, or used to update existing contacts, but only brand new contacts can be undone afterwards, so a bad update pass is permanent. Notes are one-way too: they import onto new contacts only, and Excel truncates fields at 32,000 characters, ruling it out for long note histories. Email is the other setup step: the Mail Drop Box is an address unique to the account, and forwarding mail to it or BCC-ing it attaches the message to the contacts referenced, up to a 25MB ceiling.
