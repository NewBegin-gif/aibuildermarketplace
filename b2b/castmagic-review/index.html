What Castmagic does

Turns recorded audio and video into a stack of ready-to-use assets: transcripts in 60+ languages, show notes, summaries, newsletters, social posts, clips and audiograms, with custom prompts and an AI chat across your whole library. Billed annually at $19, $48 or $139 a month for 30, 100 or 400 transcribed hours; Business & Scale starts at $699 a month by demo.

Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 19 Aug 2026

Three published tiers plus an enterprise band, and the page opens on annual billing with a 'Save 30%' label, so every headline number is the annual rate. Hobby: $19 a month billed annually, $239 a year, 30 transcribed hours, up to 3 Spaces, 50GB storage, 3 seats, no API access, no onboarding call. Starter: $48 a month billed annually, $579 a year, 100 transcribed hours, 10 Spaces, 250GB, 5 seats, API access, onboarding call.

Team: $139 a month billed annually, $1,669 a year, 400 transcribed hours, 25 Spaces, 1TB, unlimited seats with 5 included and extras at $159 a year or $19 a month each, priority support. Business & Scale: from $699 a month and $999 a month, by demo, for custom volume, seats, storage, SLA and dedicated support. Unlimited AI outputs, re-generations, media clipping and audiograms are on all three published tiers, as are 60+ transcription languages, a 10GB maximum file size and the Zoom, Google Drive, Zapier, RSS and YouTube/TikTok/Instagram imports.

The monthly-billing prices are behind a toggle that the page does not put in its source, so the only thing we can state about them is what the page states: annual saves 30%. And the hours need care. The table calls them 'transcribed hours (library total)' and the FAQ 'a growing library of transcribed hours that you build up over time', which never actually says whether the allowance renews each month or is a running ceiling. Extra hours can be bought at any time on every tier. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The toggle sits on 'Annually — Save 30%' by default, so $19, $48 and $139 are what you pay when you commit for a year; monthly billing costs more and the page does not show how much until you flip it. That is a normal enough pattern, but it means the number you remember from the pricing page is not the number you pay for a month-to-month subscription. The hours are the part to pin down before you subscribe.

The comparison table labels them 'transcribed hours (library total)', and the FAQ describes 'a growing library of transcribed hours that you build up over time'. That wording fits a monthly allowance and a lifetime ceiling equally well, and the difference between the two is enormous. 30 hours every month at $19 is a bargain for a weekly podcast. 30 hours in total is about seven episodes, and then you are buying top-ups.

Ask before you pay, and ask what happens to the hours when you downgrade. And the social proof does not agree with itself. The homepage hero says 'Loved by 100K+ podcasters and creators'; the pricing page on the same site says '75k+ Creators Served' and '75,000+ Creators & Businesses'. Both cannot be current. It is a small thing, and the kind of small thing that tells you which numbers on a marketing site have been checked recently.

Treat the 100K, the 10M minutes and the named logos as marketing, and judge the product on the trial.

What buyers report about Castmagic

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Castmagic ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • On Capterra the recurring claim is hours back rather than a better transcript. A marketing director (August 2023) says it saved hundreds of hours; a business systems coach (September 2023) uses it on coaching sessions as well as podcasts and cites custom prompts; a CEO in advertising (August 2023) puts the automated output at '95% of the way to perfect show notes'.
  • • The derived assets, not the transcription, are what people describe buying: Twitter threads, show notes, pull quotes and YouTube timestamps generated per upload (September and August 2023), and Magic Chat for querying a recording afterwards.
  • • Several reviewers single out how reachable the team was in 2023 — an accessible executive team and a Slack community (August 2023), and 'responsive leadership' (September 2023).
What they complain about
  • • The Capterra objections are all integration-shaped and small: no API into Buzzsprout (August 2023), limited export options and no WordPress or social publishing (January 2024), a weak mobile experience (September 2023), transcription that is occasionally imperfect (September 2023), and one reviewer's worry that the feature list would outgrow the simplicity (September 2023).
  • • The single Trustpilot review is the only recent one and it is the substantive one. A US producer (July 2026, two stars) says Castmagic was an early mover that has not kept pace with LLM competitors, and raises two concrete process complaints: that in their experience the subscription converted before the advertised seven-day trial had run out and a refund after the charge was refused, and that there is no way to recover credits when an episode uploads twice or lands missing a few minutes. On the first point, castmagic.io/pricing does not publish a trial length at all (checked 24 August 2026), so the seven days is that reviewer's account of what they were offered, not something the page states.

Where the ratings disagree. The two platforms are almost three years apart, and that gap is the finding. All nine Capterra reviews are five stars, all were written between August 2023 and January 2024, and most come from people under six months in — the shape of a launch-window review drive rather than a running record. Trustpilot has exactly one review, on an unclaimed profile that Trustpilot flags as having no history of asking for reviews, and it is the detailed two-star from July 2026. So the product has a glowing public record from the year it launched and virtually nothing from the market it is being sold into now, where the competition is a general-purpose model. Read the 2023 reviews as evidence the workflow works, not as evidence of what it is worth today.

When the paid plan is worth it

The three tiers move four things at once — hours, Spaces, storage, seats — but only one of them will decide it for you, and it is the hours. 30, 100, 400. Everything AI-shaped is unlimited on all three: outputs, regenerations, custom prompts, clips, audiograms, image generation. You are not buying better AI at $48 or $139, you are buying more raw material to point it at. So do the arithmetic on your own recordings.

A weekly hour-long podcast is roughly 4 hours a month and sits comfortably on Hobby. A team putting through two or three long recordings a week — webinars, sales calls, interviews — is at 20 to 40 hours a month and Starter's 100 is the honest floor. Four hundred hours is an agency or a network, and by then the seat count matters as much as the hours. Two lines are worth checking against your actual need rather than the tier.

API access does not exist on Hobby, so if the plan is to wire this into an existing pipeline, the entry price is $48 rather than $19. And Team's seats are five included with extras at $159 a year — cheap next to $1,669, but it means a ten-person team is not $139 a month, it is $139 plus roughly $66 a month in seats. Price the seats you need before comparing tiers.

How to actually use Castmagic

Settle the hours question first, in writing, with support: whether the 30, 100 or 400 transcribed hours renew each month or accumulate as a lifetime library total, and what happens to them if you downgrade. Everything else about sizing this tool depends on that one answer and the site words it both ways. Then run your most awkward recording through it rather than your cleanest — several speakers, crosstalk, jargon, an accent — because transcription accuracy is the foundation every generated asset sits on, and a summary built on a poor transcript is confidently wrong.

Set up your custom prompts early; the stock outputs are competent and generic, and the difference between this being a novelty and a workflow is a handful of prompts that produce your show notes in your format. If you intend to wire it into anything, note that API access starts at Starter, not Hobby. And if you are buying for a team, price the seats before the tier: Team includes five and charges $159 a year for each one after that.
