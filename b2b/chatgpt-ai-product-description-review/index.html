What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

'ChatGPT AI Product Description' described a category (the listing has since been renamed AI Product Description-ChatGPT), the app's own listing copy now calls itself Descrii AI Product Descriptions, and Shopify shows a different app by a different developer under a nearly identical name on the same page. Second, the credit is undefined and it is the only thing you are buying. Four tiers, one variable, no definition, and the '$1 per 100 descriptions' line that the three paid plans repeated on 20 September 2026 was not the rate on two of them (by 17 November 2026 the listing had replaced it with add on credits at 100 credits per $1).

Worse, the plan price may not be the whole bill: the listing carries Shopify's usage-based-charges line, and a merchant of twelve months, in a review edited 30 July 2026, itemised charges of $586.79, $487.49, $282.69 and $483.16 on top of a stated $149 a year plus $49 a month, marked 'billing cycle ended' and 'fee threshold reached'. That is one public account and the developer has not answered it on the listing, but nothing on the page lets you rule it out either.

Third, and the one that costs real money: this app edits your live store. Its permissions cover product descriptions and titles, Online Store pages and theme, files, locales, Shopify Markets settings, translations and navigation, and nothing on the listing describes versioning, a preview queue or a rollback. On 15 September 2026 a merchant of five months reported that it changed titles to products the store does not carry; the developer replied the next day that he had paused automatic generation for that store and was adding safeguards.

The 525 ratings read on 17 November 2026 are strong, 499 five stars, but three are one star and they are the ones that describe undoing. Export your product CSV before the first bulk run. That is your rollback.

  • • Bulk is what buyers actually credit. What they complain about
  • • Consistency across similar products is the recurring failure. A US laser-art store (edited November 2025) says that with multiple near-identical items the titles and descriptions drift out of line with the prompt in a way ChatGPT itself does not; they rate it improved since their first review and still not reliable enough for their work.
  • • A Dutch merchant (May 2026, seven days in) reports the same inconsistency and adds that it was hard to reach an actual person; the developer replied publicly the next day and offered to look at the descriptions.
  • • It rewrites the title but not the URL handle: a UK merchant (July 2025) found products kept the messy keyword slug after the title had been cleaned up. The developer later said this was fixed: we have not verified that.
  • • One Australian merchant (October 2024) puts it in four words: good, but expensive for high-volume stores.

Where the ratings disagree. 466 reviews averaging 4.9, with 447 at the top rating and not a single one-star, but read who is writing. A large share of the five-star reviews come from merchants minutes or hours after installing, so they are rating the setup and the first output; the mixed reviews are from the people with months or a year on the app. There is also a naming problem worth checking before you read any of it: the App Store lists a near-identically named app from a different developer with a similar score on the same page, so confirm the listing you are reading is the Profitonium one.

When the paid plan is worth it

The credit maths is where this ladder is unusually generous in the middle and unusually punishing at the top. Basic gives you 2,000 credits for $19. Standard gives you 11,000 for $49: five and a half times the credits for two and a half times the money, the single best step on the page. Pro gives you 110,000 for $249, ten times Standard's credits for five times its price, which is again good value per credit but only if you have tens of thousands of SKUs.

Run it against your own catalogue rather than the tier names. A store with 800 products that regenerates descriptions, titles and meta once has a one-off job in the low thousands of credits and then a trickle for new products: that is Basic, possibly Free for the trickle. A store with 30,000 SKUs doing a full pass is a Standard-to-Pro job for a month or two and can drop back down afterwards, which the 30-day billing cycle allows.

The honest caveat: you cannot do that maths precisely, because the listing never defines the credit. The headline on each plan, '1000+ descriptions' on 2,000 credits, '10,000+' on 11,000, '100,000+' on 110,000: implies roughly one credit per description with headroom. But a description with image analysis and web search is plainly not the same work as a meta title, and the page prices them identically at zero information. Generate a hundred items of the mix you actually need on the free tier and read the credit counter before you pick a plan.

One measured figure from our own test of 17 November 2026: a single product description cost 2 credits, shown before generating, so Basic's 2,000 credits is about 1,000 descriptions, as the app says. We did not price titles, meta descriptions or alt text.

    How to actually use ChatGPT AI Product Description

    Export your products to CSV before the first bulk run: there is no rollback described anywhere on the listing, and a bad pass over 5,000 SKUs is otherwise permanent. Then use the 100 free monthly credits as a measuring device rather than a trial.     Can the bill be more than the plan price?

    The listing says it can, and one merchant has published what that looked like.     How risky is 'automatic descriptions on import'?

    It is the setting most likely to cost you an afternoon, and there is now a dated example. On 15 September 2026 a merchant who had used the app for five months reported that it changed product titles and replaced them with products the store does not carry, leaving a catalogue to repair. The developer replied the next day: he had identified the issue, paused automatic generation for that store, and was adding safeguards. Fast and honest, but the repair was still the merchant's. A second reviewer, in a review edited 30 July 2026, described bulk-publishing without reviewing closely enough and finding descriptions with invented or misleading claims, then spending hours building a restrictive prompt to stop it. Nothing on the listing describes versioning, a preview queue or a rollback. So: export your product CSV first, keep automatic generation off until your custom instructions have survived a sample of twenty, and switch it on last.

    Is the dissent in the reviews worth reading?

    It is, because the complaints are consistent and narrow. The distribution on 20 September 2026 is 504 five stars, 16 four, five three, one two and three one. The developer answers almost every one personally, usually within a day, and his replies point at the same three settings each time: custom instructions, brand voice, and your own fine-tuned model.
