When the paid plan is worth it

The credit maths is where this ladder is unusually generous in the middle and unusually punishing at the top. Basic gives you 2,000 credits for $19. Standard gives you 11,000 for $49 — five and a half times the credits for two and a half times the money, the single best step on the page. Pro gives you 110,000 for $249, ten times Standard's credits for five times its price, which is again good value per credit but only if you genuinely have tens of thousands of SKUs.

Run it against your own catalogue rather than the tier names. A store with 800 products that regenerates descriptions, titles and meta once has a one-off job in the low thousands of credits and then a trickle for new products — that is Basic, possibly Free for the trickle. A store with 30,000 SKUs doing a full pass is a Standard-to-Pro job for a month or two and can drop back down afterwards, which the 30-day billing cycle allows.

The honest caveat: you cannot do that maths precisely, because the listing never defines the credit. The headline on each plan — '1000+ descriptions' on 2,000 credits, '10,000+' on 11,000, '100,000+' on 110,000 — implies roughly one credit per description with headroom. But a description with image analysis and web search is plainly not the same work as a meta title, and the page prices them identically at zero information.

Generate a hundred items of the mix you actually need on the free tier and read the credit counter before you pick a plan.

How to actually use ChatGPT AI Product Description

Export your products to CSV before the first bulk run — there is no rollback described anywhere on the listing, and a bad pass over 5,000 SKUs is otherwise permanent. Then use the 100 free monthly credits as a measuring device rather than a trial. Generate the exact mix you plan to buy — say twenty descriptions, twenty titles, twenty meta descriptions, twenty alt texts — and watch what each one costs in credits.

That ratio is the only way to size a plan, and the page does not publish it. Write your custom instructions once and properly; consistent formatting across a catalogue is where this class of tool earns its money and the default house style is generic. Turn 'automatic descriptions on import' off until you trust the output, otherwise new products go live with unreviewed copy. And if you are running the multilingual side, have someone who speaks the language read ten of them — the one lukewarm review on the page is a German merchant saying the translations are occasionally stiff.

Frequently asked questions

Can the bill be more than the plan price?

The listing says it can, and one merchant has published what that looked like. Under the four plan cards the App Store prints the standard line that recurring and usage-based charges are billed every 30 days, and nothing on the page defines the usage rate or the threshold that triggers one. In a review edited 30 July 2026, a merchant of twelve months' standing itemised charges on top of a stated $149 a year plus $49 a month: $44.97 on 22 May, $586.79 on 1 June marked 'billing cycle ended', $487.49 on 25 June marked 'fee threshold reached', $282.69 on 1 July, $483.16 on 20 July, and four one-off charges between $9.90 and $10. That is a single public account, the developer has not answered it on the listing, and none of the four plan cards mentions a usage line — so treat it as a reason to check rather than as a published rate. Before any bulk run, open your Shopify billing and look at the app's usage charges and approval thresholds, not only at the plan card. Read 20 September 2026.

How risky is 'automatic descriptions on import'?

It is the setting most likely to cost you an afternoon, and there is now a dated example. On 15 September 2026 a merchant who had used the app for five months reported that it changed product titles and replaced them with products the store does not carry, leaving a catalogue to repair. The developer replied the next day: he had identified the issue, paused automatic generation for that store, and was adding safeguards. Fast and honest, but the repair was still the merchant's. A second reviewer, in a review edited 30 July 2026, described bulk-publishing without reviewing closely enough and finding descriptions with invented or misleading claims, then spending hours building a restrictive prompt to stop it. Nothing on the listing describes versioning, a preview queue or a rollback. So: export your product CSV first, keep automatic generation off until your custom instructions have survived a sample of twenty, and switch it on last.

The rating is 4.9 from 529 reviews — is the dissent worth reading?

It is, because the complaints are consistent and narrow. The distribution on 20 September 2026 is 504 five stars, 16 four, five three, one two and three one. The low ratings say four things: the default output is generic and will not rank (December 2025, from a reviewer who had the app installed for four minutes); translations read stiffly in German (July 2026, from a merchant of over a year who is positive overall); bulk publishing without guardrails produced unsupported claims; and one merchant simply could not work out how to use it. The developer answers almost every one personally, usually within a day, and his replies point at the same three settings each time — custom instructions, brand voice, and your own fine-tuned model. Read that as the honest summary: the output is as good as the instructions you write, and the house default is not.
