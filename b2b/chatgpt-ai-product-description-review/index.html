Every paid tier carries the same strapline — 'Only $1 per 100 Descriptions' — and it only holds at the bottom of the ladder: $19 for 2,000 credits is 95 cents per hundred, $49 for 11,000 is 45 cents, $249 for 110,000 is 23 cents. When the paid plan is worth it

The credit maths is where this ladder is unusually generous in the middle and unusually punishing at the top. Basic gives you 2,000 credits for $19. Standard gives you 11,000 for $49 — five and a half times the credits for two and a half times the money, the single best step on the page. Pro gives you 110,000 for $249, ten times Standard's credits for five times its price, which is again good value per credit but only if you genuinely have tens of thousands of SKUs.

Run it against your own catalogue rather than the tier names. A store with 800 products that regenerates descriptions, titles and meta once has a one-off job in the low thousands of credits and then a trickle for new products — that is Basic, possibly Free for the trickle. A store with 30,000 SKUs doing a full pass is a Standard-to-Pro job for a month or two and can drop back down afterwards, which the 30-day billing cycle allows.

The honest caveat: you cannot do that maths precisely, because the listing never defines the credit. The headline on each plan — '1000+ descriptions' on 2,000 credits, '10,000+' on 11,000, '100,000+' on 110,000 — implies roughly one credit per description with headroom. But a description with image analysis and web search is plainly not the same work as a meta title, and the page prices them identically at zero information.

Generate a hundred items of the mix you actually need on the free tier and read the credit counter before you pick a plan.

How to actually use ChatGPT AI Product Description

Export your products to CSV before the first bulk run — there is no rollback described anywhere on the listing, and a bad pass over 5,000 SKUs is otherwise permanent. Then use the 100 free monthly credits as a measuring device rather than a trial. Generate the exact mix you plan to buy — say twenty descriptions, twenty titles, twenty meta descriptions, twenty alt texts — and watch what each one costs in credits.

That ratio is the only way to size a plan, and the page does not publish it. Write your custom instructions once and properly; consistent formatting across a catalogue is where this class of tool earns its money and the default house style is generic. Turn 'automatic descriptions on import' off until you trust the output, otherwise new products go live with unreviewed copy. And if you are running the multilingual side, have someone who speaks the language read ten of them — the one lukewarm review on the page is a German merchant saying the translations are occasionally stiff.
