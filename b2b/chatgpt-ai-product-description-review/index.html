What ChatGPT AI Product Description does

Bulk copy generator for Shopify from Profitonium: product descriptions, titles, meta tags and image alt text for thousands of products at once, in 30+ languages, with custom instruction templates and automatic descriptions on import. Free tier gives 100 credits a month; paid runs $19, $49 or $249 a month for 2,000, 11,000 or 110,000 credits. Live since 2021, rated 4.9 over 466 App Store reviews.

Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 19 Aug 2026

(read 2026-08-19 on apps.shopify.com/automated-description-writing) Four tiers, priced in credits per month. Free Trial: 100 credits a month. Basic $19 a month or $190 a year (save 17%): 2,000 credits, product descriptions, titles and meta descriptions, formatted descriptions, automatic descriptions on import. Standard $49 a month or $399 a year (save 32%): 11,000 credits, same feature list. Pro $249 a month or $1,990 a year (save 33%): 110,000 credits, same list plus priority video-call support.

All charges are billed in USD and recur every 30 days. Every paid tier carries the same strapline — 'Only $1 per 100 Descriptions' — and it only holds at the bottom of the ladder: $19 for 2,000 credits is 95 cents per hundred, $49 for 11,000 is 45 cents, $249 for 110,000 is 23 cents. So the $1 is a ceiling on the cheapest plan rather than a rate, and it assumes one credit equals one description, which the listing never states.

That is the central gap in this pricing: nowhere does the page say what a title, a meta description or an alt text costs in credits, or what the 'Pro Features' that use product images, metadata and web search cost extra. Developer Profitonium, Adugodi, Bangalore; the app launched 12 March 2021, holds Shopify's 'Built for Shopify' badge and carries a 4.9 average over 466 App Store reviews. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

The honest knock

First, the name is not a name. 'ChatGPT AI Product Description' describes a category, and on its own listing page Shopify shows 'ChatGPT‑AI Product Description' — a different app by a different developer, also 4.9, also 331 reviews, also with the Built for Shopify badge. Two more competitors on the same page use nearly the same words. Check the developer is Profitonium before you install, because the App Store search results for this phrase are a thicket. Second, the credit is undefined and it is the only thing you are buying. Four tiers, one variable, no definition — and the '$1 per 100 descriptions' line repeated on all three paid plans is not the rate on two of them. Third, and the one that costs real money if it goes wrong: this app edits your live store. Its permissions cover product descriptions and titles, Online Store pages and theme, files, locales, Shopify Markets settings, translations and navigation. Bulk generation across thousands of SKUs is exactly the operation you cannot eyeball, and nothing on the listing describes versioning, a preview queue or a rollback. The 466 reviews are genuinely strong — 96% five stars, one two-star, no one-stars, and the substantial ones come from merchants with 30,000 SKUs and over a year of use — but every one of them describes a merchant who was happy with the output, not one who needed to undo it. Export your product CSV before the first bulk run. That is your rollback.

When the paid plan is worth it

The credit maths is where this ladder is unusually generous in the middle and unusually punishing at the top. Basic gives you 2,000 credits for $19. Standard gives you 11,000 for $49 — five and a half times the credits for two and a half times the money, the single best step on the page. Pro gives you 110,000 for $249, ten times Standard's credits for five times its price, which is again good value per credit but only if you genuinely have tens of thousands of SKUs. Run it against your own catalogue rather than the tier names. A store with 800 products that regenerates descriptions, titles and meta once has a one-off job in the low thousands of credits and then a trickle for new products — that is Basic, possibly Free for the trickle. A store with 30,000 SKUs doing a full pass is a Standard-to-Pro job for a month or two and can drop back down afterwards, which the 30-day billing cycle allows. The honest caveat: you cannot do that maths precisely, because the listing never defines the credit. The headline on each plan — '1000+ descriptions' on 2,000 credits, '10,000+' on 11,000, '100,000+' on 110,000 — implies roughly one credit per description with headroom, but a description with image analysis and web search is plainly not the same work as a meta title, and the page prices them identically at zero information. Generate a hundred items of the mix you actually need on the free tier and read the credit counter before you pick a plan.

How to actually use ChatGPT AI Product Description

Export your products to CSV before the first bulk run — there is no rollback described anywhere on the listing, and a bad pass over 5,000 SKUs is otherwise permanent. Then use the 100 free monthly credits as a measuring device rather than a trial: generate the exact mix you plan to buy — say twenty descriptions, twenty titles, twenty meta descriptions, twenty alt texts — and watch what each one costs in credits, because that ratio is the only way to size a plan and the page does not publish it. Write your custom instructions once and properly; consistent formatting across a catalogue is where this class of tool earns its money and the default house style is generic. Turn 'automatic descriptions on import' off until you trust the output, otherwise new products go live with unreviewed copy. And if you are running the multilingual side, have someone who speaks the language read ten of them — the one lukewarm review on the page is a German merchant saying the translations are occasionally stiff.
