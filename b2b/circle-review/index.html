What Circle does

Community platform for creator businesses: discussions, courses, events, memberships and messaging in one branded home — plus a no-code website builder, branded iOS/Android apps, email marketing, a member CRM, built-in payments and AI agents that answer member questions.

Pricing (2026)

(2026) Professional about $89/mo billed annually ($129 monthly), Business $199/mo annual, Enterprise around $419/mo annual, plus a custom Circle Plus tier for fully branded apps. On top of the subscription Circle takes a commission on every sale you make through the platform (roughly 2% on Professional, 1% on Business, 0.5% on Plus) plus standard Stripe/PayPal processing, and the Email Hub is a paid add-on around $99/mo for 10,000 contacts. 14-day free trial, but no always-free option. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

The honest knock

The sticker is only the entry: Circle keeps a commission on every sale on top of Stripe's processing fees, and email marketing is a separate add-on — so a monetized community's real bill runs meaningfully past the plan price. Model it at your actual sales volume before choosing a tier.

Best for: creators and brands running a paid community — courses, memberships, events and discussions in one branded home, with a built-in payments stack and even branded mobile apps.
Who should skip it: if you only need a casual chat space (Discord-style) or your margins are thin on high sales volume — the platform commission plus processing fees stack against you.

The natural comparison is Skool or Mighty Networks — Skool wins on radical simplicity and flat pricing, Mighty Networks on affordable entry; Circle wins on breadth: branded apps, email, CRM, AI agents and a real payments stack. Decide by which one fits the job above, not by the louder brand.

Frequently asked questions

Is Circle worth it for a small team?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Small teams get the most out of this category when one clear problem is already costing real hours or revenue; buying ahead of that just adds cost and another login. Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.

Who should not use Circle?

Skip it if you only need a casual chat space (Discord-style) or your margins are thin on high sales volume — the platform commission plus processing fees stack against you. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
