Pricing (2026)

The plans

    10 videos per user, 300 minutes total (not per month), calendar and conf-call sync, 99-language transcript, basic AI summary, Chrome recorder, collaborative workspace; 5 licenses, 0 guests, 3 months storage
  • Pro — €24/license/month yearly, €30 monthly
    unlimited recordings, 1,000 minutes a month, 500 AI credits per license, advanced AI summaries, meeting insights, AI video editing, 10+ integrations, private channels; 50 licenses, 50 guests, 2 years storage
  • Business — €48/license/month yearly, €60 monthly — Contact us
    unlimited recording time, 1,000 AI credits per license, Claap AI across meetings, CRM auto-complete, AI-generated emails, AI coaching, deal insights, smart tables, priority support; 3 years storage
  • Enterprise — custom — Contact us
    everything in Business plus SSO (Okta), SCIM provisioning, unlimited Basic licenses, white-glove onboarding and migration support; unlimited storage

Licenses are priced per license per month in euros. The only dollar figure on the page belongs to an add-on rather than a plan: a tooltip offers the Power Recorder, which adds unlimited recording length to any paid seat for $20 per seat a month. Basic is €0: 10 videos per user, 300 minutes of recording in total rather than per month, calendar and conference-call sync, 99-language transcript, basic AI summary, the Chrome recorder extension, a collaborative workspace and — listed on every tier including this free one — an unlimited-usage API and the Claap MCP connector, capped at 5 licenses, no guests, one team, one channel, 3 video uploads and 3 months of recording storage.

Pro is €24 per license a month billed yearly or €30 monthly: unlimited recordings, 1,000 minutes a month, 500 AI credits per license pooled across the workspace, advanced AI summaries, meeting insights, AI video editing, 10+ integrations, speaker and video insights and private channels, with 50 licenses, 50 guests, 10 uploads a month and 2 years of storage. What those credits buy is spelled out in a tooltip rather than in the plan card: one credit is one Claap AI chat, AI email follow-up, coaching tab or generated insight, and summaries, chapters and the AI companion do not consume credits at all.

Business is €48 per license a month yearly or €60 monthly: unlimited recording time, 1,000 AI credits per license, Claap AI across all meetings, CRM auto-complete, AI-generated emails, AI coaching, deal insights, smart tables, admin recording controls and priority support, with 50 uploads a month and 3 years of storage. Enterprise is a custom price and adds SSO through Okta, SCIM provisioning, unlimited Basic licenses, white-glove onboarding and migration support.

Basic carries a Try for free button and Pro a Start now button; Business and Enterprise both say Contact us, and the pricing FAQ states that a free trial of a paid plan has to be unlocked by their sales team. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Pro at €24 is the recorder and note-taker. Everything Claap actually markets itself on — CRM auto-complete, AI coaching, deal insights, asking Claap AI a question across all your meetings — starts at Business, €48 per license a month on annual billing, and Business is the only paid tier with no Start now button: it says Contact us. The pricing FAQ closes the other door too, stating that a free trial of a paid plan has to be unlocked by their sales team.

So the features you came for cannot be bought or tested without a sales conversation. Your recordings also expire. Storage runs 3 months on Basic, 2 years on Pro and 3 years on Business; only Enterprise is unlimited. If the call archive is meant to be your evidence trail, that is a deletion date on your own data, and it is in the comparison table rather than in the plan cards.

Two smaller things worth reading twice, and one correction we owe you. The AI credits — 500 per license on Pro, 1,000 on Business, pooled across the workspace — do not meter the summaries: the tooltip is explicit that summaries, chapters and the AI companion use no credits, and a credit is spent on a Claap AI chat, an AI email follow-up, the coaching tab or a generated insight. What is missing is the rate: no price for an extra credit appears anywhere on the page, so a heavy month of coaching and insights cannot be costed before you commit, and the same tooltip layer is where you find the one dollar-priced item, a $20 per seat Power Recorder add-on on a euro-priced plan.

And the free tier's 300 minutes is a total, not a monthly allowance: it is a sample, not a plan you can sit on.

What Claap does

AI sales intelligence that records calls without putting a bot in the meeting, then turns them into CRM fields, follow-up emails, deal scoring and coaching. Basic is free; Pro is €24 per license a month on annual billing (€30 monthly); Business, which holds the CRM and coaching features, is €48 (€60 monthly) and quote-led.

What buyers report about Claap

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in September 2026. We have not run Claap ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Time is the benefit buyers name most, and the platform's own tag counts make that countable rather than vague: time-saving is tagged on 12 of the 28 reviews, asynchronous collaboration on 10, screen recording on 7 and remote-team collaboration on 7. A manager at Luko describes rolling it out across finance, people, risk and compliance teams and says adoption was easy; someone at Libeo, around 100 people, reports that “Claap it” became a daily verb internally. One team estimates its meetings dropped by roughly 30% over a year of use.
  • • The praise is specific about plumbing rather than mood: searchable built-in transcripts, videos embedded straight into Notion pages, Google SSO, a Zapier connection, a comments panel beside the video and a playback-speed control. Several reviewers credit the onboarding guides for making the rollout stick, and engineering teams say the video plus transcript gave them enough context to reproduce bugs without a meeting.
What they complain about
  • • One account is far more serious than the rest, and it is the most recent review on the page — posted about a year before we read it. The reviewer states they never signed up, yet the recording function activated by itself during their meetings; after contacting support three times and being assured everything had been deleted, it started again during a business meeting, and the system emailed every participant without consent. They frame it as a privacy failure and a possible GDPR breach. It is a single unconfirmed voice, but it is the only recent one, so weigh it against the bot-free capture the vendor advertises.
  • • The standing requests are threaded video replies — one reviewer names “answer with a video” as the feature that would let it beat Loom — a way for anonymous viewers to leave a name instead of anonymous view notifications, and meeting recording that is less limited than what they had.

Where the ratings disagree. The split here is not between platforms but between years. Almost every review on the page was written three to four years ago about Claap 2.0, an async video and meeting-notes tool, and the tags they praise describe that product. Claap today sells an AI Sales Assistant that applies MEDDIC, SPICED and BANT to deals. Read the older reviews as evidence about the recording, transcript and adoption layer — which is still underneath — and not as a verdict on the sales AI built on top of it, which nobody on that page has reviewed.

Pro records the call; Business is the part that sells

The step from €24 to €48 is not a bigger version of the same plan, it is the other half of the product. Pro gives you unlimited recordings, 1,000 minutes a month and better summaries: a good, cheap meeting recorder. Business is where Claap becomes the thing its homepage describes — CRM auto-complete that writes back to HubSpot, Salesforce, Attio or Pipedrive, AI-generated follow-up emails, AI coaching, deal insights, and Claap AI asking questions across every meeting instead of one.

If your reason for looking at Claap is any sentence containing the word CRM, Pro is not your tier and the €24 figure is not your price. That makes the decision unusually clean. Count the reps whose calls must reach the CRM without anyone typing, multiply by €48, and compare that against what your team currently loses to empty CRM fields. Everyone else on the team — managers who watch, colleagues who comment — can sit on free Basic licenses, up to 5 on the free workspace and 50 on the paid tiers, which is the part of this pricing model that genuinely saves money.

What the upgrade does not fix is the meter. AI credits are pooled per workspace at 500 or 1,000 per license, and the page never says what an extra credit costs. Ask for that number in writing during the sales call you are already required to have, along with what happens to recordings when they hit the 2 or 3-year storage limit.

How to actually use Claap

Start on Basic and spend the 300 minutes on the one thing that makes Claap different: capture that puts no visible participant in the call. Recording runs from the Chrome extension and the Mac app instead of from a bot that joins, and it covers in-person conversations as well as video meetings. Phone calls are a separate route entirely — they arrive by integration from Aircall, Ringover, Allo or lemlist VoIP rather than from the recorder, so test that path on its own if dialled calls are the reason you are here.

A customisable bot is available if your team would rather have one, so the no-bot default is a choice rather than a limitation. Whether it changes how your prospects behave is a judgement only your own calls can make, and it is free to find out. Watch the number, though — 300 minutes is the total for the account, not a monthly allowance, so it is roughly five hour-long calls before the free tier is spent.

While you are there, check the two things that decide whether the paid tier is worth a conversation. Confirm your stack is covered: Zoom, Google Meet and Teams for conferencing, HubSpot, Salesforce, Attio or Pipedrive for CRM, and Slack, Notion or Zapier for the rest. And read the AI summary Claap produces against your own notes from the same call, because the summary quality is what everything above it is built on — and because summaries are unmetered on every paid plan, that is a test you can run as often as you like.

Then go into the sales conversation with numbers rather than questions. Ask for a Business trial specifically — the FAQ says trials are unlocked by sales, so it is a request they expect — and ask three things in writing: the top-up price of AI credits, what happens to a recording when it reaches the storage limit, and whether the euro price is fixed or converted for non-euro customers.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Claap cost?

Per license per month, in euros: Basic €0, Pro €24 on annual billing or €30 monthly, Business €48 or €60, Enterprise on quote (read 4 September 2026, re-checked 8 September). Free Basic licenses for colleagues who only watch and comment are included on every tier, up to 5 on the free workspace and 50 on Pro and Business. One extra sits outside those euro prices: a Power Recorder add-on that lifts the recording-length limit on any paid seat, priced in dollars at $20 per seat a month.

Can I try or buy the CRM features without talking to sales?

No. CRM auto-complete, AI coaching and deal insights start on the Business tier, and Business has no self-serve checkout — the button says Contact us. The pricing FAQ also states that a free trial of a paid plan is unlocked by their sales team, so both the purchase and the test run through a conversation.

Does Claap put a bot in my meetings?

Not unless you want one. Claap's default is a no-bot recorder that captures Zoom, Google Meet, Teams and in-person conversations from the Chrome extension and the Mac app, without a visible participant joining the call. Phone calls do not run through that recorder — see the next question. A customisable bot is available for teams that prefer it, and the free tier is enough to test which your prospects react better to.

Can Claap record my phone calls?

Not itself, and this corrects an earlier version of this review. Claap's own documentation keeps phone calls in a category of their own: they reach Claap through an integration with one of four phone systems — Aircall, Ringover, Allo or lemlist VoIP — and what arrives is a recording your phone system already made, imported within a few minutes of the call ending and then transcribed, summarised and matched to a CRM contact by phone number. The recording has to happen upstream, and the Aircall guide is blunt about it: calls which are not recorded in Aircall will not be imported. Two details decide whether this fits your team. Imports carry a minimum call length, set to 2 minutes by default and adjustable to no minimum, 15 or 30 seconds, 1 minute or 5 minutes — leave the default alone and short calls simply never appear. And the Allo route needs a Business license specifically, because it runs on Claap's API and the article states API access is not available on Basic or Pro; Allo is one-way and will not backfill calls from before you connected it. If your reps dial from anything outside those four systems, phone calls are not part of what you are buying (help.claap.io, Calls & VOIP collection, read 8 September 2026).

Do AI credits pay for my meeting summaries?

No, and this is a correction to an earlier version of this review. Claap's pricing tooltip states that one credit is one Claap AI chat, AI email follow-up, coaching tab or generated insight, and that summaries, chapters and the AI companion do not use credits. So the 500 credits on Pro and 1,000 on Business meter the conversational and coaching layer, not the note-taking you bought the tool for — summaries are unmetered on every paid plan. What the page still does not publish is what an extra credit costs.

Can I get Claap's data into my own AI tools?

Yes, and it is not reserved for the top tier: the pricing page lists an unlimited-usage API and the Claap MCP connector among the key features of every plan, including the free Basic one. The MCP connector is the part worth knowing about, because it lets an assistant such as Claude query your call data directly instead of you exporting transcripts; Claap's homepage names Claude, ChatGPT and lemlist as the agents that consume it. Claap is part of lemlist.

How long does Claap keep my recordings?

Not forever, unless you are on Enterprise. The comparison table gives 3 months of recording storage on Basic, 2 years on Pro and 3 years on Business, with unlimited storage only on Enterprise. If your call archive is a compliance or evidence record, ask what happens at the limit before you commit.

What should I weigh before buying Sales & CRM software?

Most tools in this category plug in rather than replace. Check which direction the data flows and whether the sync is genuinely two-way, because a one-way push creates a second source of truth that your reps will quietly stop trusting. If it does aim to replace your CRM, price the migration honestly: history, custom fields and reporting rarely move across cleanly.
