What ClickUp does

The 'everything app' for project and work management. Centralize your tasks, docs, sprints, and team communication in one hub.

Pricing (2026)

A Free Forever plan (unlimited members, limited storage/features); Unlimited about $7/user/mo, Business $12/user/mo billed annually (higher monthly), plus custom Enterprise (2026). Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The everything-app breadth is also the downside — it's feature-dense with a real learning curve, and teams can drown in options. Powerful and cheap per seat, but only if you'll actually configure and adopt it.

When the paid plan is worth it

The Free Forever tier is not a trial — it carries unlimited tasks — but ClickUp caps it at 60MB of storage, and that is the ceiling that arrives first: the moment work involves attachments rather than checklists, the free plan runs out. Unlimited at $7 per user/month billed yearly restores storage, integrations and custom fields. Business at $12 per user/month billed yearly is the tier that matters once routing is supposed to run without a human, since ClickUp documents 5K automations per month there against 250K on Enterprise. The rough rule: pay for Unlimited when files and integrations enter the workflow, pay for Business when automations and dashboards carry real load, and stay free if a small group only tracks tasks. AI is priced separately on top — Brain AI at $9 per user/month, Everything AI at $28 — so an AI-first evaluation costs more than the plan table suggests. What upgrading does not fix at these tiers: API throughput. Free, Unlimited and Business all sit at 100 requests per minute per token; only Business Plus (1,000) and Enterprise (10,000) lift it, so integration-heavy setups stay throttled long after per-seat cost has stopped being the question.

How to actually use ClickUp

ClickUp does little until the Hierarchy exists. The vendor's onboarding guide puts Workspace structure first — Spaces, then Folders, then Lists, then tasks — and explicitly asks teams to agree what each level represents before anyone creates one. Roles come next: Guests, Members, Admins, Owner and custom roles are decided during setup, and permission mistakes surface afterwards as people seeing the wrong Spaces. Then import. ClickUp publishes importers for Asana, Monday.com, Jira, Confluence, Trello, Todoist, Basecamp, Wrike, Notion and CSV or Excel, and lets the result land in a brand new Space or be mapped onto an existing Folder. For CSV the import page promises only that any number of columns can be imported in any order and mapped into Lists; it documents no validation step, so mapping is checked by eye. The practical stall is not the account itself but the structural decision plus the cleanup once a migration has turned boards and Jira issue types into Lists that do not match how the team actually works. Distilled from ClickUp's own team onboarding guide (https://clickup.com/team-onboarding) for Hierarchy-first setup and the role model, and ClickUp's import page (https://clickup.com/import) for the supported migration sources and the new-Space / existing-Folder mapping choice. Plan-gated API throughput from ClickUp's developer docs (https://developer.clickup.com/docs/rate-limits). Help Center articles on the Hierarchy and Workspace setup were unreachable (403).

Frequently asked questions

Does ClickUp replace my current setup or sit on top of it?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.

Who should not use ClickUp?

Skip it if you want something simple your team adopts in a day — ClickUp's depth becomes overwhelm for a light use case. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
