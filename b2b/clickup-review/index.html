What buyers report about ClickUp

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026 (Capterra read 13 August 2026). We have not run ClickUp ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • It genuinely does replace several tools, which is why it rates around 4.7 across more than 10,000 G2 reviews.
  • • Once teams are through the setup, usability scores are high — 88% on G2.
  • • Developers on Capterra (4.6 across 4,616 reviews) single out the API rather than the boards: a plain API token with no OAuth dance, endpoints grouped the way the UI is, predictable payloads and clearly documented rate limits — one reviewer had task creation, updates and status changes working within an hour.
What they complain about
  • • The learning curve is the second most-cited complaint on G2, and "took three weeks to onboard the team" is a common phrasing. Budget for that.
  • • Slowness. "Slow loading" is tagged in over 1,100 G2 reviews, and reviewers still report lag in large workspaces after the 3.0 speed work.
  • • Feature overload — "too many features I don't use" recurs, and it makes the tool harder to teach.
  • • Docs and the mobile app trail the desktop experience.
  • • Capterra reviewers name the mobile app specifically as weaker than the desktop app, and one adds that getting the full feature set there means moving up a plan for what is largely the same thing.
  • • The lag complaints there are specific: stalls when tasks and subtasks are added in quick succession, and slow-loading dashboards in large workspaces with many subtasks.
  • • Automations are called shallow for anything complex — several Capterra reviewers say non-trivial triggers need workarounds instead of working natively.

When the paid plan is worth it

The Free Forever tier is not a trial — it carries unlimited tasks — but ClickUp caps it at 60MB of storage, and that is the ceiling that arrives first: the moment work involves attachments rather than checklists, the free plan runs out. Unlimited at $7 per user/month billed yearly restores storage, integrations and custom fields. Business at $12 per user/month billed yearly is the tier that matters once routing is supposed to run without a human, since ClickUp documents 5K automations per month there against 250K on Enterprise.

The rough rule: pay for Unlimited when files and integrations enter the workflow, pay for Business when automations and dashboards carry real load, and stay free if a small group only tracks tasks. AI is priced separately on top — Brain AI at $9 per user/month, Everything AI at $28 — so an AI-first evaluation costs more than the plan table suggests. What upgrading does not fix at these tiers: API throughput.

Free, Unlimited and Business all sit at 100 requests per minute per token; only Business Plus (1,000) and Enterprise (10,000) lift it, so integration-heavy setups stay throttled long after per-seat cost has stopped being the question.

How to actually use ClickUp

ClickUp does little until the Hierarchy exists. The vendor's onboarding guide puts Workspace structure first — Spaces, then Folders, then Lists, then tasks — and explicitly asks teams to agree what each level represents before anyone creates one. Roles come next: Guests, Members, Admins, Owner and custom roles are decided during setup, and permission mistakes surface afterwards as people seeing the wrong Spaces. Then import. ClickUp publishes importers for Asana, Monday.com, Jira, Confluence, Trello, Todoist, Basecamp, Wrike, Notion and CSV or Excel, and lets the result land in a brand new Space or be mapped onto an existing Folder.

For CSV the import page promises only that any number of columns can be imported in any order and mapped into Lists; it documents no validation step, so mapping is checked by eye. The practical stall is not the account itself but the structural decision plus the cleanup once a migration has turned boards and Jira issue types into Lists that do not match how the team actually works.

Distilled from ClickUp's own team onboarding guide (https://clickup.com/team-onboarding) for Hierarchy-first setup and the role model, and ClickUp's import page (https://clickup.com/import) for the supported migration sources and the new-Space / existing-Folder mapping choice. Plan-gated API throughput from ClickUp's developer docs (https://developer.clickup.com/docs/rate-limits). Help Center articles on the Hierarchy and Workspace setup were unreachable (403).

The natural comparison is Asana or Trello — simpler project tools. Weigh it against monday.com on the job you actually need done.

Frequently asked questions

What runs out first on the free plan?

Storage. Read on 13 August 2026: Free Forever carries unlimited tasks but 60MB of storage, 5 active automation rules and 100 automation executions a month. Tasks and checklists will not exhaust that; attachments will, and quickly — which is why the free plan tends to end the week a team starts putting files in it rather than the week they run out of tasks.

Which tier do I actually need for automations?

The step that matters is Business. The pricing page lists 100 automation executions a month on Free, 1,000 on Unlimited ($7 per user/month billed yearly), 5,000 on Business ($12) and 250,000 on Enterprise. If you are buying ClickUp because of a routing workflow that should run without a human, size against the execution count at the tier you would buy, not against the seat price.

Does paying more lift the API rate limit?

Not at the tiers most buyers compare. ClickUp's developer documentation puts Free Forever, Unlimited and Business all at 100 requests per minute per token; only Business Plus lifts it to 1,000 and Enterprise to 10,000. An integration-heavy setup therefore stays throttled long after per-seat cost has stopped being the question.

What should I weigh before buying a tool like this?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.
