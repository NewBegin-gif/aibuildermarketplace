What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Fewer, if you call it synchronously -- and one concurrent job means you queue. Anyone running a daily visibility check across a hundred keywords burns through it in about two days. Read the credit table before the price table -- the same $100 buys 250,000 credits, which is 35,700 full ChatGPT calls or 62,500 Perplexity ones, so your engine mix decides your bill more than your plan does. And nothing carries: their FAQ, read 17 September 2026, states that unused credits expire at the end of every cycle, so a quiet month is money gone rather than headroom for a busy one.

What Cloro does

API that reads AI search engines — ChatGPT, Perplexity, Copilot, Gemini, Grok, AI Overview and Google Search — and returns the sources and citations behind every query as structured JSON.

What they praise

What they complain about
  • • The reporting layer is behind the data layer. Marvil F., an SEO associate, wants more detailed reporting and deeper customisation for tracking AI visibility over time, and says the workflow takes a while to get familiar with.
  • • It stops at the query and does not reach the business. Bindu A. is explicit that the product gives no AI-referred traffic volume or business impact, so a second tool is still needed to turn mentions into visits.
  • • There is no control over the browser environment the answers are collected in. The computer-software reviewer asks for configurable locale, Accept-Language and timezone, clearer documentation of what resets in each new browser session, and a way to verify the actual exit IP per request — all three matter if you are auditing geo-targeted results.

When the paid plan is worth it

Two things decide your bill here and the plan name is neither of them. The first is the credit rate, which more than halves as you climb: $0.80 per thousand on Lite at $30, $0.40 on Hobby at $100, then $0.39, $0.37 and $0.36, reaching $0.31 on the largest Enterprise step. So the same $100 buys 250,000 credits on Hobby against 125,000 if you bought them at the Lite rate -- upgrading is a discount on volume you were going to use anyway, not a feature purchase.

Seats are unlimited from Lite up. The second is your engine mix. A full ChatGPT response costs 7 credits and a Perplexity query 4 -- it was 3 on 17 August 2026, so the gap between the dearest and cheapest AI engine narrowed from more than double to 75%, by the cheap side going up. Then the synchronous surcharge: any engine called synchronously adds 2 credits flat. On ChatGPT that is a 29% premium; on Perplexity it is now 50%.

Batch asynchronously unless you genuinely need the answer inside the request. One correction from running this ourselves on 19 August 2026: a plain async ChatGPT task billed 5 credits rather than the 7 the table lists for a full response -- that is the web-search rate. Budget from 5 and the free tier is about 100 ChatGPT answers a month, not 71.

What we found running Cloro ourselves

Hands-on · 2026-08-19

We did not just read the docs: we ran 40 pricing queries across 20 tools and two engines through the async batch endpoint for a research report of our own, then spent a further handful of credits measuring what individual requests actually cost. Cloro is an affiliate partner of ours, which is why we checked their credit table against the meter instead of quoting it.

  • A plain async ChatGPT task billed 5 credits, not the 7 the pricing table lists for a full ChatGPT response — that is the web-search rate. It held over 20 requests in a batch and again on a single measured call. Budget from 5 and the free tier is 100 ChatGPT answers, not 71.
  • The credit meter lags. Straight after a completed ChatGPT task the balance was unchanged; the charge landed about 90 seconds later. If you gate your own spending on a balance read immediately after a call, you will overshoot.
  • The harvest does not echo your request back: task.payload comes back null. Submit a batch of 40 and nothing in the response tells you which answer belongs to which query — you have to keep your own map of task id to question. This cost us a wrong result on the first pass.
  • The response shape differs per engine, so there is no single schema to code against. ChatGPT returned text, sources, citationPills and model; Perplexity returned no model but added related_queries, search_model_queries and shopping_cards.
  • Async tasks came back in roughly 30 to 40 seconds each, and on the free tier's single concurrent job a 40-task batch is a queue, not a burst — budget wall-clock time as well as credits.
  • Source counts swing wildly and that is the engine, not the API: one Perplexity call returned 10 sources, while in our 20-tool batch 14 of 20 Perplexity answers returned no sources at all.

How to actually use Cloro

Sign up for the free tier, which needs no card, and spend the first credits on one engine rather than all. The per-request cost still differs by 75% between Perplexity (4 credits, up from 3 in August) and a full ChatGPT response (7). So the cheapest way to learn the shape of the data is to run the same query across engines once and compare what comes back. Use async/batch calls unless you genuinely need the answer in-request, because sync adds 2 credits to every single call regardless of engine.

Concurrency is the other limit people hit before credits: one job on Free, ten on Lite, and a backlog of queued requests looks like slowness that is actually a plan ceiling. US state-level targeting is advertised on the homepage, which matters if you track visibility for a market rather than a country. Two additions cloro announced on 28 August 2026 change how you budget and how you wire this in: a GET /v1/credits endpoint returning your balance, your credits per cycle and the reset date, and a hosted MCP server exposing the monitor endpoints as tools to an agent client.

Neither undoes the meter lag from our August run: a charge landed about 90 seconds after it finished; their FAQ, read 17 September 2026, documents X-Credits-Remaining and X-Credits-Charged headers on every response and per-task credit reporting on batches -- the better throttle. Two later changelog entries, read 16 September 2026, are worth knowing before you wire anything up. Since 2 September 2026 the ChatGPT endpoint accepts disableWebSearch: true, which returns the answer ChatGPT gives on its own instead of the search-forced one; cloro states the cost is unchanged, and result.sources, result.citationPills and result.searchQueries then come back empty for most prompts, so keep the default whenever what you are measuring is citations rather than the model text.

And on 16 September 2026 cloro reported the Perplexity endpoint back to normal after weeks of degradation, during which log-in walls hit nearly all requests. Nothing about that endpoint changed, but it is the reason to check their status page before you read a run of empty Perplexity answers as an absence of citations.

Frequently asked questions

What does a request actually cost?

Credits, and the engine decides more than the plan does. Read on cloro.dev/pricing on 17 September 2026: a full ChatGPT response is 7 credits, ChatGPT web search 5, Copilot 5 and AI Overview including the SERP 5; Gemini, AI Mode, Grok and Perplexity are 4; Google Search and Google News are 3 plus 2 per extra page. Every synchronous call adds 2 credits flat on top, which is a 29% surcharge on ChatGPT and 50% on Perplexity, so batch asynchronously unless you need the answer inside the request. One change since we read the same table on 17 August 2026: Perplexity has gone from 3 credits to 4, a 33% rise on what was the cheapest AI engine on the list. And Grok is marked temporarily unavailable, with the page itself saying requests to it fail and not to size a plan around it.

Do unused credits carry over?

No, and their FAQ says so plainly: credits are allocated per billing cycle and any you do not use expire at the end of it. There is no pay-as-you-go option either — the trade cloro names for that is a bill that is the same every month. When the balance reaches zero, requests are rejected with a 403 until you upgrade, which takes effect immediately and prorates, or the cycle resets; they email at 80% and 100% of the allocation. Only annual Enterprise contracts include overages, along with 20% more credits a month at the same fee and 1.3× concurrency. Two details that work in your favour: credits are charged only when extraction succeeds, and an upgrade preserves the unused credits from the plan you left rather than resetting them.
