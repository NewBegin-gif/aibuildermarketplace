Credit-metered, and the credit table matters more than the plan price. Free is $0 for 500 credits a month, one concurrent job and one seat. Paid runs Lite $30 (37,500 credits, $0.80 per 1k, 10 concurrent jobs), Hobby $100 (250,000, $0.40, 20 jobs) and Starter $250 (650,000, $0.39, 50 jobs). Above that come Growth $500 (1,350,000, $0.37, 75 jobs) and Business $1,000 (2,800,000, $0.36, 100 jobs).

Then four Enterprise steps run from $2,000 to $5,000, where the rate falls to $0.31 and concurrency reaches 255. Seats are unlimited from Lite up. What a request costs varies by engine. ChatGPT full response is 7 credits, ChatGPT web search 5, Copilot 5, and AI Overview including the SERP 5. Grok is 4, AI Mode 4, Gemini 4, Perplexity 3, and Google Search and Google News 3 plus 2 per extra page.

A synchronous call adds 2 credits on top of all of those. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Fewer, if you call it synchronously — and one concurrent job means you queue. Anyone running a daily visibility check across a hundred keywords burns through it in about two days. Read the credit table before the price table — the same $100 buys 250,000 credits, which is 35,700 full ChatGPT calls or 83,000 Perplexity ones, so your engine mix decides your bill more than your plan does.

What Cloro does

API that reads AI search engines — ChatGPT, Perplexity, Copilot, Gemini, Grok, AI Overview and Google Search — and returns the sources and citations behind every query as structured JSON.

When the paid plan is worth it

Two things decide your bill here and the plan name is neither of them. The first is the credit rate, which more than halves as you climb: $0.80 per thousand on Lite at $30, $0.40 on Hobby at $100, then $0.39, $0.37 and $0.36, reaching $0.31 on the largest Enterprise step. So the same $100 buys 250,000 credits on Hobby against 125,000 if you bought them at the Lite rate -- upgrading is a discount on volume you were going to use anyway, not a feature purchase.

Seats are unlimited from Lite up, so you never pay for people. The second is your engine mix, and it swings wider than any plan step. A full ChatGPT response costs 7 credits and a Perplexity query 3, so an identical research question can cost you more than twice as much depending on which engine you point it at. Then there is the synchronous surcharge: calling any engine synchronously adds 2 credits flat.

On ChatGPT that is a 29% premium; on Perplexity it is 67%. Batch asynchronously unless you genuinely need the answer inside the request. One correction from running this ourselves on 19 August 2026: a plain async ChatGPT task billed 5 credits rather than the 7 the table lists for a full response -- that is the web-search rate. Budget from 5 and the free tier is about 100 ChatGPT answers a month, not 71.

What we found running Cloro ourselves

Hands-on · 2026-08-19

We did not just read the docs: we ran 40 pricing queries across 20 tools and two engines through the async batch endpoint for a research report of our own, then spent a further handful of credits measuring what individual requests actually cost. Cloro is an affiliate partner of ours, which is why we checked their credit table against the meter instead of quoting it.

  • A plain async ChatGPT task billed 5 credits, not the 7 the pricing table lists for a full ChatGPT response — that is the web-search rate. It held over 20 requests in a batch and again on a single measured call. Budget from 5 and the free tier is 100 ChatGPT answers, not 71.
  • The credit meter lags. Straight after a completed ChatGPT task the balance was unchanged; the charge landed about 90 seconds later. If you gate your own spending on a balance read immediately after a call, you will overshoot.
  • The harvest does not echo your request back: task.payload comes back null. Submit a batch of 40 and nothing in the response tells you which answer belongs to which query — you have to keep your own map of task id to question. This cost us a wrong result on the first pass.
  • The response shape differs per engine, so there is no single schema to code against. ChatGPT returned text, sources, citationPills and model; Perplexity returned no model but added related_queries, search_model_queries and shopping_cards.
  • Async tasks came back in roughly 30 to 40 seconds each, and on the free tier's single concurrent job a 40-task batch is a queue, not a burst — budget wall-clock time as well as credits.
  • Source counts swing wildly and that is the engine, not the API: one Perplexity call returned 10 sources, while in our 20-tool batch 14 of 20 Perplexity answers returned no sources at all.

How to actually use Cloro

Sign up for the free tier, which needs no card, and spend the first credits on one engine rather than all of them. The per-request cost differs by a factor of two between Perplexity (3 credits) and a full ChatGPT response (7). So the cheapest way to learn the shape of the data is to run the same query across engines once and compare what comes back. Use async/batch calls unless you genuinely need the answer in-request, because sync adds 2 credits to every single call regardless of engine.

Concurrency is the other limit people hit before credits: one job on Free, ten on Lite, and a backlog of queued requests looks like slowness that is actually a plan ceiling. US state-level targeting is advertised on the homepage, which matters if you are tracking visibility for a market rather than a country. Two additions cloro announced on 28 August 2026 change how you budget and how you wire this in: a GET /v1/credits endpoint that returns your remaining balance, your credits per cycle and the date the cycle resets, and a hosted MCP server that exposes the monitor endpoints as tools to an agent client.

The balance endpoint does not undo the meter lag we measured, where a charge landed about 90 seconds after the call finished, so read it as where your balance stood rather than where it stands.
