The free tier is a sampler, not a trial of the real thing: 500 credits is about 100 async ChatGPT answers at the 5-credit rate we were actually charged, and their table lists 7 for a full response.

Best for: SEO and marketing teams who need AI-search visibility as structured data — who gets cited for which query on ChatGPT, Perplexity, Copilot, Gemini and Google's AI Overview — rather than as a dashboard someone else designed.

We tested it · 2026-08-19

We did not just read the docs: we ran 40 pricing queries across 20 tools and two engines through the async batch endpoint for a research report of our own, then spent a further handful of credits measuring what individual requests actually cost. Cloro is an affiliate partner of ours, which is why we checked their credit table against the meter instead of quoting it.

  • A plain async ChatGPT task billed 5 credits, not the 7 the pricing table lists for a full ChatGPT response — that is the web-search rate. It held over 20 requests in a batch and again on a single measured call. Budget from 5 and the free tier is 100 ChatGPT answers, not 71.
  • The credit meter lags. Straight after a completed ChatGPT task the balance was unchanged; the charge landed about 90 seconds later. If you gate your own spending on a balance read immediately after a call, you will overshoot.
  • The harvest does not echo your request back: task.payload comes back null. Submit a batch of 40 and nothing in the response tells you which answer belongs to which query — you have to keep your own map of task id to question. This cost us a wrong result on the first pass.

Frequently asked questions

What does a request actually cost?

Credits, and the engine decides more than the plan does. Read on cloro.dev/pricing on 17 September 2026: a full ChatGPT response is 7 credits, ChatGPT web search 5, Copilot 5 and AI Overview including the SERP 5; Gemini, AI Mode, Grok and Perplexity are 4; Google Search and Google News are 3 plus 2 per extra page. Every synchronous call adds 2 credits flat on top, which is a 29% surcharge on ChatGPT and 50% on Perplexity, so batch asynchronously unless you need the answer inside the request. One change since we read the same table on 17 August 2026: Perplexity has gone from 3 credits to 4, a 33% rise on what was the cheapest AI engine on the list. And Grok is marked temporarily unavailable, with the page itself saying requests to it fail and not to size a plan around it.

Do unused credits carry over?

No, and their FAQ says so plainly: credits are allocated per billing cycle and any you do not use expire at the end of it. There is no pay-as-you-go option either — the trade cloro names for that is a bill that is the same every month. When the balance reaches zero, requests are rejected with a 403 until you upgrade, which takes effect immediately and prorates, or the cycle resets; they email at 80% and 100% of the allocation. Only annual Enterprise contracts include overages, along with 20% more credits a month at the same fee and 1.3× concurrency. Two details that work in your favour: credits are charged only when extraction succeeds, and an upgrade preserves the unused credits from the plan you left rather than resetting them. Which plan do I need?

Work it out from credits rather than from the plan names, because the rate per thousand more than halves as you climb: $0.80 on Lite at $30 a month for 37,500 credits, $0.40 on Hobby at $100 for 250,000, $0.39 on Starter at $250 for 650,000, then $0.37 and $0.36 on Growth and Business. Four self-serve Enterprise steps follow at $2,000, $3,000, $4,000 and $5,000 for 5,871,025, 9,306,606, 12,756,261 and 16,391,783 credits, at $0.34, $0.32, $0.31 and $0.31 — selectable in the dashboard rather than through sales. Concurrency climbs with them, from one job on Free to 255 at the top, and it is the limit people hit first: exceed it and you get a 429, which reads like slowness but is a plan ceiling.
