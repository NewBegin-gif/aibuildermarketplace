What Cloro does

API that reads AI search engines — ChatGPT, Perplexity, Copilot, Gemini, Grok, AI Overview and Google Search — and returns the sources and citations behind every query as structured JSON.

Pricing (2026)

(prices read 2026-08-17 on cloro.dev/pricing) Credit-metered, and the credit table matters more than the plan price. Free is $0 for 500 credits a month, one concurrent job and one seat. Paid runs Lite $30 (37,500 credits, $0.80 per 1k, 10 concurrent jobs), Hobby $100 (250,000, $0.40, 20 jobs), Starter $250 (650,000, $0.39, 50 jobs), Growth $500 (1,350,000, $0.37, 75 jobs), Business $1,000 (2,800,000, $0.36, 100 jobs), then four Enterprise steps from $2,000 to $5,000 where the rate falls to $0.31 and concurrency reaches 255. Seats are unlimited from Lite up. What a request costs varies by engine: ChatGPT full response 7 credits, ChatGPT web search 5, Copilot 5, AI Overview including the SERP 5, Grok 4, AI Mode 4, Gemini 4, Perplexity 3, Google Search and Google News 3 plus 2 per extra page — and a synchronous call adds 2 credits on top of all of those. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

The honest knock

The free tier is a sampler, not a trial of the real thing: 500 credits is 71 full ChatGPT responses, or 55 if you call it synchronously, and one concurrent job means you queue. Anyone running a daily visibility check across a hundred keywords burns through it in about two days. Read the credit table before the price table — the same $100 buys 250,000 credits, which is 35,700 full ChatGPT calls or 83,000 Perplexity ones, so your engine mix decides your bill more than your plan does.

How to actually use Cloro

Sign up for the free tier, which needs no card, and spend the first credits on one engine rather than all of them: the per-request cost differs by a factor of two between Perplexity (3 credits) and a full ChatGPT response (7), so the cheapest way to learn the shape of the data is to run the same query across engines once and compare what comes back. Use async/batch calls unless you genuinely need the answer in-request, because sync adds 2 credits to every single call regardless of engine. Concurrency is the other limit people hit before credits: one job on Free, ten on Lite, and a backlog of queued requests looks like slowness that is actually a plan ceiling. US state-level targeting is advertised on the homepage, which matters if you are tracking visibility for a market rather than a country. Best for: SEO and marketing teams who need AI-search visibility as structured data — who gets cited for which query on ChatGPT, Perplexity, Copilot, Gemini and Google's AI Overview — rather than as a dashboard someone else designed.
Who should skip it: if you want a finished rank-tracking dashboard rather than an API; this returns JSON with markdown, sources and citations, and you build the reporting yourself.
