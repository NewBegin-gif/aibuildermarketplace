What Close does

A high-performance sales CRM built for growth-focused startups and SMBs. Unifies calling, emailing, and SMS into one powerful inbox. Now ships with Chloe, a built-in AI teammate for notetaking, chat, pipeline suggestions and CRM enrichment, with outbound AI voice calling still in beta.

Pricing (2026)

Per-seat: Solo about $9, Essentials $35, Growth $99, Scale $139 per user/mo billed annually (higher monthly) (2026). Call minutes, SMS and Power Dialer credits are billed separately and can meaningfully raise the real cost. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The built-in calling is the draw, but call and SMS credits stack on top of the seat price — a '$99' plan can effectively cost ~$160/user once a rep dials 50+ times a day. Great for call-heavy teams, pricey if you barely dial.

When the paid plan is worth it

Close has no free plan, just a 14-day trial that does not ask for a credit card. Solo, the cheapest tier at $19 per user monthly or $9 on annual billing, caps you at one user and 10,000 leads. Essentials ($49 monthly, $35 annual) lifts both caps but still has no workflows. That is the catch: the automation most teams buy Close for, meaning workflow sequences, the power dialer and bulk email, starts at Growth ($109 monthly, $99 annual). On the official feature table, Growth is the real starting point if you run outbound at any volume; Essentials covers pipeline tracking with straightforward calling. Scale ($149 monthly, $139 annual) adds the predictive dialer, call coaching, custom roles with unique permissions, and unlimited call-recording retention, where Solo and Essentials keep recordings 30 days and Growth 90. Role-based access itself is on every plan, so do not upgrade for that alone. No tier removes the metered costs: outbound calls run around $0.02 per minute and phone numbers start at about $1 per month, on top of every subscription.

How to actually use Close

Close's own onboarding checklist puts process before data. Step 0 states that before you connect accounts or import data, it is critical to set the foundation: your sales process, meaning pipeline structure, lead statuses and opportunity stages. The docs frame this as the way to avoid rework later. Customizing lead records and custom fields is step 2, ahead of importing at step 3, so configure your fields first. Connecting email and calendar is step 1 and the docs put it at two minutes. The friction sits in communications setup. At step 5 the docs note you may be required by law to provide information before registering phone numbers, and step 6 requires A2P 10DLC registration before you can text US numbers, which is a carrier compliance process rather than a toggle in Close. Smart Views arrive at step 8, Workflows at step 9. One independent walkthrough adds two self-inflicted traps: too many pipelines cause confusion when deals overlap, and a workflow that is too aggressive burns leads who would have converted with a gentler approach. Frequently asked questions

Is Close worth it for a small team?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Small teams get the most out of this category when one clear problem is already costing real hours or revenue; buying ahead of that just adds cost and another login. Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.

Who should not use Close?

Skip it if your sales are mostly email or self-serve — a cheaper CRM without built-in calling covers you. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
