Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 18 Aug 2026

(2026-08-01, read on their own pricing page) No prices are shown. Two named plans, Standard and Pro, both stated to include 5 seats, with everything routed to 'Talk to sales' and 'Get a personalised quote'. The model is credit-based — their own wording is that 1 credit equals 1 revealed contact — with credits included in the subscription per seat, but no allowance is published.

CRM Enrichment is sold standalone or as an add-on; Data-as-a-Service is priced by volume and delivery method. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

The honest knock

That combination means the smallest deal is five seats of something you cannot price, so budget conversations start with a sales call rather than a number. The credit model is the part to nail down: one credit reveals one contact, so your real cost per usable lead is the credit price divided by your connect rate, and neither figure is published. Before the call, work out how many contacts a month you actually reveal — that is the only number that lets you judge the quote you get.

And it is worth asking whether unused credits roll over, because nothing on the page says.

What Cognism does

B2B contact data with a GDPR focus — phone-verified mobile numbers and intent signals.

What buyers report about Cognism

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Cognism ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • European coverage and GDPR-compliant sourcing is what reviewers pick it for over the American alternatives — 4.5 on G2 across 1,337+ reviews, 4.7 on Capterra.
  • • Phone-verified mobile numbers are the specific feature reviewers say justifies the price when they land.
What they complain about
  • • Data accuracy is the number one complaint by volume: 99 review mentions of inaccurate data, 62 of incorrect numbers, 58 of outdated contacts, 57 of incorrect information.
  • • North American coverage, especially mid-market, is noticeably thinner than European.
  • • Nothing is published. Platform fees start around $15,000 before per-user licences; a ten-rep team runs roughly $25,000-$30,000 a year on Grow and $40,000-$50,000 on Elevate.
  • • Annual contracts only, with reviewers describing no flexibility on billing terms and difficulty cancelling. Get the exit terms in writing before you sign.

Where the ratings disagree. 4.5 on G2 against 3.0 on Trustpilot. The G2 reviews are about the data; the Trustpilot ones are about opaque pricing, credit limits and renewal practice.

When the paid plan is worth it

Standard and Pro differ on data quality and signals rather than on scale. Pro is described as reaching more decision-makers with higher-quality mobile data, adding on-demand verification and intent on top of the same prospecting workflows. Both tiers state five seats included, so the upgrade never changes the size of the deal — the floor is five either way. Three things the upgrade does not settle. The credit allowance is published on neither plan, only the rule that one credit reveals one contact and that each seat includes an allocation, so choosing Pro tells you nothing about how many contacts you may actually reveal.

CRM Enrichment and Data-as-a-Service sit as add-ons against both tiers, so paying more does not fold them in. And coverage for your specific market is deliberately not stated: the FAQ declines general coverage claims and routes you to a call for a tailored estimate. Two details do work in your favour on either plan — re-viewing a contact you already revealed carries no charge, and the same credits are used across the prospecting platform, Enrich and the API.

How to actually use Cognism

Nothing here ends in a checkout: the pricing page routes Standard to Talk to sales and Pro to Talk to an expert, though the site does offer an interactive tour you can run without a rep. Once inside, the work is list-building and plumbing. Filters cover firmographic, technographic and signal-based criteria, and you can upload a CSV against filters such as company domain, company name and country to prospect into accounts you already care about.

Salesforce users can import CRM data and use the Cognism CRM filter to find decision-makers inside owned accounts. The integrations the vendor names are Salesforce, HubSpot, Pipedrive and Microsoft Dynamics, plus Outreach and SalesLoft, where the Chrome extension works inside Outreach as well. Export runs either to the connected CRM or sequencer, or out as CSV. Settle the compliance policy early: numbers matched against Do Not Call registries are marked DNC in the platform, and it is the customer who decides whether reps see that data, see it flagged, or not at all.

Frequently asked questions

What should I weigh before buying Sales & CRM software?

Most tools in this category plug in rather than replace. Check which direction the data flows and whether the sync is genuinely two-way, because a one-way push creates a second source of truth that your reps will quietly stop trusting. If it does aim to replace your CRM, price the migration honestly: history, custom fields and reporting rarely move across cleanly.
