What Consensus does

AI search across peer-reviewed literature: it reports what published studies found, not whether those studies are sound. A free tier covers basic search; Pro is about $15/month, or roughly $10 billed annually.

The catch

It searches peer-reviewed literature, which is its strength and its boundary: it can tell you what published studies found, not whether those studies are good. Treat it as a dramatically faster literature scan, not an oracle; click through to the papers that matter.

When the paid plan is worth it

The free plan already covers quick search and basic queries, so if you only skim abstracts occasionally, paying may not be worth it. The real break-even point is Deep Search usage: Pro adds unlimited Pro Search plus 15 Deep Searches a month, and it pays off once you're regularly leaning on that AI-synthesized analysis rather than just scanning ranked results. If 15 Deep Searches a month isn't enough — a systematic-review push burns through that fast — the Deep tier adds 200 Deep Searches a month, but it's priced and built for heavy users and small research teams, not occasional readers.

What upgrading does not fix: it won't make the underlying studies more rigorous, and it won't replace your own judgment on sample sizes or conflicting results. Teams pricing runs on shared seats with consolidated billing rather than a flat group rate, so cost scales with headcount, and annual billing lowers the effective monthly price but commits you to a year upfront.

How to actually use Consensus

The fastest path to real value is the web app at consensus.app, which works immediately without an account — you get anonymous access with a 3-paper-per-search limit. Creating a free account raises that to 10 papers per search plus 30 searches a month. For agent or IDE workflows, the MCP server also starts without an account: point an MCP-compatible client (Claude Desktop, ChatGPT, Cursor, Claude Code, VS Code Copilot, Windsurf) at https://mcp.consensus.app/mcp, complete the OAuth sign-in, and you're searching in under 2 minutes on most platforms.

Three things trip people up. The URL must end in the /mcp path or the connection fails. OAuth sessions expire after a few hours, so tools quietly stop working with 401 errors, and a plain reconnect click often doesn't fix it — a full client restart does. And Claude Code specifically sometimes needs a second attempt before the tool list populates.

Frequently asked questions

What does Consensus cost?

Our dated reading puts Pro at about $15 a month, or roughly $10 billed annually, with a free tier below it and a Deep tier above. We could not re-confirm those from the page on 17 August 2026 because it blocks plain fetches, so check the live figure before budgeting.

What is actually rationed between the tiers?

Deep searches. Pro gives unlimited ordinary Pro searches but only around 15 Deep ones; the tier above raises that to roughly 200. If your work is occasional literature checks the free or Pro tier is enough; if you run deep syntheses weekly, the Deep allowance is the only number that matters.

Is the free tier useful for research?

For finding papers and getting a sense of consensus on a question, yes. What it does not give you is the depth features that make it faster than a normal search, so treat it as a way to judge whether the interface fits your workflow rather than as the working tool.

What should I weigh before buying IT & Productivity software?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.
