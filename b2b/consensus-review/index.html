Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 28 Sep 2026

Free: $0, 10 Pro messages and up to 3 Deep reviews a month. Pro: $20 a month billed monthly, or $12 a month billed annually ($144 a year), with unlimited Pro messages, 15 Deep reviews a month and 500 API and MCP uses included, then $0.05 a use up to 1,000. Deep: $65 monthly, or $45 a month billed annually ($540 a year), with 200 Deep reviews and 2,000 API and MCP uses, then $0.05 a use up to 10,000.

Students and faculty with a school email and US healthcare professionals with an NPI number can get up to 40% off. Team and Enterprise carry no amount on the page; the vendor's machine-readable pricing file puts Team at up to 200 seats with 50 Deep searches per seat and Enterprise on contract, and it still gives Free 15 Pro messages where the rendered page says 10.

The unit that matters is Deep reviews rather than seats. Correction: our earlier reading, which we could not confirm at the time, put Pro at about $15 a month or roughly $10 annually. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What they complain about

When the paid plan is worth it

The free plan already covers quick search and basic queries, so if you only skim abstracts occasionally, paying may not be worth it. The real break-even point is Deep Search usage: Pro, $20 a month or $12 a month billed annually (read 28 September 2026), adds unlimited Pro Search plus 15 Deep Searches a month, and it pays off once you're regularly leaning on that AI-synthesized analysis rather than just scanning ranked results.

If 15 Deep Searches a month isn't enough — a systematic-review push burns through that fast — the Deep tier ($65 a month, or $45 billed annually) adds 200 Deep Searches a month, but it's priced and built for heavy users and small research teams, not occasional readers. What upgrading does not fix: it won't make the underlying studies more rigorous, and it won't replace your own judgment on sample sizes or conflicting results.

Teams pricing runs on shared seats with consolidated billing rather than a flat group rate, so cost scales with headcount, and annual billing lowers the effective monthly price but commits you to a year upfront.

How to actually use Consensus

The fastest path to real value is the web app at consensus.app, which works immediately without an account — you get anonymous access with a 3-paper-per-search limit. Creating a free account raises that to 10 papers per search plus 30 searches a month. For agent or IDE workflows, the MCP server also starts without an account: point an MCP-compatible client (Claude Desktop, ChatGPT, Cursor, Claude Code, VS Code Copilot, Windsurf) at https://mcp.consensus.app/mcp, complete the OAuth sign-in, and you're searching in under 2 minutes on most platforms.

Three things trip people up. The URL must end in the /mcp path or the connection fails. OAuth sessions expire after a few hours, so tools quietly stop working with 401 errors, and a plain reconnect click often doesn't fix it — a full client restart does. And Claude Code specifically sometimes needs a second attempt before the tool list populates.

Frequently asked questions

What does Consensus cost?

Read on the rendered pricing page on 28 September 2026: Free at $0 with 10 Pro messages and up to 3 Deep reviews a month; Pro $20 a month, or $12 a month billed annually ($144 a year); Deep $65 a month, or $45 billed annually ($540). Our earlier answer of about $15 and $10 for Pro came from third-party trackers, because the page could not be read at the time. Students and faculty with a school email and US clinicians with an NPI number can get up to 40% off.

What is actually rationed between the tiers?

Deep reviews, and now API and MCP uses. Pro gives unlimited Pro messages but 15 Deep reviews and 500 API and MCP uses a month; Deep raises that to 200 reviews and 2,000 uses, with extra uses at $0.05 each up to 1,000 and 10,000 a month respectively. Consensus's developer file (llms-full.txt) lists lower MCP search quotas, 100 a month on Pro and 500 on Deep, so check the quota in your account before you wire it into an agent. For occasional literature checks Free or Pro is enough; for weekly deep syntheses the Deep allowance is the number that matters.

Is the free tier useful for research?

For finding papers and getting a sense of consensus on a question, yes. What it does not give you is the depth features that make it faster than a normal search, so treat it as a way to judge whether the interface fits your workflow rather than as the working tool.

What should I weigh before buying IT & Productivity software?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.
