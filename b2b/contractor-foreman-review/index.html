What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The catch is the billing term. Quarterly costs 26% more than annual on the same plan — $132 against $105 on Standard — and Basic is not sold quarterly at all, so the cheapest way in requires a year paid up front. The user counts are the real ladder: 1, 3, 8, 15, unlimited. A five-person crew is not on Standard at $105, it is on Plus at $166, so count your licence holders before you compare against anything else.

What Contractor Foreman does

Construction management — estimating, scheduling, daily logs and financial tracking — priced per company rather than per seat, with unlimited projects on every tier. Annual runs $49/month for one user up to $332 for unlimited; quarterly billing costs about 26% more and Basic is annual-only.

When the paid plan is worth it

This is priced for the company rather than the seat, with unlimited projects on every tier, so the only thing the ladder buys is headcount -- and the cost per person falls sharply as you climb. Billed annually: Basic $49 a month for 1 user, Standard $105 for 3, Plus $166 for 8, Pro $221 for 15, Unlimited $332. Per user at each ceiling that is $49, $35, $20.75 and $14.73.

The expensive step is the first one. Going from one person to two means leaving Basic entirely, because there is nothing in between: $105 against $49, so your second user costs $56 a month on their own. From there it improves steadily, and a firm of eight pays less per head than a firm of three. Billing frequency matters more here than at most vendors. Quarterly costs materially more for the same plan -- Standard $132 a month against $105, which is 26% more -- and Basic is not offered quarterly at all.

If cash flow is the reason you would avoid an annual commitment, price that decision properly rather than assuming it is a few per cent.

How to actually use Contractor Foreman

The route in is a 30-day free trial with no charge until it ends; cancelling before that point costs nothing and is done in the app under Settings, then Subscription, then Cancel. Register for implementation after signing up rather than before, since the vendor pairs you with a Customer Success Manager and free one-on-one training sessions of roughly 40 minutes each, with the number of sessions set by your plan and reset annually.

Take one of those sessions early, because the 100-day money-back guarantee depends on it. It applies to annual plans only, and requires a one-on-one session within 30 days of signing up and another within the 30 days before you ask — group trainings explicitly do not count. You also need one Estimate, one Project, one Time Card and one Daily Log created in the account. Two practical notes for the first week: the vendor supports Firefox, Brave and Chrome and does not recommend Safari or Edge, and employee permissions run Full Access (All Data), Full Access (Own Data), Read Only and No Access.

Frequently asked questions

What should I weigh before buying Operations & Workflow software?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.
