What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The friendly $299 headline assumes annual prepay; month-to-month is $399. One thing people misprice, and it is not the test itself: everyone expects an experiment to end when the subscription does. The trap is the winner. Convert describes itself as a client-side tool where “adding just one snippet of code on your site will then do all of the magic”. So a winning variant you leave running in the visual editor is not a change to your site: it is a rendering by their script.

Ship the winner into your own code, or you are paying rent on a result you already bought.

    How to actually use Convert

    Installation is one tracking script, but the work that decides whether a test is trustworthy is the QA pass. Convert documents it as four stages: draft in the Visual Editor using Preview Variation URLs, then check with Force Variation URLs, then QA the whole setup while viewing the experiment report, then launch. The two URL types are not interchangeable. Preview URLs ignore your Site Area and Audience conditions, while Force Variation URLs evaluate them and only work on active experiments, never on drafts.

    Install the Chrome QA Widget first: it is the primary QA tool and it does not run on the legacy snippet, so a site still on the old script has to update before it can use the overlay. Run QA in an incognito window, because Convert's own guidance is that testers are commonly bucketed into the original and conclude the experiment is broken. If your site uses a consent platform, cookies may not be set until consent is granted, which breaks bucketing during QA.

    Does Pro give me ten times the traffic allowance of Growth?

    No, and this corrects an earlier reading of our own. On convert.com/pricing, read 20 September 2026, tested users are a slider rather than a plan feature: at its first position Growth and Pro both include 100K tested users a month, 1.2M a year, and it is Enterprise that starts at 1M a month, 12M a year. So the step from $299 to $420 on the annual rate buys limits and features at the same volume, not headroom. The comparison table, Growth against Pro: active projects per account 5 against 30, active experiences per project 5 against unlimited, active domains 10 against unlimited, goals per project 30 against 100, custom segments 5 against 10, deploys 10 against 100. Growth is also marked absent on multipage testing, multivariate testing, full stack, multi-arm bandit auto-allocate, geotargeting, weather targeting and custom pop-ups. The one place volume does show up is the overage rate: $399 per extra 100K on Growth is $3.99 per thousand, while $699 per 250K on Pro is $2.80.

    How much does paying annually actually save?

    Less on Growth than the banner suggests. The toggle says 'Up to 30% OFF' and the fine print splits it: Growth is $399 a month billed monthly and $3,588 a year, which the vendor describes as saving 25% on twelve payments of $399; Pro is $599 monthly and $5,040 a year, a 30% saving on twelve payments of $599. The effective monthly rates are the headline $299 and $420. Prices exclude VAT and GST, which are added at checkout by billing location, and 8% of the annual fee is spent on tree planting with TIST at no extra cost to you. The standard trial is 15 days with no credit card; through our link it is 30 days with full access. One cost sits outside the plans entirely and does not shrink with annual billing: each additional custom domain via CNAME is $1,000 a year on every tier. Read 20 September 2026.

    How does it compare with VWO?

    Convert answers that on its own pricing page, and it is worth knowing how. Its feature table annotates rows with what the same capability costs inside VWO's plans: custom JavaScript targeting, custom tag targeting, behaviour-based targeting, cookie-based targeting, time-of-day targeting, advanced post-segmentation and detailed report downloads are each marked as costing extra as an add-on in VWO Growth, or in VWO Growth and Pro, and it labels two of its own features with the VWO products they correspond to, Web Rollouts for deploys and Server Side testing for full stack. There is a legend translating Convert features into what they are called in the VWO app, the Enterprise tier includes a free VWO migration, and the homepage links a page called VWO Alternative. So this is Convert's own framing of the comparison, not a neutral one: the claim is that what VWO sells as add-ons is included here. Worth checking against VWO's own price page for the features you actually plan to use. Read 20 September 2026.
