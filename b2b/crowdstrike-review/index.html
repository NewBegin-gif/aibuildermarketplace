Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 22 Sep 2026

Priced per device. Falcon Go $7.99 per device per month, or $59.99 per device billed annually — capped at a maximum of 100 devices. Falcon Pro $14.99 monthly or $99.99 annually per device. Falcon Enterprise $19.99 per device per month, or $184.99 per device billed annually — a rate the 1 Aug reading recorded as unpublished. Larger tiers and managed services are quote-only.

The free trial is a single 15-day Falcon Free Trial covering Next-Gen Antivirus, Device Control and Express Support, not a trial of each paid tier. Annual subscriptions are billed in full at the start of the term and can be cancelled within 30 days for a full refund. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The first is the 100-device ceiling on Falcon Go: it is not a soft limit you grow through, it is the point where you change product. The second is the annual rate, which is worth doing the division on — $59.99 a year against $7.99 a month is $95.88, so annual saves 37%, one of the larger annual discounts in security software. Beyond that, be honest with yourself about which tier you are comparing: Go is antivirus with a good agent, and the endpoint detection and response that CrowdStrike is famous for starts at Pro.

When the paid plan is worth it

Priced per device, and the annual discount is where the tier decision quietly gets made. Falcon Go is $7.99 a device monthly or $59.99 a year -- a 37% saving. Falcon Pro is $14.99 monthly or $99.99 a year, 44%. Because the discount is bigger on Pro, the gap shrinks when you commit: paying monthly, Pro costs 88% more than Go; paying annually, only 67% more. If you were going to sign for a year anyway, the upgrade is cheaper than the monthly rates make it look.

The harder constraint is not price but the ceiling: Falcon Go is capped at 100 devices. Growing past it forces a move regardless of the feature set, so if you are near a hundred endpoints, price Pro now rather than migrating mid-year. Falcon Enterprise is $19.99 per device per month or $184.99 a year -- a 23% saving, the smallest of the three -- and everything above it is quote-only.

One caution on the trial: it is a single 15-day product covering antivirus, device control and support rather than a trial of each tier, so it cannot settle the Go-versus-Pro question -- the EDR that separates them is not in it.

How to actually use CrowdStrike

Count your devices first: the 100-device ceiling on Falcon Go is not a limit you grow through, it is the point at which you change product. If you are near it, evaluate Pro from the start rather than migrating under pressure later. Then make sure you are comparing the right thing. Falcon Go is antivirus with a very good agent; the endpoint detection and response that CrowdStrike is known for begins at Pro.

Putting Go side by side with a competitor's full EDR product compares two different categories, and it is the most common mistake here. The free trial will not settle it: it is one 15-day product covering antivirus and device control, without the EDR that separates the tiers. Take annual billing if you take anything. At $59.99 a year against $7.99 a month, Go saves 37%, and Pro at $99.99 against $14.99 saves 44% — unusually large discounts for security software, large enough to be part of the decision rather than a footnote.

Roll it out to a pilot group before the fleet. Endpoint agents are the class of software where a conflict with something already installed shows up as a slow laptop rather than an error message — find that on ten machines, not three hundred.

Frequently asked questions

The trial does not include EDR. So how do I test the tier I am actually buying?

Use the refund window rather than the trial, because they are different offers. Read 22 September 2026: the trial is a single 15-day Falcon Go product covering Next-Gen Antivirus, Device Control and Express Support, so it cannot show you the endpoint detection and response that starts at Pro. Alongside it the same page publishes a 30-day money-back assurance -- try the Falcon platform and cancel within 30 days for a full refund. That is the only route that puts the tier you are considering on your own machines. It also settles the annual question, since annual subscriptions are billed in full at the start of the term but sit inside the same 30 days: you can take the annual discount without gambling a year on it, provided you reach a decision inside the first month. Plan the pilot for week one, not week four.

What happens operationally when we cross 100 devices?

You change product, and the shop will not let you straddle it. The 100-device ceiling on Falcon Go is stated three separate times on the page read 22 September 2026 -- 'Purchases of Falcon Go are limited to a maximum of 100 devices' -- and it is a purchasing limit rather than a performance one. The cart enforces the same thing from the other side: only one bundle can be added at a time, and the page warns that adding a second will remove the first. So you cannot buy Go for the office and Pro for the servers in one order, and you cannot quietly grow through the cap. If you are within sight of a hundred endpoints, price Pro now: annually it is $99.99 a device against Go's $59.99, which is 67% more, against 88% more if you were paying monthly.

We are not in the US. Do we pay a local rate?

Not one the page publishes. Read on 22 September 2026 from a Netherlands exit, crowdstrike.com/en-us/pricing/falcon-go/ served the same three per-device rates -- $7.99, $14.99 and $19.99 monthly, $59.99, $99.99 and $184.99 annually -- and carried the footnote 'All prices listed are in US Dollars' twice. There is no currency selector on the plan cards and no separate European rate card behind the pricing route, so budget the dollar amount plus your own FX and local tax rather than expecting a euro list. Two consequences worth planning for: the annual figure is billed in full at the start of the term, so the exchange rate on one day sets the cost of the whole year, and the 30-day refund returns dollars rather than what you paid in your own currency.

What should I weigh before buying Security software?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.
