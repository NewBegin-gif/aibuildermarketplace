Two numbers decide whether this is for you, and neither is the headline.

Best for: small and mid-size companies that want a serious endpoint agent without running a security team — Falcon Go is genuinely priced for businesses under 100 devices.

Pricing (2026)

(2026-08-01, read on their own pricing page) Priced per device. Falcon Go $7.99 per device per month, or $59.99 per device billed annually — capped at a maximum of 100 devices. Falcon Pro $14.99 monthly or $99.99 annually per device. Falcon Enterprise $19.99 per device per month. Larger tiers and managed services are quote-only. A free trial is offered on every tier. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The first is the 100-device ceiling on Falcon Go: it is not a soft limit you grow through, it is the point where you change product. The second is the annual rate, which is worth doing the division on — $59.99 a year against $7.99 a month is $95.88, so annual saves 37%, one of the larger annual discounts in security software. Beyond that, be honest with yourself about which tier you are comparing: Go is antivirus with a good agent, and the endpoint detection and response that CrowdStrike is famous for starts at Pro.

When the paid plan is worth it

Priced per device, and the annual discount is where the tier decision quietly gets made. Falcon Go is $7.99 a device monthly or $59.99 a year -- a 37% saving. Falcon Pro is $14.99 monthly or $99.99 a year, which is 44%. Because the discount is bigger on Pro, the gap between the two shrinks when you commit: paying monthly, Pro costs 88% more than Go; paying annually, only 67% more. If you were going to sign for a year anyway, the upgrade is cheaper than the monthly rates make it look.

The harder constraint is not price but the ceiling: Falcon Go is capped at 100 devices. Growing past that forces a move regardless of what you think of the feature set, so if you are anywhere near a hundred endpoints, price Pro now rather than migrating mid-year. Falcon Enterprise is $19.99 per device per month with no annual rate published, and everything above that -- larger tiers and managed services -- is quote-only.

A free trial is offered on every tier, which is the right way to settle the Go-versus-Pro question on your own estate rather than on a feature list.

How to actually use CrowdStrike

Count your devices first, because the 100-device ceiling on Falcon Go is not a limit you grow through — it is the point at which you change product. If you are anywhere near it, evaluate Pro from the start rather than migrating under pressure later.

Then make sure you are comparing the right thing. Falcon Go is antivirus with a very good agent; the endpoint detection and response that CrowdStrike is known for begins at Pro. Putting Go side by side with a competitor's full EDR product is comparing two different categories, and it is the most common mistake in this evaluation. Use the free trial on the tier you would actually buy.

Take annual billing if you take anything. At $59.99 a device a year against $7.99 a month, Go saves 37%, and Pro at $99.99 against $14.99 monthly saves 44% — unusually large discounts for security software, and large enough to be part of the decision rather than a footnote.

Roll it out to a pilot group before the fleet. Endpoint agents are the class of software where a conflict with something already installed shows up as a slow laptop rather than an error message, and you want to find that on ten machines rather than three hundred.

The natural comparison is SentinelOne or Microsoft Defender for Business — the direct endpoint peers — Defender is often already in your Microsoft licence, so price that first before comparing per-device rates. Decide by which one fits the job above, not by the louder brand.

Frequently asked questions

What should I weigh before buying a tool like this?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.
