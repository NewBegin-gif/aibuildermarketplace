When the paid plan is worth it

There is nothing between the two published plans, and the gap is the whole story: Essentials starts at $100 a month and Premium starts at $1,000 a month billed yearly. A tenfold step with no middle rung means the decision is rarely about features -- it is about whether you have outgrown Essentials enough to justify an order-of-magnitude increase, and most teams have not.

What makes this easier to plan than most tools in the category is that the overage rates are published, which is genuinely unusual: additional people and object profiles cost $0.009 each, an extra thousand emails $0.12, and AI credits $10 per 100,000. You can therefore model growth on Essentials properly instead of discovering it at renewal. Do that model before considering Premium, because paying overages on Essentials is often far cheaper than the jump.

The practical difference beyond volume is workspaces: Essentials includes 2, Premium and Enterprise unlimited. If you need a third workspace, that is a real reason to move and it is worth asking whether they will sell one rather than the tier.

One route worth checking before paying anything: early-stage companies that have raised under $10M can get twelve months free through their startup programme -- at the Essentials floor that is $1,200.

How to actually use Customer.io

Ask about the startup programme before you look at a price. Companies that have raised under $10M can get twelve months free, which is by some distance the largest number on that page — and it is the kind of thing nobody offers you unless you ask.

Then use the published overage rates, because they let you answer a question most tools in this category will not. Essentials starts at $100 a month and Premium at $1,000, with nothing in between, and additional profiles are $0.009 each. The $900 gap between the tiers is therefore exactly 100,000 profiles of overage — so growing into overage on Essentials beats the jump to Premium for a very long way. Work out your own crossover with your own profile count before anyone tells you it is time to upgrade.

Credit where it is due: printing overage rates at all is unusual. Additional emails are $0.12 per thousand and AI credits $10 per 100,000, so a realistic monthly bill is something you can actually model rather than discover.

Count workspaces too. Essentials includes two, unlimited starts at Premium — and if you run separate brands or a staging environment, that is a structural limit rather than a volume one.

The natural comparison is Braze or Klaviyo — the direct behavioural-messaging peers — Klaviyo for ecommerce, Braze at enterprise scale; compare on cost per profile at your projected list size. Decide by which one fits the job above, not by the louder brand.

Frequently asked questions

What should I weigh before buying a tool like this?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.
