Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 12 Sep 2026

Databox renamed and repriced its whole ladder since our August reading, so the Analyst/Pro/Growth tiers we described are gone. Free is $0 forever: 1 user, 3 data sources, 1 dashboard, 10 custom metrics, 50 AI credits a month, daily sync. Billed annually: Analyst $71/mo (1 user, 5 sources, unlimited dashboards and reports, 150 credits, hourly sync), Team Core $199/mo (3 users, 10 sources, 500 credits), Team Scale $319/mo (10 users, 30 sources, 1,000 credits, sub-accounts) and Agency from $79/mo (unlimited users, 20 sources, 300 credits), plus a quoted Custom tier.

Monthly those read $249, $399 and from $99; annual billing is 20% off. Databox states it does not charge per seat. Extra data sources no longer carry a per-source fee: each plan includes a fixed number and more means moving up a tier, except on Agency, which adds client packs of 1 client, 5 sources and 50 credits for $20/mo annually ($25 monthly).

Add-ons are priced separately: branding and white-labeling $200/mo, advanced security management $80/mo, OKRs $160/mo, fiscal calendar $40/mo, 15-minute sync $14/mo per source, and flexible AI credit top-ups. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

Correction after re-checking: (re-verified 2026-09-12) Databox restructured its pricing between our 13 August and 12 September 2026 readings and every plan name in our earlier text is gone. Analyst rose from $64 to $71 a month on annual billing. Pro at $159 and Growth at $399 were replaced by Team Core at $199 and Team Scale at $319 annually ($249 and $399 monthly), and a new Agency plan starts at $79 annually. Two mechanisms we described no longer exist. The $5.60 per additional data source is gone: extra sources now force a tier jump, and only Agency can self-serve capacity, through $20-a-month client packs. And 15-minute sync is no longer included at the top of the ladder but sold as a $14 per-source add-on, with hourly the fastest sync on every paid plan. Analyst's credit allowance is also lower than the 500 we recorded: 150 a month. We have dropped the '11 months of history' figure from our free-plan description because it no longer appears on the page.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The per-source fee is gone, and for some buyers that is worse rather than better: each plan includes a fixed number of data sources and going over means moving up a whole tier, so one extra integration can mean the $199 Team Core to $319 Team Scale step rather than $5.60. Only the Agency plan sells capacity piecemeal, in $20 client packs. AI credits are a shared monthly pool for Genie and the MCP server, so heavy AI use moves you up a tier as fast as data does, and 15-minute sync — once part of the top plan — is now a $14 per-source add-on on top of the subscription.

Power users may also find the custom-metric and data-modelling depth limited next to a full BI tool.

What Databox does

Centralized KPI dashboards. Pull data from all your software tools into one clean interface to monitor your business health. A free Skills Marketplace adds guided analyses on top: the paid-ads skill standardizes every ad channel into one efficiency table with CPA gauges against your own targets, judged per funnel role. A native MCP server (mcp.databox.com) puts those metrics inside ChatGPT or Claude — ingest data and query it in plain language. It runs on every paid plan, not on Free, and its queries spend from the same monthly AI-credit pool as Genie.

What they praise
  • • Connecting several data sources is genuinely quick, and reviewers who compared visualisation tools call it the smoothest of them.
  • • Priced competitively for small and midsize teams, with straightforward onboarding.

What they complain about
  • • Granular data modelling and advanced custom metrics are where it runs out. If you need those, check before you commit.
  • • Reviewers report templates breaking and individual metrics quietly stopping.
  • • Dashboards do not always refresh promptly.
  • • Report customisation is repeatedly named as the thing that could be better.
  • • Product Hunt reviewers report refresh latency on the entry-level tiers, and say part of it traces back to the upstream provider's rate limits rather than to Databox itself — which matches Databox's own troubleshooting documentation.
  • • The MCP server is read-only: the same reviewers want to generate reports programmatically and cannot.
  • • Feeding it unconventional JSON means reshaping the data before ingestion.

When the paid plan is worth it

Databox's free tier is unusually workable for one person: the pricing page lists 1 user, 3 data sources, 1 dashboard, 10 custom metrics, 50 AI credits a month and daily sync. That covers a freelancer who checks a handful of channels daily. Analyst, at $71 a month on annual billing, adds a fifth data source, 150 AI credits and hourly sync, but it is still a single seat, so it is a solo analyst's upgrade rather than a team's.

Team Core at $199 is where collaboration starts, with 3 users and 10 sources, and Team Scale at $319 buys 10 users, 30 sources and sub-accounts. Databox does not charge per seat, so the ladder is about sources and credits, not headcount. Upgrading pointedly does not fix data freshness that comes from the source side. Databox's own troubleshooting documentation states that data sources do not sync independently and only pull when metrics are actively in use, and that provider limits and account activity shape the real refresh rate.

No paid tier includes 15-minute sync either: hourly is the fastest, and faster costs $14 per source. A higher plan also does not retroactively manufacture history that was never collected.

How to actually use Databox

The shortest route to something useful is sign-up, then one connection, then a template. Databox's help center points new accounts to Data Manager > Available Integrations or Data Manager > Data Sources, where the integration is chosen and the user grants consent for Databox to access the data. From there, importing a pre-built Databoard and editing it in the Designer beats assembling Datablocks by hand. Two things reliably stall people.

The first is access: the person setting up Databox often lacks admin rights on the upstream tool, and the documented workaround is sending a colleague a connect request by email from the link at the bottom of the new connection dialog. The second is empty tiles after a successful connection, because metrics are scheduled only once they are first used in the app, so a brand-new metric can look blank before its first pull lands.

Frequently asked questions

Is the free plan usable, or is it really a trial?

Both exist, separately. Read on 12 September 2026: Free is $0 forever with 1 user, 3 data sources, 1 dashboard, 10 custom metrics, 50 AI credits a month and daily sync — workable for one person watching a handful of channels. Separately you can trial any paid plan for 14 days without a card. Do not assume the trial lapses quietly into Free: Databox's own FAQ says that if no payment details are added by the end of the trial, your data stops syncing and scheduled reports and notifications are paused.

What will it cost with our actual tool stack?

Count sources, not seats — Databox states it does not charge per seat. Read on 12 September 2026, on annual billing: Analyst $71 a month covers 1 user and 5 sources; Team Core $199 covers 3 users and 10 sources; Team Scale $319 covers 10 users and 30 sources. Going past your source count means moving up a tier rather than paying per source, because the $5.60-per-source line we recorded in August is gone. The exception is Agency, from $79 a month with unlimited users and 20 sources, where client packs add 1 client, 5 sources and 50 credits for $20 a month each.

Will a higher plan make the data fresher?

Barely, and less than it used to. Sync is daily on Free and hourly on every paid plan, so the 15-minute interval that came with the old Growth tier is no longer something a plan buys: it is a $14 per-source add-on. Databox's own troubleshooting documentation also says data sources do not sync independently and only pull when metrics are actively in use, and that upstream provider limits shape the real refresh rate. Upgrading cannot backfill history that was never collected. Read 12 September 2026.

What should I weigh before buying Operations & Workflow software?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.
