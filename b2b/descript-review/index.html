What Descript does

AI-powered video and podcast editor: edit media by editing the text transcript — cut, overdub and remove filler words like editing a doc — with screen recording, AI voice cloning and an AI assistant (Underlord) built in.

Pricing (2026)

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

It's per-editor pricing with two meters running underneath: media hours AND monthly AI credits. Heavy podcast or video volume burns through the caps fast, and the free tier watermarks 720p exports — model your real production hours before picking a tier. The API changes the arithmetic rather than escaping it: their own API page states access is included for paying users at no additional cost, but the work it does draws from the same AI credits and media minutes, so automating a pipeline burns the caps faster, not slower.

It also stops just short of the finish — the same page says direct media file exports are not supported yet, so the export step stays a manual one in the app. (read on their own API page, 2026-08-14)

When the paid plan is worth it

Two meters run underneath the seat price and they settle the tier long before the feature list does: media hours a month, and AI credits. Free gives one hour of media, 720p exports with a watermark and 100 AI credits once rather than monthly. Hobbyist is $16 per person a month billed annually ($24 monthly) for 10 hours and 400 credits with watermark-free 1080p; Creator is $24 ($35) for 30 hours and 800 credits at 4K; Business is $50 ($65) for 40 hours and 1,500 credits.

Read the steps and the ladder shows where it stops being about volume. Hobbyist to Creator triples the hours and doubles the credits for $8 more a month, the best-value step on the list. Creator to Business adds ten hours for roughly double the price, so if footage is the constraint, Business is not the answer to it.

What upgrading does not do is remove the meter. Every paid tier is capped, Business included, so a heavy weekly show can exhaust the top self-serve plan and still be metered. And because API work draws on the same credits and media minutes, automating a pipeline spends the allowance faster rather than escaping it.

How to actually use Descript

The vendor's own workflow has six steps and starts before the footage does: Record, Import or Generate. With no recording at all you can type a script, pick an AI Speaker voice and have the voiceover produced. Whatever comes in is transcribed automatically, and step two is the reason people arrive: delete a word or a sentence in the transcript and the media is cut with it, so rearranging audio or video is cut, copy and paste.

Structure comes next. You break the project into scenes, adding one wherever the visuals should change, and apply layouts such as split-screen or picture-in-picture. For pacing that text editing cannot reach, you drag directly in the wordbar rather than moving to a separate timeline.

The polish pass is where the AI credits go: Underlord for generation, Studio Sound for audio, Eye Contact for gaze. Finish by sharing a hosted version, where viewers leave timestamped comments on the video itself, or by exporting locally to MP4, MP3, WAV or GIF.

Distilled from Descript help centre: "Get started with Descript", plus the getting-started, script-editing and export-and-share sections of help.descript.com and the Descript pricing page, read 21 August 2026.

Best for: podcasters and video creators who want to edit by editing the transcript — cutting, overdubbing and stripping filler words as easily as editing a doc, and teams who want those same edits triggered from code or from an AI assistant via Descript’s API and MCP connector rather than from the app.
Who should skip it: if you need frame-precise, effects-heavy editing (a traditional NLE fits better) or your monthly volume blows past the media-hour caps.

Frequently asked questions

What does Descript cost?

Per person per month: $16 on Hobbyist, $24 on Creator and $50 on Business when billed annually, or $24, $35 and $65 billed monthly. Annual saves up to 35%. There is a free plan with 60 minutes a month and watermarked exports. Read on 17 August 2026.

Which tier do I need for a weekly podcast?

Count hours, not features: the tiers cap transcription and editing at roughly 10, 30 and 40 media hours a month. A weekly hour-long show with raw multitrack footage will pass Hobbyist's allowance quickly even though the editing features would have been enough.

Is the free plan usable for real work?

For trying the text-based editing, yes. For publishing, no — exports are watermarked and capped at 720p, and 60 minutes a month is a single short episode. It is a genuine demo of the editing model rather than a working tier.

Does Descript replace my current setup or sit on top of it?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.

Who should not use Descript?

Skip it if you need frame-precise, effects-heavy editing (a traditional NLE fits better) or your monthly volume blows past the media-hour caps. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
