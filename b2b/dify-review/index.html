What Dify does

Leading open-source platform for building agentic AI workflows and LLM apps. Visual builder, RAG pipelines and model-agnostic — ship AI features without reinventing the plumbing.

Pricing (2026)

Genuinely open-source and free to self-host (you pay only infrastructure plus your own LLM API bills); the managed cloud adds a free Sandbox, Professional about $59/mo and Team around $159/mo (2026). LLM inference always bills separately from your provider. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

'Free' self-hosting isn't free — you own the servers, updates and reliability, and LLM tokens cost extra on every plan. It's powerful, but you need some technical comfort to run and maintain it well.

What buyers report about Dify

Recurring themes from Product Hunt and practitioner reviews, read in August 2026. We have not run Dify ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • The visual workflow builder genuinely bridges no-code and pro-code, with RAG, agents, model management and observability in one place.
  • • Self-hosting is free under Apache 2.0 and removes every usage cap. For a regulated industry, owning the code changes the compliance conversation entirely.
What they complain about
  • • Self-hosting means you own the infrastructure. Without engineering capacity that is a burden, not a saving.
  • • SSO and access control are passable rather than polished — a problem at larger organisations.
  • • Documentation lags behind the features, and reviewers ask repeatedly for more of it.
  • • Cloud plans cap out: free Sandbox has 200 message credits, Professional $59/month for 5,000, Team $159 for 10,000. Growing teams get pushed to a custom quote.
Best for: technical teams that want to build and ship LLM apps and agents on an open-source platform they can self-host and control.

Frequently asked questions

Is Dify secure and reliable enough to depend on?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.

Who should not use Dify?

Skip it if you want a no-maintenance, non-technical builder — a fully managed no-code AI tool saves you the infra and upkeep. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
