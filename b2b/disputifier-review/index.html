What Disputifier does

Chargeback automation for Shopify: submits dispute evidence for you, buys chargeback alerts that refund incoming disputes before they land, and screens orders for fraud and late shipments. Free to install; recovery costs 20% of every won chargeback (capped at $250 a win), alerts run $17-$27.50 each and the two prevention add-ons $0.05 an order.

The honest knock

Free to install is doing a lot of work here. All four services bill per event — a fifth of each recovery, $17 to $27.50 an alert, five cents an order twice over — so the cost lands after the fact and scales with exactly the thing you are trying to reduce. Model your worst chargeback month before you switch alerts on, not your average one. The reviews deserve a straight look too. The 4.5 average across 84 reviews is not a middle: 88% are five stars and 11% are one star, with almost nothing in between. The most recent one-star, from June 2026, describes a security incident at the vendor and a dispute over unpaid invoices and billing descriptors; Disputifier replied in July that it does not block merchants from moving providers and disputes the account of the charges. We have not verified either side and are not taking one. What the pattern does show is where the friction sits: merchants either rate this perfect or fall out with it over invoices, not over whether disputes were won. And know what alerts cannot reach. Coverage depends on the shopper's bank and card: smaller issuers do not participate, Amex and Discover frequently cannot be set up, Klarna alerts do not exist yet and PayPal alerts are recent. The prevention rate the vendor quotes is a ceiling for a well-covered card mix, not a promise for yours.

When the paid plan is worth it

There are no tiers here, so the question is not which plan but which of the four services to switch on — and each has its own break-even. Recovery is the straightforward one: 20% of what they win, capped at $250, and nothing when they lose. On a $50 dispute you pay $10; on a $2,000 dispute the cap means $250 rather than $400. It only costs you money on chargebacks you would have won yourself, so the honest comparison is not against zero but against your current in-house win rate. The vendor's own claim is that it wins around 60% of disputes; if you already win about that, you are paying a fifth of your recoveries for the hours back, not for extra revenue. Alerts run the other way, and they are the line to watch. At $17 to $27.50 an alert you pay per intercepted dispute whether or not you would have won it, and the alert resolves by refunding the shopper — so you lose the order revenue as well as the fee. That trade works when the alternative is a chargeback you would probably lose plus a processor fee plus a hit to your dispute ratio, and it works badly on high-margin orders you would have won. The vendor says alerts stop 60-80% of incoming chargebacks, reaching 95% with the right processor and customer mix. The two $0.05-per-order add-ons, fraud screening and late-shipment notifications, are the cheapest and by far the most predictable: on 10,000 orders a month they are $500 each. Unlike the other two they charge on every order rather than on every problem, which is the only part of this bill you can forecast.

How to actually use Disputifier

Start with recovery only, and start by measuring your own win rate over the last three months — 20% of recoveries is worth paying only against what you were actually winning before. Leave alerts off for the first month: that is the line item that runs away, and its value depends entirely on which banks and card types your shoppers use, so ask which of your processors and cards are actually covered before enabling them. If you do turn alerts on, set a threshold above which disputes are fought instead of auto-refunded — their portal supports it, and without it your largest orders get refunded automatically. Use the 7-day risk-free trial to check the integration with your processor (Stripe, PayPal, Klarna, Authorize.net, Afterpay, Recharge) rather than to judge results; a week is not a dispute cycle. And keep your own record of every invoice and descriptor change from day one, because billing is where the negative reviews cluster. Frequently asked questions

Is Disputifier safe to run my finances through?

The bar here is higher than features. Check who actually holds the money and under what licence, whether client funds are segregated, what audit trail and permission controls you get, and how support behaves when something goes wrong mid-transaction. Anything touching payments should be able to answer those in writing; if it cannot, that is your answer.

Who should not use Disputifier?

Skip it if your dispute volume is low or lumpy, you need a predictable monthly bill, or your margins are high enough that auto-refunding an alerted order costs more than losing the occasional dispute. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
