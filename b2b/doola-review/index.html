doola Review (2026)

Financial OperationsResearched assessment · by Daniel HaketUpdated 2026-09-02

Formation is the cheap part; the recurring bookkeeping and tax tiers are where the real cost sits, and state fees are extra on top of doola's price. Convenient especially for non-US founders, but a local accountant may be cheaper once you're established.

Best for: founders (often non-US) who want US LLC/C-Corp formation, EIN and compliance handled in one place without the paperwork.
Who should skip it: if you're a US resident comfortable filing formation yourself — the state fee plus a cheap registered agent costs far less than the bundle.

Frequently asked questions

What does it cost to form a US company with doola?

$297 for the first year on Starter, plus your state's filing fee, which runs between $50 and $500 depending on the state. That covers formation, EIN, registered agent and a US address, and renews at $300 a year. Read on 17 August 2026.

What are the bigger packages for?

Tax and Compliance at $1,999 a year and Business-in-a-Box at $2,999 add filings, compliance and bookkeeping support rather than more formation. Both were discounted 25% when we read the page on 24 August 2026 — to $1,499 and $2,249, part of an up-to-$750-off sale that doola dates to 31 August — so check whether that offer is still running before working from the list price.

What is not included?

State fees, always, and they are the part that varies most by where you incorporate. Budget $50 to $500 on top of whichever package you take, and note that the renewal ($300 on Starter) is slightly above the first-year price rather than below it.

Is doola safe to run my finances through?

doola isn't a bank and it doesn't hold or move your money. It forms the entity, acts as registered agent and keeps the filings and books straight; where it helps you open a US business account, the account and the balance sit with the partner bank, not with doola. So judge it on filing accuracy and accountability rather than custody: who is registered agent of record, what happens — and who pays — if a filing or return is late or wrong, how your EIN and identity documents are stored, and whether you can move your entity and records elsewhere without a fight. Get those answers in writing before you commit to a multi-year bundle.
