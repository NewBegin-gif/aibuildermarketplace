What Dripify does

Cloud-based LinkedIn outreach automation — drip sequences of connection requests, messages, InMails and profile views, with configurable daily limits and a built-in email finder. From $39 per user/month billed annually, 7-day free trial without a card.

Pricing (2026)

(2026-07-24, read on their own pricing page) Four tiers, priced per user: Basic $39, Pro $59 and Advanced $79 per user per month billed annually, plus an Enterprise tier that is quote-only. A 7-day free trial runs without a credit card. Those are the annual-billing rates. Month to month the same tiers read $59, $79 and $99 per user (both columns read in a live browser session on 2026-07-24), so the 'up to 35% off' the toggle advertises is real but front-loaded — you pay a year up front to get it, and the discount shrinks as you go up the range: about 34 percent on Basic, 25 on Pro, 20 on Advanced. What each tier buys is unusually consequential here: Basic gets you one drip campaign and what the page calls 'limited daily quotas'; unlimited campaigns and full quotas start at Pro; Advanced adds multi-team management and the safety feature Dripify itself recommends. Every tier includes 100 email-finder credits a month. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Understand what you are buying before the price matters: LinkedIn prohibits this entire product category. Its User Agreement bans using 'bots or other unauthorized automated methods' to 'send or redirect messages', and its prohibited-software page says members using such tools 'risk having their accounts restricted or shut down'. There is no carve-out for cloud tools or low volumes, and Dripify is not an outlier here — every competitor sits on the same side of that line. Dripify's own documentation is candid about the consequence, warning that LinkedIn 'may issue warnings, temporary restrictions, or even permanent bans', and it publishes a recovery procedure for exactly that scenario. Two things follow. First, the advertised ceiling is not a working speed: the app allows up to 75 connection requests a day, while Dripify's own setup guide tells beginners to run 10 to 15, and brand-new accounts 2 to 5, building up 10 to 20 percent a week. Anyone buying on the headline number is buying capacity they are told not to use. Second, Activity Control — the feature Dripify names as its best protection, which adjusts your pace automatically — sits on the $79 Advanced tier. On Basic and Pro, safe pacing is your own manual responsibility. And when a vendor writes that a feature 'makes sure your LinkedIn account won't get flagged', read that as a claim about a third party's enforcement decisions that no vendor can actually guarantee.

What they praise

  • • Reviewers running modest volumes report campaigns working as described.
What they complain about
  • • Account restriction risk is the headline. Independent testing across 50 LinkedIn accounts found a 23% restriction rate within 90 days, and after LinkedIn's May 2026 enforcement wave user reports moved into the 27-34% range. Weigh that against what your LinkedIn profile is worth to you.
  • • Billing continuing after cancellation, bugs in the billing module and confusing auto-renewal all recur.
  • • No email integration, so multi-channel sequencing is out.
  • • Outreach messages read generic unless you do the personalisation work yourself, and support is slow.

When the paid plan is worth it

Basic at $39 is a trial-in-disguise: one drip campaign and quotas the page itself calls limited. If you run more than a single sequence, Pro at $59 is the real entry point — that is where unlimited campaigns, full daily quotas, a dedicated inbox, CSV export and webhooks live. The jump to Advanced at $79 buys one thing that matters more than the rest of the feature list: Activity Control, which Dripify's own safety guide tells you to switch on, and which adjusts your daily connecting and messaging limits based on your account history. If your LinkedIn account carries real professional value, that $20 gap is cheap insurance for the category's main risk. Two things no tier fixes: pricing is per user, so a team of five pays five times, and LinkedIn's own weekly invitation ceiling still applies no matter which plan you are on — as Dripify puts it, no automation can overcome the limits set by LinkedIn.

How to actually use Dripify

Setup is genuinely light: Dripify runs in the cloud, so there is no download and no Chrome extension for the core product — you connect your LinkedIn account and campaigns keep running with your browser closed. The friction is not technical, it is behavioural. Set your daily limits before your first campaign, not after: Settings → Limits & Activity has a slider per action type and a range dropdown that randomises volume, so a setting of 60 with range 5 sends between 55 and 65. Start at 10 to 15 connection requests a day, or 2 to 5 on a brand-new account, and raise by 10 to 20 percent a week — that is Dripify's own guidance, not ours. Two administrative traps worth knowing up front, both from the vendor's documentation: removing a team member from your workspace does not stop billing for that seat, which has to go through support, and a downgrade only takes effect at the end of your paid term while an upgrade bills immediately. Reviewers across several platforms report unexpected renewals and difficulty cancelling; the help centre documents how to buy a plan but has no article on cancelling one, so put your renewal date in your calendar yourself. Distilled from Dripify's own help centre (daily limits, safety algorithm, account and billing articles) plus LinkedIn's User Agreement and prohibited-software pages

Best for: sales teams that already run LinkedIn outreach, understand that automation breaches LinkedIn's terms, and accept that risk with their eyes open — the cloud architecture means sequences run with your browser closed and no extension injected into your session.
Who should skip it: if anyone whose LinkedIn account is not expendable, since a restriction can be permanent and no tool can promise otherwise; or anyone tempted by the 75-a-day ceiling, because the vendor's own advice is roughly a fifth of that.

Frequently asked questions

Does Dripify replace my CRM or plug into it?

Most tools in this category plug in rather than replace. Check which direction the data flows and whether the sync is genuinely two-way, because a one-way push creates a second source of truth that your reps will quietly stop trusting. If it does aim to replace your CRM, price the migration honestly: history, custom fields and reporting rarely move across cleanly.
