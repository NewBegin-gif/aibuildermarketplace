What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Starter already carries unlimited orders, unlimited products and automatic fulfilment — the whole product, in other words. Professional at $99 adds live monthly training, priority support and a success manager; Elite at $197 adds monthly one-on-one coaching on top. You are buying people, not capability: nothing above Starter makes the coffee ship faster or cost less per bag. The annual rate is the move worth noticing in the other direction, since $240 a year against $30 a month is a third off the same tier.

Beyond that the model is narrow by design — one product category, roasted and shipped from their own US facilities in 3-5 business days, with no route to source elsewhere or negotiate the per-order cost.

What Dripshipper does

Private-label coffee and tea dropshipping for Shopify: they roast, bag and ship from their own US facilities within 3-5 business days, with your brand on the packaging. Starter $30 a month or $240 a year, Professional $99, Elite $197, all with a 14-day trial and per-order product and shipping cost on top.

What buyers report about Dripshipper

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Dripshipper ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • The fulfilment side is what merchants keep crediting: roasted to order, bagged, labelled and shipped without the store owner touching it, with orders flowing through automatically once the connection is made.
  • • Product quality carries the positive reviews — several merchants say their own customers compliment the coffee unprompted, which is the part a dropshipper cannot fix later.
  • • White-labelling is the reason people stay: your bag, your brand, someone else's roastery, and enough of it automated that the merchant's time goes into marketing instead of packing.
What they complain about
  • • Margin is the recurring complaint and it is arithmetic, not opinion. Merchants put the landed cost of a 12oz bag at about $14: one calculates that $19 retail leaves $5 (August 2022), another says a 76% markup was needed to clear anything once the subscription was counted (January 2026), and a third simply calls the pricing uncompetitive (November 2025).
  • • Support timing is the second theme, across years rather than in one bad week: 24-hour replies during a store launch (January 2026), a scheduled Zoom the vendor did not attend after a $99 setup package was paid (May 2025), and a deaf merchant who was given no interpreter for a call (May 2025).
  • • A no-refund, no-exchange policy on product means a customer complaint is yours to absorb, and one merchant describes an order that fell out of the system over a holiday and support asking them to recreate the paid order — which duplicated the charge notification to their customer and corrupted their books.

Where the ratings disagree. The split in this record is between people testing the idea and people running it. Short-tenure reviewers rate the app; multi-year merchants rate the supply chain, and that is where the criticism lives — one reviewer with over a year on the platform sums it up as paying a monthly fee to sit as a third party with no direct line to the supplier, and eventually went to roasters directly. Nothing above the entry tier changes any of that: the higher plans buy coaching and a success manager, not cheaper coffee. So price one bag end to end, including the subscription, before you decide the category works for you at all.

When the paid plan is worth it

The one clear saving here is on the entry plan and it is larger than the usual annual discount. Starter is $30 a month, or $240 for a year, which is $20 a month -- a third off rather than the ten or twenty per cent most vendors offer. If you are going to run a private-label coffee store for a year at all, that is $120 saved for a decision you were making anyway.

The steps above it are steep: Professional at $99 is more than three times Starter, and Elite at $197 is double again. What those tiers add is not stated on the App Store listing we read, and no annual rate is shown for either -- so ask what changes and whether the annual discount extends upward before you move. Every plan carries a 14-day free trial, which is the right way to find out.

As with any dropshipping app, the subscription is the smaller line: product and shipping cost is charged per order on top, billed in USD and recurring every 30 days. Work out your per-order margin before the plan price matters at all.

How to actually use Dripshipper

Start on Starter and use the 14-day trial on the coffee rather than on the app: order your own product with your own label and time the 3-5 business day claim from your own address. Work the numbers on a single bag before you list a range. Per-order product and shipping cost sits on top of the $30, and coffee is heavy, so shipping eats a larger share of the margin here than in most dropshipping.

If the model holds, move Starter to the annual $240 instead of moving up the ladder — Professional and Elite buy training and a success manager, not better fulfilment. And have the label design finished before launch, since the private-label packaging is the only thing separating your store from every other Dripshipper store.
