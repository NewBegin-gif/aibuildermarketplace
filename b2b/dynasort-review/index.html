Dynasort Review (2026)

E-commerceResearched assessment · by Daniel HaketUpdated 2026-09-20

Sorting driven by 40+ blended signals is powerful and opaque in the same breath: a recipe that ranks by margin, velocity and stock at once will sometimes bury a product you wanted seen, and there is real trial and error in getting the recipes to match how you actually merchandise.

Best for: Shopify stores with large or fast-changing catalogues where product order is a merchandising decision — seasonal ranges, frequent stock-outs, wide margin spreads — and nobody who wants to re-order collections by hand every week..
Who should skip it: if your catalogue is small enough for one person to curate in an afternoon, the order barely changes, or you are not on Shopify — at $99 a month for the first paid tier, the sorting has to be earning its fee.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The jump from free to paid is steep at $99 a month, and it buys capacity without capability — custom attributes, partner integration and product boost all start at $249. The paid cards also publish no serve allowance, so the meter the free tier is capped on (10,000 serves a month) disappears from the page exactly where you start paying. And the evidence base is thin: 4.6 from 15 reviews, with fourteen fives and a single one-star and nothing in between.

It is Shopify-only, so it is not an option if any part of your catalogue lives elsewhere.

What Dynasort does

Shopify app that re-sorts collections, variant lists, search results and upsell blocks automatically, using recipes built from 60+ signals such as sales, margin, inventory and reviews. Free Demo tier; paid $99 to $499 a month.

Pro is the feature step, Enterprise is the volume step

Read the three paid cards as two different purchases rather than as one ladder. Basic at $99 a month buys capacity and nothing else: 25 sorted collections, 10 managed, 20 recipes, a 30-minute re-sort, with custom attributes, partner integration and product boost all switched off. Pro at $249 is where the product turns on rather than where it gets bigger — those three lines flip to yes, while the capacity gain is modest at 100 sorted collections, 25 managed and 25 recipes.

Enterprise at $499 is the opposite kind of step: it adds no capability the Pro card does not already have, and instead multiplies volume, with 1,000 sorted collections, 100 managed, 250 recipes and a re-sort every 10 minutes. So buy Pro for what the app can do and Enterprise for how much of your catalogue it does it to. What no tier fixes is the work: the recipes still have to be built and watched, a sort blended from several signals will still bury products you wanted seen, and the paid cards publish no serve allowance at all, so the meter the free tier is capped on is not on the page at any price.

How to actually use Dynasort

Use the free Demo tier properly rather than as a preview: it sorts 2 collections around the clock, which is exactly enough for a controlled comparison. Pick your best-selling collection and one you suspect is badly ordered, record the conversion rate and revenue per visit for both before you change anything, and let a single recipe run for a full two weeks. Start with one clear signal — push sold-out products down, or sort by sell-through — before you blend margin and inventory into it, because a recipe built from six signals at once is one you cannot reason about when a product disappears from view.

Watch which products drop as much as which ones rise; that is where automated merchandising goes wrong. Then ask what serve allowance comes with the tier you are buying, because only the free Demo card publishes one and the paid cards print none, and take the full 30-day trial to see a real sales cycle.

Frequently asked questions

What does Dynasort cost?

Read on 15 September 2026 on its Shopify App Store listing: a free Demo tier, then Basic $99 a month, Pro $249 and Enterprise $499. All charges are in USD and recur every 30 days, and every paid tier carries a 30-day free trial. The Demo tier is free for as long as you like and is a working plan rather than a countdown: 2 collections sorted round the clock, 10 recipes, 2 managed collections, 10,000 serves a month and a re-sort every 60 minutes.

Which tier do I actually need?

It depends on whether you are buying features or volume. Custom attributes, partner integration and product boost are all off on Basic and on from Pro, so if you need any of those the answer is $249 regardless of how small your catalogue is. If you need none of them, Basic's 25 sorted collections and 20 recipes are the question, and Enterprise at $499 adds no capability over Pro at all — it multiplies capacity to 1,000 sorted collections, 250 recipes and a 10-minute re-sort.

How many serves do I get on a paid plan?

The listing does not say. Only the free Demo card publishes a serve allowance (10,000 a month); the three paid cards describe collections, recipes and the processing schedule but print no serve number. Since serves are what a busy store consumes fastest, get that figure from the developer in writing before you commit, and use the 30-day trial to measure your own volume.

What should I weigh before buying E-commerce software?

Check your platform and version specifically. Shopify support is close to universal in this category, while WooCommerce, BigCommerce, Magento and headless setups vary a lot, and 'supported' sometimes means a generic API rather than a maintained app. A listing in your own platform's app store, with recent reviews there, is a better signal than the vendor's logo wall.
