What EasyDMARC does

DMARC, SPF and DKIM monitoring — stop others sending email as your domain.

Pricing (2026)

(2026-08-01, read on their own pricing page) Plus €44.99/month (€35.99/month billed annually), 2 domains, 100,000–1,000,000 messages/month, 3 months of report history, 1 invited user. Premium €89.99/month (€71.99 annually), 4 domains, up to 5,000,000 messages/month, 1 year of history, unlimited users, a customer-success manager on yearly billing only. Enterprise quote-only with unlimited domains and 3 years of history. A free plan exists but the pricing page shows only the €0 without listing what it includes. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

The honest knock

You are buying domains and history, not features. Plus covers two domains; a company with a main domain, a marketing subdomain and a legacy domain it forgot about is already over. And the history window is the part that bites later: three months on Plus means that when you are investigating a spoofing campaign that started in the spring, the reports are gone. The page also does not say what happens when you pass the message volume — their own FAQ does, and the answer is that you lose access to your reports and dashboard rather than being billed for overage, which is a harsher failure mode than an overage charge and worth knowing before you size the plan.

Best for: anyone who sends email from their own domain and has not yet got DMARC to enforcement — the reporting is the part that makes that safe rather than guesswork.
Who should skip it: if you have one domain, send little email, and are willing to read raw XML reports yourself, because the free tier plus patience covers the same ground.

The natural comparison is Valimail or dmarcian — the direct DMARC-platform peers — compare on domains included and history retention, not on the feature list. Decide by which one fits the job above, not by the louder brand.

Frequently asked questions

Is EasyDMARC secure and reliable enough to depend on?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.

Who should not use EasyDMARC?

Skip it if you have one domain, send little email, and are willing to read raw XML reports yourself, because the free tier plus patience covers the same ground. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
