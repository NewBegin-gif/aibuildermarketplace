What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Plus covers two domains. Subdomains do not count toward that, the page says they are detected automatically, but a company with a main domain, a second brand and a legacy domain it forgot about is already over. The history window is the part that bites later: three months on Plus means that when you are investigating a spoofing campaign that started in the spring, the reports are gone. And watch the volume ceiling.

Read on 4 October 2026, the page's own tooltip says that once you pass your monthly quota, additional reports are not processed until the next billing cycle. There is no overage charge, just a gap in the data, and it opens precisely when a spoofing run inflates your report volume. Size the plan on your busiest month, not your average.

When the paid plan is worth it

Read the comparison table rather than the feature bullets, because what you are buying is domains, history and message volume, and since the page was rebuilt (read 4 October 2026) not seats: every tier, Plus included, now lists unlimited users. Plus at €44.99 a month (€35.99 billed annually) carries 2 domains, 3 months of history and a volume selector from 100,000 to 1,000,000 messages a month. Premium at €89.99 (€71.99 annually) carries 4 domains, 1 year of history and up to 5,000,000 messages, plus EasySPF, proactive DMARC alerts and AI agents that read the reports, with a dedicated customer-success manager on yearly billing only.

Above that, the old single Enterprise tier is now two quote-only plans: Deliverability adds DNS management, sender insights, inbox-placement tests and up to 3 years of history, and Enterprise: Email Trust adds threat intelligence plus SSO, API access and audit logs. If single sign-on is a procurement requirement, neither Plus nor Premium meets it. What no upgrade does is shorten the DNS work: SPF, DKIM and DMARC records still have to be published and moved to enforcement by you or your provider, and a higher tier only buys more history and volume to watch that happen.

How to actually use EasyDMARC

The unpaid route is genuinely useful first. Their free record checkers and generators — DMARC, SPF, DKIM, BIMI, MX and a domain scanner — let you see what your domain publishes today and generate a syntactically valid DMARC record before you sign up for anything. From there the pattern is the standard one: publish a DMARC TXT record at p=none with the reporting address pointing at the platform, let aggregate reports arrive, identify every legitimate sender they reveal, and only then tighten the policy.

Two managed options exist to keep you out of DNS afterwards. Managed DMARC routes the record through a single CNAME so policy changes happen in the dashboard rather than at your registrar, and EasySPF flattens SPF dynamically to survive the ten-lookup limit after a one-time DNS change. The pricing page no longer lists a free plan, but Plus and Premium both open with a free trial and no card. With three months of history on Plus, start collecting reports well before you need to look back at them.

Frequently asked questions

What sets my EasyDMARC tier — domains or email volume?

Both, and the volume selector is the one buyers miss. Read on easydmarc.com/pricing on 4 October 2026: the headline rate on each card is the floor for its smallest volume bracket, and each card carries its own selector on top. Plus runs from 100,000 through 500,000 to 1,000,000 messages a month; Premium adds a 5,000,000 step; the two quoted plans above it take custom volume. So 44.99 a month on Plus (35.99 billed annually) buys 2 domains and three months of report history at the smallest bracket, and 89.99 on Premium (71.99 annually) buys 4 domains, a year of history, EasySPF, proactive alerts and AI agents that read the reports. Users are unlimited on both, where on 17 September Plus allowed one invited user, so team size no longer pushes you up a tier. Annual billing is marked as 20% off plus bonus features.

What happens if I go over my monthly email volume?

You lose data rather than money. Read on 4 October 2026, the quota tooltip on the pricing page says that once you pass your monthly quota, additional reports are not processed until the next billing cycle. There is no overage rate on the page, so nothing extra is billed; the reports for the rest of that month simply never reach the dashboard. On 17 September the FAQ described it more harshly, as losing access to reports and dashboard until you upgrade. That FAQ is no longer on the page, so the tooltip is the current statement. Either way the risk lands in the situation you bought the tool for: a spoofing campaign that spikes your report volume is exactly the month the counter runs out. Size the plan on your busiest month.

I have more than two domains — what does that cost?

The page still will not give you a per-domain price. Read 4 October 2026: Plus covers 2 domains, Premium 4, and the two quoted plans, Deliverability and Enterprise: Email Trust, go up to unlimited. There is no published per-domain add-on rate, so a third domain means Premium and a fifth means a sales conversation. One rule works in your favour: subdomains are detected automatically and do not count toward the limit, so a marketing subdomain is not a second domain. Count your separately registered domains instead, including the parked and legacy ones, because those are exactly the domains an attacker will spoof and the reason the reporting exists. Data history scales with the same ladder: 3 months on Plus, 1 year on Premium, up to 3 years on the quoted plans.

What should I weigh before buying Security software?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.
