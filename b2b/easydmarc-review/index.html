Plus is $US 44.99 a month, or $US 35.99 billed annually, for 2 domains, three months of report history and one invited user. Premium is $US 89.99, or $US 71.99 annually, for 4 domains, a year of history, TLS reports and a dedicated CS manager on the yearly plan. Enterprise is quoted and carries three years of history, unlimited users and managed services.

Annual billing is marked as 20% off plus bonus features, and MSPs and MSSPs get a separate tab with their own ladder. The number that actually sets your tier is monthly email volume: each card has its own selector, running 100,000 to 1,000,000 messages on Plus and up to 5,000,000 on Premium, so the headline is the floor for the smallest bracket.

We first read these as euro amounts from the Netherlands on 1 Aug; the figures do not convert — €44.99 there is $44.99 here. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Plus covers two domains; a company with a main domain, a marketing subdomain and a legacy domain it forgot about is already over. And the history window is the part that bites later: three months on Plus means that when you are investigating a spoofing campaign that started in the spring, the reports are gone. The page also does not say what happens when you pass the message volume. Their own FAQ does: you lose access to your reports and dashboard rather than being billed for overage.

That is a harsher failure mode than an overage charge, and worth knowing before you size the plan.

When the paid plan is worth it

Read the comparison table rather than the feature bullets, because what you are buying is domains, history and message volume. Free carries 1 domain, 1,000 messages a month, 14 days of history and 1 invited user. Plus at €44.99 a month (€35.99 billed annually) takes that to 2 domains, 3 months of history and still 1 invited user. Premium at €89.99 (€71.99 annually) is the first tier with room for a team: 4 domains, 1 year of history and unlimited invited users.

Enterprise is quote-only for up to unlimited domains, 3 years of history and the managed pieces. So upgrading to Plus does not give you a second person in the tool — that gap closes only at Premium, and the dedicated customer-success manager it adds is stated as yearly billing only. Nor does any upgrade shorten the DNS work: SPF, DKIM and DMARC records still have to be published and moved to enforcement by you or your provider. Prices are exclusive of taxes.

How to actually use EasyDMARC

The unpaid route is genuinely useful first. Their free record checkers and generators — DMARC, SPF, DKIM, BIMI, MX and a domain scanner — let you see what your domain publishes today and generate a syntactically valid DMARC record before you sign up for anything. From there the pattern is the standard one: publish a DMARC TXT record at p=none with the reporting address pointing at the platform, let aggregate reports arrive, identify every legitimate sender they reveal, and only then tighten the policy.

Two managed options exist to keep you out of DNS afterwards. Managed DMARC routes the record through a single CNAME so policy changes happen in the dashboard rather than at your registrar, and EasySPF flattens SPF dynamically to survive the ten-lookup limit after a one-time DNS change. The free tier's 14-day history is the constraint while you are still learning what your senders are.
