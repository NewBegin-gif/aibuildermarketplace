What EcomXray does

Shows cost price, gross margin, current stock and net units sold over 30, 90 and 365 days for every Shopify product, with restock advice from real sales velocity. The analytics are free; Pro at $29 a month adds in-place product editing, SEO fields, an activity log and a Chrome extension.

The honest knock

The split between the two tiers is unusually clean, and worth seeing clearly: everything this app is described as doing, the margins, the net units, the restock advice, is free. The $29 buys editing, not insight. If you are happy making changes in the Shopify admin you already have, the free plan is the whole product and the paid one is a convenience layer over it.

The track record is the real caveat. The app launched on 20 July 2026 and has no reviews at all yet. Nothing is known about how it behaves at scale, how it handles a catalogue in the tens of thousands, or how quickly support answers, because nobody has said so in public.

And its numbers are only as good as your cost prices. Gross margin comes out of Shopify's own cost-per-item field, so if that field is empty or stale across your catalogue, the margin column is decoration. Fill the cost prices in first, then judge the app.

When the paid plan is worth it

The split between the two tiers is unusually clean, and once you see it the decision takes about a minute. Everything this app is sold on — cost price and gross margin, net units sold over 30, 90 and 365 days with refunds, cancellations and test orders stripped out, 30-day restock recommendations and a revenue rank for your top products — is on the FREE plan. The $29 a month buys editing, not insight: changing product name, description and price in place, SEO fields with automatic URL redirects, publishing to channels, managing collections, a full product activity log, and a Chrome extension that shows stock value and lets you edit from your own storefront pages.

So the question is not whether the analysis is worth paying for. It is whether you would rather make the changes here than in the Shopify admin you already have open. For a merchant working through a long restock list that is a real saving of clicks; for one who edits a handful of products a week it is $348 a year for a convenience layer.

Two things to settle before either plan. The margin column is only as good as your cost prices, because it comes straight out of Shopify's own cost-per-item field — if that is empty or stale across your catalogue, the numbers are decoration, and filling it in is work you do before the app helps. And the track record is thin: the app launched on 20 July 2026 and has no public reviews at all, so nothing is known about how it behaves on a catalogue in the tens of thousands or how fast support answers. The free plan costs nothing to find out with, which is the sensible way round here. Pro carries a 14-day free trial and there is no annual rate on the listing; charges are in USD and recur every 30 days.

How to actually use EcomXray

This is a Shopify app, so the install is the Shopify one: Install from the listing, approve the data access, and the app appears in your admin. There is a free plan and a 14-day trial on Pro, so nothing forces a payment decision on day one.

The step that actually decides whether the app is useful happens before you open it. Gross margin is computed from Shopify's own cost-per-item field, which is empty by default on most catalogues. If you install first and look second, you will see a margin column full of blanks and conclude the app does not work. Export your products, fill in cost per item, re-import, and only then judge the numbers.

After that the reading order is the one the listing implies: revenue rank to find your top products, net units over 30, 90 and 365 days to see the trend with refunds and test orders already excluded, and the 30-day restock recommendation as the action. On Pro the Chrome extension puts stock value and inline editing on your own storefront pages, which is the piece worth trying during the trial — it is the part that cannot be replicated in the admin.
