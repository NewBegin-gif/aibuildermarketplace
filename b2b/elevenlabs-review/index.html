Full pricing notes (5 more paragraphs)

And credits are shared across every product, so speech, music, dubbing and video all draw down the same monthly balance. On a paid plan unused credits roll over for up to two months, the balance caps at three times the monthly quota, but downgrading or cancelling forfeits whatever is left at the end of the cycle, and the free plan has no rollover at all.

Pay-as-you-go top-up credits sit outside that rollover cap, and upgrading carries unused credits into the next cycle. The FAQ now lists what the shared pool costs per product: text to speech 1 credit per character, speech to text 330 credits a minute, music 900 a minute, voice changer and isolator 1,000 a minute, sound effects 200 per generation, and dubbing 2,000 to 10,000 credits a minute depending on watermark and Studio.

When the paid plan is worth it

The credit discount stops working about halfway up, which changes where the sensible stopping point is. Per thousand credits: Starter is $6 for 30k (20 cents), Creator $22 for about 121k (18.2 cents), Pro $99 for about 600k (16.5 cents) and Scale $299 for about 1.8M (16.6 cents). Pro and Scale cost effectively the same per credit, Scale is a fraction dearer. So the volume discount runs from Starter to Pro and then flattens entirely; above Pro you are buying capacity and seats, not a better rate.

The step that actually gates something is the first one. Free gives 10k credits a month but the commercial licence starts at Starter, so if the audio is going anywhere near a customer, $6 is the real floor regardless of volume. Above that: Pro adds 44.1kHz PCM output, Scale adds three workspace seats, and Business at $990 adds low-latency text-to-speech, which matters only if you are generating speech inside a live interaction.

Overage is metered per minute and it does taper: roughly $0.36 down to $0.17 depending on tier. That taper is worth checking against your own overrun pattern, because on the cheaper tiers a habit of running over can cost more than the next plan up. And note that Creator advertises 50% off the first month ($11), which is a discount on month one only, budget the $22.

    How to actually use ElevenLabs

    Run your hardest paragraph through the free tier before you pay for anything. Not your cleanest sentence: the one with the product names, the acronyms, the surname nobody pronounces right and the number that could be read three ways. Voice realism here leads the market, and the place it still stumbles is exactly that material. While you do it, count your retakes, because that is the number that prices this product.

    Credits are the meter, and you are charged per generation request rather than per download. The FAQ does allow a limited number of free regenerations, but only while the content and certain settings stay unchanged, and the page tells you before you press the button whether this one is free: change a word and you pay again. Divide the credits you actually spent by the finished minutes you kept, and you have a real credits-per-minute figure rather than the one the calculator assumes.

    Read the free tier's licence before you publish anything. Ten thousand credits a month costs nothing but carries no commercial licence, so anything that goes out commercially needs at least the $6 Starter: this is the step people miss, and it is a licensing problem rather than a quality one. When you do upgrade, note that Creator's first month is half price at $11. Testing free, then taking a discounted Creator month, is a cheaper route to finding your real usage than jumping to Pro on an estimate.

    Test on the newest model. On 28 September 2026 ElevenLabs launched Eleven v4 and a low-latency Eleven v4 Turbo, available in ElevenCreative, ElevenAgents and the API. The launch post says both support more than 90 languages, follow inline audio tags such as [laughs] or a described delivery more closely than earlier models, handle IPA pronunciations more reliably, and can make an instant voice clone from 10 seconds of audio. Those are the vendor's claims, and the hard paragraph above is the way to check them on your own material.

    How many minutes of audio does the $22 Creator plan actually give me?

    It depends which product you spend the credits on, because they all draw on one pool. Read 23 September 2026, Creator's 121,000 monthly credits come out at about 121 minutes of text to speech in the plan table, since speech costs 1 credit per character. The same FAQ prices speech to text at 330 credits a minute, music at 900, the voice changer and isolator at 1,000, and dubbing at 2,000 credits a minute for automatic dubbing with a watermark up to 10,000 for Dubbing Studio without one. So 121,000 credits is roughly 134 minutes of music or 12 to 60 minutes of dubbing. And every retake is a new generation charge unless the page tells you beforehand that this one is free, so budget below the headline minutes.

    If I buy a bigger plan and use it slowly, do I lose the unused credits?

    Not straight away. Read 23 September 2026, the pricing FAQ says unused credits on a paid plan roll over for up to two months, so your balance can reach at most three times the monthly quota, as long as the subscription stays active. The catch is on the way down: downgrading or cancelling keeps the plan until the end of the cycle, then drops you to Free and expires every unused paid credit. The free plan has no rollover. Upgrading is the safe direction, since unused credits carry into the next cycle, and pay-as-you-go top-up credits are kept outside the rollover cap. So start on the smaller plan and move up; a big plan you later step down from loses whatever it banked.

    Is paying yearly worth it for ElevenLabs?

    Only if you are sure of the tier. Read 23 September 2026, annual billing is ten months for twelve (the FAQ writes it as monthly price times 10), which is $5 a month for Starter, $18.33 for Creator, $82.50 for Pro, $249.17 for Scale and $825 for Business. The saving is real, but the credit rules punish guessing high: stepping down forfeits unused paid credits at the end of the cycle. Creator's first month at $11 is also a monthly-plan offer. A cheaper way to find your level is a month or two on monthly billing, counting credits per finished minute, then switching the tier you actually use to yearly. Running over is priced in the plan table at about $0.20 per extra minute on Starter, $0.18 on Creator and $0.17 from Pro upward. Until 1 December 2026 the sums change for two tiers: the Black Friday offer (read 23 November 2026) takes up to 55% off the first year of the Creator and Pro annual plans, with Creator at $99 for the year instead of $220. That discount covers the first year only, so the forfeit rule above still decides whether Creator is the right tier.

    Can I use audio from the free ElevenLabs plan commercially?

    Not on the strength of the pricing page. Read 30 October 2026, elevenlabs.io/pricing lists the Commercial License as something Starter adds on top of Free, at $6 a month, so the Free plan's 10k monthly credits are a test budget rather than a cheaper licence for client or monetised work. The legal scope of the licence and the rights to specific voices are set in ElevenLabs' terms, which this review has not assessed.
