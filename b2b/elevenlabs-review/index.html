Every rate held for a second month. Free $0 with 10k credits/mo; Starter $6/mo (30k credits, commercial license); Creator $22/mo — first month 50% off at $11 — with ~121k credits. Then Pro $99/mo (~600k credits, 44.1kHz PCM), Scale $299/mo (~1.8M credits, 3 workspace seats) and Business $990/mo (~6M credits, 10 seats). Extra usage runs roughly $0.36 down to $0.17 per additional minute depending on tier.

Two things the plan cards do not put next to the price. Annual billing is sold as ten months rather than twelve — the FAQ writes it out as annual price = monthly price × 10, which works out to $5 for Starter, $18.33 for Creator, $82.50 for Pro, $249.17 for Scale and $825 for Business. And credits are shared across every product, so speech, music, dubbing and video all draw down the same monthly balance.

On a paid plan unused credits roll over for up to two months — the balance caps at three times the monthly quota — but downgrading or cancelling forfeits whatever is left at the end of the cycle, and the free plan has no rollover at all. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

When the paid plan is worth it

The credit discount stops working about halfway up, which changes where the sensible stopping point is. Per thousand credits: Starter is $6 for 30k (20 cents), Creator $22 for about 121k (18.2 cents), Pro $99 for about 600k (16.5 cents) and Scale $299 for about 1.8M (16.6 cents). Pro and Scale cost effectively the same per credit -- Scale is a fraction dearer. So the volume discount runs from Starter to Pro and then flattens entirely; above Pro you are buying capacity and seats, not a better rate.

The step that actually gates something is the first one. Free gives 10k credits a month but the commercial licence starts at Starter, so if the audio is going anywhere near a customer, $6 is the real floor regardless of volume. Above that: Pro adds 44.1kHz PCM output, Scale adds three workspace seats, and Business at $990 adds low-latency text-to-speech, which matters only if you are generating speech inside a live interaction.

Overage is metered per minute and it does taper: roughly $0.36 down to $0.17 depending on tier. That taper is worth checking against your own overrun pattern, because on the cheaper tiers a habit of running over can cost more than the next plan up. And note that Creator advertises 50% off the first month ($11), which is a discount on month one only -- budget the $22.

How to actually use ElevenLabs

Run your hardest paragraph through the free tier before you pay for anything. Not your cleanest sentence — the one with the product names, the acronyms, the surname nobody pronounces right and the number that could be read three ways. Voice realism here genuinely leads the market, and the place it still stumbles is exactly that material. While you do it, count your retakes, because that is the number that prices this product.

Credits are the meter, and you are charged per generation request rather than per download. The FAQ does allow a limited number of free regenerations, but only while the content and certain settings stay unchanged, and the page tells you before you press the button whether this one is free — change a word and you pay again. Divide the credits you actually spent by the finished minutes you kept, and you have a real credits-per-minute figure rather than the one the calculator assumes.

Read the free tier's licence before you publish anything. Ten thousand credits a month costs nothing but carries no commercial licence, so anything that goes out commercially needs at least the $6 Starter — this is the step people miss, and it is a licensing problem rather than a quality one. When you do upgrade, note that Creator's first month is half price at $11. Testing free, then taking a discounted Creator month, is a cheaper route to finding your real usage than jumping to Pro on an estimate.
