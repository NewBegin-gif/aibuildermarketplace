When the paid plan is worth it

The unusual thing here is that credits never expire, and that single fact should decide how you buy. Pay-as-you-go runs 1,000 checks for $5 (half a cent each), 5,000 for $17, 10,000 for $27, 25,000 for $54, 50,000 for $98 and 100,000 for $186 -- which is $0.00186 a check, about 63% cheaper per check than the smallest bundle. Because nothing expires, buying the biggest bundle you can justify beats buying small repeatedly.

A monthly subscription saves between 6% and 10% against pay-as-you-go, and the discount grows with volume rather than sitting flat: 5,000 credits cost $16 a month against $17 bought outright, while 100,000 cost $168 against $186. A subscription also bills every month whether you verify a list or not, and its credits reset at the end of each billing cycle — EmailListVerify support confirmed on 24 September 2026 that unused subscription credits are lost — while a pay-as-you-go balance waits until you need it.

For anyone cleaning lists in bursts, before a campaign or after an import, that single-digit discount is cheap insurance to skip; for steady weekly volume it is free money. Decide on your rhythm, not on the percentage. One rate to note separately: the email finder costs 5 credits per address found when you supply a name and 10 when you supply only a domain -- five to ten times the price of verifying one, charged only on a high- or medium-confidence result.

Budget those two apart. The API is included at every level, and the free validator, inbox-placement test and DMARC/SPF generators cost nothing at all.

How to actually use EmailListVerify

Estimate your volume for the year, not the month, because credits never expire. That single fact removes the usual risk of buying too big, and the spread is severe: 1,000 credits at $5 is $0.005 a check, while 50,000 at $98 is $0.00196. Buying fifty thousand credits a thousand at a time would cost $250 for the same work — so if you will get through a large pack within a sensible horizon, buy it now and save well over half.

Test with the free validator first. There is a free email validator, an inbox-placement test and DMARC and SPF generators on the site, and running a sample of addresses you already know the status of tells you whether the verdicts match reality before you commit credits to a list. Budget the email finder separately. It consumes five credits per address found when you give it a name, and ten when you give it only a domain, so a 1,000-credit pack finds at most two hundred addresses rather than a thousand — and only a hundred from bare domains.

You are charged only for high- or medium-confidence hits, but the distinction still catches people out halfway through a prospecting run. And fix your authentication while you are there. The DMARC and SPF generators are free, and a clean list sent from an unauthenticated domain still lands in spam — verification improves your list, not your reputation.

Frequently asked questions

The page says a subscription saves 10% over pay-as-you-go. Does it?

Not at any volume they print. Work the two columns against each other and the saving runs from 5.9% to 9.7%: 5,000 credits cost $17 one-off against $16 a month, 10,000 $27 against $25, 25,000 $54 against $49, 50,000 $98 against $89, and 100,000 $186 against $168. That last one, $18 off $186, is the closest to the promise and still short of it. The 10% is a headline, not a rate, and the real discount grows with volume rather than being flat — so at the small end you are paying a subscription for a saving of one dollar.

Do my credits disappear at the end of the month?

Only on the monthly subscription. The FAQ says pay-as-you-go credits do not expire and keep working until you use them up, and every pay-as-you-go entry in their pricing data repeats the phrase. The pricing page itself says nothing about leftover subscription credits, so we asked EmailListVerify support, and on 24 September 2026 they answered plainly: monthly subscription credits reset every billing cycle, and whatever you have not used in that month is lost. So if you buy the $168 subscription for 100,000 a month and use 60,000, the other 40,000 are gone. That is the real price of the subscription discount — it only pays if you reliably use the allowance every month.

I want to find addresses, not just check them. What does that cost?

It comes out of the same balance, which is convenient and easy to underestimate. The FAQ says you can split credits between verification and the email finder as you like. What a lookup costs depends on what you give it: EmailListVerify support confirmed on 24 September 2026 that a search costs 5 credits when you supply a first name, a last name or both, and 10 credits when you supply only the domain — and that you are charged only when it returns a result at high or medium confidence, so a search that finds nothing costs nothing. At the smallest $5 pack that is about 2.5 cents per found address with a name and 5 cents with a domain only; at the 100,000-a-month subscription rate about 0.8 and 1.7 cents. There is no separate finder allowance to budget — a heavy finding week eats the list-cleaning credits you bought it for.

Tools in this class typically ship native connectors for the big CMS, CRM and email platforms and fall back on Zapier, webhooks or an API for everything else. The practical question is whether your stack is on the native list or needs glue, because a missing native connector normally means either manual work or a paid middleware layer.
