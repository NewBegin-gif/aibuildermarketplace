What EmailListVerify does

Email verification with never-expiring pay-as-you-go credits — bulk list cleaning, real-time API, inbox-placement testing and free DMARC/SPF tools, plus a credit-based email finder.

Pricing (2026)

(2026) Pay-as-you-go credits that never expire: 1,000 for $5 ($0.005/check), 5,000 for $17, 10,000 for $27, 25,000 for $54, 50,000 for $98 ($0.00196/check). A monthly subscription saves ~10% vs pay-as-you-go; API included. The email finder costs 5 credits per found address. Free tools: email validator, inbox-placement test, DMARC/SPF generators. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The $5 entry pack is the most expensive way to buy: $0.005 per check versus $0.00196 at the 50k tier — a 2.5x spread that rewards guessing your volume upfront. And the email finder quietly consumes 5 credits per found address, so a 1,000-credit pack finds 200 emails, not 1,000.

What they praise
  • • Credits never expire, unlike competitors that void them after twelve months. For a team that buys in bulk for occasional projects that removes the whole cost pressure.
  • • It undercuts the category badly — around $24 per 10,000 addresses against ZeroBounce at $75 and NeverBounce at $50. 4.5 on G2, 4.7 on Trustpilot.
What they complain about
  • • The advertised 97% accuracy is not what independent comparisons measure — real-world results land closer to 91% depending on the list. Catch-all domains are where it misclassifies, and that is exactly where enterprise lists live.
  • • Reviewers describe the accuracy tiers as disingenuous: cheaper verification produces cheaper results.
  • • At least one reviewer reports a refund refused and an error unacknowledged.

When the paid plan is worth it

The unusual thing here is that credits never expire, and that single fact should decide how you buy. Pay-as-you-go runs 1,000 checks for $5 (half a cent each), 5,000 for $17, 10,000 for $27, 25,000 for $54 and 50,000 for $98 -- which is $0.00196 a check, about 61% cheaper per check than the smallest bundle. Because nothing expires, buying the biggest bundle you can justify is simply cheaper than buying small repeatedly, with no risk of wasting the balance. A monthly subscription saves roughly 10% against pay-as-you-go. Weigh that against what you give up: a subscription bills every month whether you verify a list or not, while a pay-as-you-go balance sits there until you need it. For anyone cleaning lists in bursts -- before a campaign, after an import, at a quarterly tidy -- the 10% is cheap insurance to skip. For steady weekly volume it is free money. Decide on your rhythm, not on the percentage. One rate to note separately: the email finder costs 5 credits per address found, so finding an address is five times the price of verifying one. Budget those two activities apart. The API is included at every level, and the free tools -- validator, inbox-placement test, DMARC and SPF generators -- cost nothing at all.
