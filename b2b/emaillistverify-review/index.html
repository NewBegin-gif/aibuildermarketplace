When the paid plan is worth it

The unusual thing here is that credits never expire, and that single fact should decide how you buy. Pay-as-you-go runs 1,000 checks for $5 (half a cent each), 5,000 for $17, 10,000 for $27, 25,000 for $54, 50,000 for $98 and 100,000 for $186 -- which is $0.00186 a check, about 63% cheaper per check than the smallest bundle. Because nothing expires, buying the biggest bundle you can justify beats buying small repeatedly.

A monthly subscription saves between 6% and 10% against pay-as-you-go, and the discount grows with volume rather than sitting flat: 5,000 credits cost $16 a month against $17 bought outright, while 100,000 cost $168 against $186. A subscription also bills every month whether you verify a list or not, while a pay-as-you-go balance waits until you need it. For anyone cleaning lists in bursts, before a campaign or after an import, that single-digit discount is cheap insurance to skip; for steady weekly volume it is free money.

Decide on your rhythm, not on the percentage. One rate to note separately: the email finder costs 5 credits per address found -- five times the price of verifying one. Budget those two apart. The API is included at every level, and the free validator, inbox-placement test and DMARC/SPF generators cost nothing at all.

How to actually use EmailListVerify

Estimate your volume for the year, not the month, because credits never expire. That single fact removes the usual risk of buying too big, and the spread is severe: 1,000 credits at $5 is $0.005 a check, while 50,000 at $98 is $0.00196. Buying fifty thousand credits a thousand at a time would cost $250 for the same work — so if you will get through a large pack within a sensible horizon, buy it now and save well over half.

Test with the free validator first. There is a free email validator, an inbox-placement test and DMARC and SPF generators on the site, and running a sample of addresses you already know the status of tells you whether the verdicts match reality before you commit credits to a list. Budget the email finder separately. It consumes five credits per address found, so a 1,000-credit pack finds two hundred addresses rather than a thousand — a distinction that catches people out halfway through a prospecting run.

And fix your authentication while you are there. The DMARC and SPF generators are free, and a clean list sent from an unauthenticated domain still lands in spam — verification improves your list, not your reputation.
