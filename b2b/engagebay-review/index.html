What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The meter is contacts AND seats at once: prices are per user, and each tier caps contacts — Basic’s 500 contacts fills fast, and the Growth jump is steep ($15 → $65 per user). The advertised ‘starts at $12.74’ assumes biennial prepay — two years upfront for the friendly number. Real automation only starts at Growth.

Recurring themes from G2, Capterra and Tekpon, read in August 2026. We have not run EngageBay ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise

What they complain about

When the paid plan is worth it

EngageBay's free plan covers a single early-stage user managing up to 250 contacts and sending up to 1,000 branded emails a month, with basic forms and one landing page - enough to validate a small pipeline before paying anything. The push to Basic ($14.99 per user per month) comes fast: free tops out at 250 contacts, and Basic only lifts that to 500, so a team running any real outreach volume outgrows it within weeks. Growth ($64.99 per user per month) becomes worth it mainly for automation depth - 10 workflows with up to 15 nodes each - rather than for more contacts, since 5,000 contacts is a jump most small teams will not need right away. What upgrading does not fix is the per-user pricing itself: every tier is priced per seat, so adding sales or marketing staff multiplies the bill directly, meaning headcount growth compounds cost independently of contact volume or automation needs.

How to actually use EngageBay

Getting first value from EngageBay starts with setting up the account, then either importing an existing contact list by CSV, with fields mapped during import, or connecting a mailbox such as Gmail or Outlook to sync existing contacts. Before any marketing email will reliably land in inboxes, the sending domain needs DKIM and SPF verification: EngageBay generates the records under Account Settings, but adding them as DNS TXT records happens at the domain registrar, outside EngageBay, and confirmation depends on DNS propagation there. This is a common stalling point because it requires registrar access rather than just an EngageBay login, and it cannot be rushed from within the product. A second practical snag is that a bulk contact import can immediately exceed the free plan's 250-contact and 1,000-email caps, which silently blocks further sends until the list is trimmed or the account is upgraded. Automation workflows only become useful once contacts and at least one integration are already connected. Frequently asked questions

Does EngageBay replace my CRM or plug into it?

Most tools in this category plug in rather than replace. Check which direction the data flows and whether the sync is genuinely two-way, because a one-way push creates a second source of truth that your reps will quietly stop trusting. If it does aim to replace your CRM, price the migration honestly: history, custom fields and reporting rarely move across cleanly.

Who should not use EngageBay?

Skip it if your contact list is big (per-tier caps push you up fast) or you need best-of-breed depth in one area — all-in-one means good-at-everything, not best-at-anything. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
