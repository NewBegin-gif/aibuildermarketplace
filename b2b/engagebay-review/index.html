When the paid plan is worth it

EngageBay's free plan covers a single early-stage user managing up to 250 contacts and sending up to 1,000 branded emails a month, with basic forms and one landing page - enough to validate a small pipeline before paying anything. The push to Basic ($14.99 per user per month) comes fast: free tops out at 250 contacts, and Basic only lifts that to 500, so a team running any real outreach volume outgrows it within weeks.

Growth ($64.99 per user per month) becomes worth it mainly for automation depth - 10 workflows with up to 15 nodes each - rather than for more contacts, since 5,000 contacts is a jump most small teams will not need right away. What upgrading does not fix is the per-user pricing itself. Every tier is priced per seat, so adding sales or marketing staff multiplies the bill directly — headcount growth compounds cost independently of contact volume or automation needs.

How to actually use EngageBay

Getting first value from EngageBay starts with setting up the account. Then either import an existing contact list by CSV, mapping fields during import, or connect a mailbox such as Gmail or Outlook to sync existing contacts. Before any marketing email will reliably land in inboxes, the sending domain needs DKIM and SPF verification. EngageBay generates the records under Account Settings, but adding them as DNS TXT records happens at the domain registrar, outside EngageBay, and confirmation depends on DNS propagation there.

This is a common stalling point because it requires registrar access rather than just an EngageBay login, and it cannot be rushed from within the product. A second practical snag is that a bulk contact import can immediately exceed the free plan's 250-contact and 1,000-email caps, which silently blocks further sends until the list is trimmed or the account is upgraded. Automation workflows only become useful once contacts and at least one integration are already connected.

Frequently asked questions

What does EngageBay actually cost per user?

Three All-in-One tiers on monthly billing, read on engagebay.com/pricing on 18 September 2026: Basic $14.99, Growth $64.99 and Pro $119.99 per user per month, on top of a free plan at $0. The page opens on a biennial ‘15% off’ view ($12.74, $55.24, $101.99) with a yearly step in between ($13.79, $59.79, $110.39), so the number you see first is the one that asks you to prepay two years. Buying a single bay instead of the suite starts at $11.04 per user per month on that same biennial display.

How many contacts do I get, and what happens when I fill the tier?

Contacts are the real meter and they are capped on every tier: 250 on Free, 500 on Basic, 5,000 on Growth and 10,000 on Pro. That top figure is worth checking against anything older you read, including us — our August entry recorded 50,000 on Pro, and on 18 September 2026 the plan card reads 10,000. Past that there is no published price at all: the page says to schedule a call to discuss expanding your contact limit. So the highest number EngageBay publishes is now 10,000 contacts, at $119.99 per user per month.

Is the free plan a real plan or a trial?

A real plan, with a size limit rather than a clock. It is $0 for 250 contacts and carries email marketing, CRM, helpdesk and live chat, and the card states it is free for teams of up to 15 members (read 18 September 2026). The catch is not time but capability: marketing automation first appears on Growth at $64.99 per user per month, four tiers up in price from Basic. If automation is the reason you are shopping, the free plan will show you the CRM but not the thing you came for.

What should I weigh before buying Sales & CRM software?

Most tools in this category plug in rather than replace. Check which direction the data flows and whether the sync is genuinely two-way, because a one-way push creates a second source of truth that your reps will quietly stop trusting. If it does aim to replace your CRM, price the migration honestly: history, custom fields and reporting rarely move across cleanly.
