What Envoy does

Workplace platform for visitor sign-in, desk and room booking, deliveries and emergency notifications.

Pricing (2026)

(2026-08-01, read on their own pricing page) Five products, each metered differently and all billed annually. Visitors: Basic free, Premium $362 per location per month, Enterprise quote-only. Emergency Notifications $2 per user per month. Reservations $5 per bookable resource per month. Screens $12 per device per month. Deliveries $250 per delivery location per month. Every product section carries the line 'Price excludes platform fee', and the page never says what that platform fee is. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

The honest knock

None of the prices on that page is a price you can budget from, because every one of them is followed by 'Price excludes platform fee' and the fee has no number anywhere. That is the first thing to ask for. The second is how the meters stack, because they are all in different units: per location, per user, per bookable resource, per device, per delivery location. An office with forty bookable desks, six screens and one delivery point is paying on four clocks at once — $362 for the location, $200 for the desks, $72 for the screens and $250 for deliveries, which is $884 a month before the platform fee and before emergency notifications at $2 a head. Price the combination you actually want, not the product you came for.

What buyers report about Envoy

Recurring themes from G2, Capterra and Reddit, read in August 2026. We have not run Envoy ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • 4.7 on G2 — sign-in, pre-registration and automated host notifications work, and the branded front desk looks professional.
  • • Quick to set up with a modern interface that receptionists and visitors both understand.
What they complain about
  • • Modular pricing stacks unpredictably, and it is per location. On top of the platform fee: Reservations $60 per resource a year, Visitors Premium $4,344 per location, Deliveries $3,000, Screens $144 per device, Emergency Notifications $24 per user.
  • • SSO, block-list screening, guest Wi-Fi and access control are all gated to Premium or Enterprise.
  • • Room and desk booking is treated as a secondary layer rather than a first-class feature, and the rules engine is shallow.
  • • Standard runs $109 per location a month annually, Premium from roughly $362. Multi-location businesses feel that immediately.
Best for: companies with a real office and a front desk problem — visitor compliance, desk booking and deliveries in one system, where the per-location price is spread over enough employees to be worth it.
Who should skip it: if you are remote-first or have one small office — a visitor log and a shared calendar cover the same ground, and the per-location floor is priced for buildings rather than teams.

The natural comparison is Robin or OfficeSpace — the direct workplace-platform peers — compare the total across every module you would switch on, and get all three to name their platform fee. Decide by which one fits the job above, not by the louder brand.

Frequently asked questions

Is Envoy secure and reliable enough to depend on?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.

Who should not use Envoy?

Skip it if you are remote-first or have one small office — a visitor log and a shared calendar cover the same ground, and the per-location floor is priced for buildings rather than teams. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
