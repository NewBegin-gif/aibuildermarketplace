Visitors: Basic free, Premium $362 per location per month, Enterprise quote-only. Emergency Notifications $2 per user per month. Reservations $5 per bookable resource per month. Screens $12 per device per month. Deliveries $250 per delivery location per month. What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Four of the five product cards are followed by 'Price excludes platform fee', and the platform page says outright that the platform is required to use Envoy products and is charged as a flat annual fee — with its own Premium and Enterprise tiers to pick first, and no number published anywhere. That is the first thing to ask for. The second is how the meters stack, because they are all in different units: per location, per user, per bookable resource, per device, per delivery location.

An office with forty bookable desks, six screens and one delivery point is paying on four clocks at once: $362 for the location, $200 for the desks, $72 for the screens and $250 for deliveries. That is $884 a month before the platform fee, and before emergency notifications at $2 a head. Price the combination you actually want, not the product you came for.

When the paid plan is worth it

Before comparing any of these numbers to a competitor, know that none of them is a complete price. Four of the five product cards carry the line “Price excludes platform fee”, and the platform page settles the rest: the platform is required to use Envoy products, and visitor management is itself a module running on it. That page calls the fee a flat annual one and prints no figure — but Envoy's own llms.txt does print it: Core Platform at $4,000 a year and Premium Platform at $8,000 a year, both flat.

The two sources name different ladders, Premium and Enterprise on the page against Core and Premium in the file, so confirm which one your quote assumes. Published and billed annually: Visitors is free on Basic, $362 per location per month on Premium and quote-only on Enterprise; Emergency Notifications $2 per user; Reservations $5 per bookable resource; Screens $12 per device; Deliveries $250 per delivery location. Moving up the platform tier buys administration, analytics and integrations.

It does not lower a single module rate, and it does not merge the meters: locations, users, desks, screens and delivery points keep ticking separately whatever the platform costs.

How to actually use Envoy

Ask what the platform fee is before anything else — but walk in already knowing the number. Envoy's platform page says the platform is required and is priced as a flat annual fee without printing an amount; its llms.txt prints two: Core Platform at $4,000 a year and Premium Platform at $8,000. The page and the file name different tiers, so ask which ladder the quote uses. Then count your meters, because they come in five units: locations for Visitors at $362 a month each, users for Emergency Notifications at $2, bookable resources for Reservations at $5, devices for Screens at $12, and delivery locations for Deliveries at $250.

An office with forty desks, six screens and one delivery point runs four clocks at once and lands near $884 a month before the platform fee. Everything is billed annually, so ask what happens if your counts change mid-term.
