What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Four of the five product cards are followed by 'Price excludes platform fee', and the platform page says outright that the platform is required to use Envoy products and is charged as a flat annual fee: with its own Premium and Enterprise tiers to pick first, and no number published anywhere on the site. That is the first thing to ask for. The second is how the meters stack, because they are all in different units: per location, per user, per bookable resource, per device, per delivery location.

An office with forty bookable desks, six screens and one delivery point is paying on four clocks at once: $362 for the location, $200 for the desks, $250 for deliveries, and $180 for the screens rather than the $72 the card implies, because Envoy's own llms.txt sets a 15-device minimum that the pricing page never mentions. That is $992 a month before the platform fee, and before emergency notifications, which the same file bills at $2 a head across your entire employee directory rather than across the people who use them. Price the combination you actually want, not the product you came for.

When the paid plan is worth it

Before comparing any of these numbers to a competitor, know that none of them is a complete price. Four of the five product cards carry the line “Price excludes platform fee”, and the platform page settles the rest: the platform is required to use Envoy products, and visitor management is itself a module running on it. That page calls the fee a flat annual one and prints no figure, but Envoy's own llms.txt does print it: Core Platform at $4,000 a year and Premium Platform at $8,000 a year, both flat.

The two sources name different ladders, Premium and Enterprise on the page against Core and Premium in the file, so confirm which one your quote assumes. Published and billed annually: Visitors is free on Basic, $362 per location per month on Premium and quote-only on Enterprise; Emergency Notifications $2 per user; Reservations $5 per bookable resource; Screens $12 per device; Deliveries $250 per delivery location. Moving up the platform tier buys administration, analytics and integrations.

It does not lower a single module rate, and it does not merge the meters: locations, users, desks, screens and delivery points keep ticking separately whatever the platform costs.

How to actually use Envoy

Ask what the platform fee is before anything else, but walk in already knowing the number. Envoy's platform page says the platform is required and is priced as a flat annual fee without printing an amount; its llms.txt prints two: Core Platform at $4,000 a year and Premium Platform at $8,000. The page and the file name different tiers, so ask which ladder the quote uses. Then count your meters, because they come in five units: locations for Visitors at $362 a month, users for Emergency Notifications at $2, bookable resources for Reservations at $5, devices for Screens at $12 and delivery locations for Deliveries at $250.

Two carry fine print that only the llms.txt prints: Screens has a 15-device minimum, and Emergency Notifications is billed on your full employee directory, not on the people who use it. An office with forty desks, six screens and one delivery point therefore pays for fifteen screens regardless, landing near $992 a month before the platform fee. Everything is billed annually, so ask what happens if your counts change mid-term.

Distilled from Envoy's pricing page, its workplace-management-platform page and its llms.txt, read 3 September 2026; all three re-read 4 October 2026 (served HTML): module rates, the JSON-LD (billingDuration P1Y), the four platform-fee lines, the "flat annual fee" and "required" answers on the platform page, and the llms.txt figures unchanged; the file also gives annual module figures ($60, $24, $144 and $2,999).

The natural comparison is Robin or OfficeSpace: the direct workplace-platform peers: compare the total across every module you would switch on, and get all three to name their platform fee.

Frequently asked questions

What will Envoy actually cost us per year?

Add a platform fee to every module rate you see. Read on 16 September and again on 4 October 2026, envoy.com/pricing publishes Visitors at free on Basic (capped at 100 entries a month), $362 per location per month on Premium and custom on Enterprise, with Emergency Notifications at $2 per user, Reservations at $5 per bookable resource, Screens at $12 per device and Deliveries at $250 per delivery location: all billed annually, and four of those five cards print 'Price excludes platform fee'. The platform page says the fee is mandatory and flat-annual but prints no figure; envoy.com/llms.txt, read the same day, does: Core Platform $4,000 a year, Premium Platform $8,000 a year. Budget the fee first and the modules second.

Is the Enterprise plan really quote-only?

The pricing page says custom, but Envoy's own machine-readable file is less coy. Read on 16 September and 4 October 2026, envoy.com/llms.txt puts Visitors Enterprise at roughly $9,700 per location per year, about $808 a month, and it also names a Standard tier at roughly $1,440 a location per year ($120 a month) that the pricing page does not show at all. The page's ladder is Basic, Premium, Enterprise; the file's is Standard, Premium, Enterprise. If a $120 tier fits you better than the $362 one, you will not find it by reading the page, so ask for it by name and ask which ladder the quote is built on.

What is capped or has a minimum that the price does not show?

Four things, and three of them only appear away from the plan cards. Basic Visitors stops at 100 entries a month. Enterprise includes ID scanning at 500 scans per license per year, with paid add-ons above that. Screens is quoted at $12 a device with no floor on the card, but envoy.com/llms.txt (read 16 September and 4 October 2026) states a 15-device minimum, so a six-screen office pays for fifteen: $180 a month rather than $72. And Emergency Notifications, priced at $2 per user, is billed in that same file on your full employee directory rather than on the people who use it, which makes it a headcount charge and not a usage one.
