What Expandi does

LinkedIn and email outreach automation for sales teams and agencies — smart limits, warm-up and multi-account management.

Pricing (2026)

(2026) 7-day free trial, no free plan. Business $99/mo month-to-month, or $79/mo on annual billing (2 months free). Higher tiers add role and permission management and centralized billing; pricing scales with seats. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

There is no free tier and no cheap entry — Expandi starts at $99 a month per seat, and the friendlier $79 assumes annual commitment. It's also LinkedIn automation, which sits in a grey zone against LinkedIn's own terms: powerful for outreach, but a platform-risk you own.

What buyers report about Expandi

Recurring themes from G2, Capterra and practitioner write-ups, read in August 2026. We have not run Expandi ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Dedicated residential IPs, randomised human-like delays, configurable daily caps and an automatic pause when LinkedIn issues a security challenge — the safety engineering is real and more than most competitors do.
  • • Reviewers running careful, low-volume campaigns report it working as advertised.
What they complain about
  • • Bans still happen. 67% of users report account restrictions, and reviewers describe LinkedIn accounts banned within 30 to 90 days with little recourse — one G2 reviewer lost a ten-year-old account with 8,000+ connections. Weigh that against the value of your own profile before you start.
  • • $99 per seat per month, or $79 annual — and the personalisation everyone wants costs more on top: Hyperise $69, Sendspark $49.
  • • Reviewers report being billed for more seats than they bought, with no resolution, and cancellation not always stopping the charges.
  • • The interface takes longer to learn than competitors'.

When the paid plan is worth it

Expandi has no free plan. The only unpaid option is a 7-day free trial, which the vendor says gives access to all features without limitations, so the decision is really $99 per month against $79 per month on annual billing, which the pricing page frames as 20% off, or two months free. Price is per seat, and one seat covers one LinkedIn account at a time, so cost scales with the number of profiles you run rather than with volume. The arithmetic is simple: a seat has to return more than $99 a month in pipeline from a single LinkedIn identity. Agency pricing is quote-only and aimed at 10+ seats. What paying more does not fix is the ceiling. Sending rate is set by LinkedIn, not by your plan. Expandi's own gradual warm-up starts a new account at 5 actions a day and tops out at 21, and the same article notes LinkedIn enforces a weekly cap of 100 connection requests. A second seat buys a second account, not a faster one. Image and GIF personalization and video personalization are billed on top of the subscription.

How to actually use Expandi

The shortest route is the 7-day trial: connect your LinkedIn account, then check it is fully synced before doing anything else. Expandi's onboarding article says an account showing a 'Resolve issues' button, or 'temp_name' instead of the real name, is not fully connected, and automation will not work until it is. Where people lose time is going too fast. For a brand-new profile the vendor tells you to complete the profile, make 20-30 manual connection requests to people you know, publish a post, and let the account sit for at least 3-5 days before automating. Gradual warm-up then schedules 5 actions on day one and adds 3 every two days up to 21, so week one is deliberately small, and toggling warm-up off and on resets that progress. Set active times to 5 days a week with at least 2 inactive days, start with a Connector campaign, and note that 2FA is required for mobile connector campaigns and for Sales Navigator or Recruiter lead lists. Frequently asked questions

Does Expandi replace my CRM or plug into it?

Most tools in this category plug in rather than replace. Check which direction the data flows and whether the sync is genuinely two-way, because a one-way push creates a second source of truth that your reps will quietly stop trusting. If it does aim to replace your CRM, price the migration honestly: history, custom fields and reporting rarely move across cleanly.

Who should not use Expandi?

Skip it if you send occasional connection requests (LinkedIn's native tools suffice), or you're uneasy about automation against LinkedIn's terms of service. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
