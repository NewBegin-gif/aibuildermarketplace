What ExpertEx does

A unified AI studio: chat across multiple models (GPT-4, Gemini), generate images, video and voice, and reuse prompts — multilingual, all in one workspace.

Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 22 Aug 2026

There are four credit tiers, monthly against yearly with two months free. Starter is $10 or $7 for 1,000 credits, and Premium $30 or $20 for 3,000. Ultra is $75 or $45 for 7,500, and Professional $250 or $149 for 25,000. Paid three-day trials sit alongside them at $1 for Premium (1,000 credits), $2 for Ultra (2,000) and $5 for Professional (5,000), with full access and cancellation before renewal.

The page's own calculator prices the work at 25 credits per product photo, 250 per short video ad and 2,000 per full reel, and reels require Ultra or above. Credits roll over month to month while the subscription is active and are lost on cancellation; you can top up on demand or upgrade mid-month, with upgrades prorated immediately and downgrades taking effect at the next billing date. Prompt Builder is free to use before subscribing. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Starter is sold as 65 product photos and 10 short video ads a month on 1,000 credits; at the calculator's own rates that work costs 4,125 credits, four times the allowance. The card numbers only reconcile if each is taken alone, and at roughly 15 credits a photo and 100 a video. The same 4.2x mismatch repeats on every tier, so it is a systematic difference rather than a typo.

Ask which rate governs before you buy, because the calculator will steer you to a plan two rungs above what the card implies. The second thing to price is that this is a credit product with no free tier. Prompt Builder is free; generating is not. And the cheap trials ($1, $2, $5) are three days rather than a month -- enough to test output quality, not enough to test a monthly workflow.

And the fundamental one: AI product photography is judged on whether the output is publishable without a retoucher. Run your own worst product through the trial -- a reflective bottle, a garment with fine texture -- rather than the kind of thing that looks good in every demo.

When the paid plan is worth it

Read this as a credit ladder, not a feature ladder: every plan includes the same studios and what you buy is volume. Monthly against yearly: Starter is $10 or $7 for 1,000 credits, Premium $30 or $20 for 3,000, Ultra $75 or $45 for 7,500, and Professional $250 or $149 for 25,000. Yearly billing is advertised as two months free, and that holds -- $7 against $10 is 30% off.

Per credit the ladder rewards volume properly. On yearly billing Starter works out at $0.0070 a credit, Premium $0.0067, Ultra $0.0060 and Professional $0.0060. That is a 14% improvement from bottom to top -- real, but not the reason to climb. The actual reason is capability. Reels and Shorts Studio, and the E-commerce Studio that pushes results to your live store, start at Ultra. Full reels cost 2,000 credits and require Ultra or above. If reels are part of the plan, the tier is chosen for you.

Now the arithmetic that decides your tier, and the reason to check it yourself. The calculator on the page charges 25 credits a product photo and 250 a short video ad. At those rates 1,000 credits is forty photos, or four video ads -- while the Starter card advertises 65 photos and 10 video ads. Those two statements cannot both be true, and the same fourfold gap repeats on every tier. Work out your month in the calculator's currency (photos times 25, videos times 250, reels times 2,000), then ask the vendor which rate is billed. The difference between the two readings is the difference between Starter and Ultra.

Two terms that soften the risk of guessing wrong. Credits roll over month to month as long as the subscription is active, so a quiet month is banked rather than lost. Cancelling forfeits the balance, though, which makes the annual plan a commitment of stored value as well as money. And you can top up on demand or upgrade mid-month without interrupting work, with upgrades prorated immediately. So starting a rung low is recoverable; starting a rung high is not.

How to actually use ExpertEx

There is a free way in that is not a trial: Prompt Builder is free to use before subscribing, and it turns a rough idea into a working prompt. Use it first, because it costs nothing and it tells you whether the tool understands your product category at all.

The paid on-ramp is unusual and worth reading carefully: three-day trials at $1 for Premium, $2 for Ultra and $5 for Professional, each carrying real credits (1,000, 2,000 and 5,000) and full access. Three days is enough to judge output quality and not enough to judge a monthly workflow. So plan what you will generate before you start the clock. And note that it renews unless you cancel before the period ends.

The fastest route to something publishable is the store connection rather than one-off generation. The product is built around pasting your store and getting content back. Brand DNA applies your brand's style across everything you make -- one brand on Premium, up to three on Ultra, up to ten profiles on Professional. On Ultra and above the E-commerce Studio pushes results straight to the live store. The catalog reshoot works in batches: 12 SKUs on Premium, 20 on Ultra, 25 across all stores on Professional.

What to test in those three days, in this order. Your hardest product first rather than your easiest. Then a full catalog reshoot batch, to see whether the output is consistent across SKUs. And only then the store push. Consistency across a catalogue is where AI product photography usually falls down, and it is invisible if you generate one image at a time.

The natural comparison is Poe / ChatGPT — single-model or aggregator chat tools. Decide by which one fits the job above, not by the louder brand.
