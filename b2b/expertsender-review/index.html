When the paid plan is worth it

There is no free tier and no self-serve plan, so the decision is not which tier to buy but whether you are above the floor. The vendor's own site puts pricing from around $450 a month, scaling with volume and channels, and states plainly that it targets stores doing at least 30,000 unique monthly visits. Those two numbers belong together: at exactly that traffic, $450 a month is about $0.015 per visit, or $5,400 a year that the platform has to earn back out of email, SMS, push and on-site messaging combined.

That arithmetic is the whole buying decision. Below roughly 30,000 visits a month you are paying an established-store price on starter-store traffic, and no feature list closes that gap — a self-serve, pay-as-you-grow tool is the rational start, and you can move here later. Above it, the case changes shape: the pitch is one unified customer data platform rather than four channel tools stitched together. The comparison to run is against what you currently pay for email plus SMS plus push plus on-site, not against the cheapest email tool alone.

What the price includes that self-serve tools do not is a managed, dedicated-partner setup, and that is worth pricing honestly. It is real value if your team has no one to own deliverability and segmentation, and it is a cost you are paying twice if you already employ that person. The entry point is a free consultation, so the number you should walk in with is your monthly visits and your current combined spend across all four channels.

How to actually use ExpertSender

Check your own traffic against their qualifying line before you book anything. ExpertSender explicitly targets stores doing at least 30,000 unique visits a month, and that is not marketing positioning — below it the platform genuinely cannot pay for itself at a $450 monthly floor. Because there is no self-serve plan, the free consultation is your only look at the product, so treat it as an evaluation rather than a sales call.

Bring three numbers: monthly unique visits, list size, and what your current email and SMS revenue actually is. Ask what the $450 floor includes in send volume and channels, and at what volume the price steps, since it scales with both. Then convert the fee into orders. $450 a month is $5,400 a year; divide that by your average order value and you have the number of extra orders the platform has to generate before it breaks even. Ask them to talk about stores your size specifically, not their flagship case studies.
