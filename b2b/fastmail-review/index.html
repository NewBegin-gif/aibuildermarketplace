What Fastmail does

Private, ad-free email hosting with your own domain. Fast, secure email, calendars and contacts from an independent provider that doesn't mine your data. From 1 August 2026 you pick EU (Amsterdam) or US data residency, encrypted at rest on Fastmail's own servers — note that EU data is initially still replicated and backed up to the US while that infrastructure matures.

Pricing (2026)

(2026) Individual about $5/mo annual; Duo/Family tiers push per-person cost under ~$2.50; business per-user tiers: Basic $4, Standard $6, Professional $10/user/mo (up to 150GB/user). 30-day full trial, no card; multi-year prepay discounts. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

You're paying for what the free giants monetize differently: no ads, no profiling, real support and standards-first email (JMAP/IMAP everywhere). The honest limits: it's email/calendar/contacts, not a Workspace-style office suite, so document collaboration lives elsewhere, and migrating decades of Gmail habits (filters, integrations) takes a weekend of care. For owning your address on your own domain, it's the boring, excellent choice.

Best for: individuals and small businesses that want fast, private, ad-free email on their own domain with proper support and masked-email aliases.
Who should skip it: if your team lives in Google Docs/Drive collaboration (Workspace bundles that), or free mail genuinely serves you and privacy isn't a priority.

The natural comparison is Proton Mail or Google Workspace — Proton leads on E2EE, Workspace on suite breadth — Fastmail wins on speed, standards support and price for pure email. Weigh the honest alternatives on our Gmail alternatives page.

Frequently asked questions

Is Fastmail secure and reliable enough to depend on?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.

Who should not use Fastmail?

Skip it if your team lives in Google Docs/Drive collaboration (Workspace bundles that), or free mail genuinely serves you and privacy isn't a priority. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
