Fastmail Review (2026)

By Daan Haket · Tested hands-on · written with an AI assistant · who does what

You're paying for what the free giants monetize differently: no ads, no profiling, real support and standards-first email (JMAP/IMAP everywhere). Throughout this review we weigh it against Gmail.

Best for

individuals and small businesses that want fast, private, ad-free email on their own domain with proper support and masked-email aliases.

Skip it if

your team lives in Google Docs/Drive collaboration (Workspace bundles that), or free mail genuinely serves you and privacy isn't a priority.

What Fastmail does

Private, ad-free email hosting with your own domain. Fast, secure email, calendars and contacts from an independent provider that doesn't mine your data. From 1 August 2026 you pick EU (Amsterdam) or US data residency, encrypted at rest on Fastmail's own servers — note that EU data is initially still replicated and backed up to the US while that infrastructure matures.

When the paid plan is worth it

Fastmail has no free plan. You get a trial of up to 30 days, so the question is which tier, not whether to pay. On the pricing page as it renders for a European visitor, Individual is €6 per month billed monthly and €5 per month billed yearly, which is €60 for 12 months. Duo covers 2 people at €8 per month yearly, and Family up to 6 people at €11 per month yearly, €132 for 12 months, so once you are past a single mailbox the shared tiers beat separate Individual subscriptions.

The feature rows are identical across the personal plans: custom domains, Masked Email, third-party app access and 60 GB per person. Paying more buys seats, not capabilities. For teams, Basic at €3 per user per month yearly gives 6 GB per user and leaves out custom domains and third-party apps such as Outlook and iPhone Mail, so it only suits people who stay in the web app. Standard at €5 per user per month yearly restores both.

No upgrade lifts the 70 MB ceiling on a single sent email, and the pricing page's own FAQ (read 15 October 2026) is plain about money back: Fastmail does not offer refunds for unused subscriptions and tells you to use the free trial to decide. You can cancel at any time and no further payments are taken. Its terms of service (section 10) leave any refund of prepaid fees to Fastmail's discretion.

How to actually use Fastmail

Sign up for the trial, then go to Settings, Migration, Import and choose Gmail / Google Workspace. You authenticate with Google, pick what to import and click Do it. Fastmail warns that imports take time on large accounts and that you must not start another migration while the first is still running, because that produces 2 copies of every message. Expect folder sprawl as well: every Gmail label becomes a folder, and a message carrying several labels ends up in several folders, though Fastmail states those extra copies do not count against your quota.

You can convert folders to labels afterwards in Settings, Folders. The other snags are documented too. Third-party clients such as Outlook or the iPhone Mail app will not take your normal password; each one needs its own app password. And the trial is deliberately throttled: 120 messages per day, a maximum of five email addresses, and only the first five users active, with SMS verification sometimes requested. Get your custom domain working before the 30 days end.

Distilled from Fastmail pricing page, Fastmail help centre articles and the Fastmail blog, read 5 August 2026; pricing re-read 29 September 2026 at fastmail.com/pricing (served as /pricing/nl/ in euros): all three personal and three business plans on the 1-, 12-, 24- and 36-month terms, storage per plan and the trial of up to 30 days; re-read 15 October 2026 on /pricing/us/ and /pricing/nl/, all terms for the personal and business plans plus the pricing-page FAQ on cancellation and refunds.

The natural comparison is Proton Mail or Google Workspace — Proton leads on E2EE, Workspace on suite breadth — Fastmail wins on speed, standards support and price for pure email. Weigh it against Gmail on the job you actually need done.

What does Fastmail cost?

Read on 15 October 2026 in euros (the US page shows the same numerals in dollars, before tax): Individual is €6 a month monthly, €5 if you prepay a year (€60), €4.75 for two years (€114) and €4.67 for three (€168). Duo is €10 monthly, €8 on a year (€96), €182.40 for two years and €268.80 for three. Family is €14 monthly, €11 on a year (€132), €250.60 for two years and €369.60 for three. Euro prices include tax.

Is the multi-year prepay worth it?

It is where the discount actually lives. Going from monthly to a year saves about 17%; going to three years saves about 22%. But the step from two years to three is worth only about eight cents a month, so the sensible stopping point for most people is the 12- or 24-month prepay rather than the longest one.

Can I get a refund if I stop?

Not for unused time. The FAQ on Fastmail's pricing page says it does not offer refunds for unused subscriptions and tells you to use the free trial of up to 30 days to decide. You can cancel at any time and no further payments are taken, which matters most before you prepay two or three years.

Which alternatives are people weighing it against?

The measured search on our pages is for Fastmail alternatives, and the honest framing is what you are paying for: Fastmail sells a fast, ad-free mail host with your own domain, not an encrypted-by-default suite. If end-to-end encryption is the requirement, the comparison is with Proton Mail rather than with cheaper mailbox hosts; if it is price alone, bundled mailboxes from a web host will undercut it.
