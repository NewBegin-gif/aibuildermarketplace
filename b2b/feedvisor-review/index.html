What actually drives the quote

Feedvisor publishes no price, and we checked that three ways on 17 August 2026. feedvisor.com/pricing returns a 404. Their own llms.txt lists every public page and contains no pricing page at all. And our monthly captures of feedvisor.com/plans from July and August are byte-for-byte identical, with no amount in either. So there is no tier to weigh — the useful question is what moves the number, and the site answers it: pricing is hybrid and varies with SKU count, ad spend and complexity.

That shape matters more than any headline. A hybrid model with an ad-spend component means your bill grows with the thing you are trying to make more efficient. So the percentage and its cap are the two things to get in writing before anything else. Ask what is charged on SKU count, what is charged on spend, and what happens in a peak month. Q4 is where a spend-linked fee bites hardest, and it is the month you can least afford a surprise.

The figures in circulation are not Feedvisor's. Roughly $100 a month for an entry tier, and roughly $1,500 a month for the full Feedvisor360 platform, are numbers we carried before. They come from third parties or from sales conversations, not from the vendor. We repeat them only as a sanity check on what you are offered. What the split does suggest is where the value sits. The algorithmic Buy-Box strategy that holds prices UP rather than racing them down, plus ad management and analysts, is the 360-tier story — and that is enterprise software for serious catalogues.

If your Amazon revenue cannot absorb a fee at that level as a small percentage, a simpler repricer is the rational choice and no demo will change that.

How to actually use Feedvisor

Bring three numbers to the call, because the quote is built from them: SKU count, monthly ad spend and monthly Amazon revenue. Feedvisor publishes no price at all — /pricing returns a 404 and its own llms.txt lists no pricing page — and its own site says pricing is hybrid and varies with catalogue size, ad spend and complexity. There is no way to arrive at a figure without that conversation, so arrive prepared for it.

Ask one specific question early: is the algorithmic Buy-Box strategy included in the tier you are being quoted? That is the part Feedvisor is famous for — the repricing that holds prices up rather than racing them down — and it sits on the full 360 platform rather than the entry on-ramp. A quote for the on-ramp is a quote for a different product. Then sanity-check the figure as a percentage of your Amazon revenue rather than in absolute terms.

Enterprise repricing and ad management are justified as a small share of what they manage; if the quote is not a small share of yours, a simpler repricer is the rational choice and there are several. And treat any figure you have read elsewhere, including ours, as third-party indication rather than a price.

Frequently asked questions

What does Feedvisor cost?

Feedvisor does not say. We checked the pricing URL (404), their own machine-readable site index (no pricing page) and two monthly captures of the plans page (no amounts) on 17 August 2026. Figures circulating elsewhere — around $100 a month at the entry level, around $1,500 a month for the full platform — do not come from Feedvisor, so verify anything you are quoted against your own catalogue rather than against a number you read online.

How does it compare with Informed.co and the cheaper repricers?

The comparison people search for, and the honest split is scale rather than features. Feedvisor's algorithmic repricer optimises Buy Box share against margin in a way rule-based tools cannot, and reviewers above roughly $1M of Amazon revenue say the results justify the fee. Below that, one reviewer found it only marginally better than free Amazon tooling with the fee eating the incremental margin. With no published price, the only way to settle it is to get the quote and run it against your own gross margin.

Is Feedvisor only for Amazon?

Yes, and it matters for the total bill. Feedvisor is an Amazon platform; Shopify, eBay and other channels need separate tooling, so a multi-channel seller is budgeting for Feedvisor plus something else rather than for one system.

What should I weigh before buying a tool like this?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Small teams get the most out of this category when one clear problem is already costing real hours or revenue; buying ahead of that just adds cost and another login. Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.
