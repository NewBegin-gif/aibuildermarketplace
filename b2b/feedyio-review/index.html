Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 10 Sep 2026

Nine published tiers, billed in USD every 30 days through Shopify. The Shopify listing shows only the first four. Free is permanent and covers up to 30 products or 100 variants, one feed, the primary market only, a daily product update and email support. Basic at $9.99 a month raises that to 1,000 products or 2,000 variants, up to five feeds, hourly product updates and the named comparison-shopping integrations (Heureka, Favi and similar), still on the primary market and language only.

Advanced at $19.99 covers 2,500 products or 5,000 variants, up to ten feeds, and all markets and languages. Pro at $29.99 covers 6,000 products or 12,000 variants and up to 30 feeds. The listing stops there and says additional plans are available; the vendor's own pricing page prices them: Enterprise $39.99 (10,000 products / 20,000 variants / 40 feeds), Enterprise Plus $59.99 (20,000 / 40,000 / 40), Enterprise Plus 2 $89.99 (30,000 / 60,000 / 40), Enterprise Plus 3 $99.99 (50,000 / 100,000 / 40) and Enterprise Plus 4 $119.99 (65,000 / 130,000 / 40).

Every paid tier has a 7-day free trial. One inconsistency on that page: the line under the table says every paid plan unlocks Shopify markets, translations and inventory levels, while the Basic card above it lists the primary market and primary language only — the Shopify listing agrees with the card, so confirm before buying Basic for a second market. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The first is catalogue size: the ladder counts products and variants, and it runs further than the Shopify listing suggests — the listing stops at Pro ($29.99, 6,000 products) and says additional plans are available, while the vendor's own pricing page publishes five more tiers up to $119.99 for 65,000 products. The second is freshness: the free tier updates products once a day and hourly updates start at $9.99, which matters when stock moves faster than a day.

Multi-market and multi-language feeds only appear at $19.99. The app is also younger and smaller than the shelf leaders: launched 16 May 2022 by happyapps.cz in Prague, with 115 reviews against thousands for the established Google Shopping feed apps. And the standout integrations — Heureka, Zboží.cz, Árukereső, Glami, Favi, Kaufland — are Central and Eastern European channels; that is the app's real edge, but it is not an edge if you only sell through Google and Meta.

What Feedyio does

Shopify product-feed engine (by happyapps.cz) that generates and syncs feeds for Google, Meta, Pinterest, TikTok, Bing and 130-plus marketplaces and price-comparison sites, with strong coverage of the Central European channels (Heureka, Glami, Kaufland, Favi). Custom XML, CSV, TXT or JSON feeds are built from your own Liquid template, with category mapping, attribute rules and validation before products get rejected. Free tier up to 30 products; Basic $9.99 a month, Advanced $19.99, Pro $29.99, capped on catalogue size. Launched May 2022, 115 reviews as of 10 Sep 2026.

When the paid plan is worth it

The plan table on the Shopify listing stops at Pro and points to ‘additional plans’, which makes the top of the ladder look like a sales conversation. It is not. feedyio.com/pricing publishes all nine tiers (read 10 September 2026): above Pro sit Enterprise at $39.99 for 10,000 products, Enterprise Plus at $59.99 for 20,000, then $89.99 for 30,000, $99.99 for 50,000 and $119.99 for 65,000. Note what stops scaling. The feed allowance freezes at 40 from Enterprise upward, so each step above $39.99 buys catalogue room and nothing else.

What upgrading does not fix is the refresh you may think you are buying. The tier governs how often Feedyio rebuilds the file; the marketplace decides how often it fetches, and the vendor's own TikTok guidance tells merchants to set that schedule on the channel side. Rejections work the same way — a currency that does not match the channel catalogue, a missing image, a policy breach — and no tier changes that verdict.

How to actually use Feedyio

Build the feed on Free before you pay for it. Free has no time limit and carries 30 products, enough to prove the output shape against a channel that has rejected you before. The path in the app is fixed: Create feed, then either pick a marketplace template or type ‘custom’ in the search box for a custom XML, CSV, TXT or JSON feed, then work through General, Mapping, Product source, Exclude products and Additional fields before saving and copying the feed URL.

Open that URL in a browser yourself before handing it over. The vendor's list of common TikTok mistakes puts a currency mismatch first and a broken or password-protected feed URL third, and both show up in a browser in seconds. Then set the fetch schedule on the channel, because the feed URL stays live and the channel decides when to read it, and open the channel's diagnostics after the first import rather than reading silence as success.

The natural comparison is Simprosys Google Shopping Feed, Koongo: Sell on Marketplaces — the difference is breadth of channel against depth of track record. Simprosys is the shelf leader for the Google, Microsoft, Meta and Pinterest set and carries thousands of reviews (read 10 September 2026 on the Shopify App Store); Feedyio carries 115 and is three years old. What Feedyio has instead is the template editor — you write your own XML, CSV, TXT or JSON in Liquid rather than picking from a fixed list — and a preconfigured set of European comparison and marketplace channels that the Google-first apps treat as an afterthought. If your channels are Google and Meta, buy on track record. If your channels include Heureka, Glami, Kaufland or a marketplace that wants a feed shaped its own way, Feedyio is the one built for that, and the free tier lets you check the output shape before paying..
