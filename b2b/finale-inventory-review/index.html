What Finale Inventory does

Inventory and warehouse management for e-commerce and wholesale: multi-channel stock sync, purchasing and barcode workflows that scale with order volume.

Pricing (2026)

Self-Starter around $99/mo (one user, up to ~500 orders/mo); mid-tiers roughly $200–$800/mo add multi-channel sync and advanced reporting; enterprise $1,000+/mo. Month-to-month or annual, no setup fees (2026). Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

If you do a few dozen orders a month, this is more inventory engine, and cost, than you need. The interface is functional rather than flashy: you're buying depth, not polish.

What they complain about

When the paid plan is worth it

Our record carries approximate figures here rather than a read plan table, so treat these as the shape. Self-Starter is around $99 a month for one user and up to roughly 500 orders a month, mid-tiers roughly $200 to $800 adding multi-channel sync and advanced reporting, and enterprise above $1,000. Confirm the exact tier boundaries on their page before budgeting -- the interesting question is where your order volume actually sits, and that is the line the published summary is vaguest about.

What the ladder buys is worth understanding even at this resolution. Self-Starter is a single-user, single-channel product: it will run one shop and one person. The mid-tier jump is where multi-channel sync appears, and that is a capability rather than a volume increase. If you sell on more than one marketplace, the entry tier does not do the job at any order volume. So the $99 is not a cheaper version of what you need.

Two terms in your favour: billing can be month-to-month rather than annual, and there are no setup fees. For an inventory system that matters more than usual, because migrating stock data is the expensive part of switching and a monthly term keeps that decision reversible while you find out whether the sync works with your channels.

How to actually use Finale Inventory

Count two things before you choose a tier, because they are the two caps on the entry plan: orders a month and people who need a login. Self-Starter at around $99 covers one user and roughly 500 orders, and a growing multi-channel seller passes both quickly.

Then use the fact that there are no setup fees and month-to-month billing is available, which is unusual in inventory software. You can start without a year's commitment and without an implementation invoice, so run a real month — actual receipts, actual picks, an actual stocktake — rather than a configured demo.

Put the person who will live in it in front of it on day one. This is functional rather than flashy, and you are buying depth rather than polish; whether your warehouse team will use it daily is a question only they can answer, and it decides more than the tier does. If you do a few dozen orders a month, stop here — this is more inventory engine, and more cost, than the job needs.

The natural comparison is Cin7 Core or Katana — comparable multi-channel inventory platforms — weigh by which sales channels and integrations you actually run. Decide by which one fits the job above, not by the louder brand.
