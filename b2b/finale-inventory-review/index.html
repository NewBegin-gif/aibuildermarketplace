Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 31 Aug 2026

Essentials starts at $499/mo (positioned for 1-2 warehouses); Growth starts at $799/mo and adds the Mobile Barcode WMS module plus email, phone and Zoom support; Enterprise is custom pricing on request and adds API and EDI access, premium support with SLAs and a dedicated account manager. Both published figures are 'starting at' and no per-tier order, SKU or user cap is published -- the vendor's FAQ says overage means additional usage fees or a plan upgrade, with the thresholds written into your agreement.

Every plan routes through Request a Demo; there is no self-serve signup or trial. Most customers sign 12-, 24- or 36-month terms. Standard onboarding is included and go-live is quoted at 2-6 weeks. Stated platform ceilings: up to 300 users, 1.5 million orders per month and 1.5 million products. The Self-Starter tier at around $99/mo that our earlier record described is no longer on the pricing page. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

There is no self-serve tier and no published order or user cap -- every plan goes through Request a Demo, most customers sign 12- to 36-month terms, and API and EDI access are Enterprise-only. If you do a few dozen orders a month, this is far more inventory engine, and far more cost, than the job needs.

When the paid plan is worth it

Finale publishes two starting prices and no caps, and that changes what the tier decision is actually about. Essentials starts at $499 a month, Growth at $799, and Enterprise is quoted. Both numbers are floors rather than ceilings, and where you land inside them is settled in a demo, not on the page. What the extra $300 buys is specific: Growth adds the Mobile Barcode WMS module and email, phone and Zoom support.

What it does not buy is headroom, because no order, SKU or user limit is published for either tier. So if you are upgrading to raise a cap, ask what the thresholds in your own agreement are -- Finale's FAQ says exceeding them means additional usage fees or a plan upgrade, with the numbers set in the contract rather than on the site. Upgrading to Growth also does not get you the API.

API and EDI access sit on Enterprise only, so if anything in your stack has to talk to Finale directly, the middle tier is not the answer at any price and you are in a quote conversation from the start. And no tier shortens the process: every plan is Request a Demo, most customers sign 12-, 24- or 36-month terms, and go-live runs two to six weeks.

How to actually use Finale Inventory

Count the two things the price page does not settle: which of the 40+ sales-channel integrations you need, and whether anything must talk to Finale over the API. The first decides whether Essentials at $499 covers you; the second forces Enterprise outright, because API and EDI access are gated there rather than sold as an add-on. Then treat the demo as the pricing page, because it is. Both published figures are 'starting at', and the thresholds that trigger overage fees live in your agreement rather than on the site.

Go into the call with your monthly order, SKU and user counts, and get those limits and the per-user rate in writing before you sign. Budget the term rather than the month. Most customers sign 12, 24 or 36 months, so the month-to-month start you might expect from smaller tools is not on offer, and there is no self-serve trial. Standard onboarding is included and go-live runs two to six weeks -- ask what falls outside it, since QuickBooks configuration and workflow consulting can cost extra.

What should I weigh before buying Operations & Workflow software?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.
