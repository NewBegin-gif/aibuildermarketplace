What Fireflies.ai does

AI meeting assistant that records, transcribes and summarizes your calls across Zoom, Google Meet and Teams — searchable notes, action items and CRM sync, so nobody takes minutes by hand.

When the paid plan is worth it

The free plan is the unusual part and it is worth being precise about: transcription is unlimited, and it is the AI on top that is limited. So the question is never whether you can afford to record your meetings — you can, at zero — but whether the summarising, searching and asking-questions layer earns about $10 a seat a month on Pro or about $19 on Business.

Read that as a per-seat commitment rather than a team price, because it multiplies: ten people on Business is roughly $190 a month, and the honest test is whether ten people genuinely need AI summaries or whether two do and the rest just need the transcript. Fireflies does not stop the other eight from reading a free transcript, so a mixed setup is a legitimate way to buy this.

The cap that actually bites on the paid tiers is AI credits. Volume of meetings is not the constraint; volume of AI processing is, and those are different numbers in a way that is easy to get wrong when budgeting from headcount. Track how many meetings actually get summarised in the first month rather than how many get recorded.

One thing that no tier changes and that belongs in the decision: an AI notetaker joins your calls. If your meetings touch anything where consent or confidentiality is a question, that is a policy conversation before it is a pricing one, and the free tier does not make it go away.

How to actually use Fireflies.ai

First value is a transcript, and the fastest route to one is the calendar connection rather than the app: connect Google or Microsoft calendar and Fireflies joins scheduled meetings as a participant. The alternative for one-off calls is inviting the notetaker to the meeting directly. The vendor's own guide is unusually deep here — Getting Started runs to about sixty articles and Integrations to a hundred and thirty-seven — so if your stack is anything other than plain Google Meet or Zoom, the specific article for it exists.

Two things to read before you rely on it. There are documented storage and rate limits, which is the constraint that surfaces on a busy week rather than on day one, and there is a troubleshooting path for failed transcriptions — worth knowing where it is before a call you needed goes missing. Both sit in the guide under their own headings.

The step teams most often skip is the one that decides whether anyone reads the output: deciding where transcripts land. Fireflies integrates with a long list of tools, and a transcript that arrives in the CRM or the project tracker gets used, while one that stays in Fireflies gets forgotten. Set that up in the first week, while the habit is still forming.
