Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 31 Aug 2026

Free: 20 responses a month, no card required. Essentials $25/mo billed monthly or $17/mo billed yearly ($204/yr) -- 500 responses a month, 1 administrator, unlimited quizzes and questions, question bank, live quizzes, auto grading. Premium $39/mo monthly or $25/mo yearly ($300/yr) -- 2,000 responses a month, 2 administrators or trainers, learning paths, selling quizzes and courses, custom certificates, embedding, self-registration. Enterprise $89/mo monthly or $60/mo yearly ($720/yr) -- 25,000 responses a month, 15 administrators or trainers, APIs and webhooks, single sign-on, white-label emails, custom CSS, 100 custom groups, 24/7 priority support.

Enterprise+ is tailored and quoted by sales. Yearly billing works out 32-36% cheaper than monthly, which is more than the 'save over 25%' the toggle advertises. Purchases over $300 can be invoiced for bank transfer or cheque against an additional $50 fee. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

When the paid plan is worth it

The monthly response caps are the business model, and the per-response arithmetic runs the opposite way from what the stickers suggest. Free stops at 20 responses a month. On yearly billing Essentials is $17 for 500, Premium $25 for 2,000, and Enterprise $60 for 25,000. Work out the cost per response and the ladder is steep rather than flat: about $0.034 on Essentials, $0.0125 on Premium and $0.0024 on Enterprise.

Premium is close to three times cheaper per response than Essentials for eight dollars more a month, and the 1,500 extra responses it adds work out at roughly half a cent each. If you are anywhere near the 500 ceiling, moving up is the cheap decision rather than the expensive one -- which is the reverse of how these ladders usually read. What upgrading does not fix is the calendar.

The caps reset monthly, so a plan sized on your average still breaks on the month an exam cohort lands, and no tier converts unused responses into next month's allowance. Nor does volume buy you people: administrator seats are rationed separately at one, two and fifteen, sharing a login is against the terms, and so a second trainer who needs their own account forces Premium no matter how few responses you collect.

How to actually use FlexiQuiz

The vendor describes the flow in three steps -- Create, Publish, Analyze -- and the useful detail is what sits inside each. You build the quiz from question types and a question bank, set test options such as timing and grading, then publish and share it; auto-grading removes the manual work afterwards, and the analytics come back per respondent and question. What to check on the free plan before committing is not the features but the ceiling.

Free allows 20 responses a month with no card, which is enough to prove the quiz works and nowhere near enough to run a cohort, so treat it as a build-and-test environment rather than a trial of the real thing. Note that unlimited quizzes and questions and the question bank are already on Essentials, so a long question bank is not what pushes you up a tier -- the response cap and administrator count are.

The practical order that saves the most rework: build one complete quiz on Free, including certificates and timing if you need them, run it past a handful of real respondents, then count how many responses a normal month produces and how many people need their own login. Those two numbers, not the feature list, pick your plan.

What should I weigh before buying Operations & Workflow software?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything.
