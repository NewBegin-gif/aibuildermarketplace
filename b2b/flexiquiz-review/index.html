FlexiQuiz Review (2026)

Operations & WorkflowResearched assessment · by Daniel HaketUpdated 2026-09-25

The monthly response caps are the business model: 20 on free, 500 on Essentials, 2,000 on Premium and 25,000 on Enterprise -- one viral quiz or one big exam cohort and you're shopping a tier up.

Best for: educators, trainers and small teams that want professional graded quizzes, exams and simple courses — with certificates, timed tests, question banks and analytics — without building out a full LMS.
Who should skip it: if you need a complete learning platform with deep integrations, or your volumes run past a few thousand responses a month — the caps make it the wrong shape.

Frequently asked questions

I have been quoted $25 a month. Which plan is that?

Either of two, and the page defaults to the reading that flatters it. Read 22 September 2026, $25 a month is Essentials billed monthly and it is also Premium billed yearly -- the same number, two different products. Essentials at $25 monthly gives 500 responses a month and one administrator. Premium at $25 a month on the yearly plan, $300 up front, gives 2,000 responses, two administrators, learning paths, custom certificates, embedding, self-registration and the ability to sell quizzes and courses. The cards open on the yearly view, so the $25 you see first is the better of the two. If you are willing to pay a year in advance, there is no version of this where monthly Essentials is the right buy at that price: the same money buys four times the responses and a second seat. Check which toggle the quote was taken from before you compare it with anything.

The page says save over 25% for paying yearly. Is that the real figure?

It is understated, on all three plans. Read 22 September 2026: the monthly rates behind the billing toggle are $25, $39 and $89 for Essentials, Premium and Enterprise, and the yearly equivalents are $17, $25 and $60 -- so the actual discounts are 32%, 36% and 33%, against the 'save over 25%' and 'more than 3 months free' the page advertises. On Premium it is closer to four and a half months free than three. The one thing to weigh against it is cash timing rather than price: yearly means $204, $300 or $720 up front, and if the bill runs over $300 and you need an invoice for bank transfer or cheque instead of a card, that costs an additional $50. Since Premium's yearly total is exactly $300, whether you land on the wrong side of that threshold depends on which plan you pick.

Both the third and the fourth plan are called Enterprise. What is the difference?

One has a price and one does not, and the page gives them the same name. Read 22 September 2026, the third card is Enterprise at $60 a month yearly or $89 monthly, and it is specific: 25,000 responses a month, 15 administrators or trainers, APIs and webhooks, single sign-on, white-label emails, custom CSS, 100 custom groups and 24/7 priority support. Beside it sits a second block, also headed Enterprise, described as 'designed for teams of all sizes with all the great features of Enterprise' plus expert strategic support, customization and unlimited options, with no figure and a Contact our sales team button. So the published tier is the one you can buy and the quote-only one is a superset of it; if someone tells you they are 'on Enterprise', that sentence does not say which. The practical trigger for the quoted version is the word unlimited, since every named tier is bounded -- responses, seats, groups, respondent portals and storage all have numbers.

What should I weigh before buying Operations & Workflow software?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.
