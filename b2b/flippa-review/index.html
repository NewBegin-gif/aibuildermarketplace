What Flippa does

Marketplace for buying and selling online businesses, SaaS, apps and domains. Self-serve, so the due diligence is yours. Sellers pay a listing fee from about $29 plus a success fee starting near 3% — a bracket rate on the whole sale price, not a marginal one.

When the paid plan is worth it

The listing fee is not the price. Sellers pay a small upfront fee plus a success fee when the business sells, and the success fee is the number that decides everything. It is 10% on the lower asking-price bands, stepping DOWN as the price rises: 9%, 8%, 7%, 6% and 5% through the higher bands to $50M and above.

Read that carefully, because the rate is a bracket on the WHOLE sale price rather than a marginal rate on the slice above each threshold. That has a consequence worth knowing before you set an asking price: just under a band edge you pay the higher rate on everything. Around the $250K boundary, 10% of $250,000 is $25,000 while 9% of a fraction more is about $22,500 — asking slightly more can leave you with roughly $2,500 more in hand. Confirm the exact band edges against your own listing before planning around it, but do check them: this is the one place on the page where a small change in your asking price moves four figures.

At the small end the listing options are flat and cheap: $29 for a 60-day self-service listing, $49 for a boosted three-month one, or $199 per six months for Premium, which adds visibility and NDA protection. Flippa notes there is no monthly billing on that last one. For a sub-$10K asset those fees, not the commission, are the whole cost.

Buyers browse free, and that is genuinely free: AI matching, Deal Room, FlippaPay, escrow and multi-currency are all included. The paid buyer subscription is $49 a month or $388 a year — the annual works out about 34% below twelve monthly payments — and what it buys is time rather than features: 21 days of exclusive early access to new listings valued over $10,000. That is worth it only if you are actively hunting and would lose deals to a three-week delay. Escrow charges apply on top of all of the above.

How to actually use Flippa

Flippa documents selling as four steps, and the first one is where the work is. List means showcasing the business with validated data rather than typed-in claims: Flippa integrates with fifteen financial, e-commerce and accounting platforms so the numbers come from the source, and connecting those is what separates a listing buyers take seriously from one they skip. Do that before you write a word of the description.

Match is handled by the platform's AI matching engine, which the vendor says delivers over ten million buyer-seller matches a month, and you can also find and invite specific buyers yourself using their stated interests, budgets and acquisition history. Negotiate gives you buyer shortlists, integrated legal documents and due-diligence tooling. Payments, transfers and insurance close it out in one place, with escrow.

What to prepare before any of that: this is a self-serve marketplace, not a broker. Verification and listing quality vary, so sellers should expect tyre-kickers and buyers should expect to do their own due diligence. Have your financials clean and connected, decide your floor price with the success-fee bands in mind, and budget attention for the negotiation rather than assuming someone else runs it for you.

Best for: buyers and sellers of small-to-mid online businesses, SaaS, apps and domains who want the largest marketplace and are comfortable doing their own due diligence.
Who should skip it: if you're selling a large, premium business where a dedicated broker's vetting and negotiation earns its higher fee, or you want a hands-off sale.

The natural comparison is Empire Flippers or FE International — brokered marketplaces that vet harder and charge more — better for larger, cleaner exits; Flippa wins on reach and low entry cost. Decide by which one fits the job above, not by the louder brand.

Frequently asked questions

What does it really cost to sell on Flippa?

Two charges, and the second dwarfs the first. The listing fee for a small asset is $29 for 60 days, $49 for a boosted three-month listing or $199 for six months of Premium. The success fee is 10% of the sale price on the lower bands. On a $10,000 sale that is $1,000 plus your listing fee plus escrow costs — so budget around a tenth of the proceeds, not a flat fee. Read on 17 August 2026.

Does the commission get cheaper for a bigger business?

Yes, and this is the opposite of what many summaries say. The success fee starts at 10% on the smaller asking-price bands and steps down as the price rises: 9%, 8%, 7%, 6% and 5% through the higher bands up to $50M+. The percentage falls as the amount rises, so the effective rate on a large sale is roughly half that on a small one.

Is there a Flippa discount code?

We have found none, and people search for one often enough that it is worth saying. What exists instead is a choice of listing terms — $29, $49 or $199 for six months of Premium — and, on the buying side, a subscription at $49 a month or $388 a year, which is roughly two months free if you take the year. The success fee itself is set by the asking-price band and is not discounted.

Is Flippa safe to run my finances through?

The bar here is higher than features. Check who actually holds the money and under what licence, whether client funds are segregated, what audit trail and permission controls you get, and how support behaves when something goes wrong mid-transaction. Anything touching payments should be able to answer those in writing; if it cannot, that is your answer.

Who should not use Flippa?

Skip it if you're selling a large, premium business where a dedicated broker's vetting and negotiation earns its higher fee, or you want a hands-off sale. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
