What Flocksy does

A dedicated creative team for one flat monthly fee. Unlimited graphic design, video editing, motion graphics and web design — your marketing never waits on a freelancer again.

Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 6 Aug 2026

Flat monthly plans priced by dedicated creative hours per business day: $1,199/mo for 2h/day, $1,699 (3h/day), $2,199 (4h/day), $2,599 (5h/day), $2,999 (6h/day), $3,399 (7h/day) and $3,799/mo for 8h/day, each billed annually and shown per month. Plans start at 2 daily hours and scale to 8, with custom teams beyond that; month-to-month costs more — on the 4h/day plan Flocksy prices annual billing as saving $10,800/yr versus monthly. The pricing page carries a money-back guarantee. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The economics are simple and honest — against even one freelance designer's day rate, the math works IF you feed it steady volume; if your design needs are sporadic, a flat monthly fee is paying for silence.

When the paid plan is worth it

Convert this to an hourly rate before comparing it to anything, because that is what you are actually buying. The plans run from $1,199 a month for 2 dedicated creative hours per business day up to $3,799 for 8 hours, with $1,699, $2,199, $2,599, $2,999 and $3,399 in between. At roughly 21 business days a month that is 42 hours for $1,199 -- about $28.55 an hour -- falling to 168 hours for $3,799, or about $22.61 an hour. Roughly a fifth cheaper per hour at the top, and the discount is gradual rather than stepped, so there is no single tier that is obviously the sweet spot.

That hourly figure is the comparison that matters. Against a freelance designer or an agency retainer, $23 to $29 an hour for a managed team is the number to weigh -- not the monthly total, which only tells you how much capacity you committed to. The risk in this model is the reverse of most software: you pay for hours whether you have work ready or not, so the tier to pick is the one you can reliably feed, not the one you could theoretically use.

Billing frequency is a large lever here. Every plan is shown per month but billed annually, and month-to-month costs materially more -- on the 4 hours a day plan Flocksy puts the annual saving at $10,800 a year. Their pricing page carries a money-back guarantee, which is the thing to read carefully before committing a year at that scale.

How to actually use Flocksy

Count your actual design hours over the last three months before you pick a daily capacity, because the plans are sold in hours per business day and unused capacity is not banked. Two hours a day is about 42 hours a month at $1,199 — roughly $28.55 an hour — and eight hours a day works out at $22.61. Against a freelancer's day rate those numbers are good, but only if you fill them: a quarter that averaged fifteen hours of design is a quarter spent paying for silence.

Write your brand guidelines before the first brief. Subscription design lives or dies on how much re-work each request needs, and the fastest way to waste your daily capacity is three rounds of revisions that a one-page style guide would have prevented.

Then plan the queue rather than the volume. Unlimited here means an unlimited queue, not unlimited throughput — work happens at your plan's daily capacity — so a launch week needs briefs submitted well ahead rather than on the day. Turnaround expectations are where these relationships sour, so agree them in writing in week one. Note that monthly billing costs considerably more than annual, and that the pricing page carries a money-back guarantee.

The natural comparison is Kimp or Design Pickle — the unlimited-design subscription peers — compare real turnaround and daily capacity at equal price, not the 'unlimited' word. Decide by which one fits the job above, not by the louder brand.
