folk Review (2026)No Mobile App, No Automation

Growth & RevenueResearched assessment · by Daniel HaketUpdated 2026-08-27

Two real gaps: there's no mobile app (browser only — the #1 complaint), and no proper workflow automation on any plan, just trigger-based emails. Enrichment is capped (~500–1,000/mo) and email sequences need at least Premium. Throughout this review we weigh it against HubSpot.

Best for: small sales teams, agencies and VC/partnership teams who want a clean, fast-to-set-up CRM with a great LinkedIn enrichment extension.

What folk does

Lightweight, collaborative CRM for managing contacts and relationships. Centralize your network and personalize outreach without the bloat. A mobile app (Contacts by folk) adds on-the-go search, one-tap contact capture with business-card scanning, voice notes and interaction tracking across WhatsApp, LinkedIn, email and calendar.

What buyers report about folk

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run folk ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • 4.5 on both G2 (318 reviews) and Capterra, with 70% five-star and ease of use the number one named pro.
  • • A 14-day full-feature trial with no card required.
What they complain about
  • • As of April 2026 there is no iOS or Android app at all. The web interface works on a phone but is clearly built for desktop.
  • • Workflow automation is missing, analytics are basic, and email sends and enrichment credits carry monthly caps.
  • • Native integrations are limited — most things go through Zapier or Make — and there are no cross-group custom fields.
  • • It fits teams up to around fifty. Beyond that, forecasting, automation and revenue attribution are gaps. Standard is $24 per member per month billed yearly ($30 monthly), Premium $48 ($60) and Enterprise from $80 ($100), with no free plan (re-verified 2026-08-06).
  • • When the trial ends your account locks, though your data is kept.

When the paid plan is worth it

This is one of the cleaner ladders we have read, and the price itself gives you no signal at all: Premium is exactly double Standard at every billing mode -- $30 against $60 monthly, $24 against $48 yearly. When the multiple is that tidy it usually means the tiers were drawn around features rather than around cost, so the decision is entirely a question of which capabilities you need. Do not read the doubling as an indication that Premium is twice the product.

The annual discount is a uniform 20% across all three tiers: $288, $576 and from $960 per member per year. Because it is priced per member rather than per company, that discount compounds with headcount -- a team of ten saves $720 a year on Standard by paying up front, which is worth more than most feature differences at this level. Enterprise starts at $100 a member monthly and is the only open-ended one; “from” means the quote can go up.

The trial is 14 days and needs no credit card, which is the right way to settle the Standard-versus-Premium question rather than reasoning about it.

How to actually use folk

Check two gaps against your workflow before you start the trial, because neither is something you can buy your way out of with a higher tier. There is no mobile app at all — folk is browser-only, which is the single most common complaint — so if your team updates the CRM between meetings on a phone, test exactly that on day one. And there is no proper workflow automation on any plan, only trigger-based emails; if you were expecting multi-step automations, no tier provides them.

Then check where email sequences sit, because they need at least Premium. If outreach is why you are buying, your entry is $48 a member a month on annual billing — $576 per member per year — rather than the $24 Standard rate, and that doubling should be in your comparison from the start. Watch the enrichment cap as a meter rather than a feature: roughly 500 to 1,000 a month depending on plan, which for a team doing real prospecting is a monthly budget rather than an allowance you forget about.

Use the 14-day trial, which needs no card, on your own messy contact import rather than a clean sample. folk's genuine argument is that it is pleasant enough that people actually keep it updated — and that only shows up once your real duplicates, half-finished records and inconsistent company names are in it.
