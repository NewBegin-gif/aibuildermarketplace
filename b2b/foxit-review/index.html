What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Foxit's whole pitch is being the honest fraction of Acrobat's price, and it largely delivers — the catches are modular: e-signing is a separate subscription (with a 5-user minimum on the business tier), and the AI features are an add-on, so the 'full Acrobat replacement' basket costs more than the headline editor. Still typically well under Adobe; just price your actual basket, not the first line.

What buyers report about Foxit

Recurring themes from G2, Capterra and Software Advice, read in August 2026. We have not run Foxit ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Price is the single most-cited reason people choose it — reviewers quantify savings of 40-60% against Adobe Acrobat, and there is a perpetual licence option that escapes subscriptions entirely.
  • • From $10.99, faster than Adobe for common tasks, and 700 million users globally.
What they complain about
  • • The Mac version is materially less capable than Windows. On Apple Silicon, OCR is disabled in native mode and Rights Management tools are unavailable on M1/M2. Check your platform before buying.
  • • Pop-ups pushing additional features and upgrades irritate reviewers.
  • • OCR struggles with unusual fonts and poor scans.
  • • The basic versions are quite limited and the full version is expensive for occasional use — advanced features and multiple logins both need higher tiers.
Best for: individuals and businesses that want full PDF editing, OCR and organizing at a fraction of Acrobat's subscription — especially multi-seat deployments.
Who should skip it: if you only read and occasionally annotate PDFs (free readers cover it), or your org is contractually deep in the Adobe ecosystem.

The natural comparison is Adobe Acrobat or PDF-XChange — Acrobat is the incumbent at 2-3x the price; PDF-XChange undercuts further on Windows — Foxit balances polish, platform breadth and price. Weigh it against Adobe Acrobat on the job you actually need done.

Frequently asked questions

Is Foxit secure and reliable enough to depend on?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.

Who should not use Foxit?

Skip it if you only read and occasionally annotate PDFs (free readers cover it), or your org is contractually deep in the Adobe ecosystem. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
