Pricing (2026)

PDF Editor is $129.99 per user per year, or from $10.99 per user per month. PDF Editor+ is $159.99 per user per year and now bundles what used to be a second bill: mobile apps, AI Smart Redact, cloud storage and Foxit eSign with 150 envelopes a year. A perpetual licence, Foxit PDF Editor 14, is $209.99 once per user for Windows or macOS, covering editing, conversion, OCR, forms and security but not the AI Assistant, Smart Redact, the document management system or any cloud feature.

Standalone eSign still exists: Essentials is $120 a year with 250 envelopes, Business is $300 per licence a year with a five-licence minimum, so $1,500 is the smallest Business order. The AI Assistant paid tier is $49.99 a year for 2,000 credits a month per user; Editor and Editor+ include 20 credits a month per user, credits reset monthly and do not roll over, and the add-on is not sold for perpetual licences.

An education discount is applied automatically at checkout when you register with a school email address; Foxit no longer publishes the rate. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The catch used to be that e-signing was always a second subscription. As of our 28 August 2026 reading that is only true below Editor+, which now includes Foxit eSign with 150 envelopes a year. The trap has moved rather than gone: buy the $129.99 Editor, then find you need signatures, and Editor plus standalone eSign Essentials is $249.99 a year against $159.99 for the Editor+ that bundles it. AI is still an add-on at $49.99 a year beyond the 20 monthly credits, and it is not sold for perpetual licences at all. Price the basket, not the first line.

When the paid plan is worth it

There is almost no reason to commit annually to the base editor. PDF Editor is $129.99 a year while the monthly option runs $10.99. At $10.99 a month you pay $131.88 across a year, which is $1.89 more than the annual licence. A discount of one and a half per cent is not a reason to lock in twelve months, so unless you know you want this for years, monthly costs you almost nothing extra and keeps the exit open.

The upgrade to Editor+ at $159.99 a year is the one that changed. It used to buy mobile access and AI redaction for $30 more; it now also carries Foxit eSign with 150 envelopes a year, where buying eSign on its own starts at $120 a year for the 250-envelope Essentials plan. If you sign anything at all, that $30 step is the cheapest signature capability on the price list, and the question of whether you edit on a phone barely matters any more.

Beyond 150 envelopes the separate products are still where the money is. eSign Essentials is $120 a year for 250 envelopes; eSign Business is $300 per licence a year and carries a five-licence minimum, so a two-person firm cannot spend less than $1,500. The AI Assistant is $49.99 a year for 2,000 credits a month, on top of the 20 monthly credits every Editor seat already has, and it cannot be added to a perpetual licence.

That last restriction is the real cost of buying once at $209.99: you are choosing the 2026 feature set permanently.

How to actually use Foxit

Decide whether you will ever need a signature before you pick a tier, because that one answer is worth more than any feature comparison here. PDF Editor is $129.99 per user per year and covers editing, conversion and OCR on desktop and web. Editor+ is $159.99 and adds the mobile apps, AI Smart Redact and Foxit eSign with 150 envelopes a year. Buying the cheaper tier and adding standalone eSign Essentials later costs $249.99 a year for 250 envelopes, so unless you genuinely sign more than 150 documents a year, the $30 step up is the cheaper route to the same capability.

Take the monthly option at $10.99 per user if you are replacing Acrobat rather than adding to it. Converting a working document habit is where these switches fail, and a month is enough to find the one feature you use daily that behaves differently. Twelve monthly payments come to $131.88 against $129.99 for the year, so the annual commitment buys you almost nothing. If you know you want this for years and can live without the cloud, buy it once: the perpetual Foxit PDF Editor 14 is $209.99 per user for Windows or macOS, which pays for itself against the annual subscription in about twenty months.

Read what it leaves out first, because it is a real list: no AI Assistant, no Smart Redact, no document management system and no cloud features. And if you are in education, register with your school email address before you buy anything at all, because the discount is applied at checkout.

The natural comparison is Adobe Acrobat or PDF-XChange — Acrobat is the incumbent at 2-3x the price; PDF-XChange undercuts further on Windows — Foxit balances polish, platform breadth and price. Weigh it against Adobe Acrobat on the job you actually need done.
