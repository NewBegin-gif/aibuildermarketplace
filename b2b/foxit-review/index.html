Foxit Review (2026)Editor+ Now Bundles the E-Signing

IT & ProductivityResearched assessment · by Daniel HaketUpdated 2026-09-25

Foxit's pitch is being the honest fraction of Acrobat's price, and on the headline numbers it delivers. Throughout this review we weigh it against Adobe Acrobat.

Best for: individuals and businesses that want full PDF editing, OCR and organizing at a fraction of Acrobat's subscription — especially multi-seat deployments.
Who should skip it: if you only read and occasionally annotate PDFs (free readers cover it), or your org is contractually deep in the Adobe ecosystem.

Frequently asked questions

Can I pay $10.99 a month and cancel when I am done?

No - and this is the single most misread line on the page. Read 22 September 2026, the toggle above the plan cards offers 'Annual plan, paid in full' and 'Annual plan, billed monthly'. Both are twelve-month plans. There is no month-to-month option on Foxit PDF Editor at all, so the $10.99 does not buy an exit, it buys instalments: $131.88 across the year against $129.99 paid up front, which is $1.89 more for the same twelve months. Editor+ does the same at $13.99 a month, $167.88 against $159.99, a $7.89 premium. The practical advice inverts as a result. Pay in full if you are staying, because paying by the month costs more and commits you just as long, and do your deciding inside the 14-day free trial, which asks for no credit card and is the only genuinely cancellable part of this price list.

We only want the AI features. What is the cheapest route to them?

Not the upgrade. Read 22 September 2026, every Foxit subscription already includes 300 free AI credits per user per month - PDF Editor and PDF Editor+ alike - and stepping up to Editor+ for $30 a year adds mobile apps, Smart Redact and eSign with 150 envelopes, but not one extra credit. More credits are a separate line: the AI Assistant add-on is $49.99 a year for 2,000 credits a month, and the same add-on is advertised at 'early access pricing' of $4.99 a month, which is $59.88 over a year, so the monthly route costs $9.89 more for an identical allowance. Two limits are worth knowing before you plan around it. Credits reset every month and unused ones do not roll over, so the 2,000 is a ceiling rather than a budget. And the add-on cannot be attached to the $209.99 perpetual licence at all, which is the quiet cost of buying once: no AI, no DMS, no cloud, and no web app.

We are buying about thirty seats. Perpetual licences or subscriptions?

Thirty is exactly where the discount starts, so ask for it either way. Read 22 September 2026, Foxit publishes volume discounts of up to 30% on orders of 30 or more licences, across PDF Editor, PDF Editor+ and eSign, and explicitly on perpetual as well as subscription - with a single purchase order, one renewal date, standardised installation images for IT deployment and coverage for divisions and subsidiaries. On list price the perpetual licence at $209.99 overtakes the $129.99 subscription after about nineteen months, which looks decisive until you read what it leaves out: the perpetual Foxit PDF Editor 14 covers Windows and macOS only, with editing, conversion, OCR, forms, security and fill-and-sign, and no web app, no AI Assistant, no document management system and no cloud storage. Foxit's own FAQ makes that argument for you, and adds the detail that matters for a thirty-seat comparison against the incumbent: Adobe no longer sells perpetual Acrobat licences to new customers. So the decision is not really cost per year, it is whether your thirty people need the browser and the AI at all.

What should I weigh before buying IT & Productivity software?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.
