Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 14 Sep 2026

The headline figure is the yearly rate; paying month to month costs about 25% more, and both are shown on the page. Starter $39/mo billed yearly against $49/mo monthly: 1 seat, 1 site, 10 articles and 50 audit pages a month, Content Guard watching 10 pages. Professional $103/mo yearly against $129/mo monthly: 3 seats, 5 sites, 40 articles and 250 audit pages, extra seats $29/mo.

Scale $239/mo yearly against $299/mo monthly: 5 seats, up to 10 domains, 100 articles and 1,000 audit pages, Content Guard watching 300 pages, extra seats $29/mo. The trial is 7 days with no credit card. FraseCMS hosting is included free up to 100,000 page views a month; moving the blog to your own domain is $19/mo on top of a paid plan, or $99/mo at a million page views.

On Starter, Frase stops at the limit rather than billing you extra; on Professional and Scale you can switch on pay-as-you-go capacity, so there the sticker price is a floor rather than a ceiling. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

Correction after re-checking: Starter is $39/mo billed yearly or $49/mo month to month, Professional $103/mo yearly or $129/mo month to month, and Scale $239/mo yearly or $299/mo month to month, with Enterprise on custom pricing. Billing yearly saves 20% on every tier. Access starts with a 7-day trial that needs no credit card. What buyers report about Frase

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Frase ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • The content brief generator saves two to three hours per article, and reviewers who write SEO content regularly say that alone pays for it.
  • • A usable brief in about 90 seconds is what people name.
  • • On G2, reviewers in mid-2026 keep naming the same job: finding content gaps. Research, outlining and on-page optimisation in one editor is what they say saves the hours, alongside auditing pages they already have.
What they complain about
  • • Pricing was restructured between late 2025 and early 2026 and the entry tier went from $15 to $49. Professional at $103 is 29% above what the old Basic plus Pro add-on cost.
  • • Trustpilot reviewers report being auto-migrated to the new pricing with minimal warning.
  • • AppSumo lifetime-deal holders report being downgraded from promised unlimited use to a 4,000 words a month cap. If you bought a lifetime deal anywhere, that is the pattern to watch.
  • • The AI writing itself is described as generic — it is a research tool more than a writing one.
  • • The generic-AI complaint is not confined to one platform — G2 reviewers in June 2026 describe suggestions that read repetitive or too generic and need manual editing, and the editor slowing down on long documents.
  • • G2's own summary tags for Frase include subscription issues, which is the same thread as the migration and lifetime-deal complaints on Trustpilot rather than a separate problem.

When the paid plan is worth it

Frase has no permanent free tier for the core product. The only free path is a 7-day trial, no credit card needed, that runs Professional's feature set at reduced limits: 5 articles, 50 audit pages, 25 AI prompts, and up to 3 seats, with auto-publish and the agent's other autonomy held back. That window is long enough to judge the research and optimization workflow, but too short to prove out Content Guard, which depends on Search Console history accumulating before decay becomes visible at all.

Paying earns its keep once article volume is the binding constraint. Starter at $39/mo billed yearly, or $49/mo month to month, covers 10 articles and 50 audit pages on 1 seat and 1 site: workable for a solo operator, tight for anyone publishing weekly and revising afterwards. Professional at $103/mo yearly, or $129/mo month to month, buys 40 articles, 250 audit pages and 5 sites, the tier shaped for multi-site and client work.

Upgrading does not fix rankings. Higher tiers raise caps and widen Content Guard coverage from 10 pages to 75 and then 300; they do not change the underlying scoring, and they do not make AI engines cite you.

How to actually use Frase

Frase's own onboarding documentation puts the whole setup at about five minutes and lays out a fixed order. Start at the site selector dropdown in the top left, choose Add Site, enter your domain, and let the initial crawl run; that crawl is what populates the Brand Hub's Profile, Competitors and Assets sections, so skipping it leaves later brand and internal-link suggestions thin. Then open Settings and connect Google Search Console under Workspace integrations, documented as taking about two minutes.

This is the step it is tempting to postpone and the one that bites: Content Opportunities and Content Guard stay empty until Search Console is attached, so a fresh account can look broken when it is merely unconnected. Agencies hit a harder wall here, because the documentation requires Owner-level access to the client's Search Console instance rather than plain read permission. Finish in Optimize, under Site Health, by starting a baseline crawl.

Only once that data has landed does the Opportunities tab surface priorities worth acting on.

Frequently asked questions

What does Frase cost now, after the repricing?

Billed yearly: Starter $39, Professional $103 and Scale $239 a month; month to month those become $49, $129 and $299. Extra seats are $29 a month on the two upper plans. Those figures were identical in our July and August 2026 captures and again on 17 August 2026, so the restructuring people complain about has settled rather than being ongoing.

Will I get a surprise bill if I go over the limits?

It depends on the plan, and Frase is unusually explicit about it. On Starter the account holds at the limit until your cycle resets or you upgrade, so no overage is possible. On Professional and Scale you can switch on pay-as-you-go capacity for audit pages and AI prompts, which means the sticker price there is a floor rather than a cap. Decide deliberately which behaviour you want.

Is the lifetime deal still worth anything?

This is a question we see asked and the honest answer is uncomfortable. AppSumo lifetime-deal holders report being moved from promised unlimited use to a capped word allowance, and Trustpilot reviewers report being migrated to the new pricing with little warning. If you hold one, check what your account actually allows today rather than what the deal promised — and price the paid plans as the realistic fallback.

What should I weigh before buying Growth & Revenue software?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Small teams get the most out of this category when one clear problem is already costing real hours or revenue; buying ahead of that just adds cost and another login. Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.
