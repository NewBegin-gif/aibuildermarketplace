When the paid plan is worth it

The pay-on-success model is the honest part and it is real: credits are only consumed when verified data is actually found. But it also means the sticker price tells you almost nothing, because what you spend depends entirely on what you ask for. The number that decides your bill is the 10x. An email costs 1 credit; a mobile number costs 10. The volume discount is thinner than the slider makes it look: fifteen times the credits buys about thirteen percent off the per-credit rate, so committing to a bigger plan mostly buys you headroom rather than a better deal.

What does help is knowing your mix: 500 credits is 500 emails, or 50 mobile numbers, or any blend. At Pro's $0.055 rate that puts an email at 5.5 cents and a mobile number at 55 cents. A calling-heavy motion therefore burns a month of credits in a few days, and no plan change fixes that — only a different mix does. Credits roll over, which softens the lumpiness: three months on monthly billing, twelve on annual.

That makes annual billing worth more than its headline discount for anyone whose prospecting comes in bursts, because a quiet month is banked rather than lost. What you are paying the premium for is orchestration across more than fifteen providers rather than one database. The trade-off is exactly that: the hit rate genuinely beats a single source, and you pay more per contact than querying the one source that might have sufficed.

The test worth running is not whether FullEnrich finds more — it will — but whether the extra contacts it finds are ones your existing tool missed on your actual ICP.

How to actually use FullEnrich

Model your email-to-phone mix before you buy credits, because a mobile number costs ten times an email. Five hundred credits is 500 emails, or 50 phone numbers, or any combination — so a calling-heavy motion has an effective allowance a tenth of what the headline suggests. At $0.055 a credit on Pro's 1,000-credit step, an email is 5.5 cents and a mobile number 55 cents. The pay-on-success model makes the arithmetic unusually clean: you are charged only when verified data is found, so your credit count is a floor on results rather than a budget for attempts.

That means you can compare cost per contact against single-source databases, where you pay for the query whether or not it returns anything. And as an orchestration layer over more than fifteen providers, the hit rate is where the premium is meant to come back. Take annual billing if you can, for the rollover rather than the discount: credits carry three months on monthly billing and twelve on annual. For prospecting that runs in bursts rather than evenly, that difference is worth more than the headline saving.
