Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 28 Sep 2026

14-day free trial, no card. Six tiers: Dev $12/mo, Startup $24, Growth $49, Business $99, High Traffic $199, Enterprise from $349 — billed yearly $9, $19, $39, $79, $159 and from $279. The digits are unchanged since 31 August. CURRENCY: Geotargetly prices the same digits in more than one currency, so the symbol you see depends on where you are. Reading the page from Europe on 19 September returned these figures with a dollar sign; reading it from the same exit on 20 September returned the identical figures with a euro sign — €12, €24, €49, €99, €199 and from €349 monthly, €9 to €279 billed yearly.

The numbers did not move, only the symbol did. We quote the dollar list here because that is the currency the rest of this page's figures were read in, but a European buyer should expect to be billed the same number in euros, which is not the same amount of money — do not convert one into the other. IMPORTANT: on 19 September the page was running a live 50/50 price split test on visitors from 32 countries (US, UK, CA, AU, NZ and 27 EU states), assigned by a 30-day cookie; the alternative set is about 20% higher — $14, $29, $59, $119, $239 and from $419.

The amounts above are the lower, control set, which is also what visitors outside those 32 countries see; a fresh session on 20 September landed in that same control set. Re-read on 28 September 2026 from a European exit in three fresh sessions: the test is still running. One session landed in the control set, billed yearly €9, €19, €39, €79, €159 and from €279 a month; two landed in the higher set, which billed yearly reads €11, €23, €47, €95, €191 and from €335 a month, again with the page stating that all prices are in euros.

The page's own price table additionally enumerates an eight-step Enterprise ladder the plan card does not show: $349, $429, $519, $599, $699, $869, $1,299 and $1,739 monthly, $279 to $1,391 as yearly monthly-equivalents, with annual totals $3,350 to $16,694. Standard annual totals are $108, $230, $470, $950 and $1,910 — two dollars above twelve times the advertised monthly-equivalent on all but Dev.

That table did not render on the 20 September reading, which served only the six plan cards, so those ladder and annual-total figures still carry their 19 September date. All plans include country, state and city-level targeting on unlimited domains. The meter is PAGEVIEWS, not visitors: a pageview is consumed for every page visit, and separately for each product in use; Geo Links consumes one on link click only.

No refunds, and quota does not roll over to the next billing cycle. On quota exhaustion you get 2 days to upgrade or re-subscribe before services stop, with email alerts at 30%, 10%, 5% and 0%. Per-tier pageview quotas are still NOT reliably readable: the page shows one quota figure (500,000 monthly, 6,000,000 yearly) for the whole ladder at once, which cannot be right for six tiers. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Run out and you get two days to upgrade or re-subscribe before services stop. The friendlier advertised numbers are the yearly rates; month-to-month runs about 25% higher. And the dependency worth pricing in: the rules run from a script you drop into your site, so they exist only while that script is served. Stop the subscription and every visitor sees the same page again, wherever they are.

What Geotargetly does

Geo-targeting toolkit — location-based redirects, content swaps, popups and personalization by country, state and city, priced by traffic volume.

When the paid plan is worth it

There is no feature decision to make: all six tiers include country, state and city-level targeting, and what scales is the quota. Dev is $12 a month, Startup $24, Growth $49, Business $99, High Traffic $199 and Enterprise from $349; billed yearly those become $9, $19, $39, $79, $159 and from $279 (read 2026-08-31 on their own pricing page). The annual discount is a flat 20% at every step except Dev's 25%, so no volume incentive hides in the billing frequency.

The meter is pageviews, not visitors, and that distinction is what costs money. Their FAQ states a pageview is consumed for every page visit — and that if you run multiple products, pageviews are consumed for each product. A redirect and a popup on the same page bill two. What upgrading does not buy is a soft landing. When the quota is exhausted you get two days to upgrade or re-subscribe before services stop, and when they stop every visitor sees the unmodified page.

Alerts arrive at 30%, 10%, 5% and 0%, so the warning is there — but the failure mode is silent to your visitors.

How to actually use Geotargetly

Understand what you are renting before you build on it. The rules run from a script you add to your site, so your redirects and content swaps exist only while that script is served and the subscription is live. For something permanent — a country redirect you always want — build it server-side and use this for campaign work. Size the plan on pageviews rather than visitors, and count per product: the FAQ says each product consumes its own pageview on the same page visit, so running a redirect and a popup together doubles the draw.

Geo Links is the exception, consuming a view on the link click instead. Treat the quota alerts as a real alarm. You are warned at 30%, 10%, 5% and 0%, then given two days to upgrade or re-subscribe before service stops. Use the 14-day trial to measure two things: your true pageview consumption, and whether the script adds visible delay before the right version appears.

Frequently asked questions

Why does the price I see differ from the one written here?

Because their pricing page is running a live split test, and on 19 September 2026 you had a coin-flip chance of being shown the higher set. The script that does it sits in the page's own source: visitors from 32 countries — the US, UK, Canada, Australia, New Zealand and 27 EU states — are assigned at random to one of two price sets and the choice is stored in a cookie for 30 days, so it is set once and then sticks. The second set is about a fifth higher, rounded to neat numbers: $12 becomes $14, $24 becomes $29, $49 becomes $59, $99 becomes $119, $199 becomes $239, and Enterprise starts at $419 instead of $349. Everyone outside those 32 countries sees the lower set. The figures in this review are the lower ones. Take the number on your own screen as the one that will be charged, and treat a quote from a colleague in another country as no evidence at all.

The visible page only says Enterprise is 'from $349'. What is actually above that?

Eight steps, and the page's own price table enumerates them even though the plan card does not. Monthly they run $349, $429, $519, $599, $699, $869, $1,299 and $1,739; billed yearly the monthly-equivalents are $279, $343, $466, $524, $583, $695, $1,039 and $1,391, with annual totals from $3,350 up to $16,694. What the page does not say is which pageview quota buys which step, so you cannot self-serve your way up the enterprise ladder or check a quote against it — but you now know the ceiling is roughly five times the entry step, which is worth knowing before a sales call frames $349 as the starting point of a short climb.

Is the yearly plan really twelve times the advertised monthly-equivalent?

Two dollars more, on four of the five standard tiers. The page advertises the yearly plans as $9, $19, $39, $79 and $159 a month, but the annual totals in its own price table are $108, $230, $470, $950 and $1,910. Only the cheapest is exact: $9 times twelve is $108. The rest are $228, $468, $948 and $1,908 by the arithmetic and $230, $470, $950 and $1,910 on the bill. It is two dollars and it is not a trap, but it tells you the monthly-equivalent is rounded down for display, so budget from the annual total rather than multiplying the number in the big type.

Does unused quota carry over if I underspend?

No, and their FAQ says so in the same breath as the refund policy: no refunds are provided and the quota does not roll over to the next billing cycle. That is what makes the yearly plans genuinely different rather than just cheaper — a year's quota is handed over up front, so a quiet March subsidises a busy November, which a monthly plan cannot do. It also means the yearly plan can run out early, and their answer to that is to re-subscribe and start a fresh cycle, not to top up. Size the yearly quota against your peak months, not your average.
