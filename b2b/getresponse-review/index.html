What GetResponse does

Email marketing with landing pages, automation and webinars in one suite, priced by contact count. Starter is €16/month (€13.12 billed annually) and allows a single automation workflow; unlimited workflows start on Marketer at €54. The better rates need long commitments.

The honest knock

Priced by contact count, so the entry number climbs as your list grows, and the best value only shows on long (annual/24-month) commitments. A solid all-rounder, but you lock in to get the good rate.

What buyers report about GetResponse

Recurring themes from G2, Capterra, Trustpilot and TrustRadius, read in August 2026. We have not run GetResponse ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • It replaces several tools with one bill — email, automation, webinars and landing pages together — which is why owners of small businesses stay. 4.3 on G2 across 1,127 reviews.
  • • Easy to learn, and cheaper than the venture-backed competitors at comparable features.
  • • On TrustRadius the visual campaign builder and the funnel builder get named repeatedly as best-in-class for the money, with 24/7 chat support treated as a genuine differentiator by small teams.
What they complain about
  • • Price escalation by contact count is the most common complaint on every platform. The $19 Starter figure is at 1,000 contacts; check the price at the list size you expect in a year.
  • • One automation workflow on Starter — if automation is why you are buying, that tier is not the product.
  • • A strict no-refund policy that reviewers say applies even when the fault is GetResponse's.
  • • Difficulty cancelling, and charges continuing after a cancellation request.
  • • The technical limits TrustRadius reviewers name are ones the marketing pages do not: the API was described as needing hacking around during a migration from another platform, and integration coverage is still thinner than reviewers want.

Where the ratings disagree. 4.2 on Trustpilot across 955 reviews, but the shape tells you more than the average: 63% five-star and 27% one-star, with almost nothing between. The one-star cluster is entirely billing and cancellation — not the product. The two platforms disagree because they were written at different times. TrustRadius's newest written GetResponse review dates from January 2024 and reads as a product conversation — builders, templates, API. Trustpilot's is current and splits 63% five-star against 27% one-star, with the one-star cluster almost entirely about billing and cancellation. Neither is wrong; the older platform simply predates the complaint.

When the paid plan is worth it

The free plan is a 14-day full-feature trial wearing a free-plan badge — after those 14 days the premium features stop and you are left with a genuinely thin tier: 1,000 landing-page visitors a month, AI tools capped at 3 uses, no dynamic segmentation, contact tagging and scoring restricted, webinars capped at 10 attendees, and GetResponse branding on everything you send. Read on their own pricing page on 2 August 2026, at 1,000 contacts: Starter €16 a month or €13.12 billed annually; Marketer €54 or €44.28; Creator €62 or €50.84; Enterprise quote-only. Annual saves 18%. Prices step up at 1k, 2.5k, 5k, 10k, 25k, 50k and 100k contacts, so your bill moves with list growth rather than with headcount. The limit that bites is automation. Starter gives one workflow and three users. That is enough for a newsletter and nothing else — the moment you want a welcome series and an abandoned-cart flow at the same time you are on Marketer, which is 3.4× the price. Decide whether you are buying a newsletter tool or a marketing automation platform, because the pricing treats those as different products.

How to actually use GetResponse

Use the 14 days on the automation, not on the email design. The templates will look fine on every plan; what you cannot judge later is whether one workflow is enough for you, and that is the €16-versus-€54 decision. Import your list before you build anything, because the price is set by contact count and the tier boundaries are at 1k, 2.5k, 5k and 10k. A list of 1,050 costs the same as one of 2,500 — clean out the dead addresses first and you may land a tier lower for a year. If webinars are why you are here, check the attendee caps before the tier price. Free allows 10, Creator 100, Enterprise 1,000. That ceiling, not the monthly fee, is what usually forces the upgrade. Is the free plan a real free plan?

Only for 14 days. After that the premium features switch off and what remains is thin: 1,000 landing-page visitors a month, AI tools capped at three uses, webinars capped at ten attendees, and GetResponse branding on what you send. Treat it as a trial with a free tier attached, not as a tier you can run on.

Is GetResponse worth it for a small team?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Small teams get the most out of this category when one clear problem is already costing real hours or revenue; buying ahead of that just adds cost and another login. Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.

Who should not use GetResponse?

Skip it if you only need basic broadcasts — a leaner tool costs less without the extra modules you won't use. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
