What Getscreen does

Browser-based remote desktop and remote-support software: connect to any computer with no client install, share one-click access links, transfer files and record sessions — for IT teams, MSPs and remote support.

When the paid plan is worth it

Free covers exactly two devices with a 50MB per-file limit, which works for a single side gig but breaks down the moment you manage a third machine or need to move a file bigger than that. The paid tiers price on two separate axes. There is a per-technician seat fee ($5.00/month per technician on Standard, dropping to $4.17/month when billed annually), and a separate per-device access fee ($0.10/month per device on Standard, $0.08/month annually).

So a small team supporting many devices pays for both the seats and the devices, not one flat number. Annual billing saves 16%, but the total still climbs with headcount and fleet size, which is where the payback math gets real for anyone scaling past a couple of technicians. Standard also skips quick-support invitations entirely, unlimited only from Advanced up, and caps file transfer at 4GB while video-recording storage stays at 1GB one tier above free.

None of that metering resets with a subscription upgrade, it keeps compounding every month. The $149 Personal Lifetime deal sidesteps that math altogether: 50 devices forever and 10 invitations a day for a one-time payment, but it is scoped to individual use rather than the technician-seat model teams actually need.

How to actually use Getscreen

Signup is free and needs no card, and business users can trial every feature free for 14 days, also with no card required. Getting to core value differs by use case. For a one-off support session, you send the other person a browser link, nothing to install on their side, since browser-based delivery is the whole pitch. For unattended access to a device you control, you install the agent app instead, available for Windows, macOS, Linux, and Android.

Then add the device to your list and set up technician access rights if you are working as a team. In practice the friction shows up during that install. On Mac, the remote screen stays blank until you manually grant Screen Recording and Accessibility permissions; on Windows, antivirus and firewall software commonly blocks the agent executable outright; and on Android the app needs security restrictions loosened to run at all.

Frequently asked questions

What does Getscreen cost for two technicians and 40 computers?

Both meters apply, so work it out per line. Read 23 September 2026 on getscreen.me/en/pricing/plans/, Standard on annual payment is 4.17 per technician and 0.08 per device a month: 2 x 4.17 plus 40 x 0.08 comes to 11.54 a month, about 138 a year. Advanced at 6.67 and 0.13 gives 18.54 a month; Enterprise at 8.33 and 0.17 gives 23.46. Without the annual discount Standard is 5.00 and 0.10, so 14.00 a month. The page prints these numerals in euros to a European visitor and in dollars to a US one. The step up to Advanced is not about scale: it is what you pay for unlimited client invitations, since Standard has no quick-support invitations at all, and for unlimited file size instead of 4 GB.

Is the Getscreen lifetime deal $149 or EUR 139?

Both, depending on where you open the page. Read 23 September 2026, the page as served from the server says $149, and a browser on a European connection replaces that with EUR 139. That is different from the business plans, where the same numerals simply switch symbol. For the money you get one personal licence with 50 devices permanently and 10 quick-support invitations a day, file transfers up to 4 GB and up to 1 GB of session recording. It is sold for single personal use, so it is not a way around the per-technician seat model if more than one person needs to connect.

Do I need Advanced to use the Getscreen API?

Yes. Read 23 September 2026, the comparison table leaves the HTTP API row empty for Standard and gives Advanced 100 requests per hour per device and Enterprise 1,000. The same step decides quick support, which Standard lacks entirely, and file transfers, capped at 4 GB on Standard and unlimited above it. So if your plan is to hook remote sessions into a helpdesk or RMM script, price Advanced from the start: 6.67 per technician plus 0.13 per device a month on annual payment, against 4.17 and 0.08 on Standard. Whether 100 requests per device per hour is enough depends on how often your integration polls, which the page cannot tell you; test it on the 14-day business trial, which needs no card.

Is an IT or productivity tool secure and reliable enough to depend on?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.
