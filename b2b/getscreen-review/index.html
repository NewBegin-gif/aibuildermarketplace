What Getscreen does

Browser-based remote desktop and remote-support software: connect to any computer with no client install, share one-click access links, transfer files and record sessions — for IT teams, MSPs and remote support.

Pricing (2026)

Free plan (2 devices); paid tiers add one-time quick connections (Advanced) and white-label, roles and mass-device management (Enterprise) — plus a one-time Personal Lifetime Deal around $149, which sidesteps the recurring fees of rivals like TeamViewer (~$50/mo). Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

The honest knock

AnyDesk tends to feel faster and lower-latency, and TeamViewer carries the heavier security/compliance certifications (HIPAA, SOC 2). Getscreen's real edge is being fully browser-based — nothing to install on the other person's machine — and cheaper, especially with the lifetime deal.

When the paid plan is worth it

Free covers exactly two devices with a 50MB per-file limit, which works for a single side gig but breaks down the moment you manage a third machine or need to move a file bigger than that. The paid tiers price on two separate axes. There is a per-technician seat fee ($5.00/month per technician on Standard, dropping to $4.17/month when billed annually), and a separate per-device access fee ($0.10/month per device on Standard, $0.08/month annually).

So a small team supporting many devices pays for both the seats and the devices, not one flat number. Annual billing saves 16%, but the total still climbs with headcount and fleet size, which is where the payback math gets real for anyone scaling past a couple of technicians. Standard also skips quick-support invitations entirely, unlimited only from Advanced up, and caps file transfer at 4GB while video-recording storage stays at 1GB one tier above free.

None of that metering resets with a subscription upgrade, it keeps compounding every month. The $149 Personal Lifetime deal sidesteps that math altogether: 50 devices forever and 10 invitations a day for a one-time payment, but it is scoped to individual use rather than the technician-seat model teams actually need.

How to actually use Getscreen

Signup is free and needs no card, and business users can trial every feature free for 14 days, also with no card required. Getting to core value differs by use case. For a one-off support session, you send the other person a browser link, nothing to install on their side, since browser-based delivery is the whole pitch. For unattended access to a device you control, you install the agent app instead, available for Windows, macOS, Linux, and Android, then add the device to your list and set up technician access rights if you are working as a team.

In practice the friction shows up during that install. On Mac, the remote screen stays blank until you manually grant Screen Recording and Accessibility permissions; on Windows, antivirus and firewall software commonly blocks the agent executable outright; and on Android the app needs security restrictions loosened to run at all.

Frequently asked questions

Is Getscreen secure and reliable enough to depend on?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.

Who should not use Getscreen?

Skip it if you need the absolute lowest latency (AnyDesk) or heavy compliance certifications like HIPAA/SOC 2 (TeamViewer). Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
