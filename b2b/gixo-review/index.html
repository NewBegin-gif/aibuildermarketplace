Full pricing notes (3 more paragraphs)

The FAQ says a team can add seats above each minimum. Prism and Folio still charge one price per plan for the seats it includes: Prism Starter $10 (1 seat, $90 a year), Professional $25 (2 seats, $250), Business $45 (5, $450), Enterprise $99 (10, $999); Folio Author $19 for 10 AI-drafted chapters a month and Pro $99 for 50, one seat each, with no annual figure.

What the price did

We read Gixo’s own pricing page on a schedule and keep every reading, so this is our record of the page. It is not a forecast, and not someone else’s summary. A change appears here only after we confirmed the new price on the page itself.

Four dated readings. On 19 August and 17 September 2026 the page published only a floor per product: $10 for Quill, Lumen and Prism, $19 for Business and Folio, $29 for Lex, $49 for Arc, and no tier above it. On 4 October 2026 the floors were the same and every product's full ladder was on the page, topping out at $129 a month for Quill Enterprise, $99 for Lumen and Prism Enterprise and Folio Pro, $79 for Business Enterprise, $229 for Lex Marketplace and $179 for Arc Enterprise. On 21 October 2026 the amounts were the same, but Quill, Lumen, Business, Lex and Arc had switched to per-seat pricing with a seat minimum per tier: on 4 October Quill Professional read $79 a month for 3 seats, on 21 October $79 per seat a month with a minimum of 3 seats. Prism and Folio kept one price per plan. The tier Gixo recommends moved down at the same time, from Professional to Starter on Quill, Lumen and Arc.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Gixo is priced per deliverable, not per company, and one product's plan does not include the others. A team that needs decks, articles and proposals buys Lumen plus Quill plus Arc: $69 a month for one person at the entry tiers, $133 at the Starter tiers Gixo now recommends, and at least $759 a month for three people on the Professional tiers that add shared review. There is no published bundle price; the only route the page names is procurement, where private offers and multi-product bundles go through review.

Ask for that before you subscribe to the second product, not after. The second is the seat minimums (read 21 October 2026). On 4 October Quill Professional was $79 a month for three seats; it is now $79 a seat with a three-seat minimum, and every tier above the one-seat tiers on Quill, Lumen, Business, Lex and Arc works the same way. The generation allowance behind each tier is still not stated outside Folio's chapter counts, and the first step is steep on Quill: Lite at $10 is pitched at publishing now and then, the next tier is $59. Run the 14-day trial at the volume you actually expect.

Where the ratings disagree. Treat this section as a warning about evidence rather than a verdict on the product. When the paid plan is worth it

Gixo is seven products sold separately: Quill for articles, Lumen for decks, Prism for visuals, Business for briefs, Folio for long-form, Lex for legal and Arc for proposals. Read on gixo.ai/pricing on 21 October 2026, the steps up buy seats, shared review and brand control, not a different engine, and on five of the seven products they are now priced per seat with a minimum. Lumen runs Lite $10 and Starter $25 for one seat, then Professional $45 a seat from 2 seats with sharing and comments, Business $55 from 5, Enterprise $99 from 10.

Quill jumps hardest: Lite $10 for occasional use, Starter $59 with Brand Voice, then Professional $79 a seat with Brand Kit, sharing and live review, minimum 3 seats, so $237 a month before anyone writes. Arc goes from $49 for one seat to $129 a seat from 2; Lex from a limited $29 pilot to Solo at $99. Prism keeps flat plan prices, $10 to $99 for 1 to 10 seats, and Folio is metered in chapters: Author $19 for 10 a month, Pro $99 for 50.

So upgrade when a second person has to review the work, not when the output disappoints: the page says each step keeps everything below it, and nothing in it promises better drafts. What upgrading does not fix is the product split. One plan never covers another product, there is still no published bundle, and the only route the page names is procurement review through Azure Marketplace or a private offer. Generation allowances per tier remain unpublished outside Folio.

Seven products sit behind one brand; the support centre maps them by job: Quill for publishing, Lumen for decks, Prism for visuals, Business for document-grounded briefs, Arc for RFP work, Lex for drafting and compliance, Folio for long-form. Trials are per product, not per account: Quill, Lumen, Business, Arc, Lex and Prism each run a 14-day trial with no card, while Folio's card sells monthly book plans with a free studio for planning and import.

    Upload your source files and brand rules on day one; the workflows are built around them, not a blank prompt. Buying through Azure Marketplace activates instantly and consolidates billing, the cleaner route if three card subscriptions is what procurement will not pass.

    What will Gixo actually cost me?

    Count seats before you count tiers. Read on gixo.ai/pricing on 21 October 2026, Quill, Lumen, Business, Lex and Arc are priced per seat per month, and every tier above the one-seat tiers has a seat minimum: Quill Professional is $79 a seat with at least 3 seats, so $237 a month or more; Lumen Professional $45 with at least 2 ($90); Arc Professional $129 with at least 2 ($258); Lex Small Firm $149 with at least 3 ($447). On 4 October the same figures bought the whole tier, so a team budget based on that reading is now two to ten times too low. The floors are unchanged, $10 for Quill, Lumen and Prism, $19 for Business and Folio, $29 for Lex and $49 for Arc, and Gixo now recommends the one-seat Starter tier on most products ($59 Quill, $25 Lumen, $49 Arc; Solo $99 on Lex). Prism and Folio still charge one price per plan. Generation per tier remains unpublished outside Folio's 10 or 50 chapters, so run the trial at real volume.

    Can I buy more than one Gixo product on a single plan?

    Not off the shelf. Each product is bought on its own plan and, as the FAQ put it on 17 September, 4 October and 21 October 2026, buying one product does not include the others. There is no published bundle price. The route the page names is procurement: Quill, Lumen, Business, Arc and Lex are listed on Microsoft Azure Marketplace, billed through your organisation's Microsoft billing account, and private offers and multi-product bundles are handled through procurement review. Stacked card subscriptions now add up per seat: one person on Lumen, Quill and Arc pays $69 a month at the entry tiers and $133 at the Starter tiers Gixo recommends, while a three-person team that needs shared review on all three pays at least $759 a month on the Professional tiers (3 seats each at $45, $79 and $129). Ask about the bundle before you subscribe to the second product, not after.

    Is there a free version, and what happens when the trial ends?

    There is a trial and no free tier, and the FAQ is unusually direct about it. Read 17 September, 4 October and 21 October 2026: a no-card 14-day trial with full generation, after which continued generation needs a paid plan, though existing work stays viewable in your workspace. The same answer states there is no standing unpaid tier. One caveat on Folio, the books product: the page contradicts itself. The card grid labels it “Monthly book plans” rather than a trial, while the comparison table directly below lists Folio as “14-day trial, no card” like the other six, and the introduction now adds a third version: Folio starts free for planning and import. If books are what you came for, check which of the two applies before you plan an evaluation around it. And note the clock is per product: evaluating three products means three separate 14-day trials on three separate timers.
