Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 17 Sep 2026

Gixo is not one product but seven, each sold separately, and the pricing page publishes only a floor for each. Quill (articles and content), Lumen (decks) and Prism (visuals and infographics) start at $10 a month. Business (briefs and decisions) and Folio (books and long-form) start at $19. Lex (legal and compliance) is from $29, and Arc (proposals from RFPs) from $49.

The page states prices are USD per month, billed monthly or annually, but publishes no annual rate and no tier ladder: it says the exact plan details sit on each product page, and the product pages read in August carried none, pointing instead at the plan list inside the app. The FAQ says a no-card 14-day trial with full generation, that continued generation after the trial needs a paid plan while existing work stays viewable, and that there is no standing unpaid tier.

The page contradicts itself on one product: the card grid labels Folio “Monthly book plans” while the comparison table below it lists Folio as “14-day trial, no card” like the other six. Quill, Lumen, Business, Arc and Lex can also be bought through Microsoft Azure Marketplace, and the page now states that private offers and multi-product bundles are handled through procurement review. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Gixo is priced per deliverable, not per company. So the moment a team needs decks and articles and a proposal it is buying Lumen plus Quill plus Arc — $10 + $10 + $49, or $828 a year at the published floors — rather than one plan. There is no published bundle price to fall back on; the only bundle route the page names is procurement, where it says private offers and multi-product bundles are handled through review.

Ask for that before you subscribe to the second product, not after. The second is that every number above is a floor and nothing else is public. “Plans from $10/month” is all Gixo publishes: what a higher tier costs, and what the entry tier actually allows in generations, seats or exports, is on neither the pricing page nor the product pages. You find that out inside the product. So run the 14-day trial at the volume you actually expect rather than on a sample, because the tier you land on is the price you will pay, and right now it is a guess.

When the paid plan is worth it

There is no premium plan to weigh here, and that is the thing to understand before you compare Gixo to anything else. It is seven products sold separately: Quill for articles, Lumen for decks, Prism for visuals, Business for briefs, Folio for long-form, Lex for legal and Arc for proposals. The pricing page publishes only a floor for each -- from $10 for Quill, Lumen and Prism, from $19 for Business and Folio, from $29 for Lex and from $49 for Arc.

There is no published bundle price, so a team that wants articles, decks and visuals is looking at three subscriptions rather than one, and the from-prices add up before you have seen a single actual tier. The tiers themselves are not published anywhere readable. The pricing page says exact plan details sit on each product page; the product pages read in August carried none, pointing at the plan list inside the app.

So the honest position is that the real cost is unknown until you sign up, and the $10 is a starting point rather than a price. Upgrading does not fix that: nothing is published to upgrade to. What you can use without committing: a 14-day no-card trial with full generation, after which the FAQ says continued generation needs a paid plan while existing work stays viewable, and no standing unpaid tier.

Five of the seven are on Azure Marketplace, where the page says private offers and multi-product bundles go through procurement review -- the only bundle route it names.

How to actually use Gixo

The first decision is which Gixo you are buying. Seven products sit behind one brand; the support centre maps them by job: Quill for publishing, Lumen for decks, Prism for visuals, Business for document-grounded briefs, Arc for RFP work, Lex for drafting and compliance, Folio for long-form. Trials are per product, not per account: Quill, Lumen, Business, Arc, Lex and Prism each run a 14-day trial with no card, while Folio has none.

Upload your source files and brand rules on day one; the workflows are built around them, not a blank prompt. Buying through Azure Marketplace activates instantly and consolidates billing -- the cleaner route if three card subscriptions is what procurement will not pass.

Frequently asked questions

What will Gixo actually cost me?

Nobody outside the product can tell you, and that is the most useful thing to know before you start. Read on gixo.ai/pricing on 17 September 2026, the page publishes a floor per product and nothing else: Quill, Lumen and Prism from $10 a month, Business and Folio from $19, Lex from $29, Arc from $49, in USD, billed monthly or annually. What a higher tier costs, and what the entry tier allows in generations, seats or exports, is on neither the pricing page nor the product pages — the page says the exact plan details sit on each product page, and those pages point at the plan list inside the app. No annual rate is published either, despite annual billing being offered. So treat the floor as an entry ticket, not a budget, and run the trial at the volume you actually expect rather than on a sample.

Can I buy more than one Gixo product on a single plan?

Not off the shelf. Each product is bought on its own plan and, as the FAQ put it on 17 September 2026, buying one product does not include the others. There is no published bundle price. The one route the page does name is procurement: Quill, Lumen, Business, Arc and Lex are listed on Microsoft Azure Marketplace, and the page says private offers and multi-product bundles are handled through procurement review. That is worth using rather than stacking card subscriptions, because the arithmetic gets ugly fast — a team that needs decks, articles and proposals is buying Lumen plus Quill plus Arc, $69 a month or $828 a year at the published floors, before anyone has seen a real tier. Ask about the bundle before you subscribe to the second product, not after.

Is there a free version, and what happens when the trial ends?

There is a trial and no free tier, and the FAQ is unusually direct about it. Read 17 September 2026: a no-card 14-day trial with full generation, after which continued generation needs a paid plan, though existing work stays viewable in your workspace. The same answer states there is no standing unpaid tier. One caveat on Folio, the books product: the page contradicts itself. The card grid labels it “Monthly book plans” rather than a trial, while the comparison table directly below lists Folio as “14-day trial, no card” like the other six. If books are what you came for, check which of the two applies before you plan an evaluation around it. And note the clock is per product: evaluating three products means three separate 14-day trials on three separate timers.
