Pricing (2026)

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Gixo is priced per deliverable, not per company, and one product's plan does not include the others. A team that needs decks, articles and proposals buys Lumen plus Quill plus Arc: $69 a month at the one-seat entry tiers, and $253 a month at the tiers Gixo itself recommends for each (Lumen Professional $45, Quill Professional $79, Arc Professional $129). There is no published bundle price; the only route the page names is procurement, where private offers and multi-product bundles go through review.

Ask for that before you subscribe to the second product, not after. The second is the shape of the ladders, now that they are public (read 4 October 2026). The steps buy seats, review and brand control; the generation allowance behind each tier is still not stated outside Folio's chapter counts. And the first step is steep on Quill: Lite at $10 is pitched at publishing now and then, the next tier is $59.

Run the 14-day trial at the volume you actually expect, because that volume decides which of those two prices you pay.

When the paid plan is worth it

Gixo is seven products sold separately: Quill for articles, Lumen for decks, Prism for visuals, Business for briefs, Folio for long-form, Lex for legal and Arc for proposals. Since our September reading each one has a published ladder, so the premium question finally has an answer per product. Read on gixo.ai/pricing on 4 October 2026, the steps mostly buy seats and collaboration, not a different engine. Lumen runs Lite $10 and Starter $25 for one seat, Professional $45 for two with sharing and comments, Business $55 for five with brand themes, Enterprise $99 for ten.

Quill jumps hardest: Lite $10 for occasional use, then Starter $59, Professional $79 for three seats with Brand Voice and live review, Business $99, Enterprise $129. Arc goes from $49 for one seat to $129 for two, $149 for five and $179 for ten; Lex from a limited $29 pilot to Solo at $99. Folio is the exception, metered in chapters: Author $19 for 10 AI-drafted chapters a month, Pro $99 for 50.

So upgrade when a second person has to review the work, not when the output disappoints: the page says each step keeps everything below it and adds seats, shared review or brand control, and nothing in it promises better drafts. What upgrading also does not fix is the product split. One plan never covers another product, and there is still no published bundle; the only route the page names is procurement review through Azure Marketplace or a private offer. Generation allowances per tier remain unpublished outside Folio.

How to actually use Gixo

The first decision is which Gixo you are buying. Seven products sit behind one brand; the support centre maps them by job: Quill for publishing, Lumen for decks, Prism for visuals, Business for document-grounded briefs, Arc for RFP work, Lex for drafting and compliance, Folio for long-form. Trials are per product, not per account: Quill, Lumen, Business, Arc, Lex and Prism each run a 14-day trial with no card, while Folio's card sells monthly book plans with a free studio for planning and import.

Upload your source files and brand rules on day one; the workflows are built around them, not a blank prompt. Buying through Azure Marketplace activates instantly and consolidates billing -- the cleaner route if three card subscriptions is what procurement will not pass.

Frequently asked questions

What will Gixo actually cost me?

More than the headline, and for the first time you can work out how much. Read on gixo.ai/pricing on 4 October 2026, every product now publishes its ladder, in USD per month with an annual figure. The floors are unchanged, $10 for Quill, Lumen and Prism, $19 for Business and Folio, $29 for Lex and $49 for Arc, but they are one-seat entry tiers, and the tier Gixo recommends costs between roughly two and a half and eight times as much: Quill Professional $79 for three seats, Lumen Professional $45 for two, Arc Professional $129 for two, Lex Solo $99. The steps mostly add seats and review. What the page still does not quantify is how much generation each tier allows, except Folio's 10 or 50 chapters a month. So budget at the tier that matches the number of reviewers you actually have, and run the trial at real volume to learn whether the entry tier's allowance holds.

Can I buy more than one Gixo product on a single plan?

Not off the shelf. Each product is bought on its own plan and, as the FAQ put it on 17 September and again on 4 October 2026, buying one product does not include the others. There is no published bundle price. The one route the page does name is procurement: Quill, Lumen, Business, Arc and Lex are listed on Microsoft Azure Marketplace, and the page says private offers and multi-product bundles are handled through procurement review. That is worth using rather than stacking card subscriptions, because the arithmetic gets ugly fast — a team that needs decks, articles and proposals is buying Lumen plus Quill plus Arc, $69 a month at the one-seat entry tiers and $253 at the three tiers Gixo recommends, now that the real tiers are published. Ask about the bundle before you subscribe to the second product, not after.

Is there a free version, and what happens when the trial ends?

There is a trial and no free tier, and the FAQ is unusually direct about it. Read 17 September and 4 October 2026: a no-card 14-day trial with full generation, after which continued generation needs a paid plan, though existing work stays viewable in your workspace. The same answer states there is no standing unpaid tier. One caveat on Folio, the books product: the page contradicts itself. The card grid labels it “Monthly book plans” rather than a trial, while the comparison table directly below lists Folio as “14-day trial, no card” like the other six, and the introduction now adds a third version: Folio starts free for planning and import. If books are what you came for, check which of the two applies before you plan an evaluation around it. And note the clock is per product: evaluating three products means three separate 14-day trials on three separate timers.

What should I weigh before buying Design & Content software?

Up to a point, and that point is usually set by your plan. Check whether brand kits, custom fonts, colour locking and template controls sit on the tier you would actually buy, since these are common upsells. Test it with your own assets during the trial rather than the demo content, which is always chosen to flatter the output.
