What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Your bill tracks how busy the app is rather than how many people own a licence. A write-heavy workflow can pass the included 5,000 updates and add 2 cents each without anyone changing plan. The jump from Maker to Business is also steep: $49 to $199 per month on annual billing, and the row ceiling only moves from 25k to 100k. Per Glide's partner team on 18 August 2026, those ceilings track the data source rather than the plan.

25k applies to synced spreadsheet sources whatever you pay, and the 100k+ figures belong to their Big Table source. So an upgrade alone does not lift a spreadsheet-backed app past 25k rows. The pricing page is hard to read for a reason Glide does not dispute. Asked about the Individual and Business tabs, the same team called them audience segmentation. They agreed on 18 August 2026 that the comparison tables confuse, because they never put all plans side by side.

Worth knowing too that the pricing above is for the classic Glide builder. GlideOS, the AI-era product Glide advertises on its own homepage, is a separate and newer thing. So do not assume a feature you saw demoed there is included in the plan you are buying. Glide told its partners on 12 August 2026 that Classic is not being retired. The two products co-exist, and Classic stays on sale, supported and updated, so buying it is not a bet on a platform being wound down.

What does change is where you land: glideapps.com now leads with GlideOS, and the Classic pages sit under /classic.

What buyers report about Glide

What they complain about
  • • It caps at 25,000 rows. One team that grew a Glide app to $100k a year had to rebuild natively — a six-figure rebuild that the row ceiling made unavoidable.
  • • Per-user pricing from $25/month adds up faster than comparable no-code tools.
  • • No native mobile app and no App Store route, and no native push notifications — you fall back on email or Slack.
  • • You build inside Glide's components, not your own architecture. Matching a brand exactly is hard, and unusual requirements hit a wall.

If you are pricing the tool you have read about for years, the main pricing page is not it. On Classic, re-read on 3 September 2026 and unchanged: a genuinely free tier (unlimited drafts, one editor, up to 25k rows), Explorer at $19 a month billed yearly or $25 monthly, Maker at $49 or $60, and Business at $199 yearly-billed or $249 monthly with unlimited apps, 30 users included, 5,000 updates and up to 100k rows.

Note the seat arithmetic. $199 across 30 included users is $6.63 a head, while extra users cost $5 on annual billing, so a growing team gets better value rather than worse. How to actually use Glide

Work out which product you are buying first, because Glide now sells two. glideapps.com/pricing shows GlideOS, the newer AI builder on its own separate subscription; the classic spreadsheet-to-app builder lives at glideapps.com/classic/pricing. A feature you saw demonstrated on the homepage is not necessarily in the plan you are about to buy. Glide told its partners on 12 August 2026 that Classic is not being retired, and stays on sale, supported and updated.

So choosing it is not a bet on a product being wound down. Then settle your row ceiling by moving your data, not your plan. Per Glide's own partner team on 18 August 2026, the 25,000-row limit applies to synced spreadsheet sources regardless of what you pay, and the 100,000-plus figures belong to their Big Table source. So an app backed by a spreadsheet does not get more rows by upgrading — it gets them by changing where the data lives.

Estimate updates, not just seats, because that is the meter people miss. Business includes 5,000 updates a month and charges two cents each beyond that. Thirty users making ten writes a day on twenty-one working days is 6,300 — so a perfectly ordinary internal app passes the allowance without anyone changing plan. Work out writes per user per day before you choose. Use the free Classic tier to build something real first: unlimited drafts, one editor and up to 25,000 rows is enough to prove the concept.

Take the 14-day Business trial only once you know your user and update counts. Extra users are $5 a month on annual billing, and VAT is added at checkout.

Frequently asked questions

What should I weigh before buying IT & Productivity software?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.
