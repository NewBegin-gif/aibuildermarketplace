What Glide does

No-code app builder — now sold as Glide Classic, next to the newer GlideOS — that turns spreadsheets and databases into working business apps: internal tools, client portals and field apps, with workflows and an API.

Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 4 Aug 2026

(re-verified 2026-08-04, read in a live browser session on both pricing pages, both tabs and both billing toggles) The classic spreadsheet-to-app builder still has a genuinely free tier: unlimited drafts, one editor, up to 25k rows. Its paid plans are unchanged, but they now live on glideapps.com/classic/pricing instead of the main pricing page. On the individual tab, Explorer starts at $19 per month billed yearly or $25 billed monthly (one app, 100 personal users, 250 updates, up to 25k rows) and Maker at $49 per month billed yearly or $60 monthly.

On the business tab, Business starts at $199 per month billed yearly or $249 monthly, with unlimited apps, 30 users included, 5,000 updates and up to 100k rows. Extra users are $5 each on annual billing and $6 on monthly, and extra updates cost 2 cents each. Enterprise is quote-only. VAT is added at checkout, and Business comes with a 14-day free trial.

Glide now sells two products from two pages. What glideapps.com/pricing shows today is GlideOS, the newer AI builder, on its own separate subscription. Free carries one project and limited credits. Solo is $25 per month with 5 projects and credit bundles from 100 to 10,000 per month. Team is $125 per month for 5 members, with $10 per additional member and 50 credits included per member.

Enterprise is quote-only. Glide states that annual GlideOS plans are coming soon, so the GlideOS prices above are monthly only. One caveat on the row ceilings, from Glide's own partner team on 18 August 2026. The 25k limit applies to synced spreadsheet sources regardless of plan type, and the 100k+ figures apply to their Big Table data source. So which ceiling you hit is set by where the data lives as much as by what you pay.

They also confirmed the Individual and Business tabs on the pricing page are audience segmentation rather than two halves of one plan ladder. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Your bill tracks how busy the app is rather than how many people own a licence. A write-heavy workflow can pass the included 5,000 updates and add 2 cents each without anyone changing plan. The jump from Maker to Business is also steep: $49 to $199 per month on annual billing, and the row ceiling only moves from 25k to 100k. Per Glide's partner team on 18 August 2026, those ceilings track the data source rather than the plan.

25k applies to synced spreadsheet sources whatever you pay, and the 100k+ figures belong to their Big Table source. So an upgrade alone does not lift a spreadsheet-backed app past 25k rows. The pricing page is hard to read for a reason Glide does not dispute. Asked about the Individual and Business tabs, the same team called them audience segmentation. They agreed on 18 August 2026 that the comparison tables confuse, because they never put all plans side by side.

Worth knowing too that the pricing above is for the classic Glide builder. GlideOS, the AI-era product Glide advertises on its own homepage, is a separate and newer thing. So do not assume a feature you saw demoed there is included in the plan you are buying. Glide told its partners on 12 August 2026 that Classic is not being retired. The two products co-exist, and Classic stays on sale, supported and updated, so buying it is not a bet on a platform being wound down.

What does change is where you land: glideapps.com now leads with GlideOS, and the Classic pages sit under /classic.

What buyers report about Glide

Recurring themes from G2 and practitioner reviews, read in August 2026. We have not run Glide ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Speed to a working app. Reviewers describe going from idea to a usable internal tool in an afternoon, with no code.
  • • Genuinely usable free tier and a clean interface.
What they complain about
  • • It caps at 25,000 rows. One team that grew a Glide app to $100k a year had to rebuild natively — a six-figure rebuild that the row ceiling made unavoidable.
  • • Per-user pricing from $25/month adds up faster than comparable no-code tools.
  • • No native mobile app and no App Store route, and no native push notifications — you fall back on email or Slack.
  • • You build inside Glide's components, not your own architecture. Matching a brand exactly is hard, and unusual requirements hit a wall.

When the paid plan is worth it

Before comparing tiers, make sure you are looking at the right product. Glide now sells two things from two pages. The classic spreadsheet-to-app builder lives on glideapps.com/classic/pricing. Meanwhile glideapps.com/pricing -- the address most people reach for -- shows GlideOS, the newer AI builder, on its own separate subscription. If you are pricing the tool you have read about for years, the main pricing page is not it.

On Classic: there is a genuinely free tier (unlimited drafts, one editor, up to 25k rows). Explorer is $19 a month billed yearly or $25 monthly for one app, 100 personal users and 250 updates; Maker is $49 or $60. Business is $199 yearly-billed or $249 monthly, with unlimited apps, 30 users included, 5,000 updates and up to 100k rows. Note the seat arithmetic there. $199 across 30 included users is $6.63 a head, while extra users cost $5 on annual billing. So additional people are cheaper than the ones you already have, and a growing team gets better value rather than worse. Extra updates run 2 cents each.

The ceiling that catches people is not on the price list at all. We have it from Glide's own partner team on 18 August 2026: the 25k row limit applies to synced spreadsheet sources regardless of which plan you buy, while the 100k+ figures apply to their Big Table data source. Which ceiling you hit is therefore set by where your data lives, not by what you pay -- upgrading will not raise a limit that comes from using a synced sheet. They also confirmed the Individual and Business tabs are audience segmentation rather than two halves of one ladder.

On GlideOS: Free with one project, Solo $25 a month with 5 projects, Team $125 for 5 members with $10 per additional member. The first five members cost $25 each and every one after that costs $10, so a team of eight pays less per head than a team of five. Glide says annual GlideOS plans are coming, so those prices are monthly only for now. VAT is added at checkout and Business carries a 14-day trial.

How to actually use Glide

Work out which product you are buying first, because Glide now sells two. glideapps.com/pricing shows GlideOS, the newer AI builder on its own separate subscription; the classic spreadsheet-to-app builder lives at glideapps.com/classic/pricing. A feature you saw demonstrated on the homepage is not necessarily in the plan you are about to buy. Glide told its partners on 12 August 2026 that Classic is not being retired, and stays on sale, supported and updated. So choosing it is not a bet on a product being wound down.

Then settle your row ceiling by moving your data, not your plan. Per Glide's own partner team on 18 August 2026, the 25,000-row limit applies to synced spreadsheet sources regardless of what you pay, and the 100,000-plus figures belong to their Big Table source. So an app backed by a spreadsheet does not get more rows by upgrading — it gets them by changing where the data lives.

Estimate updates, not just seats, because that is the meter people miss. Business includes 5,000 updates a month and charges two cents each beyond that. Thirty users making ten writes a day on twenty-one working days is 6,300 — so a perfectly ordinary internal app passes the allowance without anyone changing plan. Work out writes per user per day before you choose.

Use the free Classic tier to build something real first: unlimited drafts, one editor and up to 25,000 rows is enough to prove the concept. Take the 14-day Business trial only once you know your user and update counts. Extra users are $5 a month on annual billing, and VAT is added at checkout.

Frequently asked questions

What should I weigh before buying a tool like this?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.
