What Goflow does

Multichannel e-commerce operations: orders, inventory, listings and shipping across all your marketplaces in one dashboard. Goflow Core is free forever (500 orders, 3 users) and automates Amazon MCF fulfilment for TikTok Shop orders; the vendor states eligible orders may qualify for 35% savings on Amazon MCF rates (vendor mail, 27 Jul 2026). Goflow lists 250+ integrations across marketplaces, shipping, 3PL and accounting (goflow.com, 12 Aug 2026), and a nocnoc integration keeps Latin American listings, inventory, orders and fulfilment in sync from inside Goflow (vendor mail, 5 Aug 2026).

Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 12 Aug 2026

(2026, re-verified 12 Aug 2026 on goflow.com/pricing) Goflow Core is free forever at $0/mo with no credit card, covering 500 orders, 3 users, 1,000 newly created listings and every Goflow feature. Paid tiers add unlimited channels, users, integrations and listings — Launch $499/mo (750 orders), Scale $1,449/mo (10,000 orders plus 100 wholesale/EDI orders), Enterprise custom. Billing is month-to-month with no setup or implementation fees, but EDI, SFTP and AS2 connections carry a one-time setup and testing fee of typically $500-1,300 per connection.

There is no free trial and no discount programme — the free Core plan is the trial (vendor mail, 12 Aug 2026). Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The catch is the cliff above it: orders past the Core allowance are paused until you upgrade, Goflow states you cannot move back down to Core once you go paid, and the next step up is $499/mo. Budget the EDI setup separately: $500-1,300 one-time per connection, multiplied by the number of retail partners.

What buyers report about Goflow

Recurring themes from G2, Capterra, Software Advice and Research.com, read in August 2026. We have not run Goflow ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Integration breadth is the recurring praise — Amazon, eBay, Walmart, Target, Shopify and Magento plus EDI, 3PL and ERP; Goflow's own site lists 250+ integrations (checked 12 Aug 2026). For a genuinely multi-channel seller that breadth is the point.
  • • Reviewers running it for years describe it as easy to learn, with updates shipping constantly.
What they complain about
  • • Paid plans start at $499/month and scale with order volume. The free Core plan stops at 500 orders, 3 users and 1,000 created listings, and reviewers below roughly that volume say the automation depth does not pay for itself until you cross into a paid tier.
  • • One reviewer describes a refund dispute where their bank confirmed Goflow's payment processor blocked an ACH dispute, leading them to file a formal fraud complaint with the New York Attorney General's Internet and Technology Bureau. That is one customer's account and an open allegation, not a finding — but it is the kind of thing to weigh before committing at this price.

When the paid plan is worth it

Look at the order allowances side by side before anything else, because they explain what you are actually buying. Core is free forever and covers 500 orders, 3 users and 1,000 newly created listings -- with every Goflow feature included. Launch is $499 a month and covers 750 orders. So the first paid tier adds 250 orders for $499, which makes no sense at all if you read it as a volume upgrade.

It is not one. What Launch buys is unlimited channels, users, integrations and listings. The order count is almost incidental; the ceiling that pushes you off the free plan is far more likely to be the 3-user cap or the 1,000-listing cap than the 500 orders. Work out which of those three you will hit first, because that is the one that sets your timing.

At volume the arithmetic turns around properly: Scale at $1,449 covers 10,000 orders plus 100 wholesale or EDI orders, which is about 14 cents an order against 67 cents on Launch. The gap between Launch and Scale is where the value is, not the gap between free and Launch.

Two things to budget separately. EDI, SFTP and AS2 connections carry a one-time setup and testing fee of typically $500 to $1,300 per connection -- with several trading partners that is a real line. And there is no free trial and no discount programme: the vendor told us on 12 August 2026 that the free Core plan is the trial. Billing is month-to-month with no setup or implementation fees otherwise.

How to actually use Goflow

Run your real operation on Core, because there is no free trial and Core is the trial — free forever at $0, covering 500 orders, 3 users, 1,000 newly created listings and every feature in the platform. You are not evaluating a cut-down version; you are evaluating the whole thing at low volume.

Know the two cliffs before you approach them. Orders past the Core allowance are paused rather than billed, so hitting 500 stops your fulfilment instead of costing you money — watch the counter, especially in a peak month. And Goflow states you cannot move back down to Core once you go paid, so the upgrade is one-directional even though billing is month-to-month.

Time that upgrade on confidence rather than on a single busy month, because the marginal cost at the bottom of Launch is severe: $499 buys 750 orders where Core gave you 500, so those first 250 extra orders cost $2 each. At Launch's full 750 you are at $0.67 an order and at Scale's 10,000 you are at $0.14 — the plan only becomes good value once you are genuinely using it.

Budget EDI separately and early. There are no setup or implementation fees on the software, but EDI, SFTP and AS2 connections carry a one-time setup and testing fee of typically $500 to $1,300 each. Multiply that by the number of retail partners you trade with before you compare Goflow to anything.

The natural comparison is Linnworks or Extensiv — the multichannel-ops peer set — compare at your order volume and count the EDI connections you'll actually need. Decide by which one fits the job above, not by the louder brand.
