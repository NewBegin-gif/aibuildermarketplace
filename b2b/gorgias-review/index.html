What Gorgias does

Helpdesk built for ecommerce: tickets, automations and an AI agent, priced by volume rather than by seat.

Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 1 Aug 2026

(2026-08-01, read on their own pricing page) Not priced per agent — their own line. Two meters that stack. Starter is $40/month all in: a $10/month helpdesk with 50 tickets included and $0.40 per ticket beyond, plus the AI Agent at $30/month with 30 automated interactions and $1.50 per interaction beyond. Basic is $90/month month-to-month or $77/month billed annually ($924 a year), including 300 tickets and 30 automated interactions, with the same $0.40 and $1.50 overage rates.

Higher tiers scale the ticket allowance; their own framing is 50 to 5,000 tickets a month. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

The honest knock

Never priced per agent is a real advantage and worth saying plainly: your whole team can be in the inbox without the bill moving. The cost sits on two meters instead, and both bite. Tickets beyond your allowance are $0.40 each, so a bad week costs money rather than attention. And the AI agent — the thing the homepage leads with — is a separate $30 a month on the entry plan, which is three times the helpdesk it sits on, with automated interactions past 30 charged at $1.50 each.

So the honest entry price for the product as advertised is $40, not $10. Count a normal month's tickets and a busy month's, and price both.

What buyers report about Gorgias

Recurring themes from G2, Capterra and practitioner reviews, read in August 2026. We have not run Gorgias ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • It bills per ticket, not per seat, so your whole company can log in without buying licences. Reviewers name that as the reason it fits collaborative teams.
  • • Fast to set up, and the partner ecosystem is the largest in ecommerce support.
What they complain about
  • • Per-ticket billing means your cost tracks volume, not headcount — and it is genuinely hard to forecast. There is a cliff between Basic (300 tickets, $60/mo) and Pro (2,000 tickets, $360/mo), so stores landing at 400-800 tickets pay the most per ticket of anyone.
  • • The AI Agent costs about $1.00 per resolved conversation ($0.90 annual) on top of the ticket, and reviewers report it double-billing. It is Shopify-only.
  • • Voice and SMS carry their own per-ticket charges of roughly $0.40 to $1.20.
  • • Reviewers report costs rising over time while support responsiveness fell. Reporting is shallow for data-heavy teams.

When the paid plan is worth it

This is not priced per agent -- their own line -- and instead runs two meters that stack, so work out both before comparing anything. Starter is $40 a month all in: a $10 helpdesk with 50 tickets included and $0.40 per ticket beyond, plus the AI Agent at $30 with 30 automated interactions and $1.50 per interaction beyond. Basic is $90 month-to-month or $77 billed annually ($924 a year), including 300 tickets and 30 interactions at the same overage rates.

The crossover is calculable and it comes sooner than you would guess. On Starter, tickets beyond the first 50 cost $0.40, so reaching Basic’s $90 takes 125 extra tickets: at 175 tickets a month the two plans cost the same, and above that Starter is the more expensive option. If your desk handles more than about 175 tickets, move up -- and if it handles fewer than 50, note that Starter’s included allowance already makes each ticket cost 20 cents rather than the 40 the overage rate suggests.

The AI meter deserves separate attention because it does not scale with the tiers: both Starter and Basic include exactly 30 automated interactions, and every one after that is $1.50. That is nearly four times the cost of a human-handled ticket, so an AI agent that works well is the line most likely to surprise you. Model it on expected automation rate, not on ticket count.

Higher tiers scale the ticket allowance; the vendor frames the ladder as 50 to 5,000 tickets a month.

How to actually use Gorgias

Count a normal month's tickets and a busy month's, separately, because the overage is where this bills. Tickets past your allowance are $0.40 each, so a bad week costs you money rather than just attention — and the allowances are small at the bottom: 50 tickets on Starter, 300 on Basic.

Do the crossover before you pick. Starter at $40 plus $0.40 a ticket reaches Basic's annual $77 at about 143 tickets a month — so below that Starter is cheaper and above it Basic is, by a widening margin. At 300 tickets Starter would cost $140 against Basic's $77.

Price the product as advertised rather than the headline. The AI agent is a separate $30 a month on the entry plan — three times the $10 helpdesk it sits on — so the honest entry price for what the homepage sells is $40, not $10. Automated interactions past the included 30 are $1.50 each, so twenty extra doubles your bill.

Then take the advantage where it is real: this is not priced per agent, which their own page says plainly. Put your whole team in the inbox, including the people who only answer occasionally — it costs nothing and it is the main reason to choose this over a per-seat helpdesk.

Frequently asked questions

Is Gorgias secure and reliable enough to depend on?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.

Who should not use Gorgias?

Skip it if your volume is steady and low — 50 tickets a month is the entry allowance, and below that a shared inbox costs nothing; also skip the AI agent until you know your automated-interaction count, since that is the meter that runs away.
