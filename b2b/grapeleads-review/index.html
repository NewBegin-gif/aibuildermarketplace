Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 12 Sep 2026

Pay-as-you-go credit packs, bought once rather than subscribed to: Freelancer $28 for 50 credits ($0.56 each), Startup $55 for 100 credits ($0.54) and Agency $127 for 250 credits ($0.51). Bulk pricing is on request and signing up gives 20 free credits. Credits measure work, not leads: one credit starts a search and one more is charged for every filtered result, so a search that returns 20 matching businesses costs 21 credits.

The vendor also states that unused credits carry a 50% bonus when its 2.0 app ships. Mind the URL — the price list lives at /buy-credits/ while the /pricing link 404s, which is how we had this down as unpriced until now. One rounding note: the page prints $0.54 per credit for the Startup pack, but $55 for 100 credits is $0.55 — their own figure is a cent optimistic. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

When the paid plan is worth it

There is no subscription at all — you buy credit packs once: $28 for 50 credits, $55 for 100, or $127 for 250. Per credit the page prints $0.56, $0.54 and $0.51, so the volume discount across a fivefold jump is about nine per cent. Buying the big pack to save money is not the argument here. The argument is what a credit actually buys, and this is the part that changes the sums entirely.

One credit starts a search and one more is charged for every filtered result it returns, so a search that finds twenty businesses costs twenty-one credits. Read the packs through that lens and they shrink: the $28 Freelancer pack is about TWO searches of that size, Startup is four, and the $127 Agency pack is eleven. The twenty free credits you get on signup are one search, not a trial period.

That shape has a practical consequence: you are billed per result, not per usable lead. A broad filter burns credits whether or not the businesses it returns are worth calling, so the discipline that saves money here is narrowing the filter before you run it, not buying more credits. If half of a twenty-result search is worth a call, your real cost is about $1.20 per usable lead. Which makes the small pack the right first purchase rather than the expensive one.

$28 buys enough to judge the data on your own patch — the niche is Google-Maps businesses without a website, and whether that list is any good for your area is the only question that matters. One note on finding the prices at all: they live at /buy-credits/ while the site's own /pricing link 404s, which is why we had this down as unpriced for longer than we should have.

How to actually use GrapeLeads

Spend the 20 free credits on one narrow search rather than a broad one, because you are billed per result rather than per usable lead. One credit opens the search and one more is charged for every business it returns, so a query that returns twenty costs twenty-one credits whether or not any of them are worth calling. Tighten the geography and the category hard before you run it. Then judge the data, since the niche is the product: these are Google-Maps businesses without websites.

Take five of your twenty results and check them by hand — is there genuinely no site, is the phone number live, is the business still trading? Your hit rate on those five is what converts the credit price into a cost per usable lead. At $0.56 a credit, twenty-one credits is $11.76; if five are worth contacting, that is $2.35 each. Buy the smallest pack that answers the question.

Freelancer is $28 for 50 credits, which is cheap enough to test properly, and there is no subscription at all — so this is the wrong shape if you want volume prospecting at a predictable monthly cost, and the right shape for occasional targeted searches. What should I weigh before buying Sales & CRM software?

Most tools in this category plug in rather than replace. Check which direction the data flows and whether the sync is genuinely two-way, because a one-way push creates a second source of truth that your reps will quietly stop trusting. If it does aim to replace your CRM, price the migration honestly: history, custom fields and reporting rarely move across cleanly.
