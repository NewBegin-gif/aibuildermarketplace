Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 10 Sep 2026

One plan, called Complete, at $25 a month, billed in USD every 30 days, with a 14-day free trial and no free tier. There is no response limit and no per-response charge: unlimited post-purchase, NPS, CSAT and attribution responses are included at that price, on every Shopify plan. The single price also covers revenue and AOV reporting per marketing channel, targeting by order value, product, country, payment method and customer, delivery on-site, by email and on Shopify POS, exports to Shopify Flow, Klaviyo and GA4, and querying your survey data with your own AI assistant through the vendor's MCP server. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

There is no free tier and no small plan, so from day fifteen the app costs $25 a month whether it collects four responses or forty thousand — a low-volume store pays the same as a high-volume one, and several of the nearest alternatives do publish a free plan. The second thing to weigh is access rather than price: the listing's data section asks for customer data including sensitive, device and activity data, plus orders, staff accounts and the online store, which is a lot of reach for a survey tool and worth reading the privacy policy over.

The evidence base is otherwise good for a Shopify app — launched 17 August 2017 by Union Works Apps in London, and 222 reviews on 10 September 2026 of which 215 are five-star and none below four.

There is nothing to upgrade to

One plan at $25 a month, nothing above it and nothing below it, so the only decision left is Grapevine against the meter its rivals run. Its own pricing page makes that argument openly: unlimited surveys and unlimited responses, every integration from the moment you install, the same $25 whether you collect fifty responses a month or fifty thousand, and no inflated monthly rate designed to push you onto an annual contract.

Do that comparison at your own volume, because a flat fee is the expensive option at the bottom of the range. A store collecting a handful of answers a month can sit on a competitor's free tier for nothing while this costs $300 a year; the flat rate only starts winning at the volume where a usage-priced app would have moved you up a tier. What the $25 does not buy is a response rate.

Placement, display rules and how many questions you ask decide that, and since none of them sit behind a paywall here, a survey nobody answers cannot be fixed by spending more. It does not buy data custody either: uninstall, and everything is deleted after thirty days.

How to actually use Grapevine

Let the setup assistant build the first survey. There are three routes in — the assistant, the template library, or from scratch — and the assistant is the short one. Decide where the survey runs before you decide what it asks. The options are the thank-you page, the order status page, a hosted on-site page with its own URL, an embed on any page, one-click links in email, and a Shopify POS tile.

On the two post-purchase pages the vendor's own advice is to place the app block near the top; further down the page costs you answers. Use Display Rules rather than a second survey to split audiences — new against returning, order value, products in the order, or the customer tags you already keep in Shopify — so first-time buyers and VIPs get different questions without rebuilding those segments inside the app.

Switch on the one-response-per-customer setting before you launch, not after. And export before you leave: the CSV sits in each survey's Report or Responses view, and uninstalling wipes responses, stats and configurations permanently after thirty days.

(thirty days of retention after uninstall, then permanent deletion; CSV download from each survey's Report or Responses view). Pricing read the same day on grapevine-surveys.com/pages/pricing, which states the single $25 plan with unlimited surveys and responses, every feature from install, no usage tiers or per-response fees, and no annual rate — and on apps.shopify.com/grapevine, which shows the same $25 Complete plan with a 14-day free trial.

The natural comparison is Zigpoll Customer Surveys, Fairing Post Purchase Surveys — all three do post-purchase and attribution surveys, and the honest split is volume against entry price. Zigpoll and Fairing both advertise a free plan on their listings (read 10 September 2026), so a store sending a few dozen surveys a month can start at nothing; Grapevine has no free tier and asks $25 from the end of the trial. What Grapevine buys for that is the absence of a response meter — the plan states unlimited responses with no extra charge — plus revenue and AOV per channel and an MCP endpoint so you can interrogate the answers with your own AI assistant. Check the response limits on the free plans before you assume they are cheaper for you: a free plan that caps at a few hundred responses is a different product from an uncapped one..
