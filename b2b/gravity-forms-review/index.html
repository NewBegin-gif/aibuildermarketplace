Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 1 Sep 2026

Annual WordPress licences, read 1 Sep 2026 on gravityforms.com/pricing/: Basic $59/yr (1 site), Pro $159/yr (3 sites, payment add-ons such as Stripe, Square and PayPal Checkout), Elite $259/yr (unlimited sites, WordPress Multisite, priority support and every first-party add-on). No renewal rate is published on the page. The third-party extensions most advanced builds lean on are separate purchases with their own ladders: Gravity Perks $59/$169/$299 a year for 1/3/unlimited sites, Gravity Flow $99/$299/$447 for 1/3/50 sites, and GravityKit $119 for GravityView, $199 for GravityView Pro and $396 for the All Access Pass (all three were discounted for a first year at the time of reading). Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

The honest knock

Two honest notes. It's developer-friendly rather than beginner-slick; non-technical users may find hosted builders more polished. For a WordPress site, though, it's the reliable gold standard.

What Gravity Forms does

Self-hosted WordPress form builder with conditional logic, payments and deep integrations. Licences run about $59/year for one site up to $259 for unlimited. WordPress-only, and much of the advanced power sits in paid add-ons that can add $99–349/year.

What buyers report about Gravity Forms

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Gravity Forms ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • The renewal price is the same as the first year — unusual in WordPress plugins, and it makes the real cost lower than it first looks.
  • • $59 a year for one site, $159 Pro for three, $259 Elite with everything. Conditional logic and add-ons cover almost any form you need.
What they complain about
  • • An expired licence cannot be reactivated. You buy a new one. Miss the renewal and you are repurchasing, not renewing.
  • • A lapsed licence leaves the plugin working but cuts off updates including security patches, and blocks new installs. For anything commercial that is not tenable.
  • • No lifetime licence and no monthly option, so a six-week campaign still costs a year.
  • • Some specialty add-ons come from third-party developers at $30-80 a year each on top.

When the paid plan is worth it

The decision rule turns on one number: how many sites you run. Basic is $59 a year for 1 site, Pro $159 for 3 sites plus payments, and Elite $259 for unlimited sites, WordPress Multisite, priority support and every first-party add-on. Run the per-site sum and the crossover lands exactly at three. Two Basic licences cost $118, less than Pro at $159; three cost $177, more. So at one or two sites Basic is cheaper even bought twice, and at three or more Pro wins on price alone.

Pro also unlocks payments, so if you need to take money on a single site the site count stops deciding. Elite is the one to think about differently. Beyond unlimited sites it bundles all the first-party add-ons, and that is where the real money in this ecosystem sits: the heavy lifting is done by third-party extensions with their own ladders. Gravity Perks runs $59, $169 or $299 a year; Gravity Flow $99, $299 or $447; GravityView $119, with a $199 Pro tier and a $396 all-access pass.

Several of those upper tiers cost more than Elite itself. List the extensions you intend to use before comparing the three tiers -- the licence is usually the smaller half of the bill.

How to actually use Gravity Forms

Count your sites and decide whether you need payments — those two questions settle the tier on their own. Basic at $59 a year covers one site, Pro at $159 covers three and adds payments, and Elite at $259 covers unlimited sites, WordPress Multisite and every first-party add-on. The arithmetic is simple: Elite beats buying separate Basic licences from five sites onward, and it is cheaper than a second Pro licence the moment you pass three.

Before you pick, list the add-ons your build actually needs. Elite includes the first-party ones, but the extensions that do much of the heavy lifting in advanced setups — GravityView, Gravity Flow, the Perks collection — are third-party and run from $59 to $447 a year by tier. A genuinely advanced form can cost more in extensions than in the licence, and that total is the number to compare against a hosted builder.

And check the obvious constraint first: this is a WordPress plugin and nothing else. If part of your stack is not WordPress, Gravity Forms does not reach it. It is developer-friendly rather than beginner-slick, so if the person building your forms is not technical, a hosted builder will feel more polished — and if they are, this is the reliable standard for a reason.

The natural comparison is Typeform or Jotform — hosted SaaS builders that are slicker and platform-agnostic; Gravity Forms wins on WordPress control and a flat annual cost.
