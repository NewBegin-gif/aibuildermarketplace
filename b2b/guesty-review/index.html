When the paid plan is worth it

Guesty Lite reads as about $9 a month for one to three listings, and that number is close to irrelevant. The real price is the roughly 1% charged per reservation, and one percent of your bookings passes the base fee at about $900 of monthly reservation value — which is a single week in most markets.

So model this as a percentage tool, not a subscription. A listing turning over $3,000 a month costs about $39 all in, of which the base fee is under a quarter. At $8,000 it is roughly $89, and the $9 has become a rounding error. That is not necessarily bad: a fee that scales with revenue is easier to carry in a slow season than a flat one. But it does mean comparing Guesty to a flat-rate rival on the headline number gives you the wrong answer at every level of occupancy.

Above three listings the published pricing stops. Pro and Enterprise are custom-quoted, so growth here means a sales conversation rather than clicking a bigger plan, and that is worth knowing before you build your operation around it. Ask what replaces the 1% at that tier, because a percentage that made sense on three listings behaves very differently across thirty.

The honest counterweight: this is a full property-management platform, and for a single listing that is heavy. Onboarding has a real learning curve and the platform earns its keep at scale. If you have one apartment and want a channel manager, you are buying an operations system to get one.

How to actually use Guesty

Model the percentage, not the base fee, because that is where the money goes. Guesty Lite is about $9 a month plus roughly 1% per reservation, so a listing turning over $3,000 a month in bookings pays $39 — of which the percentage is three quarters. At $10,000 a month it is $109 and the percentage is more than nine tenths of the bill. Work it out against your own occupancy before comparing Guesty to a flat-fee rival.

Count your listings too. Lite covers one to three; beyond that you are into Pro or Enterprise, which are quoted rather than published, so growth here means a sales conversation rather than a plan change.

Budget the onboarding time as well as the fee. This has a genuine learning curve and it earns its keep at scale rather than on a single listing — so if you are running one property, be honest about whether you need a platform or a calendar. Set aside a proper block of time for the initial setup rather than fitting it around a changeover day.

Does Guesty replace my current setup or sit on top of it?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything.
