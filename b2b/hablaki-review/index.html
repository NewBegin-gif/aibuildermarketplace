The honest knock

It is not fully offline: the first model download, licence activation and periodic licence checks need an internet connection. On Intel Macs and Windows it transcribes more slowly, with a lighter voice component. There is no mobile app yet; Android is on the roadmap. Refunds are standard only within 14 days of purchase.

What Hablaki does

Voice dictation for Mac and Windows that runs on your own computer: press a key, speak, and the text is pasted where your cursor is in any app.

Little to weigh at $9.99

There is only one paid option, so the question is whether the trial convinces you. At $9.99 once for two computers, the price is low next to dictation apps that bill monthly; the risk sits in the details instead. Check during the 14-day trial that your language comes out cleanly, that the speed on your machine is acceptable (Hablaki itself says Intel Macs and Windows PCs transcribe a bit slower with a lighter voice component), and that pasting works in the apps you actually write in.

How to actually use Hablaki

Download the app from hablaki.com for Mac or Windows and start the 14-day trial; no card or account is needed. On first launch it downloads its voice component: about 1.6 GB on Apple silicon Macs, or 480 MB on Intel Macs and Windows (145 MB if you pick the lightweight one), so do that on a decent connection. On a Mac, grant the microphone and accessibility permissions it asks for, and possibly input monitoring.

Then press F5 (or hold fn) on a Mac, or F9 or right Ctrl on Windows, speak, and press again: the text is pasted where your cursor is. The app also transcribes audio files on your computer, separate from the free web transcriber. Spanish is the default language; switch to English, French, Portuguese, German or Italian in Settings, or let it detect the language. When you buy, the licence key arrives by email and goes into Settings, on the License tab.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Hablaki cost?

Read on 2 November 2026: $9.99 once, for a lifetime licence on up to 2 computers, Mac or Windows. There is no subscription; updates are included, and a 14-day trial needs no card.

Does my voice go to a server?

Hablaki says no: speech recognition runs on your own computer and no audio is sent to a server. It does need the internet for the first model download, licence activation and periodic licence checks. If a dictation fails, the audio stays in a temporary folder on your device for up to 24 hours so you can retry it.

Which languages does it understand?

The app lists six on its site: Spanish by default, plus English, French, Portuguese, German and Italian, which you choose in Settings or let it detect. Hablaki told us by email on 3 November 2026 that the underlying speech model supports more than 99 languages, but that this is not a guarantee of equal accuracy or app validation in every language. If your language is not among the six, test it in the 14-day trial before you buy.

What do I need to run it?

A Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or later) or an Intel chip, or a 64-bit Windows 10 or 11 PC. The voice component it downloads is about 1.6 GB on Apple silicon and 480 MB on Intel and Windows, or 145 MB for the lightweight one.

Can I get a refund?

Yes, within 14 days of purchase: email hola.hablaki@gmail.com from the address you bought with, with your order number or licence key. Lemon Squeezy processes the refund and the licence is deactivated. After 14 days Hablaki looks at each case.

Can it transcribe audio files?

Yes. Besides dictation, the desktop app transcribes audio files; its homepage says it does so with no limit on length or number and without the audio leaving your device (read 3 November 2026). The free web transcriber is a separate, beta option, limited to 5 transcriptions a day of up to 1 hour each.
