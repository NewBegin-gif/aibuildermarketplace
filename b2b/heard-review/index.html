Heard Review (2026)

Financial OperationsResearched assessment · by Daniel HaketUpdated 2026-09-22

Mind the fine print between tiers: the entry plan does not include your annual tax filing (a ~$750 one-off on Lite), it is annual billing only, and messy back-months cost extra as catch-up fees. Price the tier that actually includes filing.

Best for: therapists and health-and-wellness practice owners — SLPs, dieticians, chiropractors, audiologists, coaches, massage and physical therapists — who want books, quarterly estimates and filing handled by specialists who know their niche.
Who should skip it: if you are comfortable doing your own books in a general tool at a fraction of the price, or your business sits outside the health-and-wellness practice niche.

Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 5 Sep 2026

Three plans, billed upfront per year with the monthly equivalent shown: Lite $129/mo or $1,548 a year, Essential $169/mo or $2,028, Premium $255/mo or $3,060. Lite covers done-for-you bookkeeping, financial reports and quarterly and annual tax support for solo sole proprietors; Essential adds personal tax preparation and filing; Premium is for group practices and S corps and brings payroll in as an add-on. Annual tax prep and filing is a $750 one-off add-on on Lite.

Frequently asked questions

What does Heard cost a therapist in private practice?

$129 a month for Lite, $169 for Essential and $255 for Premium, all billed annually — $1,548, $2,028 and $3,060 for the year. Read on 17 August 2026. Budget the annual tax filing separately: the page prices that as a $750 one-off on top.

Which plan actually includes my tax return?

Essential is the first one that includes personal tax preparation and filing. Lite covers bookkeeping, reports and quarterly tax support but leaves the annual filing as the $750 add-on, which closes most of the gap to Essential — so if you were going to pay for the filing anyway, compare $1,548 plus $750 against $2,028 rather than $129 against $169.

Is it really cheaper than a CPA?

Heard's own claim is that it replaces a CPA, bookkeeper and payroll provider that would typically cost $5,000 or more a year, against $3,060 for their top tier. That is their arithmetic, not ours, and it assumes you would otherwise buy all three. The honest test is what you pay now for bookkeeping alone — if that is under about $130 a month, the saving is smaller than the headline suggests.

What should I weigh before buying Financial Operations software?

The bar here is higher than features. Check who actually holds the money and under what licence, whether client funds are segregated, what audit trail and permission controls you get, and how support behaves when something goes wrong mid-transaction. Anything touching payments should be able to answer those in writing; if it cannot, that is your answer.
