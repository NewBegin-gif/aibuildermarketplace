What actually drives the quote

There is no plan to choose, so there is no upgrade to weigh: HiBob publishes no price list at all: its pricing pages describe custom pricing without an amount, and neither our July nor our August 2026 capture retrieved one. Re-verified on 17 August and 28 September 2026 and still true. The useful question is what moves the number you will be quoted, and the structure is known even when the figures are not: pricing is per employee per month, with modules bolted on.

That gives you two levers and one trap. The levers are headcount and module count. The trap is that both change after you sign. Headcount changes because you are buying an HRIS precisely because you are growing. Module count changes because HiBob's own account of implementation puts Core HR go-lives at 60 to 120 days, with each additional module such as Talent or Compensation adding another 20 to 30 days.

So the single most important thing to get in writing is not this year's rate but what happens to the per-employee rate as headcount changes mid-term, in both directions. A rate that only ratchets upward is a very different contract from one that flexes. Third-party estimates put the per-employee rate in the mid teens per month with a one-off implementation fee of roughly 10 to 20% of the annual contract.

We are repeating those as estimates and not as HiBob's numbers, because HiBob has published neither — and the whole thing is negotiated anyway. Use them as a sanity check on what you are offered, not as a budget. One cost sits outside the licence entirely and is easy to miss: Sandbox is a separately purchased capability. Without it you have nowhere safe to test configuration changes on a system that holds your payroll-adjacent data — so decide whether you need it before the negotiation rather than discovering it during the first change request.

And a related warning from HiBob's own developer docs. API service users start with no permissions enabled, and a call can return 200 OK with fields silently missing when the service user lacks access — so a half-configured integration looks like empty data rather than an error. That is an implementation cost measured in confusion rather than in money.

How to actually use HiBob

There is no self-serve signup. HiBob's pricing page is a demo request form, so the route to first value starts with a sales conversation and a contract, not an account. After signing, an implementation manager runs the first data import and walks through the errors with you, which means your real preparation work is having a clean employee spreadsheet ready before that session rather than assembled during it. HiBob's own account of the process puts Core HR go-lives at an average of 60 to 120 days, with each additional module such as Talent or Compensation adding another 20-30 days per module.

The documented sticking points sit on the integration side. Sandbox is a separately purchased capability, so without it you have nowhere safe to test changes. The people search endpoint allows 50 requests per minute.

Frequently asked questions

What does HiBob cost?

HiBob does not say. No amount in either of our monthly captures, none on 17 August 2026 and none on 28 September 2026, when its pricing link led to a demo form. It is priced per employee per month with modules, and negotiated. The estimates you will find elsewhere sit in the mid-teens per employee per month, but they are third-party guesses rather than HiBob's list.

What should the quote include?

Three things people get caught by: the per-employee rate and which modules it covers, the one-off implementation fee (commonly quoted at 10-20% of the annual value), and what happens when headcount moves during the term — whether you are billed on actual employees or on a committed minimum.

Is quote-only a warning sign here?

Not for this class of product — HR platforms at this level are almost all negotiated, because the price genuinely depends on headcount and modules. What matters is that you cannot comparison-shop from the website, so budget time for parallel quotes rather than expecting to compare list prices.

What should I weigh before buying Operations & Workflow software?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.
