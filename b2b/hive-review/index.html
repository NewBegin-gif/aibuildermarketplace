Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 3 Sep 2026

Free forever for up to 10 workspace members, with 1,000 AI credits a month and 200MB of storage; Starter $5 per user a month and Teams $12 per user a month on yearly billing, which the page states saves 33% against monthly; Enterprise is custom. Read the Starter row before you pay for it: it lifts storage to unlimited and AI credits to 3,000 a month, but it keeps the same 10-member cap as Free and adds a 10-project cap.

Only Teams, at $12 with 5,000 AI credits, gives unlimited workspace members. The add-ons are the part to budget: proofing and approvals, timesheets, team resourcing, advanced dashboards, SSO and enterprise controls, and external users each cost $5 per user a month on top, which their own calculator confirms. Two further add-ons, CRM and Finance, are listed on the page with no price at all. The external-user add-on includes 5 external licences per seat. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

When the paid plan is worth it

The free tier covers up to ten members, about $120 a month of product at Teams pricing, so the first question is whether you have outgrown free at all. What paying $5 for Starter does not buy: the same ten-member cap stays, plus a ten-project one. Only Teams at $12 gives unlimited workspace members. Both are yearly rates; the page states yearly saves 33% against monthly. The seat price is honest; the add-on grid is the business model.

Proofing and approvals, timesheets, team resourcing, advanced dashboards, SSO and enterprise controls, and external users each cost $5 a user a month on top. One add-on takes a $12 seat to $17, three take it to $27, four to $32, and all six to $42 — three and a half times the headline. Two more, CRM and Finance, carry no price at all, so $42 is a floor rather than a ceiling.

So the comparison that decides this is not Hive's $12 against a rival's $20. It is Hive's DRESSED price against an all-inclusive tool: write down the modules you cannot work without, add $5 a seat for each, and compare that. For a team needing only core project management — Gantt, Kanban, forms, chat — Hive is genuinely cheap. For an agency needing proofing, timesheets and resourcing, it is $27 a seat and the flexibility has become a surcharge.

The external-user add-on includes five external licences per seat, so it does not scale one for one with the clients you invite.

How to actually use Hive

List your must-have modules before you look at the seat price, because the add-on grid is the business model. Switch on three and a $12 Teams seat becomes $27; four makes it $32. Compare that dressed price against all-inclusive rivals rather than the headline. À la carte is good value for a team wanting two modules and punishing for one needing the full toolkit — and which you are is knowable in advance.

Use the free tier to find out. It is free forever for up to ten members — and note that Starter at $5 keeps that same ten-member cap and adds a ten-project one, so that first upgrade buys credits and storage, not room to grow; only Teams at $12 gives unlimited members. Note that the external-user add-on includes five external licences per seat, which matters if you work with clients, and that yearly billing is a third cheaper than monthly.

Frequently asked questions

What does Hive cost?

Free for up to 10 members, then $5 per user a month on Starter and $12 on Teams, billed yearly (33% off monthly). Enterprise is quoted. Read on 17 August 2026.

Why do people report paying much more than $12 a seat?

Because of the modular add-ons. Proofing and approvals, timesheets, resourcing, advanced dashboards, SSO and external users are each roughly $5 per user a month on top of the plan. An agency that needs proofing, timesheets and resourcing is at about $27 a seat, not $12 — list the modules you need before comparing with an all-inclusive competitor.

Is the free tier a real plan?

Yes, up to 10 members and marked free forever rather than a trial. That makes it genuinely usable for a small team doing task management; what it will not give you is the proofing, timesheet and resourcing layer that makes Hive an agency tool.

What should I weigh before buying Operations & Workflow software?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.
