When the paid plan is worth it

The free tier covers up to ten members, which at Teams pricing is about $120 a month of product — so the first question is not which plan but whether you have outgrown free at all. Beyond that, Starter is $5 a user a month and Teams $12 on yearly billing, which the page states saves 33% against monthly.

The seat price is honest; the add-on grid is the business model. Proofing and approvals, timesheets, team resourcing, advanced dashboards, SSO and enterprise controls, and external users each cost about $5 a user a month on top. Count them and the arithmetic is stark: one add-on takes a $12 seat to $17, three take it to $27, four to $32, and all six to $42 — three and a half times the headline.

So the comparison that decides this is not Hive's $12 against a rival's $20. It is Hive's DRESSED price against an all-inclusive tool. Write down the modules you cannot work without, add $5 a seat for each, and compare that number. For a team that needs only core project management — Gantt, Kanban, forms, chat — Hive is genuinely cheap and the a-la-carte model is a real advantage. For an agency that needs proofing, timesheets and resourcing, it is $27 a seat and the flexibility has become a surcharge.

One detail worth knowing before you price external collaboration: the external-user add-on includes five external licences per seat, so it does not scale one for one with the clients you invite.

How to actually use Hive

Proofing and approvals, timesheets, team resourcing, advanced dashboards, SSO and enterprise controls, and external users each cost about $5 per user a month on top of the plan. Switch on three and a $12 Teams seat becomes $27; switch on four and it is $32.

Compare that dressed price against all-inclusive rivals rather than the headline. À la carte pricing is genuinely good value for a team that wants two modules and genuinely punishing for one that needs the full toolkit — and which of those you are is knowable in advance.

Use the free tier to find out. It is free forever for up to ten members, which is enough to run real projects and discover which modules you actually reach for rather than which ones sound useful. Note that the external-user add-on includes five external licences per seat, which matters if you work with clients or freelancers, and that yearly billing is a third cheaper than monthly.

Frequently asked questions

What does Hive cost?

Free for up to 10 members, then $5 per user a month on Starter and $12 on Teams, billed yearly (33% off monthly). Enterprise is quoted. Read on 17 August 2026.

Why do people report paying much more than $12 a seat?

Because of the modular add-ons. Proofing and approvals, timesheets, resourcing, advanced dashboards, SSO and external users are each roughly $5 per user a month on top of the plan. An agency that needs proofing, timesheets and resourcing is at about $27 a seat, not $12 — list the modules you need before comparing with an all-inclusive competitor.

Is the free tier a real plan?

Yes, up to 10 members and marked free forever rather than a trial. That makes it genuinely usable for a small team doing task management; what it will not give you is the proofing, timesheet and resourcing layer that makes Hive an agency tool.

Does Hive replace my current setup or sit on top of it?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.

Who should not use Hive?

Skip it if you need most of the add-ons anyway (all-inclusive tools price better), or your org demands one standardized suite. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
