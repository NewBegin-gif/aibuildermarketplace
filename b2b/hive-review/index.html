Hive Review (2026)

Operations & WorkflowResearched assessment · by Daniel HaketUpdated 2026-08-15

What Hive does

Project management and team collaboration in one workspace. Plan projects, assign tasks and centralize communication so work keeps moving.

Pricing (2026)

(2026) Free tier (10 members); Starter about $5/user/mo annual ($7 monthly); Teams ~$12/user/mo annual ($18 monthly); Enterprise custom. Watch the add-ons: proofing, timesheets, goals, resourcing, analytics and automations each run ~+$5/user/mo. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The seat price is honest bait, the add-on grid is the business model: switch on three add-ons and your $12 seat becomes $27 — a la carte flexibility that punishes teams who need the full toolkit. List your must-have modules first and compare the DRESSED price against all-inclusive rivals; for teams needing only core PM, though, Hive is genuinely cheap and pleasant.

What buyers report about Hive

Recurring themes from Capterra, GetApp, SelectHub and Cloudwards, read in August 2026. We have not run Hive ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Document storage, task progress and project planning consolidated properly — teams say it replaces several tools.
  • • From $5 per user with a 14-day trial that does not ask for a card.
What they complain about
  • • The mobile app is well behind the desktop, with bugs appearing after updates.
  • • Reviewers flag the privacy policy as a genuine concern — worth reading it yourself if you handle client data.
  • • The interface is inconsistent, adds extra clicks and has a steeper learning curve than the price suggests.
  • • Automation and reporting are thinner than reviewers want, and the useful features sit on higher plans.
Best for: project teams that want flexible PM (Gantt, Kanban, forms, chat) and only pay for the modules they actually use.
Who should skip it: if you need most of the add-ons anyway (all-inclusive tools price better), or your org demands one standardized suite.

Frequently asked questions

Does Hive replace my current setup or sit on top of it?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.

Who should not use Hive?

Skip it if you need most of the add-ons anyway (all-inclusive tools price better), or your org demands one standardized suite. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
