Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 2 Sep 2026

Hopp Business publishes no subscription price because it does not charge one: the page states there are no activation costs and no minimum commitment, and billing is per ride on a single consolidated monthly invoice. Ride fares vary by city, vehicle class and demand and are not published as a rate card. The site cites 50,000+ businesses; the equivalent Bolt for Business page cites over 70,000 companies. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

The honest knock

Hopp Business charges no activation fee and no minimum commitment, so the whole cost is ride pricing, which moves with city, vehicle class and demand — and coverage thins out wherever Bolt does not already run. Check both on your own routes before you move a travel budget across.

What Hopp does

Business travel management from Bolt, branded Hopp: book and control company rides from one dashboard, with per-person and per-group spend limits, automated ride reports, expensing integrations and a single consolidated monthly invoice. No activation cost and no minimum commitment — you pay per ride.

What you actually pay for

Hopp Business does not sell seats, so there is no ladder to climb: the page states plainly that there are no activation costs and no minimum commitment. You pay for the rides your team takes, on one consolidated monthly invoice, and the platform around them — spending limits per person or group, automated ride reports, third-party expensing integrations — comes with the account rather than with a tier. That moves the buying question off price per seat and onto two other things.

First, ride pricing itself, which moves with city, vehicle class and demand; the platform is free but the journeys are not, and no published figure will tell you what your routes cost. Second, coverage, because this is a marketplace and its value collapses wherever driver supply is thin. What signing up does not solve is either of those. No plan makes rides cheaper, and no plan makes drivers appear in a city that has few.

Run a fortnight of real journeys on the routes your team actually travels, then set the invoice against whatever you reimburse today. That comparison is the whole decision here, and it is one only your own routes can settle.

How to actually use Hopp

Confirm which Hopp you are looking at before anything else, because the name is heavily reused. The business product is Hopp Business, the corporate travel side of Bolt — the site footer reads Hopp by Bolt Services US Inc. — and not any of the link-in-bio or digital-card tools that share the word. Setup is documented as three steps. Enter your business details and choose a payment method; add team members, optionally organising them into groups with their own spend and usage limits; then book.

Nothing sits in front of that, since the account carries no activation fee and no minimum commitment. Spend the first fortnight proving coverage rather than features. Have the people who travel most book their normal journeys at the times they normally travel, and watch pickup times and cancellations in the dashboard. The automated ride reports and the single monthly invoice are what remove the expense-claim admin, so confirm your expensing tool ingests them before rolling it wider.

Distilled from gethopp.com/en/business/ and bolt.eu/en/business/, both read 2 September 2026, plus the gethopp.com footer identifying the operator as Hopp by Bolt Services US Inc.

The natural comparison is Uber for Business — the obvious comparison, and it turns on coverage — check which of the two actually has drivers in the cities your team travels to, because that decides more than the dashboard does.
