What Hostinger does

Affordable, fast hosting with an AI website builder built in. Free domain, free SSL and 24/7 support — perfect for founders launching their first MVP or side project.

Pricing (2026)

Budget hosting: shared and WordPress from about $2.69/mo on long (24-48 month) terms, cloud from $3.59/mo, VPS from $5.84/mo (2026) — but renewals jump sharply (Premium ~$10.99, Business ~$16.99/mo). Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

The catch

The headline price only holds if you prepay years up front — renewals run several times the intro rate, the number-one complaint. Budget for the renewal, and be realistic that budget shared hosting isn't managed-grade performance.

When the paid plan is worth it

Hostinger's plan question is really a contract-length question, because the advertised price and the price you will actually pay are different numbers. Read on their own hosting page on 2 August 2026: Premium $2.99 a month promotional renewing at $10.99; Unlimited $3.79 renewing at $16.99; Cloud Startup $7.99 renewing at $25.99. Every one of those promotional prices requires a 48-month term. So the honest comparison is not $2.99 against a competitor's $10 — it is roughly $143 up front for four years of Premium, against $10.99 a month the moment that term ends. Work out the four-year total, not the monthly figure. The step from Premium to Unlimited buys unlimited websites and 50 GB NVMe against 3 websites and 20 GB SSD, for $0.80 a month promotional but $6 a month at renewal. If there is any chance of a fourth site, take it now — mid-term upgrades are priced at the tier you move to, not the promotion you signed. Every plan includes a free domain for one year and a 30-day money-back guarantee.

How to actually use Hostinger

Claim the free domain deliberately, not automatically. It is free for the first year only, and it becomes the thing tying you to the host — moving later means moving the domain too. If you already own a domain, point it here instead and keep the registration where it is. Use the 30-day money-back window as an actual test, not a formality. Put the real site on it — the plugins, the traffic, the email — because the complaints that matter with budget hosting show up under load, not on a fresh install. Diarise the renewal date the day you sign. On a 48-month term that is four years away and it is the single most common way people end up surprised: the price goes from $2.99 to $10.99 without anything else changing. Frequently asked questions

Is Hostinger secure and reliable enough to depend on?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.

Who should not use Hostinger?

Skip it if you run a business-critical, high-traffic site — the renewal jump and shared-tier limits make managed hosting the better long-run value. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
