What Hostinger does

Affordable, fast hosting with an AI website builder built in. Free domain, free SSL and 24/7 support — perfect for founders launching their first MVP or side project.

Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 18 Aug 2026

(re-verified 2026-08-18, read on their own web-hosting and VPS pages) Shared and WordPress hosting runs four tiers on a 48-month term: Premium $2.99/mo ($143.52 up front, 3 sites, 20 GB SSD) renewing at $10.99; Business $3.99 ($191.52, 50 sites, 50 GB SSD) renewing at $16.99; Unlimited $4.99 ($239.52, unlimited sites, 50 GB NVMe) renewing at $17.99; Cloud Startup $7.99 ($383.52, 100 GB NVMe) renewing at $25.99. VPS is a separate ladder on a 24-month term — KVM 1 at $6.49/mo (1 vCPU, 4 GB RAM, 50 GB NVMe, 4 TB bandwidth) renewing at $11.99, then KVM 2 $8.99 renewing $14.99, KVM 4 $12.99 renewing $28.99 and KVM 8 $25.99 renewing $49.99. Every plan is paid upfront for the whole term, the monthly figure being the total divided by the months, and every plan carries a 30-day money-back guarantee. Both the shared plans and the VPS plans include a free domain for one year. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the price did

We capture Hostinger’s own pricing page every month and keep the figures, so this is our record of the page — not a forecast, and not someone else’s summary.

Watch the tier names here, not only the numbers. Our July 2026 capture recorded an Unlimited plan at $191.52 for a 48-month term and our 15 August reading had it at $181.92; reading the same page on 18 August 2026, the shared range has four tiers and $191.52 is the Business plan, while Unlimited is $239.52 — $4.99 a month renewing at $17.99. What looked like a deepening discount is better read as a reshuffle: Hostinger renames and re-slots tiers between promotions, so a plan name on its own is not a stable price. Two figures have held in every observation we hold: Premium at $143.52 for the term renewing at $10.99, and Cloud Startup at $383.52 renewing at $25.99. That is the argument for anchoring on the renewal price — it is the number that decides what this costs you, and it is the number that moves least.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The headline price only holds if you prepay years up front — renewals run several times the intro rate, the number-one complaint. Budget for the renewal, and be realistic that budget shared hosting isn't managed-grade performance.

What buyers report about Hostinger

Recurring themes from independent uptime and speed tests, Trustpilot and Capterra, read in August 2026 (Capterra reviews read across July 2021 to July 2026, most of the substance from November 2024 onward). We use Hostinger ourselves, but a single account is a narrow sample, so here is what other buyers report. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Independent monitors that ran their own multi-month tests report near-perfect uptime and time-to-first-byte under 300ms from US locations — the performance is real, not just marketing.
  • • The control panel is easier than cPanel for people who are not sysadmins.
  • • Capterra reviewers running the VPS specifically name hPanel as the reason it stays manageable, with VPS, domain and hosting administered in one place. One reviewer in March 2026 describes standing up a VPS for clients without trouble and getting useful help from support on the snags.
  • • Live chat that answers without trying to upsell comes up more than once, and a reviewer in November 2024 names the do-it-yourself VPS option as the reason the price stays reasonable.
What they complain about
  • • The renewal rate. The headline price is an introductory rate on a multi-year term, and reviewers who did not read the renewal figure are the single largest complaint group.
  • • The cheapest plan has no daily backups. If the site matters, that plan is not the one you want.
  • • The renewal price is the complaint from VPS buyers too, not only from shared-hosting ones: the same March 2026 reviewer who was happy with the setup names renewal pricing against competitors as his single criticism.
  • • A cluster of billing complaints on Capterra has nothing to do with speed or uptime. A reviewer in July 2026 could not get an invoice corrected after an error in his own billing details; a verified reviewer in May 2026 describes a domain-transfer discount announced at $1 instead of free and then billed anyway, with support declining to put it right; and an August 2025 reviewer was invoiced a year after cancelling.
  • • Support quality is described as degrading into scripted answers. A July 2026 reviewer whose site was down twelve hours over a suspected phishing flag got no explanation afterwards and calls the replies mostly AI-generated; a January 2025 reviewer had a domain suspended without warning and could get neither the chatbot nor an agent to respond.

Where the ratings disagree. The independent uptime and speed tests and Capterra are measuring different things, and only one of them is about the product. The tests say the hosting performs; the Capterra complaints that repeat are billing and support — renewals, cancellations that did not stick, a discount that did not apply. Weigh them accordingly: if you prepay a four-year term you are buying the performance the tests measure and taking on the billing relationship the reviews describe, so put the renewal date in your calendar and keep your own record of what you were quoted.

When the paid plan is worth it

Hostinger's plan question is really a contract-length question, because the advertised price and the price you will actually pay are different numbers. Read on their own hosting page on 18 August 2026: Premium $2.99 a month promotional renewing at $10.99; Business $3.99 renewing at $16.99; Unlimited $4.99 renewing at $17.99; Cloud Startup $7.99 renewing at $25.99. Every one of those promotional prices requires a 48-month term. So the honest comparison is not $2.99 against a competitor's $10 — it is roughly $143 up front for four years of Premium, against $10.99 a month the moment that term ends. Work out the four-year total, not the monthly figure. The step up from Premium buys room: Business gives 50 websites and 50 GB SSD against 3 websites and 20 GB SSD for $1 a month promotional but $6 a month at renewal, and Unlimited adds unlimited websites and NVMe storage for $2 a month promotional and $7 at renewal. If there is any chance of a fourth site, take the step now — mid-term upgrades are priced at the tier you move to, not the promotion you signed. Every plan includes a free domain for one year and a 30-day money-back guarantee.

How to actually use Hostinger

Claim the free domain deliberately, not automatically. It is free for the first year only, and it becomes the thing tying you to the host — moving later means moving the domain too. If you already own a domain, point it here instead and keep the registration where it is. Use the 30-day money-back window as an actual test, not a formality. Put the real site on it — the plugins, the traffic, the email — because the complaints that matter with budget hosting show up under load, not on a fresh install. Diarise the renewal date the day you sign. On a 48-month term that is four years away and it is the single most common way people end up surprised: the price goes from $2.99 to $10.99 without anything else changing. Frequently asked questions

Is Hostinger VPS priced differently from Hostinger's normal hosting?

Yes, and it is a separate ladder with a different term. Shared and WordPress hosting runs from $2.99 a month for Premium on a 48-month commitment. VPS starts at $6.49 a month for KVM 1 on a 24-month commitment, giving 1 vCPU, 4 GB RAM, 50 GB NVMe and 4 TB bandwidth, and climbs through KVM 2 at $8.99, KVM 4 at $12.99 and KVM 8 at $25.99. Both are paid upfront for the full term. Read on their own pages on 18 August 2026.

Kinsta vs Hostinger — what actually separates them?

Price model before performance. Hostinger sells a long prepaid term at a discount and then renews at several times that rate, so the honest comparison is the four-year total against the renewal, not $2.99 against anyone. Kinsta is managed hosting priced as managed hosting, with no multi-year prepayment to unlock the headline. If your site is a side project or a brochure and you will genuinely keep it four years, Hostinger wins on cost. If it is business-critical and under real load, you are comparing a shared budget tier against a managed platform, and the renewal gap narrows the saving anyway.

What will Hostinger cost me once the intro term ends?

Between three and four times the headline, and the figures are on their own page. Premium goes from $2.99 to $10.99 a month, Business from $3.99 to $16.99, Unlimited from $4.99 to $17.99, Cloud Startup from $7.99 to $25.99, and on VPS, KVM 1 goes from $6.49 to $11.99. The renewals move far less than the promotional prices do — $10.99, $16.99 and $25.99 have held in every capture we hold since July 2026 — so treat them as the real price and the intro rate as the discount. Diarise the renewal date the day you sign.

Is Hostinger secure and reliable enough to depend on?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.

Who should not use Hostinger?

Skip it if you run a business-critical, high-traffic site — the renewal jump and shared-tier limits make managed hosting the better long-run value. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
