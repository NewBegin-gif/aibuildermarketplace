When the paid plan is worth it

The subscription is not the biggest line on this bill. Card processing runs about 2.59%, and on $40,000 of monthly card revenue that is roughly $1,036 a month — more than three times the $299 MAX plan. At $100,000 it is about $2,590, nearly nine times. Whatever tier you choose, model the processing against your actual ticket value first, because that is where the money goes. Within the subscription the trap is the entry tier.

Basic is $59 a month, advertised as $20 off a $79 rate, and it covers ONE user with no GPS tracking and no QuickBooks sync — the two things most field businesses need on day one. The moment you have a second technician you are on Essentials at $149 for up to five users, which works out at $29.80 a head if you fill it and $74.50 if you have two.

Extra users beyond the tier run about $35 a month each. So the real ladder is not $59, $149, $299. For a two-person crew it is $149 whether you like it or not, and for a seven-person crew it is $149 plus two extra seats, or $219, before processing. Price the tier you will actually need rather than the one that matches your headcount today, because the jump from one to two users is the expensive one and it happens early.

There is a 14-day free trial, and the thing worth testing in it is not the scheduling — every tool in this category schedules — but whether the dispatch and invoicing flow survives a real day with your own jobs and your own crew.

How to actually use Housecall Pro

Price the card processing before the subscription, because it is the larger number. At around 2.59%, a business taking $40,000 a month on cards pays over $1,000 in processing — several times the cost of the top tier. Ask what rate you would actually get against your own monthly card volume and average ticket, and get it in writing. Then skip Basic if you run a field team. At $59 it omits GPS tracking and QuickBooks sync, which are two of the things a real crew buys this for, so most businesses land on Essentials at $149 or above.

Count your users while you are there: Essentials covers five, and extra users are about $35 each, so a seven-person crew is $219 a month before processing. Use the 14-day trial on a live job rather than a demo one. Book it, dispatch it, take the payment and send the invoice — the whole loop, on a phone, from a van if that is where your team works. Field software fails in the field, not in the office.

The natural comparison is Jobber or ServiceTitan — other field-service platforms.

Frequently asked questions

What does Housecall Pro cost?

$59 a month for Basic (one user), $149 for Essentials (up to five users) and $299 for MAX, with a 14-day free trial. Those are the discounted rates; the page shows $79 and $189 as the undiscounted equivalents for the first two. Read on 17 August 2026.

What does it really cost for a crew of five?

More than Essentials' $149. Extra users beyond the plan run about $35 a month each, and card processing is around 2.59% of what you collect. For a business taking $40,000 a month on cards, the processing fee alone exceeds $1,000 — an order of magnitude more than the subscription, and the number that should drive the comparison.

What is missing from the cheapest plan?

GPS tracking and the QuickBooks integration, both of which arrive on Essentials. For a solo operator that may not matter; for anyone dispatching vehicles or doing their own books, Basic is not the plan even though it looks like the entry point.

What should I weigh before buying Operations & Workflow software?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.
