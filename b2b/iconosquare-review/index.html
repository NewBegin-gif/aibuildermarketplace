Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 15 Sep 2026

Read on iconosquare.com/pricing from a Netherlands connection, at the profile count each plan includes. Free covers 2 social profiles, 1 user and 10 posts a month per profile, with one month of analytics history. Starter is €9 a month billed yearly, or €11 month to month, for 3 profiles, 1 user and 30 posts a month. Launch is €33 billed yearly (€39 monthly) for 5 profiles, 1 user and 100 posts.

Scale is €69 billed yearly (€83 monthly) and is the first tier with 3 users, unlimited posts, one group and two years of history. Custom starts at €116 billed yearly and covers 5 to unlimited profiles and 1 to unlimited users, with white-label reporting, API access and a dedicated CSM. The page states the yearly saving as €24, €72, €168 and €276 a year.

The ladder has changed since our 26 August reading over a US connection, which listed Launch $39, Scale $79 and an Excel tier at $139: Excel no longer appears as a named plan, its price and its white-label, API and CSM features now sit under Custom, and Starter is new below Launch. The profile count is the dial that moves the price, so a quote taken at five says little about what a team managing thirty will pay — and the tiers differ in users, posts and history, not just in profile allowance. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Analytics is where it shines — scheduling and community features exist but aren't always as deep as dedicated publishers. And the per-seat, per-profile tiering means agencies managing many brands climb toward the Custom plan quicker than the headline Starter price suggests: Starter and Launch carry one seat, Scale three, and only Custom lifts the five-profile ceiling.

What Iconosquare does

Social analytics, reporting and competitor benchmarking, deepest on Instagram and the core networks. Tiers gate on seats and profiles, so agencies managing many brands climb faster than the entry price suggests.

What buyers report about Iconosquare

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Iconosquare ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • The Instagram and Facebook analytics are genuinely excellent — about the best you can get without going enterprise, and that is what reviewers pay for.
  • • Annual billing removes the monthly admin, which smaller teams appreciate.
  • • Agencies on TrustRadius keep the tool for one job: client-facing reports that are comprehensive and good-looking enough to hand to people who are not social-media savvy, with competitor benchmarking dashboards alongside.
What they complain about
  • • Reviewers call it the most expensive option in its category, running 20-40% above competitors, and the per-user fee compounds as a team grows. $49-$79/month before add-ons.
  • • Scheduling failures are documented in reviews from 2024 through 2026 — for a publishing tool that is the thing to test first.
  • • Content creation is entirely manual and feature gaps send you to other tools anyway.
  • • Support is email-only during business hours, so a weekend problem waits. A February 2026 Trustpilot complaint documents six months of unresolved affiliate commission disputes despite promises of personal monitoring — worth knowing if you plan to promote them.
  • • Two analytics limits are named independently by different TrustRadius reviewers, and both matter for an analytics tool: Instagram organic and paid results are not cleanly separated, and the historical window for Instagram and TikTok is shorter than agencies expect.
  • • 'Quite pricey' is the summary from the same platform, matching what reviewers say everywhere else.

Where the ratings disagree. TrustRadius scores it far lower than Capterra or GetApp do, but its Iconosquare sample is small and dates from 2022, so it reflects an older product. What survives from it is the specific analytics gaps, which are still worth testing in the 14-day trial.

When the paid plan is worth it

The free plan gives you 1 user, 2 social profiles and 10 posts per month per profile, with one month of analytics history. Starter at €9 a month billed yearly is the cheapest paid step: 3 profiles, 30 posts a month, the Uma AI analyst and a full year of history. Launch at €33 billed yearly raises that to 5 social profiles and 100 posts a month and adds automated reports, one inbox for comments, DMs and mentions, and X (Twitter) publishing, so it pays for itself once you run a third account or publish more than a few times a week.

Scale at €69 billed yearly adds 3 users, unlimited posts, one group, post approval workflows, campaign and paid-performance tracking, competitor and hashtag monitoring, and two years of history. Custom starts at €116 billed yearly and is the only tier that lifts the profile and seat caps, with white-label reporting, API access and a dedicated CSM. Upgrading does not lift the account cap until Custom. Launch and Scale both include 5 social profiles, so paying more buys seats, retention and reporting, not more accounts; beyond five you are into a Custom plan, which the page lists at 5 to unlimited social profiles and 1 to unlimited users.

Upgrading also cannot recover data the networks never released. Iconosquare states it can usually pull Reach and Impressions from up to two years before you add an Instagram Business profile, and that Stories are held in the Instagram API for only 24 hours, so no tier reconstructs historic Stories.

How to actually use Iconosquare

Sign up for the 14-day free trial, which needs no credit card, then connect one profile first. Connection is where people stall, and it is Meta's rules doing most of the work. Iconosquare's help centre is explicit that you need an Instagram business profile, that it must be linked to a Facebook page, and that you must be an Admin or Editor with full permissions on that page. When a profile is missing from the picker, the documented causes are a broken Instagram-to-Facebook link or missing admin status in Business Manager, and the documented fix is to remove and re-authorise Iconosquare's Facebook permissions rather than retrying the same dialog.

The second trap is history. Iconosquare documents that follower growth, engagement rate and best time to post are estimated for any period before you connected, and for any period the account was logged out or without an active plan. So connect every profile you care about on day one, even ones you will not schedule to yet, because accurate tracking starts at connection, not at purchase.

Frequently asked questions

What does Iconosquare cost?

Read on 15 September 2026: Free is €0 for 2 social profiles, Starter €9 a month billed yearly or €11 monthly for 3, Launch €33 or €39 and Scale €69 or €83 for 5 each, and Custom from €116 billed yearly for 5 to unlimited. Yearly billing is advertised as two months free, and the page puts the saving at €24, €72, €168 and €276 a year. Watch the unit though — the price moves with how many social profiles you manage, and seats move with the tier: one user on Starter and Launch, three on Scale.

Is there really a free plan?

There is a €0 tier, but not the way most free plans work: Iconosquare presents it as free access after your 14-day trial ends. It allows 2 social profiles, 1 user, 10 posts per month per profile and basic analytics with limited reporting, and it keeps only one month of history. Useful as a landing place, not as a working plan for a business.

What are the analytics limits people run into?

Two, and reviewers on TrustRadius name them independently of each other: Instagram organic and paid results are not cleanly separated, and the historical window for Instagram and TikTok is shorter than agencies want (data retention is one year on Starter and Launch, two years on Scale). If you report on paid campaigns, test that split before you commit.

What should I weigh before buying Growth & Revenue software?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Small teams get the most out of this category when one clear problem is already costing real hours or revenue; buying ahead of that just adds cost and another login. Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.
