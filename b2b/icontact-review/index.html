Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 17 Aug 2026

No permanent free plan — a 30-day free trial, then paid tiers by contact count, with 15% off for paying yearly. At 500 contacts Standard is $9/mo and Premium $16/mo; the ladder then climbs with list size, with Standard running $14, $28, $45, $73, $110, $180, $250 and $350 a month at successive contact bands and Premium starting from $16 and $27 at the equivalent steps.

Standard covers templates, landing pages and automations; Premium adds unlimited users, social posting and the AI content assistant. Larger lists are quoted. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What buyers report about iContact

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run iContact ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Straightforward to use with customisable templates — reviewers who wanted email without a marketing degree name that.
  • • Support gets consistently good marks.
  • • The clearest outcome we have seen reported for it comes from TrustRadius: a small nonprofit describes email campaigns doubling website traffic, with donations up 12% in three months and 20% by month six after moving to iContact.
What they complain about
  • • Price rises are the recurring complaint. One reviewer went from $45 a month in early 2022 to $73 in early 2024 — $336 more a year for the same product.
  • • Pricing is subscriber-based, so cost climbs as the list grows whether or not you send more.
  • • Reporting and automation are both behind the newer platforms.
  • • The interface has gone years without meaningful updates and reviewers say it looks it. Storage, editing and basic tasks like fixing a mistake or deleting a file are called awkward.
  • • The friction reviewers name is all in the handling rather than the sending: uploading a list is slow, deleting contacts is awkward and mistakes are hard to undo, finding and reusing an earlier message is fiddly, and the block editor fights you on layout.
  • • Customisation and storage are both described as limited, and scheduling is the feature most often asked to improve. Note the TrustRadius sample here is dated — the reviews cluster in 2020 to 2022.

When the paid plan is worth it

iContact's Standard tier ($9/month for 500 contacts, per its published pricing table) suits a single marketer running basic campaigns: it includes only 1 user login, 2 contact lists, 1 segment, and 1 automation workflow. Anyone needing more than one automated sequence, more than two lists, or a second team member hits that ceiling right away and has to move to Premium, from $16/month for 500 contacts. That removes those caps and adds unlimited automations, lists, segments and users, plus social posting and an AI content assistant.

What upgrading does not fix: user-reported editor limitations (positioning graphics, wrapping text around an image) and deliverability concerns are not tier-gated features — Premium and Ultimate add capacity and AI extras, not a different sending infrastructure or a redesigned drag-and-drop builder. Dedicated IP addresses and email verification remain separate paid add-ons even at the top tier, so list-quality and inbox-placement problems can persist regardless of plan.

How to actually use iContact

Getting first value out of iContact starts with importing an existing contact list, since the platform is built around list management rather than website or product tracking. iContact checks email syntax and domain validity during import, and a List Cleanup feature keeps flagging problematic addresses afterward. New list uploads have also been reported to trigger a validation hold before a campaign is allowed to send, so a freshly imported list is not always immediately usable.

Before sending, a custom sending domain needs an SPF record added, and DKIM signing is not self-serve — it requires contacting iContact's deliverability team directly, which can stretch out full authentication. Once authenticated, the drag-and-drop builder is the fastest route to a first campaign, though users describe it as limited for finer layout work, so expect to keep early designs simple rather than pixel-precise.

Frequently asked questions

What does iContact cost?

At 500 contacts, $9 a month on Standard and $16 on Premium, with 15% off for paying yearly. The price then steps with list size — Standard runs $14, $28, $45, $73, $110, $180, $250 and $350 at successive bands. Read on 17 August 2026.

What separates Standard from Premium?

Standard covers the essentials: templates, landing pages and automations. Premium adds unlimited users, social posting and the AI content assistant. At 500 contacts that is $9 against $16, so the decision is about team access and channels rather than about sending volume.

Is there a free plan?

Not any more — a 30-day free trial, and then a paid tier. Introductory offers advertising a first month for around a dollar appear on the page periodically, so check what the second month costs before treating that as the price.
