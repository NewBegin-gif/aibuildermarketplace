What IDrive does

Cloud backup across many devices, with versioning, snapshots and physical seed-and-restore shipping. Personal is 5 TB at $9.99/month, shown at $6.99 on annual billing — but that discount is first-year only and the renewal step is about 43%, so budget on year-two rates.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The first-year price is a teaser and the renewal is the price — IDrive says so itself under the plan table, and on the current numbers the step from discounted to standard is about 43% (Personal 5 TB goes from $6.99 to $9.99 a month). Budget on year-two rates, not the banner. Beyond that, the honest backup fundamentals apply: a backup you never test-restore is a hope, not a backup, and the first full backup of multi-TB data takes weeks over the wire unless you use their physical Express seed shipping.

What buyers report about IDrive

Recurring themes from Trustpilot, Capterra and backup-service reviews, read in August 2026. We have not run IDrive ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • The storage-per-euro is unmatched — 5 TB at $69.65 the first year, and it backs up unlimited devices on one account.
  • • You can usually keep the promotional rate by contacting support a few weeks before renewal and asking, or by cancelling and re-signing with a fresh code.
What they complain about
  • • Year-two renewal is about 42% above the promotional price unless you actively push back. Diarise it.
  • • 2.6 out of 5 on Trustpilot, mostly billing and cancellation. One reviewer reports being charged $118.41 for a yearly plan they never signed up for, without notice, and others describe refund chases running months.
  • • Initial backups are slow — reviewers report 1 TB taking five days or more on normal broadband.
  • • The desktop and web interfaces are dated, and support responses are slow.

When the paid plan is worth it

The catch here is printed on IDrive’s own pricing page in their own words, and it changes every number on it: the discounted price applies to the first year, and standard pricing applies from year two. Personal 5 TB shows $6.99 a month against a standard $9.99 -- so year one costs $83.88 and year two $119.88, a 43% increase for a service you have by then filled with your backups. Budget from $9.99 and treat the first year as a discount rather than the price. The same applies at every size, up to $99.99 standard ($69.99 discounted) at 100 TB. Credit where it is due: the entry pricing is genuinely cheap. The free Basic tier is 10 GB, and IDrive Mini runs $0.25 a month for 100 GB or $0.83 for 500 GB, which is unusually low for backup storage. The tier names are worth reading carefully because they overlap in price but not in shape. Team starts at the same $9.99 as Personal but covers 5 computers and 5 users on 5 TB. Business starts at $19.99 for only 500 GB -- a twentieth of the storage for double the price -- because what it buys is unlimited users rather than capacity. Choosing on the headline number rather than on the unit will land you on the wrong one. Add-ons sit outside all of that: server backup at $5 a month per server, and Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace backup at $20 per seat per year.

Best for: individuals and small businesses that want cheap multi-device cloud backup with versioning, snapshots and physical seed/restore shipping.
Who should skip it: if you need real-time sync-and-collaborate storage (that's Drive/Dropbox territory, not backup), or you won't tolerate the year-two renewal jump.

The natural comparison is Backblaze or Acronis — Backblaze is simpler flat-rate per computer; Acronis adds security suites — IDrive wins on multi-device flexibility per dollar, as long as you price in the year-two step-up. Weigh it against Backblaze on the job you actually need done.

Frequently asked questions

Is IDrive secure and reliable enough to depend on?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.

Who should not use IDrive?

Skip it if you need real-time sync-and-collaborate storage (that's Drive/Dropbox territory, not backup), or you won't tolerate the year-two renewal jump. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
