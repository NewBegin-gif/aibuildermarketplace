What actually drives the quote

There is no paid plan to weigh here, because there are no plans. Increff publishes no pricing at all — its own /pricing URL returns a 404, and our monthly captures of the site through July and August 2026 produced no amount whatsoever. Their partnership team confirmed to us directly on 28 July 2026 that every deal is priced case by case. So the useful question is not which tier to buy but what moves the number you will be quoted, and there the vendor is consistent: warehouses, users, and which modules you take (WMS, merchandising).

That gives you three variables to fix before you ask, and fixing them is what makes competing quotes comparable. Decide your warehouse count, your named user count, and your module scope in writing first; ask for the quote against that specification; and ask explicitly what each additional warehouse and each additional block of users costs, because those are the lines that grow after signature. A quote that arrives as one number with no unit rates behind it cannot be compared against anything, including next year's version of itself.

Budget separately for what sits outside the licence, because it is documented and it is not small. Increff's own onboarding checklist requires handhelds running Android 8 or above with Chrome 90 or above, barcode label printers at 200 DPI minimum, QR-code printers at 600 DPI minimum, and 512 KBps of bandwidth per device — per warehouse. Product master data has to be loaded through CSV templates capped at 50,000 rows per file, and the packing PC needs a local print application running as a Tomcat service.

None of that appears in a software quote, and warehouses that skip it stall on scanning failures rather than on the platform. The honest framing: opaque enterprise pricing is itself a signal about who this is for. Algorithmic merchandising and inventory optimization only repay their cost at a catalogue and volume large enough for the optimization to move real money. If you are a small or mid-sized seller, the absence of a published price is telling you something before you ever reach the sales call.

How to actually use Increff

The shortest route to value runs through Increff's own onboarding checklist, which the support portal says must be completed before Go Live. Hardware comes first: the WMS hardware guide asks for handhelds running Android 8 or above with Chrome 90 or above, barcode label printers at 200 DPI minimum, QR-code printers at 600 DPI minimum, and 512 KBps of bandwidth per device. Warehouses that skip this stall on scanning and label failures rather than on software.

Product master data is the next gate. In Omni the path is OMS, then Products, then Product Setting: download the CSV template, fill it, upload it. The documentation caps each file at 50,000 rows, rejects duplicate SKUs and SKUs already present in Omni OMS, and warns that the descriptive row beneath the headers must be removed before submitting. The packing PC then needs the print application, a Tomcat service Chrome must be allowed to reach on localhost.

Increff markets floor training as under 30 minutes; the configuration around it is where schedules actually slip.

Frequently asked questions

What does Increff cost?

Increff does not publish a price, and we have now checked that three ways: the pricing URL 404s, and two monthly captures of the site contain no amount. Their partnership team told us directly on 28 July 2026 that every deal is priced case by case on warehouses, users and modules. Expect a quote, and expect it to depend on your operation rather than on a tier.

What should I ask for in the quote?

The three things that drive their number: how many warehouses you run, how many users need access, and which modules you actually take — WMS and merchandising are priced as separate pieces. Asking for a per-warehouse and per-module breakdown makes an enterprise quote comparable with alternatives that do publish tiers.

Is it worth evaluating without a published price?

It depends on where you are. Increff is built for multi-warehouse retail operations, so if you are running one warehouse and a handful of SKUs, the implementation weight will outrun the benefit regardless of the number. If you are past that point, the lack of a public price is normal for this class of software and not in itself a warning sign.

What should I weigh before buying Operations & Workflow software?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.
