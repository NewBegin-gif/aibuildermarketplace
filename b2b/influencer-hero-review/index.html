Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 18 Sep 2026

Four plans, quoted per month but charged up front for the period, and every plan carries a three-month minimum term. Standard is $519 a month: 1,000 reach-outs a month, product send-outs, 20 influencer payouts, influencer analytics, consultation and training, and one seat. Pro is $839: 5,000 reach-outs, unlimited templates, conversion-enhanced filters, 200 payouts, a dedicated account manager and three seats. Business is $1,992: 10,000 reach-outs, UGC tracking up to 1,500, custom API integrations, unlimited payouts, unlimited follower checks and eight seats.

Enterprise is quote-only. Paying quarterly saves 20% and paying annually 30% against the monthly rate, billed up front for the full period. The influencer CRM, the outreach automation and the UGC tracking are in every plan — the ladder is gated on quota and seats, not on features. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

The honest knock

Every plan carries it, so the smallest honest entry price is not $519 but three months of it — $1,557 — and that is the figure to weigh, because an influencer programme takes longer than four weeks to produce anything you can judge. Ask yourself whether you can fill 1,000 reach-outs a month for three months before you sign, not whether you can afford one. The quota is the meter and it counts attempts, not results.

A reach-out is a message sent, so a badly targeted list burns the allowance exactly as fast as a good one. Standard's 1,000 a month against Pro's 5,000 is a fivefold jump for a 62% price increase, which is the tell that they expect volume to be the binding constraint rather than features — and the feature list confirms it: the CRM, the outreach automation and the UGC tracking are in every tier from the bottom.

What the page does not publish is what happens when you run out. There is no overage rate anywhere on it, so the only stated remedy for exceeding 1,000 reach-outs is the next plan up at $320 a month more. That is a meaningful gap in a product sold on volume. Payouts are the second meter and the one that bites at the bottom. Standard covers 20 influencer payouts; Pro covers 200.

If your programme pays creators rather than only gifting them, 20 is a pilot, and the jump to a real programme is the jump to $839. Seats follow the same shape — one, three, eight — so a two-person team is pushed to Pro by headcount alone, regardless of volume.

Three months up front is the price, not one

Take the headline figures and turn them into what you will actually be charged, because this page quotes per month and bills by the period. Every plan has a three-month minimum. So Standard is $1,557 committed, Pro is $2,517 and Business is $5,976 — those are the entry tickets. Paying quarterly instead of monthly takes 20% off, and annually 30%, both charged up front for the whole period, so an annual Standard commitment is a four-figure decision made once.

Now the ladder itself. Standard to Pro is $320 a month more, a 62% increase, and it buys five times the reach-outs (1,000 to 5,000), ten times the payouts (20 to 200) and three seats instead of one. Measured per reach-out that is a real improvement: Standard is about 52 cents a message, Pro about 17 cents, Business about 20 cents. The cheapest message on the page is Pro's, not Business's — Business buys UGC tracking, custom API integrations, unlimited payouts and eight seats, and it costs more per message to get them.

That makes the tiering question unusually clean. If you will send more than about 1,600 messages a month, Pro is cheaper per message than Standard is at its own cap, and you should be on it. If you will not, the binding constraint is probably payouts or seats rather than volume, and the honest answer may be that the programme is not ready for a three-month commitment at all. What no tier buys you is a published overage rate.

Exceed your reach-outs and the page offers no per-unit price, only the next plan. Budget the tier you will grow into rather than the one you fit today.

How to actually use Influencer Hero

Decide the employment of the budget before the tier: this is a three-month commitment on every plan, so the question is whether you can run a programme for a quarter, not whether you can afford a month. Size yourself on reach-outs first, because that is the meter. Count the creators you would realistically contact in a month, remembering that a reach-out is a message sent and not a reply received — a poorly targeted list spends the quota at exactly the same rate as a good one.

Standard's 1,000 works out at about 52 cents a message; if your number is above roughly 1,600, Pro is cheaper per message and you should start there. Then check the two limits that catch people out at the bottom. Standard includes 20 influencer payouts a month and a single seat. If you pay creators rather than only sending product, or if more than one person will work in the tool, those are the constraints that move you up rather than volume.

Connect the affiliate side early. The platform carries creator discovery, outreach automation, a CRM, UGC tracking and affiliate management together, and the argument for paying this much rather than assembling it from cheaper parts rests on those pieces sharing one record of each creator. Running outreach here while tracking payouts in a spreadsheet gives you the price of the suite and the value of a mailbox. The vendor also circulates a link that opens a Calendly demo booking instead — useful if you want the walkthrough, but it is a sales call rather than a trial, and there is no self-serve sign-up published anywhere on the pricing page.
