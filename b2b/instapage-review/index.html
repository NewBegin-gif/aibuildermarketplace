Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 4 Sep 2026

Instapage does publish plan prices; they are not on the URL we had recorded. instapage.com/en/pricing redirects to the homepage at instapage.com/lp, and the plan table sits at instapage.com/plans. Our 26 August reading landed on the redirect, found no figures and concluded that prices had been withdrawn; the figures below match the last published rates this dossier already held, so the more likely explanation is a moved page rather than a withdrawal.

Three tiers sit there. Create is $99 a month month-to-month or $79 billed annually, for 15,000 unique monthly visitors. Optimize is $199 or $159 annually for 30,000 unique monthly visitors, with selectors for 50,000 and for a custom volume — the custom option defaults to 50,000 and hands you to sales. Convert is quote-only. Annual billing is advertised as 'save up to 20%', and every tier carries unlimited contacts.

The 14-day free trial asks for a credit card and is capped at 2,500 unique monthly visitors; Instapage's own FAQ states that exceeding that cap ends the trial automatically and moves you onto the paid plan you selected. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The 14-day trial needs a card and carries its own cap of 2,500 visitors; crossing it ends the trial and starts the paid plan by itself. And there's the ownership question the feature list does not raise: publishing to your own domain means pointing a CNAME at Instapage — their help centre carries a per-registrar guide for exactly that — so the page is served by them and lives as long as the subscription does.

Their own docs add a second catch worth knowing before you standardise on it: “we lock domains under the first Instapage account that uses them”. Treat these pages as rented campaign infrastructure, not as part of your site, and keep the copy somewhere you control.

What Instapage does

Enterprise landing-page and conversion platform: build, personalize and A/B-test post-click landing pages at scale, with ad-to-page mapping, heatmaps and fast AMP pages — built for paid-ads and demand-gen teams.

What the visitor ceiling costs

Instapage prices on unique monthly visitors, and that is the only number that moves. Create is $79 a month billed annually ($99 month-to-month) for 15,000 visitors; Optimize is $159 ($199) for 30,000, with selectors for 50,000 and for a custom volume that quietly defaults to 50,000 and hands you to sales. Work that out per thousand visitors and it is $5.27 on Create against $5.30 on Optimize — the same rate.

Moving up does not buy cheaper traffic; it buys server-side A/B testing, experimentation and the lead-generation features. What upgrading does not fix is ownership. Publishing to your own domain means pointing a CNAME at Instapage, so the page is served by them and lives exactly as long as the subscription does, and their own docs lock a domain under the first Instapage account that uses it. No tier changes that.

Nor does any tier change the threshold question: this is post-click optimisation for serious ad spend, and below real paid-traffic volume $79 a month buys tooling you cannot feed.

How to actually use Instapage

Point the domain at your own Instapage account, not your agency's. Their own documentation says domains are locked under the first Instapage account that uses them, so if someone else sets up the CNAME during a campaign, untangling it later is a support conversation rather than a setting. Get this right at the start; it is not easily reversible. Then plan the trial around its own cap, which is much tighter than the plan you are testing.

The 14 days need a credit card, and the trial allows 2,500 unique monthly visitors — a sixth of what Create carries. Instapage's FAQ says crossing that line ends the trial automatically and starts the paid subscription on the plan you picked, so point a slice of traffic you control at it rather than a live campaign you cannot throttle. And treat these pages as rented campaign infrastructure. The page is served by Instapage for exactly as long as you pay, so keep your copy, your images and your layout decisions somewhere you control.
