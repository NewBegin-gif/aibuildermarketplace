When the paid plan is worth it

The ladder counts products OR VARIANTS, and that word is where stores get caught. Basic is $49 a month for up to 100, Professional $179 for 1,000, Premium $249 for 2,000 and Business $399 for 5,000, each with a 14-day trial and roughly 17% off on annual billing. Above that the vendor now publishes an Enterprise tier at $749 a month ($7,490 a year) for up to 10,000 SKUs. Count your variants before you read those prices.

Per variant the ladder is genuinely generous -- $0.49 on Basic falling to $0.08 on Business and $0.075 on Enterprise -- but that only helps if your variant count sits comfortably inside a band. Sitting just above a boundary is the expensive place to be: 1,050 variants pays the same $249 as 2,000. The tiers above Premium used to be unquotable: the vendor published three different figures for Business across its own pages.

As of 8 September 2026 that is fixed -- listing, pricing page and homepage all say $399, and Enterprise is openly priced at $749 rather than negotiated. If you were quoted $499 earlier this year, that number is no longer published anywhere. One operational cost no tier covers, and no upgrade removes: automatic repricing is not a switch you leave alone. Buying Business does not make the automatch more careful; it only raises the SKU ceiling.

Set floor prices before you enable dynamic pricing, and audit what the automatch decided in the first fortnight -- that fortnight of attention is part of the price at every tier.

Frequently asked questions

What does Intelis cost?

Four tiers on the Shopify listing, re-read on 19 September 2026 and unchanged since 8 September, each with a 14-day free trial and about 17% off on annual billing. Basic is $49 a month or $490 a year for up to 100 products or variants. Professional is $179 or $1,790 for 1,000. Premium is $249 or $2,490 for 2,000. Business is $399 or $3,990 for 5,000. The vendor’s own pages add an Enterprise tier at $749 a month ($7,490 a year) for up to 10,000 SKUs.

Does the plan count products or variants?

Variants, and this is the most common way stores misprice this app. A rail of forty garments that comes in five sizes and three colours counts as six hundred, which puts a shop most people would describe as small on the $179 rung instead of the $49 one. Do that sum before you read the price list. Per variant the ladder is genuinely generous -- $0.49 on Basic falling to $0.08 on Business -- but only if your count sits comfortably inside a band. Sitting just over a boundary is the expensive place to be: 1,050 variants pays the same $249 as 2,000 does.

Why do I see a different price for Intelis elsewhere?

Because the vendor’s own pages used to disagree with the Shopify listing above Premium. On 21 August 2026 the listing said Business was $399 while intelis.ai/pricing said $499 with a $4,990 annual rate, and Enterprise appeared as $749 on the homepage and $849 on the pricing page. That is resolved: on 8 September all three pages matched, and the listing’s figures are unchanged on 19 September. But a comparison article written earlier this year may still carry the old numbers, and the Shopify listing is what Shopify billing actually charges you, so start from there.

What should I set up before turning dynamic pricing on?

Floor prices first, an audit of the matching second. Automatic repricing is not a feature you switch on and leave: an unbounded rule set in a competitive category is how a store races itself to the bottom, and a floor is the only thing that stops it. Matching is done by AI across Google Shopping and rival storefronts, and one bad match sends your price chasing something that is not really your product. Spend the 14-day trial checking what the automatch decided on your top twenty sellers, product by product -- that is the work the trial is for.

What should I weigh before buying E-commerce software?

Check your platform and version specifically. Shopify support is close to universal in this category, while WooCommerce, BigCommerce, Magento and headless setups vary a lot, and 'supported' sometimes means a generic API rather than a maintained app. A listing in your own platform's app store, with recent reviews there, is a better signal than the vendor's logo wall.
