What involve.me does

Interactive funnel builder: quizzes, calculators, assessments and payment pages with built-in email sequences — free tier to start, paid tiers priced by live-funnel count from $19/mo annual.

Pricing (2026)

(verified 17 Jul 2026) Free tier to start; Start $19/mo billed annually ($29 month-to-month) with 3 live funnels and 1 user; Grow $49/mo annual ($69 monthly) with 5 funnels and 2 users; Scale $119/mo annual ($139 monthly) with 25 funnels, A/B testing and lead verification; Enterprise from $399/mo on annual contracts only. Extra seats $10/user/mo. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The tier ladder is really a funnel-count ladder — 3 live funnels on Start evaporates once you run campaigns in parallel, and the advertised prices are annual: month-to-month runs $10-20 higher per tier. And the honest fundamental for any interactive-funnel tool: quizzes and calculators convert traffic you already have — they don’t create it, so validate on the free tier before the subscription outruns the pipeline.

What buyers report about involve.me

Recurring themes from G2, Capterra and GetApp, read in August 2026. We have not run involve.me ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Quick to build an interactive funnel, quiz or calculator with no technical knowledge — that is what reviewers consistently name.
  • • There is a free plan to test the concept before paying.
What they complain about
  • • The free tier is 3 live funnels, 1 user, 500 visits a month and 30 days of data retention — enough to try, not to run.
  • • Integrations, conditional logic, analytics and watermark removal all sit behind paid plans. Reviewers object specifically to paying for the middle plan just to remove the watermark, and to Google Tag being blocked on lower tiers.
  • • $19 to $399 depending on plan, and startups on tight budgets find the entry tier limiting.
