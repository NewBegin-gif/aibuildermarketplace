Inworld sells voice and model APIs: text-to-speech, speech-to-text and realtime conversation. On-Demand is $0 and pay as you go: the billing docs say it includes up to 70 minutes of text-to-speech and 400 minutes of speech-to-text free, after which Realtime TTS-2 costs $25 per million input characters, TTS-2 Flash $15 and STT 1 $0.15 an hour of audio. The monthly plans lower those rates and raise the limits.

Creator is $25 a month, with 500 custom voices and 4 professional clones, TTS-2 at $20 and Flash at $10 per million characters. Builder is $100 a month, with 3,000 voices and 10 clones, TTS-2 at $17.50 and Flash at $9. Developer is $300 a month, with 10,000 voices, 25 clones and priority email, TTS-2 at $15 and Flash at $8. Growth is $1,500 a month, with 30,000 voices and 40 clones, TTS-2 at $12.50 and Flash at $7, and lists zero data retention, HIPAA and a BAA as add-ons.

What Inworld AI does

Voice and model APIs for developers building consumer apps: realtime text-to-speech, speech-to-text, voice cloning and realtime conversation, pay as you go or on monthly plans.

Volume, voices and concurrency decide the plan

On-Demand is enough to build and test: no subscription, a free allowance of about 70 minutes of speech, and then the highest per-character rates. A plan is worth it once your monthly usage at On-Demand rates costs more than the plan's price at its lower rates, or when you hit a limit. Creator at $25 a month cuts TTS-2 from $25 to $20 per million characters and doubles concurrent generations from 5 to 10; Builder at $100 raises concurrency to 50, for an app that serves many users at the same moment.

Two terms change the sum. Credits roll over for up to three months only while you keep a paid plan, and cancelling or downgrading forfeits what is left at the end of the period. Payments are non-refundable. Size the plan on a month of real traffic in On-Demand first, and remember that spaces, punctuation and markup tags are billed as characters too.

How to actually use Inworld AI

Inworld's text-to-speech quickstart starts with creating an account, then a Standard API key in the API Keys area of the portal, which you keep in an environment variable on your server rather than in client code. Choose the API that fits the job: speech generation, transcription or a realtime conversation. Realtime sessions have no per-minute or per-session price; you pay for the underlying speech and model use. Before you go to production, follow the production guide: set limits and watch usage by product in the portal.

Concurrency is capped per plan, at 5 simultaneous text-to-speech generations on On-Demand, 10 on Creator, 50 on Builder, 150 on Developer and 500 on Growth. To cancel, open the billing page, click Manage plan and cancel there; it takes effect at the end of the billing period.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Inworld cost?

Read 15 October 2026: On-Demand is $0 and pay as you go, Creator $25 a month, Builder $100, Developer $300, Growth $1,500 and Enterprise custom. Usage is billed on top at the plan's rates, for example Realtime TTS-2 at $25 per million input characters on On-Demand and $20 on Creator.

Is there a free tier?

On-Demand has no subscription fee, and the billing docs say it includes up to 70 minutes of text-to-speech and up to 400 minutes of speech-to-text for free. After that you pay per use.

What happens to unused credits?

Subscription credits roll over for up to 3 months while an eligible paid plan stays active. When you downgrade or cancel, the change takes effect at the end of the billing period and any remaining credits are forfeited. Extra credits you buy expire one year after purchase.

How many requests can run at once?

Inworld's concurrency page lists 5 simultaneous text-to-speech generations on On-Demand, 10 on Creator, 50 on Builder, 150 on Developer, 500 on Growth and custom on Enterprise.

Can I get a refund?

The terms say payments are non-refundable, except where required by law. Paid plans renew automatically until you cancel.
