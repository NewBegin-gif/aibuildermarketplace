All plans are priced per site per month; yearly billing saves 16%. Essentials is €4.99 billed yearly or €5.99 monthly ($5.99 / $6.99) for up to 25K pageviews, a standard privacy and cookie policy with up to 20 clauses, one language, partial branding removal and monthly site scans. Advanced, marked most popular, is €19.99 yearly or €21.99 monthly ($24.99 / $27.99) for up to 50K pageviews, complete legal documents with up to 30 clauses, all languages and geo-targeting.

Ultimate is €79.99 yearly or €89.99 monthly ($99.99 / $119.99) for up to 150K pageviews, unlimited clauses, full branding removal, hourly scans, a mobile SDK, detailed analytics and consent recovery. Above the quota every plan pays €0.05 per 1,000 extra pageviews. Forms proof of consent and the newsletter opt-in booster are €5 a month per 1,000 consents. A free plan exists (its limits are not on the pricing page but in the help centre, see below), every paid plan can be tried for 14 days, and agency and enterprise plans are quoted separately.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Essentials allows one language and up to 20 policy clauses; multilingual sites start on Advanced at €19.99 a month. The free plan's limits are not on the pricing page. What iubenda does

Compliance platform for websites and apps: cookie consent banner with consent log, generated privacy, cookie and terms documents, and Google Consent Mode, priced per site by pageviews.

Essentials covers one language; traffic and languages push you up

For a small site in one language, Essentials at €4.99 a month billed yearly gives a cookie banner, a consent log and a generated privacy and cookie policy. Two limits move most buyers up: a second language and more than 25K pageviews a month. Both lead to Advanced at €19.99, four times the price. Traffic overage is cheap per unit (€0.05 per 1,000 pageviews), so a site slightly over its quota can stay on its plan; 100K extra pageviews a month adds €5.

Every plan is per site, so ten client sites means ten subscriptions unless you go through the agency programme. Ultimate is for high-traffic sites that need consent recovery, analytics and an app SDK. The quota works differently on Free and on paid plans, and that decides when Free stops being enough. iubenda's pricing FAQs (updated 27 August 2026) say paid plans keep working above the quota and the extra pageviews are billed on a separate invoice at the start of the next month.

Free has no extra usage: above 5,000 pageviews the banner is switched off for the rest of the month. The overquota article (same date) describes what visitors then get: no banner, only strictly necessary scripts and no consent collected, so analytics and ad tags that wait for consent stay off. A site that runs close to 5,000 pageviews a month is safer on Essentials, where the same overshoot costs a few cents.

The volume discount is graduated by band, not applied to the whole account: sites 1 to 25 pay full price, 26 to 50 get 20% off, 51 to 100 30%, 101 to 200 40% and from 201 50%. In iubenda's own example of 67 sites, 25 pay full price, 24 get 20% off and 18 get 30% off. Against Consentmo, the Shopify consent app in our files, the free tiers point in opposite directions.

Consentmo's free tier has no cap on banner impressions (read 8 September 2026), while iubenda's free banner stops at 5,000 pageviews; iubenda in return works on any website or app and generates the privacy and cookie policy itself.

How to actually use iubenda

Start on the free plan or a 14-day trial and run the site scan to see which cookies and services iubenda finds. Check your monthly pageviews and the languages you publish in before choosing a plan; those two decide between Essentials and Advanced. Agencies should ask for agency pricing rather than buying per site. Match the banner's language to the plan before going live. The overquota article lists a language mismatch as a cause of a silent banner: if the embed code asks for a language the plan does not include, the banner does not appear and no valid consent is collected.

Free and Essentials allow one language only. The automatic compliance scan runs once every 6 months on Free, monthly on Essentials and Advanced, and hourly on Ultimate. One plan covers a domain and its subdomains for the banner and the policies, but not for the accessibility widget: each Lite or Standard accessibility plan is tied to a single domain or subdomain, so blog.example.com needs a plan of its own. A pageview is counted on every page load where the banner script runs, and the account settings show the month's usage so far.

Frequently asked questions

How much does iubenda cost?

Read on 9 October 2026, per site per month excl. VAT: Essentials €4.99 yearly or €5.99 monthly, Advanced €19.99 or €21.99, Ultimate €79.99 or €89.99. A free plan exists.

What happens if my site exceeds the iubenda pageview limit?

Every plan charges €0.05 per 1,000 pageviews above its quota of 25K, 50K or 150K a month.

Does iubenda support Google Consent Mode v2?

The comparison table lists Google Consent Mode v2 and IAB TCF among the integrations; iubenda says it is a Google-certified CMP partner.

How do I cancel iubenda?

Per website, by switching off auto-renewal. iubenda's cancellation article (updated 5 October 2026) has you open the site in the dashboard, go to Settings and then Manage subscription, and turn the auto-renewal toggle off. That cancels the subscription; switching it back on restores it with your documents and configuration. Cancelling does not delete the account, which has its own deletion guide, and the article says a subscription can also be paused temporarily.

What does the Consent Database cost?

€5 or $6 for each started block of 1,000 consents a month, counted from the first consent and billed on a separate monthly invoice for the month before. The pricing FAQs (updated 27 August 2026) count every change again: a visitor who subscribes to a newsletter and later unsubscribes uses two. Their own example is 1,020 consents in a month costing $12, because the second block has started. It needs a paid plan, and a month with no consents costs nothing.

What happens to my money when I downgrade?

It becomes credit. The pricing FAQs say unused subscription fee is converted and applied to the lower plan, that moving to a cheaper or free plan may leave credit for a later purchase, and that some credits expire after 12 months. Downgrading to Essentials also deactivates the terms and conditions document.

Who is behind iubenda?

iubenda S.r.l. in Milan, founded in 2011, says it serves more than 150,000 customers in over 100 countries (llms.txt, read 24 October 2026). Its site footer says the company is directed and coordinated by team.blue NV and is the parent of consentmanager, CookieFirst and Complianz, three other consent tools a buyer may be comparing it with.
