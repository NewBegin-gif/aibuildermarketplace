What Jibble does

Time and attendance tracking with facial recognition, GPS and payroll-ready timesheets.

Pricing (2026)

(2026-08-01, read on their own pricing page) Their own claim on that page is a free forever plan with unlimited users, described as '100% free', with no credit card required. Paid tiers exist above it but their prices are rendered in the browser and were not visible in the page we could read, so we are not quoting them. The page leads with a savings calculator rather than a price table. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

The honest knock

Free forever with unlimited users is a real claim and unusual in this category, where per-seat pricing is the norm — that alone makes it worth a look for a team on a tight budget. Two things to establish before you rely on it. First, what the free tier actually excludes, because 'unlimited users' and 'unlimited features' are different promises and the paid tiers exist for a reason. Second, note that their pricing page leads with a calculator showing what you supposedly save rather than what you pay: that is a marketing choice, and the number it produces rests on assumptions about payroll leakage that are theirs, not measured in your business.

What buyers report about Jibble

Recurring themes from Capterra, G2, GetApp and Trustpilot, read in August 2026. We have not run Jibble ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • An aggressively generous free tier: facial recognition, GPS, kiosk mode, automated timesheets and overtime rules free for unlimited users. Nothing else in the category does that.
  • • Around 4.5 average across the review sites as of early 2026.
What they complain about
  • • Syncing across devices is the most common complaint on the directories, and Trustpilot adds bugs appearing after you edit a time entry, plus desktop app problems.
  • • Facial recognition is slow and unreliable in poor light or after an appearance change — and staff find it intrusive.
  • • Battery drain, particularly on Android.
  • • Premium is $4.49 per user annually, Ultimate $7.99.

What the free plan really covers

Jibble's own pricing page claims a free forever plan with unlimited users, described as '100% free' and needing no credit card. In a category where per-seat billing is the norm, that is genuinely unusual and it makes the first question different from every other tool here: not which tier, but whether you need a tier at all. We are not quoting the paid prices, because we could not read them — they render in the browser and were not visible in the page we captured. Anything we printed would be a guess dressed as research. What we can say is what to establish, and there are two things. First, what the free tier excludes. 'Unlimited users' and 'unlimited features' are different promises, and the paid tiers exist for a reason. Ask specifically about the things that usually sit behind a boundary in this category: approvals, overtime rules, payroll export, and how far back the reporting goes. A free plan that cannot export to your payroll system moves the work rather than removing it. Second, note how the page is built. It leads with a savings calculator rather than a price table — a number showing what you supposedly save instead of what you pay. That calculation rests on assumptions about payroll leakage that are theirs, not measured in your business, and it is not a substitute for the rate you will be charged. One thing that is a legal question rather than a pricing one: if you plan to use facial recognition for clock-ins, check your local rules on biometric data before you roll it out. That constraint does not appear on any tier.

Best for: teams that need attendance records without a per-seat bill — if the free tier covers your case it is genuinely free, which nothing else in this category offers.
Who should skip it: if you need the paid features, because you will have to get those prices from them directly; also skip facial recognition without checking your local rules on biometric data, which is a legal question rather than a pricing one.

The natural comparison is Clockify or Toggl Track — the direct time-tracking peers — both have free tiers too, so compare on what each free plan excludes rather than on the headline. Decide by which one fits the job above, not by the louder brand.

Frequently asked questions

Is Jibble compliant with local employment rules?

Compliance is country by country, so the honest answer is: only where the vendor says it is, and only for the entity type you actually have. Ask which countries are covered by its own entities versus a local partner, who carries the liability if something is filed wrong, and how changes in local law reach you. Get those answers in the contract rather than the sales deck.

Skip it if you need the paid features, because you will have to get those prices from them directly; also skip facial recognition without checking your local rules on biometric data, which is a legal question rather than a pricing one.
