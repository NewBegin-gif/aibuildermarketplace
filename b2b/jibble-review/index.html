The honest knock

Two things to establish before you rely on it. First, what the free tier actually excludes, because 'unlimited users' and 'unlimited features' are different promises and the paid tiers exist for a reason. Second, note that their pricing page leads with a calculator showing what you supposedly save rather than what you pay. That is a marketing choice, and the number it produces rests on assumptions about payroll leakage that are theirs, not measured in your business.

What the free plan really covers

Jibble's homepage claims a free forever plan with unlimited users, '100% free' and no credit card. In a category where per-seat billing is the norm that is unusual, and it makes the first question different from every other tool here: not which tier, but whether you need a tier at all. We are not quoting paid prices, because on 30 August 2026 there were none to quote. jibble.io/pricing redirects to /upgrade-plans, that redirects to the homepage, and /plans does the same.

The pricing page has been retired, and nothing left on the site prices a paid tier. On 2 September 2026 Jibble's partnerships team confirmed to us in writing that there is no external pricing page, so this is a decision on their side rather than a page we failed to read. What we can say is what to establish, and there are two things. First, what the free tier excludes.

'Unlimited users' and 'unlimited features' are different promises, and the paid tiers exist for a reason. Ask specifically about the things that usually sit behind a boundary in this category: approvals, overtime rules, payroll export, and how far back the reporting goes. A free plan that cannot export to your payroll system moves the work rather than removing it. Second, note what stands where a price table would. An impact calculator projects your annual savings — for 100 people at $25 an hour it prints $270,400 — on three assumptions it publishes in small type: 40 hours a week, 48 working weeks, and 5.5% payroll leakage.

That last figure is theirs, not measured in your business. One thing that is a legal question rather than a pricing one: if you plan to use facial recognition for clock-ins, check your local rules on biometric data before you roll it out. That constraint does not appear on any tier.

Jibble no longer publishes a price for anything above the free plan — /pricing and /plans both redirect to the homepage — so ask for those in writing too. Your own baseline is simple enough: how many hours a month does someone spend chasing and correcting timesheets today, and how many hours end up disputed? That is what a paid tier would have to beat.
