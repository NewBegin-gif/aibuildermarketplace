When the paid plan is worth it

The crossover between pay-as-you-go and the cheapest subscription is about eleven cases a year, and it is worth knowing because it arrives much sooner than the price difference suggests. Pay-as-you-go is $95 per case. Jubilee 36 is $995 a year, which is 10.5 cases at the PAYG rate -- so from the eleventh filing onward the subscription is free money, and it covers you to 36. Above that the per-case cost keeps falling steeply.

At each tier ceiling: $27.64 a case on Jubilee 36, $16.63 on Jubilee 150 ($2,495), $13.32 on Jubilee 300 ($3,995) and $9.99 on Jubilee 500 ($4,995). That is a tenfold improvement on the PAYG rate at the top, and the ladder is unusually generous in the middle -- Jubilee 300 costs only 60% more than Jubilee 150 for double the filings. The overage rates follow the same shape and are the number to check if your volume is lumpy: $34.99 a case over on Jubilee 36, then $26.99, $17.99 and $12.99.

Even the worst overage rate is well under the $95 PAYG price, so under-buying a tier is a mild mistake rather than an expensive one -- start lower than you expect and move up. Seats are the one thing that barely moves: 2 users on the entry tier, 3 on the two middle tiers, 5 on Jubilee 500. If you have a larger team, that ceiling arrives before the filing ceiling does.

How to actually use Jubilee

Count your annual filings, because that single number picks your plan. Pay-as-you-go is $95 a case and the smallest subscription is $995 a year, so the crossover is about eleven filings — below that, pay per case; above it, Jubilee 36 is already cheaper, at an effective $27.64 a case against $95. Then size slightly low rather than high, because the overage rates make that the cheaper mistake. Jubilee 36 plus overage at $34.99 a case stays cheaper than Jubilee 150 all the way up to about 79 filings a year.

The per-case rates keep improving as you climb — $27.64 on Jubilee 36, $16.63 on 150, $13.32 on 300 and $9.99 on 500 — so the ladder is genuinely good value at volume; you just do not need to buy ahead of yourself. Count users as well as cases, since they move separately: two on Jubilee 36, three on both 150 and 300, five on 500. A three-person practice filing modest volume is choosing on seats rather than on cases.

And be clear about scope before any of that. This is bankruptcy petition preparation and case management, and its value is entirely bounded by that practice area — the automation and court-form handling are exactly the point for a bankruptcy practice and close to worthless outside one.
