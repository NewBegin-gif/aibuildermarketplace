When the paid plan is worth it

There is one line in this pricing that changes everything and it is missing from most summaries: Essentials charges a 5% transaction fee on your sales, and every tier above it charges nothing.

The step from Essentials to Growth is $60 a month ($59 against $119). At 5%, that $60 is covered by $1,200 of monthly sales — so above roughly $1,200 a month in revenue, the cheapest plan is the most expensive one. The gap widens fast: at $5,000 a month Essentials costs $309 against Growth's $119, and at $10,000 it is $559 against $119. If you are selling anything at all, Essentials is a false economy, and the only people it genuinely suits are those still building and not yet charging.

The rest of the ladder is more conventional: Advanced $229 and Professional $549 monthly, with yearly billing at $52, $99, $189 and $429 — advertised as up to 22% off, and the discount does grow as you climb (12% on Essentials, 22% on Professional). Essentials also caps you at 10,000 emails and texts a month against unlimited above it, and includes one custom domain.

So the practical advice is unusually clear-cut. Work out your expected monthly sales before picking a tier; if it clears about $1,200, start on Growth and never look at Essentials again. There is a 14-day trial, and the thing to test in it is breadth rather than depth: Kartra's case is funnels, email, checkout, memberships and scheduling under one roof instead of five subscriptions. If polished course delivery is your core need rather than one of five, a dedicated creator platform does that piece better.

How to actually use Kartra

Work out your monthly sales before you choose a tier, because Essentials carries a 5% transaction fee and every tier above it charges nothing. That single line settles the decision arithmetically: the $60 monthly gap between Essentials and Growth equals 5% of $1,200, so the moment you sell more than $1,200 a month, Growth is literally the cheaper plan. On yearly billing the crossover falls to about $940. At $10,000 a month in sales the fee alone is $500 — eight times the difference between the plans.

Check the second cap while you are there. Essentials sends 10,000 emails and texts a month against unlimited above it, and includes one custom domain. So the entry tier is constrained on two axes at once, and both of them tighten exactly as the business starts working.

Use the 14-day trial to build one real funnel end to end — page, form, email sequence, checkout — rather than exploring the feature list. Kartra's argument is breadth: one system instead of five, with the pieces already talking to each other. That is only worth anything if the whole loop works for your product, and a fortnight is enough to find out.

And be honest about what you are buying. If polished course delivery is your core need, Kajabi's creator features are stronger. Kartra wins on all-in-one breadth, not on course depth.

Frequently asked questions

What does Kartra cost?

Essentials $59 a month or $52 billed yearly, Growth $119 / $99, Advanced $229 / $189 and Professional $549 / $429, with yearly saving up to 22% and a 14-day trial. Read on 17 August 2026.

Why is Essentials cheaper than it looks expensive?

Because of the transaction fee. Essentials takes 5% of your sales; every tier above it takes 0%. If you sell $2,000 a month that fee is $100, already more than the $60 difference to Growth. Unless you are pre-revenue, the entry tier is usually the more expensive choice — work it out against your own monthly sales before picking it.

How does it compare with Kajabi?

That comparison is the single most-searched thing about Kartra on our pages. Both bundle courses, checkout, email and pages into one login, so the honest split is not features but what you give up: Kartra's entry tier is cheaper on paper but carries the 5% sales fee, and its email allowance is capped at 10,000 sends a month until you reach Growth. Compare the tier you would actually run on, including the fee, rather than the advertised starting prices.

Is Kartra worth it for a small team?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Small teams get the most out of this category when one clear problem is already costing real hours or revenue; buying ahead of that just adds cost and another login. Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.

Who should not use Kartra?

Skip it if you only need one of those pieces — a dedicated tool does that piece better and cheaper than an all-in-one. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
