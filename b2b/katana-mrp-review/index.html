Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 17 Aug 2026

A Free plan covering 30 SKUs with unlimited users, integrations and locations plus full features and API, then Core starting at $299/mo with unlimited SKUs and users and 24/7 support, and an Advantage tier on custom pricing. The add-ons are where the real cost sits and they are priced separately: manufacturing routings and insights $199/mo, end-to-end traceability via batch or serial numbers $249/mo, warehouse management with bin locations and pick-and-pack $149/mo, plus an optional $2,000 item on the page. Higher monthly sales-order volume also raises the effective cost. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

When the paid plan is worth it

Katana's free tier is not a trial, which is what makes the split unusual. The vendor documents it as carrying all Katana features and add-ons, unlimited users, unlimited integrations and unlimited locations, capped only on catalogue size at 30 SKUs. That single cap is the whole decision. A workshop with a narrow product range can run a genuine operation on the free plan indefinitely, and Katana documents a 15-day grace period during which the SKU ceiling lifts, so a growing catalogue does not stall mid-import.

Paying starts to make sense once the catalogue permanently outgrows that cap, since the Core plan starts at $299/month for unlimited SKUs with support available 24/7. What upgrading explicitly does not buy is a predictable flat bill. Core includes 1 inventory location and bills per delivered sales order, so extra sites and order growth lift the effective cost above the headline. Depth is priced apart as well: Traceability at $249/mo, Manufacturing Management at $199/mo and Warehouse Management at $149/mo.

Buyers who upgrade expecting batch traceability or shop-floor control to be bundled will be billed for it separately.

How to actually use Katana MRP

The fastest route to something real is to leave the demo data in place at first. Katana's setup guide recommends editing the sample products, recipes and sales orders before entering anything of your own, then clearing it later with the Demo data button in the navigation bar. Set base currency and units of measure under Settings before importing, because those choices propagate into every item created afterwards. Bulk loading runs through Settings > Data import > Add new products, where an .xlsx template is downloaded.

Each row represents a variant, and the Product name field groups variants under one product. The import itself takes a few minutes depending on item count. The usual snag is the matching rule. Rows are matched on Variant code / SKU and existing items are skipped rather than updated, so a corrected sheet re-imported over the same SKUs silently changes nothing; a separate Update template is required. Field limits also trip people up: category names cap at 35 characters and units of measure at 7.

Distilled from Vendor pricing page, flexible-pricing page, onboarding overview and Katana's own support knowledge base articles, read 4 August 2026.

The natural comparison is Cin7 Core or Fishbowl — comparable manufacturing/inventory platforms — weigh by which add-ons you'd actually enable and your order volume.

Frequently asked questions

What does Katana really cost once it is set up?

Core starts at $299 a month, but the add-ons are priced on top and most manufacturers need at least one: routings and insights $199, batch or serial traceability $249, warehouse management $149, each per month. A shop that needs traceability is therefore looking at $548 a month before sales-order volume is counted. Read on 17 August 2026.

Is the free plan usable or is it a demo?

It is genuinely usable within one hard limit: 30 SKUs. Within that you get unlimited users, unlimited locations, the integrations and the API, which is unusually generous — most free manufacturing tiers cap users first. If your catalogue is under 30 SKUs it is a real plan; the moment it is not, you are on Core.

What are people comparing Katana with?

The measured search on our pages is for alternatives, and the reason is almost always the $299 floor: small manufacturers look at Katana, see the entry price plus the add-ons, and go looking for something cheaper. The fair comparison is total cost including the modules you need rather than the headline, because the cheaper tools often price those same modules in.

What should I weigh before buying Operations & Workflow software?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.
