What Keap does

CRM, sales and marketing automation built for small businesses. Capture leads, automate follow-up and manage your pipeline in one place.

Pricing (2026)

It starts at $299/mo billed monthly for the whole platform, with no feature-based tiers and no free plan. Users and contacts are added on top via the calculator on their pricing page. Keap also requires paid implementation services — strategy consulting, data import and migration — quoted separately before you go live (re-verified 2026-07-21). Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

The honest knock

Cost is the big one (56% of reviewers flag it), the interface feels dated, and there's a real 2–4 week learning curve on the automation builder. Billing and cancellation complaints are common, and email design is thinner than dedicated email tools.

What buyers report about Keap

Recurring themes from Capterra, G2 and Trustpilot, read in August 2026. We have not run Keap ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Every feature is included at every tier — you pay for usage limits, not for unlocking things. Reviewers evaluating the automation are genuinely impressed by what it does.
  • • For a service business that wants CRM, email and payments in one system, it is a real all-in-one.
What they complain about
  • • Cancelling an annual contract early costs $299, and you must telephone support at least 10 days before your next billing date to start the process. Put that date in your calendar the day you sign.
  • • From $249/month on annual billing with no free plan, plus a mandatory onboarding package of $499-$1,500 up front. 56% of Capterra reviewers call it too expensive.
  • • In-app chat waits exceed 30 minutes — on a platform costing $300+ a month.
  • • Recurring billing errors and double charges are reported.

Where the ratings disagree. 1.2 out of 5 on Trustpilot — the lowest we have found. The split is stark: people evaluating the automation are impressed, people trying to cancel or dispute a charge are not. Both groups are describing the same company.

What the $299 does not include

Keap starts at $299 a month billed monthly for the whole platform. There are no feature tiers to weigh and no free plan, so the usual question — which plan — does not apply. The question that does is what sits outside that number, and there are two things.

Users and contacts are added on top, through the calculator on their own pricing page. That makes $299 a floor for the smallest possible configuration rather than a price for your configuration, and it is the first thing to put a real number against.

The second is larger and easier to miss: Keap requires paid implementation services — strategy consulting, data import and migration — quoted separately before you go live. That is not an optional onboarding package you can decline to save money; it is part of the purchase. Ask for that quote alongside the subscription, because a first-year cost built from $299 times twelve will be wrong by whatever implementation comes to.

Budget time as well as money. Reviewers put the automation builder at a two-to-four-week learning curve, and cost is the single most flagged complaint — 56% of reviewers raise it. Billing and cancellation complaints are common enough to be worth reading the terms on before signing rather than after. None of that makes it the wrong tool for a service business that genuinely wants CRM, email, payments and automation in one place and will invest the weeks to learn it. It does mean this is a commitment decision rather than a subscription decision.

How to actually use Keap

Ask for the implementation quote before the subscription quote. Keap requires paid implementation services — strategy consulting, data import and migration — that are quoted separately and are not on the pricing page, and you cannot go live without them. Get the figure and the timeline in writing alongside the $299 a month, because the two together are what you are actually agreeing to.

Then use the calculator on their pricing page with your real numbers. There are no feature-based tiers here — you get the whole platform — but users and contacts are added on top of the base, so your contact count is what makes the monthly figure yours rather than theirs.

Name the person who will own the automation builder and give them time. The reported reality is a two-to-four-week learning curve, and an automation platform nobody has learned is the most expensive kind of software there is. Cost is the most-flagged complaint here — 56% of reviewers raise it — and that complaint is usually about value rather than the number, which is another way of saying it is about adoption.

Get the cancellation terms in writing on the day you sign. Billing and cancellation complaints are common enough to be a pattern, and the time to establish notice periods and refund conditions is before the implementation invoice, not after.
