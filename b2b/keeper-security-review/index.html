Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 17 Sep 2026

Three business tiers, all billed annually: Business Starter at $2.00 per user per month ($24 a year), described as protection for 5-10 users; Business at $4.00 per user per month ($48 a year); Enterprise at $6.00 per user per month ($72 a year), sold through Request a Quote. The amounts are injected by script, so a page fetched without rendering shows the tiers with the figures missing.

Personal and family plans are priced on their own page: Personal (Keeper Unlimited) $42.99 billed annually and Family $91.99 billed annually. The dossier previously carried those last two euro figures as dollar amounts. The add-ons carry their own list, also in US dollars: BreachWatch and KeeperChat $24 per user per year (€22 on the euro list), Advanced Reporting & Alerts and Compliance Reports $12 per user per year (€11), Endpoint Privilege Manager $36 per endpoint per year, Secure File Storage from $132 per year for 100GB (€108), Professional Services from $270 per year, with Connection Manager and Keeper Secrets Manager priced only on request. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

The honest knock

Read the add-ons page next to the plan page and the shape becomes clear: BreachWatch and KeeperChat are $24 per user per year each, Advanced Reporting & Alerts and Compliance Reports $12 per user per year each, endpoint privilege management $36 per endpoint per year — so a Business seat at $4.00 a month can quietly double once you add the monitoring and reporting most buyers assume is included, and Connection Manager and Secrets Manager are not even priced.

Annual billing is stated on every tier, so there is no month-to-month option to test with, and Business Starter is scoped at 5–10 users. Ask for the base plan and each add-on you need as separate lines, because a bundled quote here hides which part is actually costing you.

What buyers report about Keeper Security

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Keeper Security ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • 256-bit AES with a strict zero-knowledge model, never breached, and third-party security audits published regularly. That is the record you want from a password manager.
  • • One of the most feature-rich options available, and easy to use with it.
What they complain about
  • • Auto-renewal is the recurring complaint: reviewers report being charged before the renewal date, and at least one reports it renewing again after they had switched auto-renewal off.
  • • There is no money-back guarantee and no refunds. Combined with the renewal behaviour above, that is the thing to manage.
  • • Breach scanning and secure file storage are paid add-ons rather than included — unusual in a security-first package.
  • • Pricing and discounting have shifted over time, making renewal quotes hard to reconcile with what you were originally told.

When the paid plan is worth it

Keeper's business page does publish per-user prices — $2.00, $4.00 and $6.00 per user per month, all billed annually — but the figures are rendered client-side, so they are invisible to anything that reads the page without running its scripts. What the tiers buy is a question of boundaries. Business Starter is stated as protection for 5–10 users and covers the encrypted vault, admin console, sharing and autofill. Business adds shared team folders, delegated administration, advanced organisational structure and integrations, and a free Family Plan for every user.

Enterprise is where provisioning lives: SCIM, AD/LDAP and SSO/SAML, advanced two-factor, role-based access control and developer APIs, and it is the one tier routed through sales rather than a checkout. What no upgrade folds in is the security posture the product pages advertise. Endpoint Privilege Manager, BreachWatch, Secure File Storage, Connection Manager, Secrets Manager, Advanced Reporting & Alerts, Compliance Reports, KeeperChat and Professional Services are all sold from a separate add-ons page, and two of them — Connection Manager and Secrets Manager — are quote-only there. Every tier is billed annually, so there is no monthly option to test with.

How to actually use Keeper Security

The trial is the way in and it is fully functional. Their guide sends you to the business trial sign-up or Try it Free on the homepage, then asks for a business email address. Two choices made at sign-up are hard to undo. First the data centre: US, EU, AU, CA and JP are offered, with US GovCloud on request, and the documentation says that picking the wrong region means contacting support to delete the trial and start again.

Second the administrator master password: because of the zero-knowledge architecture, Keeper support cannot reset it, so the guide recommends turning on Account Recovery with a recovery phrase from the Settings screen straight after logging in. After verifying the email you land in the Admin Console, where Add Users invites the rest of the team or additional administrators, each of whom sets their own master password.

Frequently asked questions

What does Keeper cost per user?

Three business tiers, all billed annually, read on their business pricing page on 17 September 2026: Business Starter $2.00 per user per month ($24 a year), Business $4.00 ($48), Enterprise $6.00 ($72) and sold through Request a Quote rather than a checkout. There is no month-to-month option on any tier, so there is nothing to test with short of the free trial. Two practical warnings. The figures are written into the page by script, so a fetch that does not run JavaScript reports that Keeper prices nothing — an easy way to conclude wrongly that this is a quote-only product. And the price list is regional, not converted: a Dutch visitor is served €2.00 and €4.00 on the first two tiers, matching the dollars, but €5.50 on Enterprise against $6.00. Check which list you are looking at before you budget.

What is not included in the per-user price?

Most of the security posture the product pages advertise, and it is sold from a separate add-ons page. Read on 17 September 2026, in US dollars: BreachWatch $24 per user per year, KeeperChat $24 per user per year, Advanced Reporting & Alerts $12 per user per year, Compliance Reports $12 per user per year, Endpoint Privilege Manager $36 per endpoint per year, Secure File Storage from $132 a year for 100GB, Professional Services from $270 a year. Connection Manager and Keeper Secrets Manager are not priced at all — both read “Request a Quote”. Do the arithmetic before you compare: a Business seat at $4.00 a month is $48 a year, and adding dark-web monitoring plus reporting plus compliance takes it to $96 — double, for three things many buyers assume are in the plan. Ask for the base plan and each add-on as separate lines.

Is a business plan right for a two-person team?

Probably not, and Keeper's own scoping says so. Business Starter is described on the pricing page as protection for 5-10 users, read 17 September 2026, so a pair is below the band the tier is sold for. Their consumer plans cover the same job for less: Personal (Keeper Unlimited) at $42.99 billed annually and Family at $91.99 billed annually, both US list prices. What you give up by going that route is the reason the business tiers exist — the admin console, delegated administration, shared team folders, audit and, at Enterprise, SCIM, AD/LDAP and SSO/SAML provisioning and role-based access control. If the need is genuinely a shared vault between two people, the Family plan is the cheaper honest answer. Note also that the Business tier includes a free Family plan for every user, which changes the arithmetic once you are actually at five people.

What should I weigh before buying Security software?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.
