When the paid plan is worth it

There is no free tier and no discount code — Kinsta told us directly on 28 July 2026 that it gives no partner-only or affiliate-only discounts, and that the two months free on annual plans is open to everyone. So the decision is single-site against the agency tiers. A single site starts around $35 a month. Business 1, the first genuine multi-site tier, runs $115 a month; by Business 2 the per-site cost is roughly $22.50 across 6–10 sites, and Business 3 covers 20 sites, 400K visits, 50 GB and 6 PHP workers. The crossover is around four or five client sites — below that you are paying for headroom you will not use. The number that decides it is not the plan price but the overage behaviour. Exceeding your visit cap bills per 1,000 additional visits, with email warnings at 80% and 100%. If overages approach the cost of your plan, or hit $500, whichever is less, Kinsta may require immediate payment or an upgrade. On a client site during a traffic spike that is a decision made under pressure — ask about the bandwidth-based plan instead if your traffic is spiky.

How to actually use Kinsta

Start with the migration, because it is free and most people do not realise how free. Kinsta's own docs describe basic migrations as free and unlimited, with the only limit being the number of WordPress installs your plan allows. Request one in MyKinsta under WordPress sites, the Migrations tab, then Request new migration — either from your current host or from a backup link on Drive or Dropbox. Budget the time honestly: their stated turnaround is up to 2 business days, and you are told if it will take longer. If you need it faster, expedited migration is $49 per site targeting 8 hours between 09:00 and 23:00 UTC on weekdays — and agency plans include free expedited credits scaled to the number of sites, so check what you already have before paying. Then set the traffic alerts up before you need them. The 80% and 100% warnings are the only thing standing between a normal month and an upgrade you did not plan — make sure they reach a person, not a shared inbox nobody reads. Frequently asked questions

Is Kinsta secure and reliable enough to depend on?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.

Who should not use Kinsta?

Skip it if you run a small, low-traffic site on a tight budget — entry-level shared hosting or a caching plugin gets you most of the way for a fraction of the price. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
