What buyers report about Kinsta

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Kinsta ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • 4.8 on both Trustpilot (1,000+ reviews) and G2 — performance and support are what reviewers rate, consistently.
  • • It does what managed WordPress hosting is supposed to do, and testers running multiple sites confirm it.
  • • TrustRadius reviewers who run client sites name the staging environment and the dashboard as the reasons they moved, and several describe migrations that were handled for them without downtime.
What they complain about
  • • From $35/month, roughly ten times entry-level shared hosting. Price appears in nearly every critical review.
  • • Visit-based plans charge overage when traffic spikes, and reviewers report being caught out. There is now a bandwidth-based option that avoids this — ask for it if your traffic is spiky.
  • • CDN overage charges have pushed reviewers toward the $625 Enterprise package.
  • • 2025-2026 reviews mention longer support response times as the customer base grew, and difficulty reaching the billing team specifically.
  • • The TrustRadius complaints are about missing conveniences rather than about performance, and they are specific: only one staging environment per site, no way to restore just the database or just the files from a backup, no integrated email hosting (you need a separate provider), and limited Cloudflare control from inside the Kinsta panel.
  • • One reviewer notes the help centre is thorough but assumes technical knowledge — worth knowing if you are buying managed hosting precisely to avoid that.

Where the ratings disagree. TrustRadius is the outlier and the reason is the sample: its Kinsta reviews are few, mostly from 2022, and several carry an 'Incentivized' label. That is a snapshot of Kinsta before the price and support complaints of 2025-2026 that Trustpilot and G2 carry. Read it for the feature gaps, not for the current state of support.

When the paid plan is worth it

There is no free tier and no discount code — Kinsta told us directly on 28 July 2026 that it gives no partner-only or affiliate-only discounts, and that the two months free on annual plans is open to everyone. So the decision is single-site against the agency tiers. A single site starts around $35 a month. Business 1, the first genuine multi-site tier, runs $115 a month; by Business 2 the per-site cost is roughly $22.50 across 6–10 sites, and Business 3 covers 20 sites, 400K visits, 50 GB and 6 PHP workers.

The crossover is around four or five client sites — below that you are paying for headroom you will not use. The number that decides it is not the plan price but the overage behaviour. Exceeding your visit cap bills per 1,000 additional visits, with email warnings at 80% and 100%. If overages approach the cost of your plan, or hit $500, whichever is less, Kinsta may require immediate payment or an upgrade.

On a client site during a traffic spike that is a decision made under pressure — ask about the bandwidth-based plan instead if your traffic is spiky.

How to actually use Kinsta

Start with the migration, because it is free and most people do not realise how free. Kinsta's own docs describe basic migrations as free and unlimited, with the only limit being the number of WordPress installs your plan allows. Request one in MyKinsta under WordPress sites, the Migrations tab, then Request new migration — either from your current host or from a backup link on Drive or Dropbox. Budget the time honestly: their stated turnaround is up to 2 business days, and you are told if it will take longer.

If you need it faster, expedited migration is $49 per site, targeting 8 hours between 09:00 and 23:00 UTC on weekdays. Agency plans include free expedited credits scaled to the number of sites, so check what you already have before paying. Then set the traffic alerts up before you need them. The 80% and 100% warnings are the only thing standing between a normal month and an upgrade you did not plan — make sure they reach a person, not a shared inbox nobody reads.

Frequently asked questions

What does Kinsta cost for one WordPress site?

$35 a month billed monthly, or $30 a month billed annually, charged as $350 for the year with the first month free — read on their own plans page on 17 August 2026, in USD and excluding tax. That buys one WordPress install, 10GB storage, 125GB CDN bandwidth and 14 days of backups.

Will a traffic spike push me over my plan?

Less likely than it used to be. Kinsta now lets you meter the plan on bandwidth instead of visits: the entry single-site plan is either 20GB of server bandwidth or 35,000 visits, and you pick which. Overage on visit-based plans is the complaint that recurs in older reviews, so if your traffic is spiky, choose the bandwidth option deliberately rather than accepting the default.

Is there a Kinsta discount code worth hunting for?

No, and that is worth saying plainly because the search results are full of pages implying otherwise. Kinsta told us directly on 28 July 2026 that it offers no partner-only or affiliate-only discounts. The two months free on annual billing and the free first month are the public offers, available to everyone, and they were still on the page on 17 August 2026.

What should I weigh before buying IT & Productivity software?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.
