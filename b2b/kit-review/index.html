When the paid plan is worth it

Read every paid number on Kit's pricing page as at 1,000 subscribers, because that is what they are. Kit bills by list size, so the $39 on Creator and the $79 on Pro are the prices for a small list and both climb as you grow. Look up the rate for your own list before you compare anything -- the tier names tell you what you get, not what you pay.

The free plan is the exception, and in 2026 it got both more generous and thinner: $0 for up to 10,000 subscribers with unlimited landing pages, forms and broadcasts, audience tagging and segmentation, and digital products -- but Visual Automations and email sequences left it for Creator at the same time. So the reason to pay is automation depth rather than capacity, and that is now a hard line rather than a soft ceiling.

Kit's affiliate briefing of 2 September 2026 states that only new signups are affected and that accounts already on the Newsletter plan keep what they had. The Creator-to-Pro step is more specific than most: Pro adds subscriber scoring, engagement analytics and deliverability reporting. Those are diagnostic tools rather than features you use daily, so they earn their keep when your list is big enough that deliverability problems cost real money -- and at that size the per-subscriber rate matters more than the $40 difference anyway.

Kit's own affiliate briefing of 25 August 2026 puts more behind that tier than the diagnostics headline suggests, and dates the additions to its Craft + Commerce 2026 event: Subscriber Signals, which alerts you the moment a high-value subscriber joins, A/B testing with up to five variants, links that stay editable after a broadcast has gone out, SparkLoop's newsletter referral system, unlimited team seats with collaborative editing, and 24/7 priority support.

That list is Kit's rather than ours -- we have not put those features through a test -- but it is the list to weigh if seats or referrals matter to you, because those are the parts you can judge on paper. Annual billing saves 17% ($390 against $468 on Creator, $790 against $948 on Pro). Paid plans open with a 14-day trial without a card, and migrations are free, which removes the usual reason to stay somewhere worse.

How to actually use Kit

Prune your list before you price a plan, because Kit bills by subscriber count and a cold subscriber costs exactly what a fan does. Removing people who have not opened anything in a year is free, improves your deliverability and lowers your tier — it is the highest-return hour in this whole process and most people do it after they start paying rather than before. Then read the price for your own list size rather than the page's.

Every figure Kit publishes is quoted for 1,000 email subscribers — Creator at $39 a month, Pro at $79, or $33 and $66 billed yearly — and the number moves with your list, so the published rates are a starting point rather than your bill. Use the free plan properly, but know what it no longer does. It covers up to 10,000 subscribers with unlimited landing pages, forms and broadcasts, so it is a genuine sending plan rather than a trial — but Visual Automations and email sequences are not part of it any more.

Nothing runs while you sleep on the free tier. The step to Creator is triggered by wanting a welcome sequence, not by passing a subscriber count. If you are switching from another tool, take the free migration and the 14-day trial together. Migrations are free and the trial needs no card, which makes the switching cost unusually low. The thing to test in those two weeks is whether Pro's subscriber scoring and deliverability reporting change what you do, because that is the part of the tier you cannot judge from a feature list.

The rest of what Pro adds (five-variant A/B tests, editable links after sending, SparkLoop referrals, unlimited team seats) you can weigh on paper before the trial even starts. If you are going to wire Kit into anything else, do the webhooks yourself rather than paying a connector to do it. Kit has a dedicated webhooks page at app.kit.com/webhooks, so registering an endpoint is a form you fill in rather than an API call you write, and one endpoint can listen to as many event types as you like.

Kit's own developer documentation lists 28 event types and marks 23 of them as delivering today: subscriber created, activated, unsubscribed, bounced and complained, form signups, sequence starts and completions, tags added and removed, custom-field changes, plus resource events for tags, custom fields, sequences, broadcasts and published posts. The remaining five — product purchased, link clicked, email opened and two landing-page events — are listed as planned, and the API rejects a subscription to them, so check the status column before you design around one.

Two details decide whether that sync survives real life. Deliveries are signed with HMAC-SHA256 so you can verify they came from Kit, and a failed delivery gets up to eight attempts — the original plus seven retries, backing off 30s, 2m, 15m, 1h, 4h, 12h and 24h — over roughly 41 hours rather than being dropped — that is the difference between your endpoint being down for an afternoon and losing a day of subscribers.

Kit also still runs an older webhooks resource that fires one event per webhook and drops what fails; it keeps working and Kit says it is not going away, but build anything new on the endpoint version.

Distilled from Kit's own pricing page, kit.com/pricing, and its developer documentation at developers.kit.com, both read 10 September 2026. The pricing page carries the free plan at $0 up to 10,000 email subscribers, Creator at $39 a month and Pro at $79, or $33 and $66 billed yearly (the page also prints the yearly totals $390 and $790), every figure quoted for 1,000 subscribers, with a 14-day trial and free migrations. The webhook details come from developers.kit.com/webhooks: the event-types page lists 28 events of which 5 are marked Planned — landing_page.created, landing_page.deleted, subscriber.email_opened, subscriber.link_clicked and subscriber.product_purchased — and states that 'Planned events can't be subscribed to yet: the API rejects them with a 422'; the verifying-signatures page gives X-Kit-Signature as HMAC-SHA256; and the retries page states 'Failed deliveries are retried with backoff: 30s, 2m, 15m, 1h, 4h, 12h, then 24h — up to 8 attempts (the original plus 7 retries) over about 41 hours'. The legacy resource appears in the same navigation as 'Webhooks (Legacy)'.
