What Kit does

Email marketing built for creators (formerly ConvertKit). Grow your list with landing pages and forms, then automate newsletters and sell digital products from one place; a beta Kit MCP connects AI assistants like Claude directly to your account. Recent additions: a new landing-page editor, Dynamic Content, Newsletter Sponsorships, and App Store integrations on paid plans — SMS via SlickText, Patreon member sync (tier and support data in automations), Luma/Eventbrite event sync, and two-way HubSpot CRM sync on Pro.

Pricing (2026)

(re-verified 2026-08-09) The free Newsletter plan is $0/mo and covers 1,000 email subscribers, with one basic Visual Automation plus unlimited landing pages, forms and broadcasts. Creator is $39/mo billed monthly, or $33/mo billed yearly ($390 a year, saving $78). Pro — the tier that adds subscriber scoring, engagement analytics and deliverability reporting — is $79/mo billed monthly, or $66/mo billed yearly ($790 a year, saving $158). Paying yearly saves 17%. Every price on the page is quoted for 1,000 email subscribers and Kit bills by list size, so read the tier for your own list before you budget. Paid plans open with a 14-day free trial without a card, and migrations are free. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Two honest notes. Subscriber-based pricing counts everyone on your list — cold subscribers cost the same as fans, so prune before you price. And the free plan is smaller than Kit's reputation suggests: it stops at 1,000 subscribers (re-verified 2026-08-09), so the month your list outgrows that number you are on Creator at $39/mo, or $33/mo if you pay a year up front. The creator-first features (tip jars, paid newsletters, recommendations) remain the real differentiator.

What buyers report about Kit

Recurring themes from practitioner reviews and Reddit, read in August 2026. We have not run Kit ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Tagging, visual automations and creator-oriented forms — it does newsletter-led business better than anything at the price, and reviewers say emails from it feel personal rather than marketed.
  • • There is a genuinely free Newsletter tier, though it stops at 1,000 subscribers.
What they complain about
  • • The price went from $15 to $33 a month at 1,000 subscribers — a 120% increase that landed alongside the rebrand from ConvertKit. Creators on Reddit were vocal that the money went into brand identity rather than the product.
  • • Pricing is per subscriber, so the bill grows with the list whether or not the extra names open anything — Creator starts at $39/month for 1,000 subscribers (re-verified 2026-08-09) and climbs from there.
  • • Design flexibility is limited. If you want ecommerce-style campaign templates, this is the wrong tool.
  • • Against budget-focused alternatives it gets expensive quickly once you leave the free plan.

Frequently asked questions

Is Kit worth it for a small team?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Small teams get the most out of this category when one clear problem is already costing real hours or revenue; buying ahead of that just adds cost and another login. Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.

Who should not use Kit?

Skip it if you're an e-commerce store (commerce-native email tools fit better) or your list is big-but-cold — clean it before paying per subscriber. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
