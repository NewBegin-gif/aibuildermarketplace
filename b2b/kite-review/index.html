Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 6 Sep 2026

Four plans, billed in USD every 30 days. The Free plan is free to install and covers the first 50 discount orders each month; the listing states that $29 a month is billed only if you go over 50 orders. Starter is $29 a month, or $299 a year at a stated 14% saving, and adds unlimited discount orders, combined offers, progress bars and up to 10K bulk codes.

Growth is $49 a month, or $499 a year at 15%, and raises bulk codes to 100K with multiple rules per offer and campaign links. Pro is $99 a month, or $999 a year at 16%, and adds custom discount functions, checkout gifts (Plus only) and segment targeting. The three paid tiers carry a 7-day free trial. Free and Starter both say 'Not for Shopify Advanced and Plus'. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The free plan covers the first 50 discount orders a month, and the same panel says $29 a month is billed once you pass them — so it is a threshold with an invoice behind it, not a free tier you can settle on. A store running one decent promotion will cross 50 discounted orders in days and be on Starter money without choosing to upgrade. The second catch is written on the plans themselves: Free and Starter are both labelled 'Not for Shopify Advanced and Plus', so if you are on either of those Shopify plans your real entry price is the $49 Growth tier, not the $29 the listing leads with.

And the feature most likely to sell the app to a large merchant — free gifts added at checkout — is marked Plus only and sits in the $99 Pro tier, which means the Plus stores it is built for pay $1,188 a year to reach it.

What Kite does

Shopify discount toolkit (by Skai Lama) built on native Shopify Functions: free gift with purchase, buy X get Y, tiered and volume discounts, progress bars and bulk codes. Free to install for the first 50 discount orders a month, then $29; Starter $29, Growth $49, Pro $99.

Where the four tiers actually diverge

The ladder is not four sizes of the same thing. Free and Starter are the same product with the meter removed — Starter's $29 buys unlimited discount orders, combined offers, progress bars and 10K bulk codes — and both are closed to Advanced and Plus stores. Growth at $49 mostly widens limits: 100K bulk codes, multiple rules per offer, campaign links, targeting logged-in customers. Pro at $99 is where a different kind of feature appears — custom discount functions, checkout gifts, targeting by customer tag and segment.

That shape decides the sum for you. If you are on Basic or Grow, start on Free and let the 50-order threshold tell you whether the app earns $29; you will know within a campaign. If you are on Advanced or Plus, ignore the first two tiers entirely — $49 is your floor, and the only question is whether checkout gifts are worth the further $50 a month to Pro.

Work that one out as revenue per gifted order, because at $1,188 a year it is competing with dedicated upsell apps rather than with the rest of this ladder.

How to actually use Kite

Installation runs from the App Store, so setup starts inside your Shopify admin rather than at a separate signup, and the listing says the app works with Checkout and Shopify Admin alongside a list of cart and page builders — All Slide Carts, Checkout Wizard, Easy Bundles, GemPages, iCart and UpCart — which is worth checking against whatever is already on your storefront before you install. Use the step most merchants skip: the listing carries a View demo store link, so you can watch a gift appear at a cart threshold and a progress bar fill on a live storefront before handing the app access to your store.

Then check your Shopify plan first, because it decides where you can start — Advanced and Plus stores are excluded from Free and Starter. On Basic or Grow, install on the free plan and build one offer, then watch the 50-order threshold: crossing it is what moves you to $29, and it is better to see that happen on a promotion you chose than on one that ran away.
