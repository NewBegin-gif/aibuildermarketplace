Koongo Review (2026)

E-commerceResearched assessment · by Daniel HaketUpdated 2026-08-27

The headline price is a base, not a bill.

Best for: Shopify merchants selling across several European marketplaces — Bol, Zalando, Allegro, Cdiscount, Miinto and the rest — who need inventory and order sync to run automatically, and who have priced their own item, feed and order counts against the surcharge table rather than the headline.
Who should skip it: if you sell on one channel or need product feeds only, where the feed-manager-only form from $14.50 or a dedicated feed app is cheaper; or your catalogue and order volume sit far enough above the included limits that the surcharges, not the plan, become the price.

Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 23 Aug 2026

Four tiers on the listing, separated by update frequency rather than by capability. Free covers one store language, 100 items or SKUs, 5 product feeds, one marketplace wizard and up to 10 orders a month, updating once a day. Lite is $39 a month and updates every 6 hours, Starter is $79 and updates every 30 minutes, and Pro is $119 and updates every 15 minutes.

All three include 1,000 items, 5 feeds, two marketplace wizards with the second free on a summer promotion, and 100 orders a month. All three also carry a 7-day free trial. Every paid tier states surcharges for extras used — items, feeds, marketplaces and orders — and each is also sold in a feed-manager-only form, from $14.50 for Lite, $29 for Starter and $41 for Pro.

All charges on the listing are billed in USD and recur every 30 days. The vendor's own pricing page sells the same four plans in euros at €0, €35, €69 and €99 a month, and states that all prices exclude VAT. Each plan is broken into Core, Marketplace Manager, Feed Manager and Ads Manager modules with separate prices. There is also a surcharge table for extra stores, items, orders, marketplaces, feeds and team seats, where an extra 100 orders runs from €10.00 down to €2.00 as volume grows.

Developer Koongo, a product of NoStress Commerce s.r.o., Prague; the app launched on 5 November 2014 and had 98 reviews at a 4.4 average at the time of writing. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Each paid plan includes 1,000 items, 5 feeds, two marketplace wizards and 100 orders a month, and the listing says plainly that surcharges apply for extras used across all four of those dimensions. For a store with a real catalogue selling on several European marketplaces, every one of those limits is one you pass in week one. Price your own numbers on koongo.com's surcharge table before you take $39 seriously.

The two pricing pages also do not quote the same bill. The App Store lists $39, $79 and $119 and bills in USD. koongo.com lists €35, €69 and €99 and states that all prices exclude VAT. Depending on where you trade and how you are registered, those are meaningfully different amounts for the same plan, and nothing on either page reconciles them. Then the record, which is the most useful thing on this listing.

4.4 from 98 reviews is the lowest average in this category among the apps Shopify shows beside it, and the breakdown explains why: 84 five-star ratings sit alongside six one-star ones. The critical reviews are specific rather than vague, which makes them worth reading in full. In July 2026 a Belgian merchant described a repricer set to Monitoring Mode that still blocked updates to live Bol offers because of MinimumPrice errors, so a delivery-promise change never reached the marketplace.

In January 2026 a UK merchant described unresolved integration errors on OnBuy and a refused refund, with Koongo replying publicly that they had reached out several times without response. Neither is disqualifying on its own. Together they describe an app whose complexity is real and whose marketplace integrations sometimes need support to untangle — which is consistent with the vendor's own listing offering personal onboarding for complex setups. One more line to read before you install: the listing states the app views sensitive customer data as well as device and activity data, and can view and edit products, orders and custom data.

Marketplace order sync needs customer details to work, so that is expected, but it makes this a processor of personal data on your behalf rather than a feed formatter.

What Koongo does

Shopify app that syncs product feeds, stock and orders across Amazon, eBay, Bol, Zalando, Allegro and 500-plus channels from one dashboard. Free tier for 100 items; paid plans from $39 a month, separated by refresh frequency, with surcharges beyond the included limits.

What they complain about
  • • Six of the 98 reviews are one star and they cluster on marketplace-specific setup rather than on missing features. Stevvex (US, three years in, April 2025) reports recurring failures with Wish and Amazon, variants published individually, $240 in marketplace penalties and a denied refund; Koongo replied that this was a seasonal account with repeated install and uninstall cycles. Artist Deluxe (France, four months, January 2026) says it took three months to get products syncing to Amazon properly. CeeKay Traders UK (14 days, January 2026) could not clear OnBuy errors even after hiring a freelancer; Koongo replied that a new trial was granted and the refund issued.
  • • The most useful review is a three-star. Fans Geek Store (Belgium, five months in, July 2026) found the repricer in monitoring mode was silently blocking marketplace updates through minimum-price errors, so their Bol delivery promise stopped updating; support misdiagnosed it before Pavel found it. Their ask is transparency about which settings block which updates, and visible error flagging — a small shop without an IT person cannot see that failure happening.
  • • Bamboebaas (Netherlands, four months in, January 2025, three stars) makes the same point about vocabulary: they needed weekly contact with support to understand what features like "Order Sync" actually do, and say the documentation leaves out what you need to set it up yourself.

Where the ratings disagree. The five-star and one-star reviews are not really disagreeing. Both describe an app whose configuration is hard and whose support is responsive; the split is whether the merchant had someone to chase tickets with. That makes the support relationship, not the feature list, the thing you are buying — and the three-star reviews are the ones that say so plainly.

When the paid plan is worth it

Read the four tiers again and notice what does not change. Items, feeds, marketplace wizards and included orders are identical on Lite, Starter and Pro. What $39, $79 and $119 buy is how often your data refreshes — every 6 hours, every 30 minutes, every 15 minutes — and how fast support answers. That makes the choice unusually clean: you are buying staleness tolerance, and the right tier is decided by how fast your stock moves, not by how big your catalogue is.

So work out what a stale hour costs you. If you sell the last unit on Amazon and Bol within the same six-hour window, the six-hour refresh is the thing that oversells you, and the $40 step to Starter is cheap insurance. If your stock turns slowly, Lite refreshing four times a day is genuinely enough and the higher tiers buy you nothing you can measure. The second sum is the modules, and it is only visible on the vendor's own page.

Koongo prices Core, Marketplace Manager, Feed Manager and Ads Manager separately, which is why the listing also advertises a feed-manager-only version of each plan from $14.50. If you want product feeds for Google Shopping and Meta but no marketplace selling, that cheaper form is the plan you actually want, and you would never find it from the headline prices. The third is the one that decides the real bill: everything past 1,000 items, 5 feeds, two marketplaces and 100 orders a month is a surcharge.

A catalogue of 20,000 SKUs selling across four marketplaces is not a $39 store on this app, and the plan price is the smallest line on that invoice.

How to actually use Koongo

Start with the surcharge table, not the trial. Count your SKUs, the feeds you need, the marketplaces you want live and your monthly order volume, then price those against koongo.com's own per-plan surcharge list. The included allowances — 1,000 items, 5 feeds, two marketplaces, 100 orders — are small enough that most real stores are buying surcharges from day one, and knowing that number before the 7-day trial starts is what stops the first invoice being a surprise.

Then connect one marketplace, not four. The failure mode described in the app's own critical reviews is a single integration behaving unexpectedly — a repricer rule blocking offer updates, an error you cannot clear — and that is far easier to diagnose when only one channel is live. Get inventory sync provably correct on the hardest marketplace you sell to before you add the rest. And take the update frequency seriously while you are in the trial.

It is the only thing separating the tiers, so watch how far your stock actually drifts between syncs on the plan you are testing. If nothing oversells on a six-hour refresh during a normal week, Lite is your plan and the higher tiers are buying you reassurance rather than accuracy.
