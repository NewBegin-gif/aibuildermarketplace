What KrispCall does

Unified cloud telephony platform. Manage virtual numbers across 100+ countries and automate bulk SMS campaigns seamlessly.

Pricing (2026)

(2026) Essential about $12/user/mo annual ($15 monthly, up to 5 users); Standard ~$32/user/mo annual ($40 monthly, unlimited users, power dialer, bulk SMS); Enterprise custom. ~20% off annual. Calls and SMS are pay-as-you-go from a separate credit balance. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Same honest math as most budget VoIP: the plan buys the platform, the meter buys the conversations — per-minute and per-SMS charges land on top of every seat, so the comparison that matters is call-rate-per-country at your volume. The 100+ country number coverage is genuinely the draw; verify number availability and local regulations for your specific target countries before committing.

When the paid plan is worth it

KrispCall's Essential tier ($12/user/month billed annually, $15 month-to-month) is workable for a small team of up to 5 users that needs basic calling, multi-level IVR, and 100+ listed integrations, with one number per seat included in the price. The concrete wall is that 5-user cap: exceeding it forces a move to Standard ($32/user/month annually, up to 50 users), which is also the only tier that unlocks call recording, power dialer, bulk SMS campaigns, and Salesforce integration — all marked unavailable on Essential's own comparison table. What upgrading does not fix is usage billing: calls and SMS are charged pay-as-you-go on top of the subscription at every tier, and running US bulk SMS campaigns still requires separate A2P 10DLC registration costs (a one-time brand fee and a one-time campaign vetting fee, plus a small recurring monthly campaign fee) regardless of plan. A Standard or Enterprise subscription buys seats and features, not lower or bundled calling and messaging costs.

How to actually use KrispCall

KrispCall's own guide describes sign-up as a 7-step flow: create the account, verify identity, then buy or port a number and add team members. New accounts start unverified, and the helpdesk states Basic KYC document review finishes within 1-2 hours, with email confirmation once it clears. Until then, an account is capped at 20 outbound SMS total and 5 phone number purchases, which is enough to test the product but not to run a real campaign. If you already have a number and want to port it in rather than buy new, KrispCall's own porting guide puts the timeline at 1-2 weeks for most numbers, narrowing to 1-10 days for individual local numbers — this is the step most likely to delay an actual go-live date. CRM connections are also tier-gated: Salesforce integration is absent from the Essential plan and only appears on Standard, so a team planning to wire KrispCall into its CRM from day one needs to budget for that tier before starting onboarding, not after.
