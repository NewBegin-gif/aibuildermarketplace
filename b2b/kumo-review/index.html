What Kumo does

Shopify wholesale app from Cloudlift GmbH: unlimited custom pricing rules that apply automatically to named customer groups, CSV import and export of price lists, B2B registration forms with an approval workflow, and prices that update in the theme without code edits. Two plans and no free tier — B2B $15 a month, PLUS $30, both with a 7-day trial; everything PLUS adds requires Shopify Plus. Built for Shopify, launched November 2024, 5.0 stars over 9 reviews as of 16 Sep 2026.

When the paid plan is worth it

There is no free tier here, so the question is not whether to pay but whether to pay twice. The $15 B2B plan carries the working app, and the listing sells its pricing rules as unlimited rather than metered, so a catalogue with four wholesale rules and one with four hundred cost the same $15 a month. Volume tiers, the registration form builder and the approval workflow all sit on that entry plan, which is where most wholesale setups stop.

The $30 PLUS plan earns its money in exactly one case: you are already on Shopify Plus. All three things it adds — company registration, product catalog assignment and checkout integration — are labelled Shopify Plus on the listing itself, and they hook into platform features a standard Shopify plan does not have. Upgrading does not buy more pricing rules, more customers, faster support or a longer evaluation; both plans run the same 7-day trial.

Nor does it buy a bigger safety margin on the app: nine reviews in the two years since launch is the installed base on either tier, and $30 a month does not change that.

How to actually use Kumo

Cloudlift's own walkthrough puts the whole setup inside the app rather than in your theme. Install from the App Store, then open Kumo > Pricing > Add Pricing: a rule is a percentage or absolute discount plus two conditions — customers by tag, optionally restricted to logged-in shoppers, and products by tag, with exclusions available on both sides. Volume tiers live under Pricing > Volume Discounts, and the guide's own worked example is 10+ units for 10% off, 20+ for 15%, 50+ for 20%.

The gate is the form. Under Forms > Create New Form a drag-and-drop builder takes business name, tax ID or reseller certificate, contact details and custom fields, and you publish it on your own B2B signup page. Submissions land in a dashboard where you approve by hand or automatically, and approval assigns the wholesale tag that your pricing rules read. That makes the tag scheme the one decision worth getting right before you build anything else.

Distilled from apps.shopify.com/b2b-wholesale-tools, read 16 September 2026: the two plan cards ($15 B2B, $30 PLUS, both with a 7-day free trial, all charges billed in USD every 30 days), the feature list, the Built for Shopify badge, the 21 November 2024 launch date and the 5.0 rating over 9 reviews. The setup steps come from Cloudlift's own guide 'How to Set Up Wholesale Pricing on Shopify (Without Shopify Plus)' at cloudlift.app/blogs/shopify/how-to-set-up-wholesale-pricing-on-shopify-without-shopify-plus, published 14 March 2025 and read the same day: the menu paths (Pricing > Add Pricing, Pricing > Volume Discounts, Forms > Create New Form), the percentage-or-absolute discount choice, the customer-tag and product-tag conditions, the drag-and-drop form fields and the 10+/20+/50+ volume example are all that page's own. Frequently asked questions

What does Kumo cost?

Two plans and no free tier, read on the Shopify App Store listing on 16 September 2026: B2B at $15 a month and PLUS at $30, both with a 7-day free trial, all charges billed in USD every 30 days. The important detail is what does not scale: the listing sells pricing rules as unlimited rather than metered, so a catalogue with four wholesale rules and one with four hundred pay the same $15. Volume tiers, the registration form builder and the approval workflow are all on the entry plan. There is no per-customer, per-order or per-code charge published, so the monthly figure is the whole cost.

Is the $30 PLUS plan worth twice the money?

Only if you are already on Shopify Plus, and then it is barely a choice. PLUS adds exactly three things over the $15 plan — company registration, product catalog assignment and checkout integration — and the listing itself labels all three as Shopify Plus features. On a standard Shopify plan they hook into platform capabilities your store does not have, so the extra $15 a month buys nothing you can switch on. Upgrading does not buy more pricing rules, more customers, faster support or a longer evaluation either: both plans run the same 7-day trial. Read 16 September 2026.

Do I need Shopify Plus to run wholesale pricing?

No, and avoiding that is the whole point of the $15 plan. On a standard Shopify plan this gives you percentage or absolute discounts scoped by customer tag and product tag, volume tiers, a drag-and-drop registration form you publish on your own B2B signup page, and an approval step that assigns the wholesale tag your pricing rules read. The sequence matters: approval sets the tag, and the tag is what the price rule looks for, so the tag scheme is the one decision worth getting right before you build anything else. The catch is on the other side — if you are already on Plus, company accounts and price lists come with the platform, and this app is solving a problem you no longer have.

What should I weigh before buying E-commerce software?

Check your platform and version specifically. Shopify support is close to universal in this category, while WooCommerce, BigCommerce, Magento and headless setups vary a lot, and 'supported' sometimes means a generic API rather than a maintained app. A listing in your own platform's app store, with recent reviews there, is a better signal than the vendor's logo wall.
