Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 10 Sep 2026

Seven published tiers, billed in USD. The Shopify listing shows only the first four and links to 'more pricing plans'; the vendor's own pricing page prices the rest. Free costs nothing and covers one language and 50 products, with limited manual and AI translation, the standard language and currency switcher and multilingual SEO. Basic is $10 a month or $100 a year, and raises that to 250 products with five glossary rules, unlimited editing of translations, bulk editing and removal of LangShop branding.

Standard is $40 a month or $400 a year: three languages, 2,000 products, 100 glossary rules, automatic sync of 50 new items, and the point at which the named engines (OpenAI, DeepL Pro, Google Cloud Translation), the advanced switcher and translation of dynamic content and third-party apps become available. Advanced is $75 a month or $750 a year: five languages, 5,000 products, 250 glossary rules, sync of 125 new items, ten content-exclusion rules and Shopify Flow support.

Beyond the listing: Pro at $120 a month or $1,200 a year adds API access with ten languages, 10,000 products, 500 glossary rules, sync of 250 and 50 exclusion rules; Enterprise at $250 or $2,500 gives 20 languages, 50,000 products, 1,000 glossary rules, sync of 1,250, 200 exclusion rules and a customer success manager; Unlimited at $500 or $5,000 lifts products, glossary, sync and exclusion rules to unlimited at 20 languages.

Annual billing is described as two months free, which is the same 17% the listing quotes. Trial length is inconsistent across the vendor's own pages: every plan card on langshop.app/pricing says 7-day, while langshop.app/faq and the Shopify listing both say 14-day. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The headline promise is 'unlimited words', but every tier caps how many products you may translate and how many languages you may run, so the meter you actually pay is catalogue size. And the named engines that the listing leads with — OpenAI, DeepL Pro, Google Cloud Translation — are a Standard-tier feature at $40 a month; below that you get limited manual and AI translation. The Shopify listing also understates the ladder: it stops at Advanced and links to 'more pricing plans', while the vendor's pricing page publishes Pro at $120, Enterprise at $250 and Unlimited at $500 — and API access, which no listing tier mentions, starts at Pro.

The app is old enough to judge (launched 15 September 2016, developer Aheadworks) and most of its 480 reviews are positive, but 23 of them are one-star, and the recent negative ones are specific rather than vague: a Slovak store reporting that metafield translations ignore both the AI store context and the glossary rules, and a Swedish store of almost three years reporting that products duplicated from another product carry the wrong translation across.

If metafields or duplicated products are central to your catalogue, test exactly that during the trial.

What LangShop does

Shopify translation and currency app (by Aheadworks) that localises products, themes, checkout, SEO and third-party app content with OpenAI, DeepL Pro or Google Cloud Translation, or with professional agency translators, and adds a geolocated language and currency switcher. Glossary and content rules keep terms consistent. Free plan for one language and 50 products; Basic $10 a month or $100 a year, Standard $40 or $400, Advanced $75 or $750, each capping languages and translated products. Launched September 2016, 480 reviews as of 10 Sep 2026.

What buyers report about LangShop

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in September 2026. We have not run LangShop ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • The weight of opinion is positive: 431 of the 479 ratings are five-star and the overall sits at 4.5 (Shopify App Store, read September 2026).
  • • The vendor answers negative reviews publicly and in detail rather than with a template — the replies name the mechanism, point at a specific setting, and say plainly when something is a limitation.
What they complain about
  • • Twenty-three one-star ratings, and the recent ones are specific. A Swedish store of almost three years reports that a product duplicated from another product inherits the wrong translation, that single words which become two words in another language pick up a stray space, and that the free translation engine produces poor output.
  • • A Polish store reports the app not working for three days straight, with support saying developers were on it and no further update.
  • • On the duplicate-translation complaint the vendor's own reply concedes there is no workaround known to them, because LangShop stores translations in the Shopify API and they are copied along with the product.

Where the ratings disagree. The support signal and the product signal point different ways. The vendor replies to almost every complaint, quickly and substantively, which is genuinely better than most. But on the most-repeated failure the reply is an explanation of a limitation, not a fix — and the mitigation it offers, Shopify Flow, is not available below the Advanced tier. Read the one-star reviews for the mechanism, not the tone.

When the paid plan is worth it

The Shopify listing shows four tiers and a link to more pricing plans; langshop.app/pricing publishes seven (read 10 September 2026). Above Advanced sit Pro at $120 a month, Enterprise at $250 and Unlimited at $500 — and Pro is the one that matters, because API access starts there and the listing never mentions it. Annual billing is two months free. The meter counts products and languages, not words. Treat the word figures in the comparison table carefully: the page says they assume 250 words per product, which holds for the 50- and 250-product rows but not above them — 2,000 products is shown as 1.5M+ words where that formula gives 500,000.

What upgrading does not fix is whether the rules are obeyed. A higher tier buys more glossary and exclusion rules, but on the most-repeated complaint — products duplicated from another product carrying the wrong translation across — the vendor's own public reply says translations live in the Shopify API and duplicate with the product, with no workaround known to them. The mitigation they suggest instead is Shopify Flow, which does not appear below Advanced.

How to actually use LangShop

Pick the plan before anything else. The vendor's getting-started guide says you choose one as soon as you install, and the plan caps how many languages you may run — one on Free and Basic, three on Standard, five on Advanced — so a wrong choice is a re-pick rather than a setting. Then decide where machine translation is allowed to be final. Glossary rules pin brand terms and phrases that must not move; exclusion rules keep whole content types out of translation, and the vendor's list names metafields, themes and metaobjects.

Both scale with tier. Spend the trial on your hardest content rather than product titles. The specific failures merchants describe on the listing are metafields and products duplicated from another product, so translate exactly those, then open the storefront in each language and read the checkout and any third-party app strings. Confirm the trial length on the Shopify charge screen too: the pricing page says seven days where the vendor FAQ and the listing both say fourteen.

The natural comparison is Hextom AI Translate & Currency, EA • Auto Language Translate — the translation shelf on the Shopify App Store is crowded and the nearest neighbours all publish a free plan, so price is a poor way to choose between them. What separates LangShop is control rather than reach: glossary rules and content rules, translation of dynamic elements (banners, pop-ups, checkout, third-party apps), a professional-agency route for the pages that must be right, and a switcher you can design. What it costs you is that the named engines and the dynamic-content translation only start at $40 a month, where the free tiers of the alternatives already translate a store after a fashion. Try the cheaper option first if your storefront is mostly static product text; move to LangShop when the wrong translation in a checkout string starts costing orders.
