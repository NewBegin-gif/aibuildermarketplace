What Leadpages does

Landing pages, pop-ups and alert bars, with unlimited traffic on every Leadpages plan where metered rivals count visitors. Conversion plans run $99 (Grow) to $399 (Scale), alongside a separate HTML Pub publishing line at $10–49. The value pick rather than the optimisation pick.

Pricing (2026)

(re-verified 2026-08-17 against Leadpages' own machine-readable pricing file, which they last updated 2026-07-10; USD) Two lines, six paid plans, no free plan, and a 7-day free trial that requires a credit card and converts unless you cancel. Publishing plans: Starter $10/mo or $8 billed annually ($96 a year), 5 pages, 1 site, no third-party integrations; Pro $29/mo or $23 annually ($276), 25 pages, 1 blog, API access; Business $49/mo or $39 annually ($468), 50 pages, 2 sites.

Optimization plans add testing and personalisation: Grow $99/mo or $79 annually ($948), unlimited pages, 4 sites, manual A/B testing; Optimize $199/mo or $159 annually ($1,908), 6 sites, multivariate testing and Smart Traffic; Scale $399/mo or $319 annually ($3,828), 10 sites, heatmaps, auto-personalisation and 10 seats. Annual billing is 20% off across the board. No plan has a traffic cap. Add-ons: $20 a month per extra seat or extra custom domain, $40 per extra client workspace on Scale, and AI credit top-ups from $2.50 for 2,000 credits down to $0.60 per thousand at the largest pack. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What buyers report about Leadpages

Recurring themes from G2, Capterra and TrustRadius, read in August 2026. We have not run Leadpages ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Simple, intuitive and fast to a live mobile-ready page — 4.3 on G2 across 230 reviews, 4.4 across 523 on G2 and Capterra combined.
  • • Long-time users report strong value and pages that genuinely look professional, and several credit support for knowing the product.
  • • TrustRadius reviewers who ran paid traffic name two things the marketing pages undersell: per-page performance tracking that makes it obvious which opt-in wins, and taking payments directly on the page so lead capture and sale sit in one tool.
What they complain about
  • • The one-star reviews describe surprise charges, subscriptions that were hard to cancel, and support going quiet after a payment problem.
  • • The page cap is what pushes people up: Starter allows 5 landing pages and one custom domain, Pro 25 pages, and unlimited pages only start on Grow. (Prices re-read 17 Aug 2026; earlier reviews quote an older Standard/Pro ladder.)
  • • Some templates feel dated and design freedom is limited relative to a general page builder.
  • • The mechanism behind the billing complaints is spelled out on TrustRadius by a reviewer whose entire one-star review is about it: subscriptions are non-refundable and there is no prorated refund if you cancel early, and support declined to make an exception. Since the 7-day trial requires a card and converts automatically, that is a decision to make before day seven.
  • • Smaller asks from the same platform: no way to archive old pages to declutter the dashboard, and the built-in payments lack options such as split payments.

When the paid plan is worth it

There is a genuine free tier, but it sits on the HTML Pub side of the product rather than in Leadpages itself. The $0/forever plan gives 5 pages to build and preview and 5,000 AI credits to start, while publishing, a custom domain and branding removal are all held back for paid plans. That makes it a design sandbox instead of a live-page tool, so anyone who needs a page an actual visitor can reach is paying from the start.

The $10/mo Starter tier is the cheapest way to publish at all, and for a solo operator running a single offer on a single domain it is usually enough. Stepping up to the Leadpages conversion plans at $99/mo for Grow only pays back when that layer is actually used: manual A/B testing is included on every plan, but Smart Traffic routing and heatmaps begin at Optimize at $199/mo. Upgrading does not fix a weak offer, does not generate traffic, and does not shorten the DNS wait. It buys measurement, not demand.

How to actually use Leadpages

The shortest route to real value runs through the AI builder rather than a blank template: describe the campaign in plain language, let it generate a branded page, then adjust it in the drag-and-drop editor. Leadpages advertises a live page in 60 seconds, and the HTML Pub side quotes a 10s average publish time, so building is rarely where the hours go. Connecting a domain is.

Publishing to an owned address means adding the domain in the custom domains area and stepping through the Entri pop-up, which the vendor documents as automated DNS setup for 30+ popular registrars and connection in under two minutes. Registrars outside that network fall back to manual DNS records, which the vendor puts at 5-10 minutes plus propagation, and that is where people stall: the page reads as finished while the domain still shows as pending until DNS resolves. Check the domain allowance before committing, since Starter and Pro include 1 custom domain, Business 2 and Grow 4.

Frequently asked questions

What does Leadpages cost, and is there a free plan?

There is no free plan — only a 7-day trial that asks for a card and starts billing when it ends. Billed annually the six plans are $8, $23, $39, $79, $159 and $319 a month for Starter, Pro, Business, Grow, Optimize and Scale; billed monthly they are $10, $29, $49, $99, $199 and $399. Annual is a flat 20% off. Read on 17 August 2026.

Which plan do I need for more than a few pages?

Page count is the wall low down: Starter allows 5 pages and one site, Pro 25 pages, Business 50 pages and two sites. Unlimited pages start at Grow ($79 a month annually), which is also where A/B testing begins — though only as a manual two-way split; three or more variants and Smart Traffic require Optimize. Another one to check early: Starter includes no third-party integrations at all, so connecting your CRM means Pro.

What do people complain about after buying?

Billing rather than the builder. Reviewers describe subscriptions being non-refundable with no proration if you cancel early, and support declining to make exceptions — one TrustRadius reviewer's whole complaint is that this was not clear enough before committing. Since the trial requires a card and converts automatically, decide before day seven rather than after.

Is Leadpages worth it for a small team?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Small teams get the most out of this category when one clear problem is already costing real hours or revenue; buying ahead of that just adds cost and another login. Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.

Who should not use Leadpages?

Skip it if you need deep A/B experimentation or enterprise features — the Conversion tier jumps to $299/mo fast. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
