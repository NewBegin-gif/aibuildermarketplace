What Lindy.ai does

An AI teammate that lives in Slack: @mention it and it works across your other tools, sits in meetings for shared context and runs jobs on a schedule. Sold per user — Plus $29.99/month for 3k credits, Pro $99.99 for 15k — so cost tracks headcount, and a removed seat runs to the end of the cycle.

The catch

Cost tracks headcount rather than output: every colleague who so much as @mentions Lindy in Slack takes a paid seat, and a seat you remove runs to the end of the cycle with no mid-cycle proration. Credits are published now, but as ranges — an everyday ask is 2-250, a big build 1,000-2,500 — so a month is still an estimate, and when the shared pool runs dry there is no top-up: Lindy pauses credit-using actions until the reset unless an admin moves the workspace to a bigger plan.

What buyers report about Lindy.ai

Recurring themes from G2, Trustpilot, Reddit, Capterra and Product Hunt, read in August 2026 (Capterra and Product Hunt read 12 August 2026). We have not run Lindy.ai ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Setup is genuinely easy — that is the most common praise across 171 G2 reviews.
  • • For supervised work — inbox triage, meeting notes, scheduling — reviewers say it does what it claims.
  • • On Product Hunt, users single out that it carries tasks across their other tools rather than only producing text, with ready-made routines for calendars, appointment reminders and reworking existing content.
  • • A Capterra reviewer praises how far the templates bend to a specific workflow — in their case reshaping a clinical note template to their own house format.
What they complain about
  • • Cost is the single most-mentioned complaint (42 mentions), followed by subscription price (35). The cause is the credit system: tasks cost roughly $0.01 to $0.10+ each depending on model, so a predictable subscription becomes a variable bill.
  • • Several reviewers report charges above their expected cap. Complex agent chains burn credits faster than people expect.
  • • Heavy users hit credit walls mid-cycle.
  • • Unsupervised reliability is where the complaints concentrate — and support is hard to reach on individual plans.
  • • A veterinary practice on Capterra reports that Lindy fails to follow clear, specific instructions more than half the time, that call recordings sometimes come back unusable, and that the AI both omits and invents points from a conversation. The same reviewer describes freezing, display problems, and support that answers by pointing at a tutorial video.
  • • Product Hunt users warn that the built-in AI for editing a workflow gets it wrong often enough that you should duplicate the workflow before letting it change anything.

Where the ratings disagree. 4.9 on G2 across 170+ reviews against 2.4 on Trustpilot. The G2 reviews are about whether it works; the Trustpilot ones are about billing, and both are true. Capterra and Product Hunt, read in August 2026, do not open a new gap so much as sharpen the existing one: the praise is still about supervised, templated work and the complaints are still about unattended reliability and support — now with a specific clinical-transcription example behind them rather than a general impression.

Best for: teams that live in Slack and want one shared teammate that sits in their meetings, runs scheduled routines and drafts replies inside the tools they already use.
Who should skip it: if you want flat, predictable automation costs across a large team — Lindy charges for every seat that touches it and the credits expire each cycle, so you pay for people whether or not they used their allowance.

The natural comparison is Zapier or Make — trigger-based automation tools with flatter pricing. Weigh it against Zapier on the job you actually need done.

Frequently asked questions

Did Lindy just get cheaper?

The entry tier did, and recently. We capture their pricing page monthly: on 7 July and again on 2 August 2026 the three published prices were $49, $99 and $199. On 12 August 2026 the same page reads $29.99, $99.99 and $199.99 per user per month. So the cheapest way in dropped by roughly a third within days, and the two higher tiers barely moved. If you were quoted $49 recently, ask.

Who counts as a paid seat?

Anyone who uses Lindy, and the definition is wider than most teams expect. Their own pricing FAQ (read 12 August 2026) says a seat is taken whether someone signs up directly, joins your workspace, or only @mentions Lindy in Slack. Colleagues who arrive that way get a 7-day free trial first; direct signups are billed immediately. Removing a seat does not refund anything — it stays active to the end of the cycle you paid for, with no mid-cycle proration.

What happens when the credits run out?

Lindy pauses credit-using actions until the next cycle rather than billing you extra — their FAQ is explicit that there is no surprise bill and no top-up, and that an admin has to move the workspace to a bigger plan to keep going. Credits do not roll over. Note that the plan allowances are described in ranges (an everyday ask is 2-250 credits, a big build 1,000-2,500), so how far your pool goes is an estimate. Reviewers on Capterra and Product Hunt in August 2026 report burning through more than they planned on complex chains.

Does Lindy.ai replace my current setup or sit on top of it?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.

Who should not use Lindy.ai?

Skip it if you want flat, predictable automation costs across a large team — Lindy charges for every seat that touches it and the credits expire each cycle, so you pay for people whether or not they used their allowance. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
