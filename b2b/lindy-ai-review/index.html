What buyers report about Lindy.ai

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026 (Capterra and Product Hunt read 12 August 2026). We have not run Lindy.ai ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Setup is genuinely easy — that is the most common praise across 171 G2 reviews.
  • • For supervised work — inbox triage, meeting notes, scheduling — reviewers say it does what it claims.
  • • On Product Hunt, users single out that it carries tasks across their other tools rather than only producing text, with ready-made routines for calendars, appointment reminders and reworking existing content.
  • • A Capterra reviewer praises how far the templates bend to a specific workflow — in their case reshaping a clinical note template to their own house format.
What they complain about
  • • Cost is the single most-mentioned complaint (42 mentions), followed by subscription price (35). The cause is the credit system: tasks cost roughly $0.01 to $0.10+ each depending on model, so a predictable subscription becomes a variable bill.
  • • Several reviewers report charges above their expected cap. Complex agent chains burn credits faster than people expect.
  • • Heavy users hit credit walls mid-cycle.
  • • Unsupervised reliability is where the complaints concentrate — and support is hard to reach on individual plans.
  • • A veterinary practice on Capterra reports that Lindy fails to follow clear, specific instructions more than half the time, that call recordings sometimes come back unusable, and that the AI both omits and invents points from a conversation. The same reviewer describes freezing, display problems, and support that answers by pointing at a tutorial video.
  • • Product Hunt users warn that the built-in AI for editing a workflow gets it wrong often enough that you should duplicate the workflow before letting it change anything.

Where the ratings disagree. 4.9 on G2 across 170+ reviews against 2.4 on Trustpilot. The G2 reviews are about whether it works; the Trustpilot ones are about billing, and both are true. Capterra and Product Hunt, read in August 2026, do not open a new gap so much as sharpen the existing one: the praise is still about supervised, templated work and the complaints are still about unattended reliability and support — now with a specific clinical-transcription example behind them rather than a general impression.

When the paid plan is worth it

Two meters run at once here and they multiply: seats and credits. Plus is $29.99 a user a month with 3,000 credits per user, Pro $99.99 with 15,000, and Max $199.99 with 35,000 — so climbing a tier buys five and then twelve times the credits for roughly three and then seven times the price. Per credit the ladder therefore improves sharply, and if credits are what you run out of, staying on Plus is the expensive choice.

But seats are the meter most teams underestimate, because of one rule: anyone who uses Lindy takes a paid seat, including a colleague who only @mentions it in Slack. In a channel where the tool is genuinely useful that spreads on its own. People who join that way get a 7-day trial first; direct signups are billed immediately. And a seat you remove runs to the end of the cycle with no mid-cycle proration, so a month of experimentation costs a full month per person who tried it.

The credits pool across the workspace, refresh each billing cycle and do NOT roll over — and there is no top-up. When the shared pool runs dry, Lindy pauses credit-using actions until the reset unless an admin moves the whole workspace to a bigger plan. That is the sharpest operational difference from most credit tools: you cannot buy your way out of a bad week mid-cycle, so size the plan for your busiest month rather than your average one.

Sizing is possible but rough. The page gives ranges: 2-250 credits for an everyday ask, 250-1,000 for deep work, 1,000-2,500 for a big build. At Plus's 3,000 per seat that is somewhere between twelve everyday asks and a single big build — a spread wide enough that the first month is a measurement rather than a plan. What no longer varies by tier: all four connect up to five inboxes, and computer use, model selection and MCP support are no longer gated.

How to actually use Lindy.ai

Decide who can @mention Lindy in Slack before you roll it out, because that is the cost driver rather than usage. Anyone who uses it takes a paid seat — including a colleague who only ever mentions it in a channel — and although people who join that way get a seven-day free trial first, direct signups are billed straight away. A seat you remove runs to the end of the cycle with no mid-cycle proration, so an accidental rollout is expensive to reverse.

Then size credits from the work, not the headcount. Every seat's credits pool into one workspace balance that refreshes each cycle and does not roll over. Lindy sizes an everyday ask at 2 to 250 credits, deep work at 250 to 1,000 and a big build at 1,000 to 2,500 — so two big builds can consume a whole Plus seat's 3,000 monthly credits on their own. Know what happens when the pool runs dry, because it is not an overage.

An underestimate therefore stops your automations mid-month rather than costing you money, which is worse for anything running in production. If you expect real volume, note the higher tiers are genuinely cheaper per credit — about one cent on Plus against 0.67 on Pro and 0.57 on Max — so an upgrade buys capacity at a discount rather than at a premium.

Frequently asked questions

Did Lindy just get cheaper?

The entry tier did, and recently. We capture their pricing page monthly: on 7 July and again on 2 August 2026 the three published prices were $49, $99 and $199. On 12 August 2026 the same page reads $29.99, $99.99 and $199.99 per user per month. So the cheapest way in dropped by roughly a third within days, and the two higher tiers barely moved. If you were quoted $49 recently, ask.

Who counts as a paid seat?

Anyone who uses Lindy, and the definition is wider than most teams expect. Their own pricing FAQ (read 12 August 2026) says a seat is taken whether someone signs up directly, joins your workspace, or only @mentions Lindy in Slack. Colleagues who arrive that way get a 7-day free trial first; direct signups are billed immediately. Removing a seat does not refund anything — it stays active to the end of the cycle you paid for, with no mid-cycle proration.

What happens when the credits run out?

Lindy pauses credit-using actions until the next cycle rather than billing you extra — their FAQ is explicit that there is no surprise bill and no top-up, and that an admin has to move the workspace to a bigger plan to keep going. Credits do not roll over. Note that the plan allowances are described in ranges (an everyday ask is 2-250 credits, a big build 1,000-2,500), so how far your pool goes is an estimate. Reviewers on Capterra and Product Hunt in August 2026 report burning through more than they planned on complex chains.

If someone leaves the team, can we re-use their free week?

No. Read on lindy.ai/pricing on 12 September 2026: the free week is one-time per teammate, so anyone who leaves and comes back goes straight onto a billed seat with immediate access. Their FAQ also says Lindy will not re-add anyone an admin removed, and that a removed seat stays active to the end of the cycle with no mid-cycle proration. The one lever in the other direction is new on the same page: admins can set credit allocations per seat, so a shared pool no longer has to be first-come, first-served.

What should I weigh before buying Operations & Workflow software?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.
