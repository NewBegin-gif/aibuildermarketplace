What Livestorm does

Browser-based webinars with registration, automated emails, polls and analytics. There is no plan price: Pro sells attendee credits at €2.50 each, bought a year up front, and one credit is spent per unique participant per session — a four-part series to 200 people costs 800 credits.

What a credit actually costs you

There is no plan price. Pro is sold as attendee credits at EUR2.50 each, bought as a yearly pack up front, with sessions up to four hours and unlimited team members; Enterprise is quote-only with twelve-hour sessions.

The definition of a credit is the whole decision and it is worth reading twice: one credit is spent for each unique participant, per session, over a twelve-month period. Per SESSION. So a four-part series to the same 200 people is not 200 credits, it is 800 — EUR2,000 rather than EUR500. That single word is the difference between a webinar programme costing what you budgeted and costing four times it, and it is the thing to model before anything else: sessions times expected attendees, for the whole year.

The rules that work in your favour are worth knowing too. Registrants who never show up cost nothing, so a no-show rate is free rather than expensive. The same person joining live and then watching that session's replay counts once. And your own team members never consume credits, which makes internal dry runs free.

What makes this a bet rather than a purchase is the timing: you buy a year of attendance in advance. A webinar that outperforms runs you out of credits; one nobody attends leaves you holding them. The page will not total it up for you either — the slider moves but no price appears until you engage. Before committing, ask the one question the page does not answer: what happens to unused credits at the twelve-month mark. If your schedule is irregular or still experimental, paying a year ahead for attendance you cannot forecast is the wrong shape, and a per-month platform is the safer place to find out what works.

Frequently asked questions

How does Livestorm's pricing actually work?

You buy a yearly pack of attendee credits at €2.50 each and spend one credit per unique person per session over a 12-month period. Two sessions attended by the same person cost two credits; joining live and then watching that session's replay costs one. Read on 17 August 2026.

Do I pay for people who register and never turn up?

No. Livestorm states that only real participants count — people who join live, watch a replay or watch on demand — so no-shows cost nothing. That is the meaningful difference from seat-based webinar tools, where you buy a room size and pay for it whether it fills or not. Your own team members are also free.

Is there a Livestorm coupon or lifetime deal?

We have found neither, and both are searched for. Because the product is sold as prepaid credits rather than a subscription, the lever that exists is volume: you buy the pack that matches your expected attendance, and unused credits sit in the 12-month window. Size the pack from last year's actual attendance rather than from registrations.

Is Livestorm worth it for a small team?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Small teams get the most out of this category when one clear problem is already costing real hours or revenue; buying ahead of that just adds cost and another login. Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.

Who should not use Livestorm?

Skip it if your webinar schedule is irregular or experimental, because paying a year ahead for attendance you cannot forecast is the wrong shape; a per-month platform is safer while you find out what works. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
