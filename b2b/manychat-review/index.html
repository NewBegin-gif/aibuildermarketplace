When the paid plan is worth it

The meter is people you message in a month, not messages sent, and the published figures are the annual rates — a distinction worth $2 to $60 a month (read 6 September 2026). Paying month to month, the ladder is Essential $17 for 250 active contacts, Pro $39 for 2,500, Business $99 for 7,500 and Advanced $199 for 25,000. Commit to a year and those become $14, $29, $69 and $139, an 18% saving at the bottom and 30% at the top.

Only contacts who send or receive a message through ManyChat in that month count, one person once however many messages you exchange (pricing FAQ, re-read 25 September 2026), so a Reel that goes wide costs you in the month the replies arrive. Crossing a band boundary does not, however, jump your bill. ManyChat bills each additional active contact instead, and the rate falls hard as you climb: on annual billing 8.2 cents on Essential, 3.8 on Pro, 1.8 on Business and 0.28 on Advanced — a 29-fold spread.

That turns 'should I upgrade' into arithmetic. Essential costs $14 and Pro $29, so the $15 gap buys about 183 contacts of overage: past roughly 433 active contacts, Pro is simply cheaper. Pro to Business breaks even around 1,053 contacts over, and Business to Advanced around 3,889. Below those lines, overage is the cheap option and upgrading wastes money. One structural line sits outside all of it: two channels on Essential, three on Pro, unlimited from Business.

A third channel is a jump to $99 a month however few contacts you have. What upgrading does not solve is the annual lock. Annual plans cannot be downgraded or switched back to monthly until the term ends, and overages are billed monthly regardless. One thing does work in your favour: upgrade inside the same billing cycle and the overage fees accrued so far are forgiven. The free tier, meanwhile, is a demo — 25 active contacts since the March 2026 change.

How to actually use ManyChat

Watch the month a campaign lands, because that is when the bill moves. ManyChat counts active contacts: anyone who sends or receives a message through it during a monthly billing period, via automations, AI, broadcasts or the inbox, and one person counts once however many messages you exchange (pricing FAQ, re-read 25 September 2026). A viral Reel that pulls in thousands of replies lands in that month's count; people who stay silent afterwards stop counting unless you broadcast to them, so the broadcast list is the cost control, not a monthly prune.

Set an overage threshold on day one. Going over your allowance does not switch your automations off; it bills you per extra contact, and the console lets you cap how many of those you are willing to pay for. Then do the arithmetic rather than upgrading on instinct: at Essential's 8.2 cents a contact, Pro only becomes the cheaper plan somewhere past 433 active contacts. And if you do upgrade mid-cycle, the overage already accrued is forgiven, so there is no reason to wait for the billing date.

Check the free tier as it stands rather than as it is remembered: 25 active contacts, two channels, one user. Anything describing a generous ManyChat free plan is out of date. Build the fallback path deliberately, because it will be used. Flows are button-and-script based, so anyone who types something unexpected drops out — decide now whether they get a human, a menu or a link, and test it as a stranger would.

Frequently asked questions

Do contacts I no longer talk to still count towards my bill?

No. ManyChat's pricing FAQ (read 25 September 2026) defines an Active Contact as a real person you interact with during a monthly billing period, through messages or automations, and counts one person once however many messages you exchange. A contact becomes active when a message is sent or received through automations, AI, broadcasts or the inbox. So someone who replied to a Reel in March and never hears from you again does not count in April; broadcasting to your whole list is what brings them back into the count.

Can I get a refund if ManyChat is not for me?

Within 30 days, yes. The FAQ (read 25 September 2026) says you can cancel at any time, and that if it has been under 30 days since you bought your plan you can cancel and get a refund by messaging support. Before paying at all, Essential and Pro carry a 14-day free trial, and the Free plan needs no credit card. Note the annual catch: an annual plan cannot be downgraded or switched to monthly until its term ends.

What happens to my bill if I upgrade in the middle of the month?

A new billing period starts immediately and the unused part of your current plan is credited towards the upgrade, per the FAQ (read 25 September 2026). If you had already gone over your contact allowance that cycle, upgrading within the same cycle forgives the overage fees so far and counts those contacts towards the new plan. Downgrades work the other way: they only take effect at the next billing period on monthly plans.

What should I weigh before buying Growth & Revenue software?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Small teams get the most out of this category when one clear problem is already costing real hours or revenue; buying ahead of that just adds cost and another login. Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.
