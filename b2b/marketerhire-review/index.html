Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 23 Sep 2026

No rates are published on the pricing page or in the FAQ. The pricing page says there is "no plan to pick and no retainer to guess at" and that after one call you are matched with a marketer "at a budget you agreed to". Published terms: a free consultation with no obligation, a match within 24-48 hours, a two-week refundable trial, and month-to-month billing you can cancel at any time.

The FAQ adds how it works: engagements are structured as a monthly retainer, with the marketer working to agreed priorities and outcomes instead of tracked hours; you are billed at project kick-off; if the first person does not work out during the initial two weeks you are rematched with another candidate, as part of the Service Contract signed at the start; invoices are monthly and can be reviewed before they are processed, by card or ACH.

The hiring form routes you by budget, either to a call with a Growth Manager or to a further job brief with a follow-up. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

When the paid plan is worth it

MarketerHire publishes no rates, so there is no tier ladder to weigh: the budget is set on a sales call, and the hiring form itself routes you by budget, either to a call with a Growth Manager or to a longer job brief and a follow-up. What you can weigh are the terms, and they are the distinctive part. The engagement is a monthly retainer, not a timesheet. The FAQ says your marketer works to agreed priorities and outcomes "instead of tracking hours".

That cuts both ways: you are not paying for idle hours, but you also cannot buy a small metered slice of a senior person, so scope is the thing you negotiate. The risk controls are real but front-loaded. You are billed at kick-off, the first two weeks are fully refundable, and inside those two weeks you can be rematched with another candidate if the first one does not fit — the FAQ ties the rematch to that initial window and to the Service Contract you sign.

After that the protection is month-to-month cancellation and the option to change scope, add a person or ask for a replacement. What a bigger budget does not fix is the brief. A senior marketer pointed at a vague goal produces vague work at a higher monthly rate, and neither the trial nor the rematch rescues a brief that was wrong. The 95% trial-to-hire rate on the pricing page is MarketerHire's own figure, and it measures whether clients kept the person, not what the person delivered.

How to actually use MarketerHire

Write the brief before the call, because it decides both the match and the budget. Name the channel, the metric and the timeframe; the form uses what you enter to decide whether you speak to a Growth Manager or get sent to a longer brief first. Get the number in writing. Nothing is published, so ask for the monthly retainer, what scope it covers and how a change of scope is priced before kick-off — you are billed at kick-off, not at the end of the trial.

Treat the two refundable weeks as a real test rather than an introduction. Give the marketer one measurable deliverable due inside that window and decide before day 14, because the full refund and the rematch are both tied to the first two weeks. After that you are month-to-month. Invoices arrive monthly and can be reviewed before they are charged, so put a review date in the calendar each month rather than letting the retainer roll.

And keep the strategy yours: what you are buying is speed and vetting — a matched senior marketer in days rather than a months-long hire — not someone to decide what the business should do.

Can I hire someone for ten hours a week?

Not as an hourly arrangement. Read 23 September 2026, the FAQ says engagements are structured as a monthly retainer and that, instead of tracking hours, the marketer works to the priorities, initiatives and outcomes you agree. So the lever is scope, not hours: a smaller engagement means a narrower brief, and the FAQ lists adjusting the scope, adding a resource, replacing the marketer or cancelling as the options if your strategy changes. If what you need is a metered block of time, ask on the call how a small scope is priced before you assume it fits, because nothing on the site describes a minimum.

What exactly is refundable in the two-week trial?

The first two weeks, and the clock starts at kick-off. Read 23 September 2026, the FAQ calls it a no-risk two-week trial that is fully refundable, says you are billed at project kick-off, and says that during those initial two weeks, if it does not work out with the first person for whatever reason, you are rematched with another candidate. It adds that this sits in the Service Contract you sign at the start. What the page does not say is how a refund is claimed or whether a rematch restarts the two weeks, so read that clause in the contract before kick-off. After the trial, the protection is month-to-month cancellation, and invoices can be reviewed before they are processed.

What should I weigh before buying Growth & Revenue software?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Small teams get the most out of this category when one clear problem is already costing real hours or revenue; buying ahead of that just adds cost and another login.
