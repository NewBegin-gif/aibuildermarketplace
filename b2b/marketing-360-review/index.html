What actually drives the quote

Pricing is quote-based, and the shape of the gate tells you something. Marketing 360 does have a pricing page — How Plans & Pricing Work — and on 6 September 2026 it published no figure of any kind. The only numbers on it are the answers you supply: a six-question form asking your industry, your website and your annual revenue band, bucketed as $0-$250K, $250K-$1M or $1M+. You are asked what you earn before you are told what it costs.

That is worth knowing whichever side of a band you sit on. So there is no ladder to weigh, and the useful preparation is knowing which three things stack. First, the core software, which reporting puts at roughly $395 to $419 a month including marketing consultations. Second, design and custom website work, separate, running from about $2,000 into five figures. Third, managed ad services, layered on top of both. Reviewers repeatedly flag that the real total climbs well above the headline, sometimes with recurring fees that were not obvious at signature — so the quote to ask for is the twelve-month all-in, itemised across those three, not a monthly software rate.

The questions that decide whether the number is fair are these. What is the contract term and what happens at renewal; is the website you pay to build yours to take with you if you leave; and is ad management charged as a flat fee or a percentage of spend. That last one matters most, because a percentage means your bill grows with the budget you are trying to spend efficiently.

What a bigger package does not solve is that lock-in. Ownership of the site, the domain and the ad accounts is a contract question, not a tier, and no amount of spend answers it for you. Settle it in writing before anything is built.

How to actually use Marketing 360

Ask for the full written quote with every line separated: the core software, the design and website build, the managed advertising services, and the ad spend itself. Reviewers repeatedly flag that the real cost climbs well above the headline, sometimes with recurring fees for features that go unused, and separated lines are the only way to see that before you sign rather than after. Mark each line as recurring or one-time while you are at it.

Design work billed monthly is a subscription in disguise, and website builds here can run from around $2,000 into five figures — a number you want beside the software fee rather than discovered later. Then ask the ownership questions before anything is built. Who owns the website, the domain and the ad accounts, and what happens to each if you leave? This is an all-in-one 'we will do it for you' model, which is genuinely valuable if you want a single vendor and a human team, and it carries more lock-in than assembling best-of-breed tools. Establishing the exit at the start is what makes the convenience worth having.
