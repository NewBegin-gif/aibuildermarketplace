What Marketing 360 does

All-in-one marketing platform for small business. Websites, CRM, ads, email and reputation management under one roof, with a unified reporting dashboard (their 'Intelligence' analytics) that pulls every channel, lead and sale into one view — and real humans to help run it.

What actually drives the quote

Pricing is quote-based, and the shape of the gate tells you something. Marketing 360 does have a pricing page — How Plans & Pricing Work — and on 25 September 2026 it still published no figure of any kind. What stands in front of a price is a form: business name, industry, website, headcount bucketed as 1-2, 3-9 or 10+, then your name, email and phone. After that the page builds a marketing report and a 90-day plan and says a specialist will reach out with your custom plans and pricing.

You hand over a phone number before you see a number. The same form promises a 14-day free trial, the one term on the page worth holding them to. So there is no ladder to weigh, and the useful preparation is knowing which three things stack. First, the core software, which reporting puts at roughly $395 to $419 a month including marketing consultations. Second, design and custom website work, separate, running from about $2,000 into five figures.

Third, managed ad services, layered on top of both. Reviewers repeatedly flag that the real total climbs well above the headline, sometimes with recurring fees that were not obvious at signature — so the quote to ask for is the twelve-month all-in, itemised across those three, not a monthly software rate. The questions that decide whether the number is fair are these. What is the contract term and what happens at renewal; is the website you pay to build yours to take with you if you leave; and is ad management charged as a flat fee or a percentage of spend.

That last one matters most, because a percentage means your bill grows with the budget you are trying to spend efficiently. What a bigger package does not solve is that lock-in. Ownership of the site, the domain and the ad accounts is a contract question, not a tier, and no amount of spend answers it for you. Settle it in writing before anything is built.

How to actually use Marketing 360

Ask for the full written quote with every line separated: the core software, the design and website build, the managed advertising services, and the ad spend itself. Reviewers repeatedly flag that the real cost climbs well above the headline, sometimes with recurring fees for features that go unused, and separated lines are the only way to see that before you sign rather than after. Mark each line as recurring or one-time while you are at it.

Design work billed monthly is a subscription in disguise, and website builds here can run from around $2,000 into five figures — a number you want beside the software fee rather than discovered later. Then ask the ownership questions before anything is built. Who owns the website, the domain and the ad accounts, and what happens to each if you leave? This is an all-in-one 'we will do it for you' model, which is genuinely valuable if you want a single vendor and a human team, and it carries more lock-in than assembling best-of-breed tools. Establishing the exit at the start is what makes the convenience worth having.

Frequently asked questions

Can I see Marketing 360's prices without talking to sales?

No. Read 25 September 2026, the How Plans & Pricing Work page carries no figure. To get one you fill in a form with your business name, industry, website, headcount (1-2, 3-9 or 10+), name, email and phone; the page then generates a marketing report and a 90-day plan and says a specialist will reach out with your custom plans and pricing, with a free 15-minute call offered. The roughly $395 to $419 a month for the core software is third-party reporting, not a vendor price.

Is there a free trial?

The pricing form says so: under the button it reads 'Plus, a 14-day free trial' (read 25 September 2026). The page does not say what the trial includes, whether a card is needed or what happens when it ends, so ask on the call and get it in writing. Design and website work, which reporting puts at around $2,000 into five figures, is a build rather than a subscription and is unlikely to be part of a trial.

What should the quote contain before I sign?

Every line separately: the core software, the design and website build, managed advertising services and the ad spend itself, each marked recurring or one-time. Then the contract questions the page does not answer: the term and renewal, whether ad management is a flat fee or a percentage of spend, and who owns the website, domain and ad accounts if you leave. Reviewers repeatedly report totals well above the headline, and an itemised twelve-month figure is the only way to see that beforehand.

What should I weigh before buying Growth & Revenue software?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Small teams get the most out of this category when one clear problem is already costing real hours or revenue; buying ahead of that just adds cost and another login. Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.
