What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Read on 19 September 2026, meeritech.com still says “5.0 on Shopify App Store”, “100% five-star reviews” and “5.0 average — 8 verified reviews on Shopify” — word for word what it said on 23 August. The listing that same morning shows 4.7 from 11 reviews: ten at five stars and one at one star. So the vendor’s figure is what you get by leaving the single bad review out, and its review count has stood still while the real one moved.

Nothing about the app is settled by that, but a marketing page that rounds its own rating up by dropping the worst review, and then does not refresh it for a month, is a useful thing to know before you weigh the rest of the page. The review base is thin whichever number you use. Eleven reviews on an app that launched in February 2024, and the three the listing shows first come from one store in the United Kingdom and two in Pakistan, one of which had been using the app for seven days when it wrote.

That is not evidence of anything wrong; it is very little evidence of anything at all, and it cannot tell you how this behaves on a store like yours. Then there is the channel itself, which is the real decision. Web push needs a browser-level subscription, and the app’s own pitch is that no email or phone number is required to get one. That cuts both ways: the list is easier to build and worthless off the browser that built it.

You cannot export those subscribers into an email tool, you cannot reach them anywhere else, and your recovery ceiling is set by opt-in rate rather than by the app. Measure the opt-in rate first — 300 free notifications a month is enough to find out — because it decides everything the paid plan can do for you. One more limit worth reading before you install: the listing names English as the only language, and Shopify Admin as the only integration.

There is no Klaviyo, no email platform, no SMS tool on that line, so this sits beside your existing stack rather than inside it.

What Meeri does

Shopify app that recovers abandoned carts with AI-written web push notifications instead of email or SMS — no address or phone needed to subscribe, and no commission on recovered revenue.

When the paid plan is worth it

There is one paid step here and it costs $4.99, so the question is not whether you can afford it but what the meter actually counts. Basic gives you 300 push notifications a month. Pro gives you 3,000 impressions. Those are two different words, and neither page defines the second one, so a notification sent to a thousand subscribers may be one notification or a thousand impressions depending on which meaning applies.

That single ambiguity decides whether Pro is ten times the free plan or roughly the same size, and it is worth one email to support before you upgrade. The more open end is the sentence next to Pro's price: additional usage-based charges are billed every 30 days. No rate is published on the App Store listing, and none appears on meeritech.com either. On a $4.99 plan an unquoted variable charge is the whole cost risk, because everything else here is small.

Ask what triggers it and what it costs per unit, and get the answer in writing before you enable the plan. What you do not pay is a commission on recovered revenue, and both tiers say so explicitly. That is genuinely unusual in cart recovery, where a percentage of what the tool recovers is the normal model, and it is the strongest thing on this pricing page.

How to actually use Meeri

Install it on the free plan and do not upgrade for at least a month. Web push lives or dies on the opt-in prompt, and the only number that matters is what share of your visitors accept it — which you cannot know in advance and which 300 free notifications a month is enough to measure. While that runs, watch two things rather than one. The opt-in rate tells you how big the channel can get.

The recovery rate on the notifications you do send tells you whether it is worth having. A high opt-in with weak recovery usually means the timing or the offer, both of which you control here, and both of which are cheaper to fix before you are paying for impressions. Before you move to Pro, get two answers in writing: whether the 3,000 on that plan counts sends or impressions, and what the additional usage-based charge is per unit.

Those are the only two unknowns on a $4.99 plan, and support answering them clearly is itself a signal.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need Meeri Pro, or is the free plan enough?

Start on Basic, because the free plan is sized to answer the only question that matters before you pay. Read on 19 September 2026 and unchanged since August, the free tier hands you 300 notifications a month — which the listing itself translates as roughly 100 recovered carts — together with unlimited subscribers and segmentation, the abandoned-cart campaign, scheduled promotions, AI-generated campaign content and intelligent delivery. Upgrading multiplies that allowance tenfold for less than five dollars, throws in unlimited campaigns and automations, and swaps basic email support for premium; neither tier takes a cut of what it recovers, and the paid one opens with a week you do not pay for. What decides the upgrade is not the feature list but your opt-in rate: web push only reaches browsers that accepted the prompt, so a month on Basic tells you how many of your visitors that actually is. If 300 notifications a month is more than you can send because too few people opted in, the paid plan buys you nothing. If you run out in the first week, it costs less than one recovered order.

What are the usage-based charges on top of the $4.99?

Nobody outside the vendor knows, and that is worth one email before you enable the plan. The Pro card says “Upgrades as you grow. Additional usage-based charges are billed every 30 days”, and the footer repeats that recurring and usage-based charges are billed every 30 days in USD. No rate appears anywhere on the listing, and meeritech.com does not print one either — both re-read on 19 September 2026. There is a second ambiguity stacked on the first: Basic is measured in 300 push notifications and Pro in 3,000 push notification impressions. Those are different words, and neither page defines the second, so one campaign sent to a thousand subscribers might count as one notification or as a thousand impressions. Between them, those two unpublished details decide whether Pro is ten times the free plan or roughly the same size, and what happens when you pass it. Ask support for the impression definition and the overage rate in writing, and keep the reply.

Is the 5.0 rating on Meeri’s website real?

No, and the gap is easy to check yourself. On 19 September 2026 meeritech.com advertises “5.0 on Shopify App Store”, “100% five-star reviews” and “5.0 average — 8 verified reviews on Shopify”. The App Store listing the same day shows 4.7 from 11 reviews, with ten at five stars and one at one star. The vendor’s number is the average you get by omitting the only bad review, and its count has been frozen at eight since at least 23 August while the listing added two more. Treat both numbers as very thin evidence rather than as a dispute worth settling: eleven reviews on an app that launched in February 2024 cannot tell you much either way, and one of the three the listing shows first was written after seven days of use. Judge this on your own opt-in rate over a free month, not on anyone’s star average.
