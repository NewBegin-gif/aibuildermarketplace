What MeetGeek does

Meeting recorder and note-taker: transcription, summaries and searchable meeting history.

Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 1 Aug 2026

(2026-08-01, read on their own pricing page) Basic is free. Pro $9.99 per user per month with 20 hours of transcription. Business $17 per user per month with unlimited transcription, aggregated analytics, exports and admin user management. Enterprise is quote-only with API and MCP access. Extra transcription hours are $0.50 each. A 14-day free trial runs on the paid tiers. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The meter is transcription hours and Pro gives you twenty a month, which is about an hour a working day. That sounds generous until you count the meetings you would actually record — a sales rep on four calls a day passes it in the first week, and the overage is $0.50 an hour. Unlimited transcription is the real reason to be on Business at $17, so treat Pro as a plan for occasional recording rather than a cheaper version of the same thing.

Worth checking before you buy: consent. A recorder that joins every meeting changes what people say in them, and that is a policy decision rather than a pricing one.

What buyers report about MeetGeek

Recurring themes from G2, Capterra and practitioner reviews, read in August 2026. We have not run MeetGeek ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Cross-meeting analytics is the standout — reviewers say it goes deeper than competitors at the same price.
  • • It removes note-taking from the call entirely, which is the whole point. Overall score around 4.0.
What they complain about
  • • Transcription accuracy is the most common complaint and it degrades noticeably on noisy calls, crosstalk, accents and non-English audio. If your meetings are multilingual, test before committing.
  • • The free tier is 3 hours a month with 3-month storage — occasional use only.
  • • Pro is $9.99 per user annually but $15.99 monthly; Business $17 annually and $28 monthly. That monthly premium is steep.

When the paid plan is worth it

Basic is free, Pro is $9.99 a user a month with twenty hours of transcription, and Business is $17 with unlimited transcription plus aggregated analytics, exports and admin user management. Extra hours are $0.50 each.

Those two numbers make the choice arithmetic rather than instinct. Twenty hours is about an hour a working day, and the overage closes the gap fast: Pro plus fourteen extra hours costs exactly what Business costs. So thirty-four hours a month is the line. Below it Pro is cheaper; above it you are paying more than $17 for less than Business gives.

Count the meetings you would actually record rather than the ones you attend, because that is where the estimate goes wrong. A sales rep on four calls a day passes twenty hours in the first week of the month. For that profile Pro is not a cheaper version of Business — it is a plan for occasional recording, and treating it as the budget option produces a bigger bill than the plan above it.

What Business adds beyond the meter is worth weighing separately: aggregated analytics across meetings, exports and admin user management. Those are team capabilities rather than individual ones, so a solo user recording heavily may genuinely only need the unlimited hours, while a team of five needs the admin layer regardless of volume. Enterprise adds API and MCP access on quote. There is a 14-day trial on the paid tiers.

One thing no tier settles: a recorder that joins every meeting changes what people say in them. Consent is a policy decision to make before the rollout, not a setting to find afterwards.

How to actually use MeetGeek

Settle consent before you switch it on, because a recorder that joins every meeting changes what people say in them — and in several jurisdictions it changes what is lawful. Write down who gets told, how they are told, and which meetings are excluded. This is a policy decision rather than a pricing one and it is the step most teams skip.

Then count the meetings you would actually record, in hours. Pro's twenty hours a month is about an hour a working day, which sounds generous until you apply it: a sales rep on four calls a day passes it in the first week. Overage runs $0.50 an hour, and Business at $17 with unlimited transcription becomes the cheaper plan past about 34 hours a month — so treat Pro as a plan for occasional recording rather than a cheaper version of the same thing.

Use the 14-day trial on your real meeting mix rather than a scheduled test call. Accents, crosstalk, a bad connection and someone dialling in from a car are what transcription accuracy actually has to survive, and every summary built on a poor transcript is confidently wrong.

Best for: people who run many external meetings and need a searchable record — the summaries earn their keep when you cannot remember which of four calls a detail came from.
Who should skip it: if your meetings are internal and few, because the note-taking in your existing conferencing tool covers that; also skip Pro if you record daily, since twenty hours will not last.

The natural comparison is Fireflies or Otter — the direct meeting-recorder peers — compare the transcription-hour allowance at your real meeting load, since that is the only meter that moves. Decide by which one fits the job above, not by the louder brand.

Frequently asked questions

Is MeetGeek secure and reliable enough to depend on?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.

Who should not use MeetGeek?

Skip it if your meetings are internal and few, because the note-taking in your existing conferencing tool covers that; also skip Pro if you record daily, since twenty hours will not last. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
