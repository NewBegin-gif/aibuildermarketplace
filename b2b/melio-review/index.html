What Melio does

Bill payments for small businesses by bank transfer or card, syncing with your accounting tools. The free Go plan covers five ACH transfers a month, then $0.50 each; Core is about $25/month. Card payments carry a flat 2.9%, which only pays off when you are deliberately floating cash.

Pricing (2026)

A free Go plan (5 free ACH/mo, then $0.50 each); paid Core about $25/mo (20 free ACH), Boost $55, Unlimited $80 (2026). Card payments a flat 2.9%; check and wire fees apply. 30-day trial. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

The catch

The free plan is genuinely useful for low bill volume, but the value case narrows as you scale into paid tiers — and the 2.9% card fee only makes sense when you're deliberately floating cash on a card for rewards or timing.

When the paid plan is worth it

The payoff from upgrading tracks bill volume more than anything else. On the free Go plan you get 5 free ACH payments a month, then $0.50 for each additional one — at that price, moving up only pays for itself once your monthly ACH count is high enough that free-tier overage fees would exceed the subscription cost itself, or once you need features Go simply doesn't offer. Core ($25/mo, $20/mo billed annually) includes 20 free ACH a month, Boost ($55/mo, $44/mo annually) includes 50, and Unlimited ($80/mo, $64/mo annually) includes unlimited free ACH, so heavy bill-pay volume is where the paid tiers earn their keep. The sharper breakpoint for growing teams is per-seat math: Core and Boost each add $10/mo per additional user ($8/mo billed annually), so a business adding several teammates can find Unlimited's flat unlimited-user pricing cheaper than stacking seats on a lower tier. What upgrading does not touch: the 2.9% card payment fee is flat across every tier, and per-item check and wire fees apply no matter which plan you're on.

How to actually use Melio

Getting started means creating an account with your business details (legal name, industry, address) and personal details, then connecting a way to pay — a bank account verified instantly through Plaid or via micro-deposits, or a card. Vendors get added the same way, with either their bank details for direct deposit or just a mailing address for a check. The first real payment happens from the Pay dashboard: start a new payment, enter the vendor, bill amount, invoice details and due date, then choose how and when the vendor gets paid. Frequently asked questions

Is Melio safe to run my finances through?

The bar here is higher than features. Check who actually holds the money and under what licence, whether client funds are segregated, what audit trail and permission controls you get, and how support behaves when something goes wrong mid-transaction. Anything touching payments should be able to answer those in writing; if it cannot, that is your answer.

Who should not use Melio?

Skip it if you already run AP through robust accounting software with bill-pay built in — a second tool adds little. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
