What Melio does

Bill payments for small businesses by bank transfer or card, syncing with your accounting tools. The free Go plan covers five ACH transfers a month, then $0.50 each; Core is about $25/month. Card payments carry a flat 2.9%, which only pays off when you are deliberately floating cash.

Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 17 Aug 2026

(re-verified 2026-08-17 on their own pricing page) A free Go plan, $0 forever, with 5 free ACH payments a month and $0.50 for each one after that. Core is $25/mo, or $20 billed annually, with 20 free ACH a month; Boost $55/mo, or $44 annually, with 50 free ACH; Unlimited $80/mo. The detail our earlier note missed: every paid plan adds $10 a month per additional user, or $8 on annual billing, so a three-person finance team on Core is $45 a month rather than $25.

Card payments carry a flat 2.9% fee and check and wire payments have their own charges. There is a 30-day trial. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What they praise
  • • Free ACH transfers and no subscription on the entry tier — reviewers name the pricing as the main draw.
  • • The QuickBooks integration and the interface are both repeatedly called easy.
  • • TrustRadius reviewers name a feature the pricing page underplays: Melio prints and mails paper cheques for you, which is what actually removes the manual work for businesses whose suppliers still want a cheque.
  • • The other repeated benefit is evidentiary — when a vendor claims they were not paid, the delivery tracking settles it in a few clicks. Scheduling payments ahead for cash-flow reasons and role-based access for the finance team come up alongside it.
What they complain about
  • • Delays are the top G2 complaint tag (17 mentions), followed by payment issues (16) and payment delays (14). One reviewer's vendor payment left the account on 12 December 2025 and had still not arrived by 9 January 2026.
  • • Reviewers report payments processing before the scheduled date, repeatedly, causing overdrafts. If you schedule against a cash-flow calendar, watch this.
  • • International payments are the clear weak spot: failed transfers, poor fee disclosure and no way to inspect recipient details after submitting.
  • • 2.9% on card payments, and support is thin on the lower tiers.

When the paid plan is worth it

The payoff from upgrading tracks bill volume more than anything else. On the free Go plan you get 5 free ACH payments a month, then $0.50 for each additional one. At that price, moving up only pays for itself once your monthly ACH count is high enough that free-tier overage fees would exceed the subscription cost itself — or once you need features Go simply doesn't offer. Core ($25/mo, $20/mo billed annually) includes 20 free ACH a month, Boost ($55/mo, $44/mo annually) includes 50, and Unlimited ($80/mo, $64/mo annually) includes unlimited free ACH, so heavy bill-pay volume is where the paid tiers earn their keep.

The sharper breakpoint for growing teams is per-seat math: Core and Boost each add $10/mo per additional user ($8/mo billed annually), so a business adding several teammates can find Unlimited's flat unlimited-user pricing cheaper than stacking seats on a lower tier. What upgrading does not touch: the 2.9% card payment fee is flat across every tier, and per-item check and wire fees apply no matter which plan you're on.

How to actually use Melio

Getting started means creating an account with your business details (legal name, industry, address) and personal details, then connecting a way to pay — a bank account verified instantly through Plaid or via micro-deposits, or a card. Vendors get added the same way, with either their bank details for direct deposit or just a mailing address for a check. The first real payment happens from the Pay dashboard: start a new payment, enter the vendor, bill amount, invoice details and due date, then choose how and when the vendor gets paid.

Where people get stuck in practice: skipping the micro-deposit bank verification step blocks payments until it's completed, and QuickBooks users report the accounting sync breaking down — bills not appearing inside Melio, the bill-pay screen stuck on "loading," or payments not posting back to the books afterward. Support is only staffed during U.S. Eastern business hours, so anything that goes wrong outside that window tends to sit unresolved until the next day.

Frequently asked questions

What does Melio cost?

The Go plan is free forever with 5 ACH payments a month, then $0.50 each. Core is $25 a month ($20 annually) with 20 free ACH, Boost $55 ($44) with 50 free ACH, and Unlimited $80. Read on 17 August 2026.

What does it cost for a team?

More than the plan price, and this is the part most summaries leave out: each additional user is $10 a month, or $8 on annual billing, on top of the plan. Core with three users is $45 a month billed monthly, not $25. Count the seats before comparing with a flat-fee competitor.

Are the payments themselves free?

ACH payments are free up to your plan's allowance and $0.50 each after it. Card payments cost a flat 2.9%, and cheques and wires carry their own fees. For a business paying mostly by ACH the effective cost is close to the subscription; for one paying suppliers by card, the 2.9% will dwarf it.

Is Melio safe to run my finances through?

The bar here is higher than features. Check who actually holds the money and under what licence, whether client funds are segregated, what audit trail and permission controls you get, and how support behaves when something goes wrong mid-transaction. Anything touching payments should be able to answer those in writing; if it cannot, that is your answer.

Who should not use Melio?

Skip it if you already run AP through robust accounting software with bill-pay built in — a second tool adds little. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
