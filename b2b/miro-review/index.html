What Miro does

Visual collaboration platform: an infinite online whiteboard for brainstorms, diagrams, workshops and planning — with templates, voting and AI that generates diagrams and summaries, built for real-time and async teamwork.

Pricing (2026)

(2026) A free plan (3 editable boards workspace-wide, unlimited members, 10 AI credits/mo); Starter about $8/member/mo billed annually ($10 monthly), Business $20/member/mo annual ($25 monthly), Enterprise custom from 30 seats. Annual saves roughly 20%; Miro AI runs on monthly credit allowances per plan. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Per-member pricing charges for every member added to the workspace, so an org-wide rollout multiplies at full headcount — and the free plan's 3-board cap is workspace-wide, not per person, so real teams outgrow it in days. Model who truly needs to edit before you buy seats.

What buyers report about Miro

Recurring themes from G2, Capterra, Gartner Peer Insights and Trustpilot, read in August 2026. We have not run Miro ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • The canvas is fast, there are 7,000+ templates, and real-time collaboration simply works — 4.6 on G2 across 12,000+ reviews.
  • • Miro AI turning a wall of sticky notes into a structured diagram is the feature reviewers name as genuinely new.
What they complain about
  • • The one-star reviews are not about the whiteboard. They are about seats added without explicit approval, view-links triggering paid licences, auto-renewal charges, and a refund path reviewers describe as obfuscated. Check who counts as a billable seat before you share a board externally.
  • • Boards with thousands of objects slow down — 773 G2 reviews flag lag.
  • • The learning curve appears in 893 G2 reviews: there is so much here that new users do not know where to start.
  • • Starter is $8 per user, Business $20. The free tier's three-board limit stops teams quickly.

Where the ratings disagree. 4.6 on G2 against 2.2 on Trustpilot across 109 reviews. Almost the entire gap is seat billing and cancellation — the product is not in dispute.

Best for: teams that think visually — brainstorms, flowcharts, retros, workshops and planning on an infinite shared canvas with strong templates and facilitation tools.
Who should skip it: if your work is mostly documents or task tracking (a doc or PM tool fits better) or most of your company would only ever need to view, not edit.

The natural comparison is FigJam or Lucidspark — FigJam wins if your designers already live in Figma, Lucidspark on diagram-heavy work; Miro wins on breadth: templates, integrations and workshop facilitation. Decide by which one fits the job above, not by the louder brand.

Frequently asked questions

Does Miro replace my current setup or sit on top of it?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.

Who should not use Miro?

Skip it if your work is mostly documents or task tracking (a doc or PM tool fits better) or most of your company would only ever need to view, not edit. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
