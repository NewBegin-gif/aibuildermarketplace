What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Two honest cautions. First, the pricing is fully hidden — no tiers, no ranges, a 404 where the pricing page should be — which for a tool that promises spend transparency is an irony worth noticing. Second, the ROI story on the homepage (‘on a $5M media budget, teams typically recover over $100K’) is the vendor’s own illustration, not an audited benchmark — ask for verified case numbers on the demo call.

What actually drives the quote

There is nothing published to weigh: the pricing page returns a 404 and the site funnels everything to 'Book a Demo', verified 17 July 2026. Subscriptions clearly exist — the affiliate programme pays per paid subscription — but the rate requires a sales conversation. Worth noticing before that call: this is a tool whose pitch is spend transparency, sold without price transparency. That is not disqualifying, but it does set the tone for what to insist on. Ask what the licence is counted on — media spend, seats, connected platforms, or data volume — because a tool that sits on top of your ad spend is often priced against it, and a percentage of spend grows with exactly the budget you are hiring it to make more efficient. The second thing to press on is the business case, because the one on the homepage is theirs. 'On a $5M media budget, teams typically recover over $100K' is the vendor's own illustration rather than an audited benchmark. Ask for verified case numbers from customers at your spend level, and note that the claim scales with waste: if your campaigns are already tightly managed, the recoverable amount is smaller and the same licence fee buys less. What is genuinely favourable about the shape: it layers on top of your existing platforms rather than replacing them, and the vendor puts setup at under thirty minutes. That makes a paid pilot cheap in time even if it is not cheap in money — so ask for a short pilot priced separately rather than an annual commitment decided from a demo.
