What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The price is still hidden — a 404 where the pricing page should be, and no pricing item in the navigation — which for a tool that promises spend transparency is an irony worth noticing, and the FAQ makes it sharper: its own introduction says it covers what the platform costs, and then no answer in it names a figure. The ROI story is also the vendor's own: the homepage tile assumes a $5M budget and puts a 3% efficiency gain at $150K alongside $100K of reallocated spend, which is 2% of the same budget. Ask for verified case numbers at your spend level on the demo call.

What buyers report about Mktg.ai

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Mktg.ai ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • It joins creative metadata to KPI data in one dashboard, so you can see which specific assets are working rather than which channels are — that is a genuinely different cut of the data.
  • • 30-day pilot programmes before committing, and reported results of 5-10 hours a week saved per analyst with 15-20% of spend reallocated within the first months.
What they complain about
  • • Those performance figures are the vendor's own. We found no independent verification and no user reviews on G2, Capterra or Trustpilot.
  • • Pricing is quote-only, scaled to team size and creative volume. Nothing is published.
  • • It competes with Bynder, Tenovos and Tableau — bigger names with real review histories. Weigh the absence of an independent track record accordingly.

What actually drives the quote

There is still no published rate — mktg.ai/pricing still returned 404 on 25 September 2026 and the navigation carries no pricing item — but the tier structure is no longer hidden, and that is new. The FAQ names two plans above the entry level, Growth and Enterprise, and says what each buys: dedicated onboarding on both, plus a dedicated customer success manager, SLA-backed response times and quarterly business reviews. Email support comes with every plan.

Custom integrations are Enterprise-only, and so is EU data residency — US data centres are the default, which for a European buyer is a contract question rather than a preference. Worth noticing before the call: the FAQ's own introduction says it covers what the platform costs, and then no answer in it names a figure. Its own homepage promises you will see what is working before the spend is gone; the one figure it does not publish is its own.

So ask what the licence is counted on — media spend, seats, connected platforms or data volume. A tool priced against your ad spend grows with the budget you hired it to shrink, and that is the one shape you cannot renegotiate later.

How to actually use Mktg.ai

Bring your own media-spend figure to the demo, because every number the vendor publishes is anchored to a $5M budget that is probably not yours. The homepage business-case tile sets out four: $5M assumed annual spend, $150K as a 3% efficiency gain, $70K of labour saved per year based on 1,050 hours, and $100K of reallocated underperforming media spend. Two describe the same budget at different rates — $150K is 3%, $100K is 2% — and the labour figure implies about $67 an hour.

The FAQ adds a third scale: 15–20% of ad spend often reallocated within the first few months. Ask which of those a customer at your spend actually reached. Ask about onboarding in the same breath. The FAQ puts real-time data in week one and full onboarding — connectors, KPI configuration, training — at two to three weeks. Then get the contract shape in writing: term, notice period, renewal, and whether your data sits in the US or the EU, since residency is an Enterprise-only option.

Frequently asked questions

How long before mktg.ai shows anything useful?

About a week for data, two to three weeks for the full setup, by the vendor's own account. The FAQ (read 25 September 2026) says most teams see real-time data within their first week, and that full onboarding — connector setup, KPI configuration and team training — typically takes two to three weeks. Dedicated onboarding is included on the Growth and Enterprise plans; the FAQ does not say what onboarding the entry plan gets, so ask.

Who owns the data I connect, and is it used to train models?

You own it, according to the FAQ (read 25 September 2026): customer data is your property under their terms, and they say they never use it to train models or share it across customers. Data is stored in US data centres by default; EU data residency is available only on Enterprise. The FAQ also lists encryption in transit and at rest, role-based access per organisation and GDPR compliance. Check the terms themselves for what happens to the data when you leave, which the FAQ does not cover.

Do I have to replace my current ad tools?

No. The FAQ (read 25 September 2026) says mktg.ai sits alongside existing tools and ingests their data, and that most teams keep buying through Meta Ads Manager, Google Ads and the like, using mktg.ai as an intelligence layer above them. The most-used integrations it names are Meta, Google Ads, TikTok, LinkedIn, YouTube and The Trade Desk; custom integrations are for Enterprise customers. It positions itself against attribution tools such as Northbeam and Triple Whale as answering which creative worked rather than which channel.

What should I weigh before buying Marketing software?

Usually yes for the major platforms, but check yours specifically. Tools in this class typically ship native connectors for the big CMS, CRM and email platforms and fall back on Zapier, webhooks or an API for everything else. The practical question is whether your stack is on the native list or needs glue, because a missing native connector normally means either manual work or a paid middleware layer. Check the integrations page for your exact tools before you buy.
