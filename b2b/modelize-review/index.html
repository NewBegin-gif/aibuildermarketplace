What Modelize does

Shopify app that generates on-model, lifestyle, studio and flat-lay product photos in bulk from your existing catalogue images, using Google's Gemini image models. Free tier is 3 one-off images; Starter $19 a month buys 100 images, Pro $49 buys 250 at up to 4K, Enterprise $99 buys 500 with extras at $0.20 each.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The economics are flat: at roughly $0.19 to $0.20 an image on every tier, moving up the ladder buys resolution, custom presets and support, never a cheaper image. Read it that way and the pricing is honest; read it as a volume discount and you will overbuy. Two other things deserve a clear head. The output quality rides on Google's Gemini image models rather than on anything Modelize owns, so results and consistency can shift when Google ships a new version, and that is outside the vendor's control as much as yours. And the app is genuinely new — launched 20 April 2026, 13 reviews at the time of writing — so the five-star average is a small sample, not a track record. Finally, an on-model photo generated by AI shows a person who does not exist; check what your ad platforms and marketplaces require you to disclose before you build a catalogue on it.

When the paid plan is worth it

Do the division before you pick a tier, because the ladder is almost perfectly flat. Starter is $19 for 100 images, which is $0.19 an image. Pro is $49 for 250, which is $0.196. Enterprise is $99 for 500, which is $0.198, and every image past the allowance costs $0.20. There is no volume discount here at any point — you pay roughly nineteen or twenty cents an image whichever plan you are on. So the tiers are not selling you cheaper images, they are selling three other things. Resolution is the first and the only hard capability difference: Free and Starter cap at 2K, Pro and Enterprise go up to 4K. If you need print or zoomable detail, that decision is made for you at $49. The second is custom presets and models, the AI-generated scene and mannequin setups you build yourself: 3 a month on Starter, 10 on Pro, unlimited on Enterprise. The third is the support line, from email to priority to dedicated. That makes the choice simpler than most: pick Starter if 2K is enough and you shoot in small batches, pick Pro the moment you need 4K or more than three custom setups a month, and only look at Enterprise if you are past 500 images a month or genuinely need unlimited custom models. Buying a higher tier for the image allowance alone gains you nothing. One thing worth knowing before you commit annually: the annual rate, advertised as two months free, is offered on Free, Starter and Pro only. Enterprise is monthly billing, so the tier with the largest bill is also the one with no yearly discount. And the free tier is a demo rather than a plan — 3 images, once.

How to actually use Modelize

Spend the three free images on your hardest product rather than your easiest one: a plain packshot will look fine from almost any tool, and what you actually need to know is whether the model shots hold up on your fabric, your finish and your worst source photo. If they do, start on Starter and watch the resolution ceiling rather than the image count — 2K is fine on a product grid and thin the moment someone zooms, and that single limit is what pushes most stores to Pro. Build your custom presets early and reuse them, because consistency across a catalogue is the whole point and three new presets a month is enough only if you are not restarting every week. Keep the originals of everything you feed in. And before the catalogue goes live, look at the images as a set rather than one at a time; AI photography fails at the level of a page full of near-identical scenes, not at the level of a single shot.
