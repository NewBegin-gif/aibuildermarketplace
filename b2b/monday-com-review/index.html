What monday.com does

Work management platform with boards, automations and views — plus separately-priced CRM, Dev and Service products.

Pricing (2026)

(2026-08-01, read on their own pricing page) Work Management: Free €0 (max 2 seats), Basic €9, Standard €12, Pro €19 per seat/month; Enterprise quote-only. Automations and integrations are capped per tier: 250 actions/month on Standard, 25,000 on Pro, 250,000 on Enterprise. API calls 1,000/day on Standard, 10,000 on Pro. monday CRM is a separate subscription (Basic €12 annual / €18 monthly, Standard €17/€25, Pro €28/€41, minimum 3 seats), and monday Dev and monday Service are separate again (Service starts at €31/seat/month). Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The seat price is not the product price. monday sells four products — Work Management, CRM, Dev and Service — each on its own subscription, and the €9 you see first is the cheapest tier of one of them. A sales team that wants the CRM pays €12 to €28 a seat on top of whatever Work Management costs, with a three-seat minimum of its own. The second number to find before you commit is the automation cap. Standard allows 250 automation actions a month across the whole account; if you are buying monday because of the automations you saw demoed, 250 is roughly one workflow running a few times a day, and the next step up is Pro at more than double the seat price. Free is capped at two seats and three boards, which makes it a preview rather than a plan.

What buyers report about monday.com

Recurring themes from Capterra, G2 and pricing analyses, read in August 2026. We have not run monday.com ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Boards that non-technical teams actually adopt, and the visual layer is genuinely good — that is why it spreads inside companies.
  • • The automation and integration library covers most of what a growing team needs without a developer.
What they complain about
  • • Seats are sold in buckets, and it is the single most-criticised thing about the pricing. Minimum three seats, then multiples of five: a team of six pays for ten, a team of sixteen pays for twenty. A solo user pays for three.
  • • The features most teams need are gated upward. Automations sit on Standard, time tracking and private boards on Pro — which makes the real entry price closer to $57/month (three Pro seats) than the $27 the headline suggests.
  • • Monthly billing costs 18-30% more than annual.
  • • Complex automations, newer board views and reporting are hard or impossible on mobile.
  • • The free plan is 2 seats and 3 boards with no automations.
Best for: teams who want one visual system for projects and work, and who will actually use the automations and views that justify the per-seat price at Standard or above.
Who should skip it: if you are two or three people who need a shared list — the free tier caps at 2 seats and 3 boards, and paying per seat for views you will not use is the most common way this gets expensive; also skip it if you only want the CRM, since that is a separate subscription and worth pricing against dedicated CRMs.

Frequently asked questions

Is monday.com secure and reliable enough to depend on?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.

Who should not use monday.com?

Skip it if you are two or three people who need a shared list — the free tier caps at 2 seats and 3 boards, and paying per seat for views you will not use is the most common way this gets expensive; also skip it if you only want the CRM, since that is a separate subscription and worth pricing against dedicated CRMs. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
