Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 6 Sep 2026

A 30-day free trial with no credit card. Pro is priced by list size across 21 steps from 500 to 1,000,000 contacts, quoted monthly, biannually (sold as 15% off) or annually (sold as 20% off). At 500 contacts it is $9 monthly, $8 biannual and $7 annual. The ladder is not monotonic per contact: paid annually, 8,000 contacts is $51 a month against $70 at 10,000, 75,000 is $333 against $499 at 100,000, and 500,000 is $2,317 against $2,829 at 600,000 — three steps up the slider that cost more per head than the step below them.

The discounts round to whole dollars and so land short at small sizes: at 1,000 contacts $16 monthly becomes $14 biannual (12.5% off) and $13 annual (18.75% off). Instead of a subscription you can buy email credits outright with no expiry: 350,000 for $350, 500,000 for $500, 750,000 for $750 and 1,000,000 for $1,000 — a flat $0.001 per email with no volume discount.

Above Pro the page shows Moosend+ (a custom plan assembled from add-ons: dedicated IPs, transactional emails, audience discovery, custom dashboards, hosted files, extra team members, SSO and SAML) and Enterprise, both quote-only. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

When the paid plan is worth it

The $7-to-$9 headline is real but it is the price at 500 contacts, the smallest of 21 steps on a slider that runs to a million. The ladder is worth reading step by step, because it does not fall smoothly. Paid annually, 8,000 contacts costs $51 a month and 10,000 costs $70 — 37% more money for 25% more list, and the worst value on the slider below 25,000. The same reversal happens twice more: 75,000 costs $333 where 100,000 costs $499, and 500,000 costs $2,317 where 600,000 costs $2,829.

Per thousand contacts, 8,000, 75,000 and 500,000 are the cheap steps, and the rung above each is dearer per head rather than cheaper. The advertised discounts also round against you at the bottom. Annual billing is sold as 20% off and biannual as 15%; at 1,000 contacts the real figures are 18.75% and 12.5%, because every amount rounds to whole dollars. What none of it buys is transactional email, SSO and SAML, a dedicated IP, hosted files or an account manager.

Those sit in Moosend+ or Enterprise and are quoted separately, so a team needing transactional sending alongside marketing is not buying the plan it thinks it is.

How to actually use Moosend

Price at your own list size and then check the step above and below it, because the ladder reverses in three places. Paid annually, 8,000 contacts is $51 a month and 10,000 is $70, so a list sitting at 9,000 is on the wrong side of the sharpest jump on the slider and trimming it under 8,000 saves $19 a month. The same trap sits at 100,000 and again at 600,000.

Then decide subscription against credits, a genuinely different meter. The packs are 350,000 emails for $350, 500,000 for $500, 750,000 for $750 and a million for $1,000 — a flat tenth of a cent per email, no volume discount at any size, and no expiry date. If you send hard for three months a year that beats paying for list size twelve months a year. Read the comparison table before assuming Pro covers you.

Frequently asked questions

What does Moosend cost?

At 500 contacts, $9 a month billed monthly, $8 biannually or $7 annually, rising with list size across 21 steps to a million contacts. Biannual billing is sold as 15% off and annual as 20%, though at small list sizes the rounding to whole dollars makes it less: at 1,000 contacts it is 12.5% and 18.75%. There is a 30-day trial with no card. Read on 6 September 2026.

Does the price per contact keep falling as my list grows?

Not everywhere. Paid annually, 8,000 contacts costs $51 a month and 10,000 costs $70 — 37% more for 25% more list. The same reversal happens at 100,000 (against 75,000) and at 600,000 (against 500,000). If your list sits just above one of those thresholds, trimming it back is worth real money.

What is the credit option for?

Senders whose volume is lumpy. Instead of a monthly subscription you buy email credits outright and they do not expire: 350,000 emails for $350, 500,000 for $500, 750,000 for $750 and a million for $1,000. That is a flat tenth of a cent per email with no volume discount at any size, so the packs are worth it for the timing rather than the rate — if you send three big campaigns a year rather than a weekly newsletter, you stop paying for list size in the months you send nothing.
