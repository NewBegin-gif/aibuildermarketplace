Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 4 Oct 2026

(2026-08-30 and 2026-10-04, read on their own pricing page) No prices are shown. gomotive.com/pricing resolves to a lead-capture form at /get-in-touch/?id=pricing — a four-step email capture before any figure appears, preceded by a geolocation prompt offering the UK site. No per-vehicle rate, no plan names with amounts, no contract length and no hardware cost is published on the pricing page, the Pro plan page or the products overview. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

The honest knock

That is the finding, and it matters more in fleet than in software generally. The real cost here is not a monthly rate: it is hardware per vehicle, a contract term that is usually multi-year, and an early-termination position you will not hear about until you want out. None of that is public. Before the demo call, decide your own numbers: vehicles, contract length you will accept, and what you currently pay per truck.

In a quote-only category the first number you hear is anchored to what they think you can pay, not to a rate card.

When the paid plan is worth it

Motive's pricing page publishes no rate card at all: gomotive.com/pricing resolves to a contact form at /get-in-touch/?id=pricing, a four-step email capture. The only plan either that page or the Pro page names is Pro. The Pro page lists six feature cards. There is automated IFTA reporting, Driver Safety with coaching for high-risk behaviours such as speeding, and 500 MB of free in-vehicle internet per month through an in-cab WiFi hotspot.

The rest are engine fault-code alerts to catch maintenance issues early, fuel-usage and idling tracking across the fleet, and App Marketplace integrations. Motive presents only one of the six as automation: IFTA reports exported with one click. What no upgrade settles is the compliance set: ELD, HOS alerts, electronic DVIRs and CSA score management sit on a product page that names no plan and no price, so nothing in the read pages ties those features to Pro.

Neither the pricing page, the Pro page nor the products overview names a hardware cost per vehicle, a contract length or renewal terms.

How to actually use Motive

There is no self-serve path with a published price: the pricing page is a four-step email capture. Motive presents the Vehicle Gateway as routing location, utilisation and health data in real time, with ELD capabilities. A developer profile at developer.gomotive.com takes Get Started, then Sign Up with full name, email, password, company and the terms checkbox. In the Motive Dashboard you open the admin view, scroll to Developers and click +Request API Key, then send the key as an X-API-Key header.

On that same Developers page a TEST MODE checkbox puts a key in Test Mode, which the docs say "will not make changes to the actual or live data". The docs name further constraints. API requests are performed on behalf of the first active admin of the fleet unless another fleet admin's user ID is included, and provisioning a separate system user means contacting support. Pagination defaults to 25 results and caps at 100. And 429 is absent from the seven documented response codes.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Motive cost per vehicle?

No figure is published on anything we can read. Re-checked on 16 September and again on 4 October 2026: gomotive.com/pricing still resolves to a four-step email capture at /get-in-touch/?id=pricing, preceded by a geolocation prompt offering the UK site, and it prints no per-vehicle rate, no plan amount, no hardware cost and no contract length. The Pro plan page names features without money attached. Even gomotive.com/llms.txt — the file vendors increasingly use to publish plain rates for machines — carries products, customer stories and outcome claims but not one price. Treat any per-vehicle number you find elsewhere as someone else's quote, not a rate card.

What evidence is there that the safety claims hold?

Motive's own, which is worth knowing before you repeat the numbers in a business case. Its llms.txt, read 16 September and again on 4 October 2026, states that some customers cut collisions by around 80% in the first year of AI Dashcam deployment 'based on internal studies', that accident-related costs fell about 63% according to its own 2026 customer ROI study, and that insurance costs dropped around 25%. Those are the vendor measuring the vendor; no independent study is cited on the pages we read. The claims may well be sound, but ask for the sample, the period and the comparison group before treating them as your expected outcome.

What do I have to get in writing before signing?

Three things the published pages leave open. First the compliance set — ELD, HOS alerts, electronic DVIRs and CSA score management — sits on a product page that names no plan and no price, so nothing readable ties it to the Pro plan that is the only plan Motive names. Second, hardware: the Vehicle Gateway and the dash cams carry no published cost, and it is not stated whether they are bought, leased or bundled. Third, term: no contract length or cancellation condition appears anywhere on the pricing route. Ask for the per-vehicle rate, the hardware line and the term as separate figures, because the quote is the only place any of them exists.
