What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

RT: Background Music Player, from RoarTheme, does the core job — upload a media file, it plays in the background — and the listing prices it at Free, full stop. It sits at 4.8 stars over 72 reviews against this app's 4.8 over 66, and it has been on the store since 5 February 2021. What the $2.99 buys over free is real but narrow: control over the player's colour, size, position, volume, loop and autoplay, sources beyond an uploaded file, and a set of AI-generated royalty-free seasonal tracks.

If all you want is one MP3 looping on the homepage, you are paying for controls you will set once and never open again. Two smaller things worth knowing before you install. The "Free plan available" badge on the listing is for development and partner stores only — on a live store there is no free tier, so read that badge as a 7-day trial. And the pitch that background music "can enhance your interaction with customers and improve conversion rate & sales" arrives with no evidence: no study, no store data, no test.

Autoplay is not entirely yours to command either, because browsers block audio that starts without a user gesture, so the autoplay switch takes effect once the shopper has interacted with the page rather than on load. At $2.99 the cost of finding out whether sound helps your store is small — but find out, rather than assume.

When the paid plan is worth it

The free plan is not a plan, it is a sandbox. Development Stores is free with every feature switched on, but the listing restricts it to development and partner stores and says payment is activated on upgrade. A live storefront runs on the Monthly Plan at $2.99, or $25.12 a year paid up front, which the listing labels a 30% saving. Nothing sits above it — no traffic tier, no play-count tier — so a shop with ten visitors a day and one with ten thousand pay the same.

What the $2.99 does not buy is the feature the plan advertises. Autoplay is sold as a paid control, but the developer's own help centre says Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari allow audio only after the visitor clicks, taps or scrolls, that no app can work around that, and that the remedy is to prompt visitors into interacting first. Paying does not license your music either: the vendor's upload guide makes the rights your responsibility.

And it does not buy evidence for the sales claim in the pitch, because the listing offers none.

How to actually use MX AI

The developer's own guide is blunter about the workflow than the listing is. To use a song from YouTube you open the video, copy the URL, run it through a third-party MP3 converter that the help centre links to while stating the tool is not affiliated with them, download the file, then open the app dashboard in your Shopify admin, upload the MP3 and save. The same guide opens by telling you that securing the rights and permissions for the song is your job.

An uploaded file is one route; the listing also accepts Spotify, SoundCloud, Apple Music, Tidal, Amazon Music and YouTube Music formats. After saving, the player is yours to style: colour, size, position, volume, loop and autoplay. If you cannot find it on the storefront afterwards, colour is the likeliest reason — one merchant on the listing needed support to recolour an icon that had blended into their background. Plan on prompting visitors to click before the music starts.
