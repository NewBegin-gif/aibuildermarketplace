What NiceJob does

Reputation marketing on autopilot. Automatically ask happy customers for reviews, showcase them on your site, and turn word-of-mouth into a steady stream of leads.

Pricing (2026)

Reviews plan about $75/mo and Pro around $125/mo (adds referrals, AI replies, booking reminders), both with a 14-day trial and a one-off setup fee near $199 (2026). No contracts. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

Correction after re-checking: The $199 setup fee applies only to the website plans: Sites at $99/month plus $199 setup, and Grow + Sites at $174/month plus $199 setup. The software-only plans carry no setup fee at all — Reviews is $75/month and Pro is $125/month, both with a 14-day free trial and no contracts. (re-verified 2026-08-07)

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Reporting and customization are fairly basic and there's a setup fee on top of the monthly — it automates review collection genuinely well, but if you want deep analytics or granular control over timing and wording, it can feel restrictive. And the honest fundamental: automation only amplifies the service you actually deliver. It keeps the review stream fresh and steady (which matters — stale reviews lose trust fast, and AI search increasingly recommends businesses on review signals), but it cannot manufacture a reputation you haven't earned.

What buyers report about NiceJob

Recurring themes from G2, Capterra, GetApp and Trustpilot, read in August 2026. We have not run NiceJob ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Review collection genuinely runs itself, and the social-proof widgets do their job. That is what customers on G2 and Capterra rate.
  • • $75/month for Reviews, $125 for Pro, both with a 14-day trial and no contract.
What they complain about
  • • The Trustpilot complaints are about sales conduct, not the product: multiple people who were never customers report daily cold calls, being told they were added to a Do Not Call list, and then being called again the next day.
  • • Reviewers report Google reviews generated through NiceJob disappearing within 24 hours — worth verifying yourself before you judge it working.
  • • Unauthorised charges after what users believed was a cancellation, and login problems persisting for years with unresponsive support.
  • • Request templates barely customise, automation has no conditional logic, and reporting is basic.

Where the ratings disagree. The software-directory ratings and the Trustpilot ratings describe two different companies. The directories rate the product; Trustpilot captures the sales calls and the billing. Both are worth reading before you sign.

When the paid plan is worth it

The jump from Reviews ($75/month) to Pro ($125/month) buys automated referral campaigns, automated (rather than manually-approved) AI review replies, repeat-booking reminders, and competitor SEO insights, not more customization of your own review requests, which stays basic on both tiers. That price gap pays for itself fastest in businesses with real repeat-visit or referral potential: HVAC, cleaning, landscaping, anywhere a happy customer plausibly comes back or refers a neighbor. For one-and-done work, like a single roof replacement or a one-time remodel, the Pro-only referral and repeat-reminder tools have less to work with, and Reviews alone already includes AI-generated reply drafts and covers the core job of asking for and displaying reviews. Pro does add competitor SEO insights and topic tracking, but that is competitive intelligence about rivals, not deeper reporting on your own campaigns. Separately, a one-time setup fee shows up only if you bundle NiceJob's Sites website builder onto either plan, it is not charged on the standalone Reviews or Pro software subscriptions themselves, and the pricing page lists no contracts on either of those two plans.

How to actually use NiceJob

Signing up starts a 14-day free trial billed with no contracts. The real setup work is getting your customers into the system: either connect an existing business tool, NiceJob lists native integrations such as HubSpot, or upload a spreadsheet of your customer list. NiceJob's help center specifies that spreadsheet needs at minimum a name and email per person, ideally a mobile number too, saved as a CSV with column headers in the first row. Once contacts are loaded, NiceJob starts automating review-request sends and displaying the reviews that come back. If the CSV import does not cooperate, support says you can email the file to their team and they will add the contacts for you. In practice, users report friction in two spots: CSV-only bulk import makes it awkward to add a single new customer later without a re-upload, and multi-location accounts report that switching between locations feels clunky, with the same review sometimes duplicating across locations. Best for: local and service businesses that want reputation marketing on offense — automatically asking happy customers for reviews and showcasing them — rather than only managing damage when a bad review lands.
Who should skip it: if you need deep reporting or highly customized review workflows, or your service quality isn't ready for more visibility yet — fix that first, then amplify it.

The natural comparison is Birdeye or Podium — heavier, pricier reputation platforms — NiceJob wins on set-and-forget simplicity for small service businesses. Decide by which one fits the job above, not by the louder brand.

Frequently asked questions

Is NiceJob worth it for a small team?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Small teams get the most out of this category when one clear problem is already costing real hours or revenue; buying ahead of that just adds cost and another login. Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.

Who should not use NiceJob?

Skip it if you need deep reporting or highly customized review workflows, or your service quality isn't ready for more visibility yet — fix that first, then amplify it. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
