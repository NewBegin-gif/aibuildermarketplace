Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 18 Sep 2026

Eight products, each priced separately, and unusually for this category every one of them carries a published figure. US Payroll Standard is $100 a month as a platform fee plus $20 per employee a month, covering multi-state payroll with W-2s and 1099s, unlimited pay schedules and payroll runs, PTO and leave policies, workforce analytics, same-day deposits, full benefits administration with 401(k) and brokered health, dental and vision, and CRM and ATS integrations; US Payroll Enterprise is custom.

On the PEO side, PEO Basic starts at $59 per employee a month without benefits, and PEO Plus at $120 with them — adding benefits enrolment, life and disability cover, 401(k), commuter benefits, medical, dental and vision, and HSA, FSA, COBRA and HRA accounts, with terms noted for groups under five employees. Internationally: Contractor Management is $49 per contractor a month, Contractor of Record (branded Niural Armor) is $299 per employee a month, Employer of Record starts at $299 per employee a month across 150-plus countries, and Global Payroll for entities you already own starts at $29 per employee a month.

One caveat about reading this page: it asks your company size first (under 25, 25 to 50, 50 to 100, or more than 100) and adjusts what it shows, so the figures above are the under-25 view and a re-read should select the same step. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

The honest knock

The prices are published — which in payroll is genuinely rare and worth crediting — but there is no self-serve sign-up anywhere on the page, so the published figure is the opening of a conversation rather than something you can act on. Expect the real number to arrive by quote, and expect it to depend on the company-size step you selected before you ever saw a price. The page changes under you, and that is easy to miss.

It asks your headcount band first and adjusts the ladder to match, so two people comparing Niural can honestly quote each other different numbers. Note which band you read when you write the figure down. The platform fee is the part that decides whether this is cheap. US Payroll is $100 a month before anyone is paid, plus $20 a head. At five employees that is $200 a month, or $40 an employee — double the headline rate.

At twenty-five it is $600, or $24 each. At a hundred it is $2,100, or $21 each. The per-employee price you are quoted only becomes the price you pay somewhere north of fifty people, and small teams are the ones most likely to read $20 and budget $20. The naming needs care too. Contractor of Record and Employer of Record are both $299 per employee a month and they are not the same product: the first keeps the worker a contractor while Niural carries the misclassification risk, the second makes Niural the legal employer in 150-plus countries.

Buying the wrong one is an expensive mistake that the identical price does nothing to prevent.

The $100 platform fee is the whole small-team question

There is no upgrade ladder here in the usual sense — there are eight separate products — so the decision is which relationship you need and, on the payroll side, whether your headcount is large enough for the pricing to behave. US Payroll Standard is $100 a month plus $20 per employee. Run that at different sizes and the effective per-head rate moves a long way: five employees is $200 a month, which is $40 each; ten is $300, or $30 each; twenty-five is $600, or $24 each; fifty is $1,100, or $22 each; a hundred is $2,100, or $21 each.

The quoted $20 is an asymptote you approach rather than a price you pay, and the smaller you are the less the headline means. The international products price per person with no platform fee, which makes them easier to compare and easier to budget. Global Payroll at $29 per employee a month is the cheapest thing on the page and the narrowest — it runs payroll through entities you already own, so it solves nothing about where you can legally hire.

Contractor Management at $49 per contractor buys contracts, payments in 150-plus countries, invoicing, tax forms and misclassification alerts, but leaves the classification risk with you. Contractor of Record at $299 moves that risk to Niural while keeping the worker a contractor. Employer of Record, also from $299, makes Niural the legal employer. That last pair is where the money and the mistake both live. Two products, the same published price, and completely different legal positions.

Decide which relationship you actually need before you compare vendors on price, because the cheapest quote for the wrong structure is not a saving.

How to actually use Niural AI

Pick the employment relationship before you pick the vendor. Niural sells eight products and four of them look interchangeable from a price list: Global Payroll at $29 runs payroll through entities you already own; Contractor Management at $49 handles contracts and cross-border payments but leaves classification risk with you; Contractor of Record at $299 keeps the worker a contractor and moves that risk to Niural; Employer of Record, also from $299, makes Niural the legal employer in 150-plus countries.

The two at $299 are not variants of each other. Write down which one your situation requires before you take a call, because the identical price will not stop you buying the wrong one. On the US side, do the platform-fee arithmetic at your own headcount. $100 a month plus $20 an employee is $40 each at five people and $21 each at a hundred. If you are small, compare the total rather than the per-head figure.

Note your company-size band when you read the page. It asks for it before showing anything and adjusts the ladder accordingly, so a figure quoted without the band attached is not reproducible — and a re-read weeks later should select the same step or the comparison is meaningless. Expect a demo either way. Every tier, including the cheapest, has Book a demo as its only call to action; there is no published self-serve route.

Go in with your headcount, the states or countries involved, and the specific product name, and ask for the quote in writing against that product. If benefits are the point, read the PEO split carefully: PEO Basic from $59 is explicitly without benefits, and PEO Plus from $120 is the one carrying medical, dental, vision, 401(k), life and disability and the HSA, FSA, COBRA and HRA accounts — with terms the page flags for groups under five employees.

Frequently asked questions

What does Niural cost?

Eight products, priced separately, every one with a figure attached — which in payroll is rare enough to be worth crediting. On the US side there is a platform fee plus a per-head rate for payroll, and two PEO bands that split on whether benefits are included. Everything international is per person with no platform fee, and runs from $29 a month at the narrow end, where Niural only processes payroll through entities you already own, up to $299 at the wide end, where it becomes the legal employer in the country concerned. The full ladder with every figure is in the pricing section above, read on niural.com/pricing on 18 September 2026. What the list does not include is a way to buy: every tier ends in Book a demo.

What will US payroll actually cost at my headcount?

More per head than the $20 suggests, until you are fairly large, because of the $100 monthly platform fee. Five employees is $200 a month, which is $40 each. Ten is $300, or $30 each. Twenty-five is $600, or $24 each. Fifty is $1,100, or $22 each. A hundred is $2,100, or $21 each. The quoted per-employee rate is a figure you approach rather than one you pay, and small teams are the ones most likely to budget $20 and be charged double.

What is the difference between Contractor of Record and Employer of Record?

They cost the same — $299 per employee a month — and they are not the same thing. Contractor of Record keeps the worker a contractor and moves the misclassification liability to Niural. Employer of Record makes Niural the legal employer in the country concerned, with local contracts, payroll, benefits and termination support. Decide which relationship your situation actually requires before you compare vendors, because the identical price will not stop you buying the wrong structure.

Can I sign up without talking to sales?

No. Every tier on the pricing page, including the $29 Global Payroll one, has Book a demo as its only call to action, and no self-serve route is published anywhere. One more thing to note when you compare quotes: the page asks your company size first — under 25, 25 to 50, 50 to 100, or more than 100 — and adjusts the ladder to match, so a figure is only reproducible if you record which band you were shown.

What should I weigh before buying HR & People software?

Compliance is country by country, so the honest answer is: only where the vendor says it is, and only for the entity type you actually have. Ask which countries are covered by its own entities versus a local partner, who carries the liability if something is filed wrong, and how changes in local law reach you. Get those answers in the contract rather than the sales deck.
