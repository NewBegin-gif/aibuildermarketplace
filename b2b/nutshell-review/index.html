Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 5 Sep 2026

Five CRM tiers on annual billing: Foundation $13, Growth $25, Pro $42, Business $59 and Enterprise $79 per user a month, with monthly billing about 15% higher (the page states the saving per tier as $6 to $10 a month per user). Unlimited contacts and full API access come with every tier, which is the unusual part — most CRMs ration one or both.

Frequently asked questions

What does Nutshell cost per user?

On annual billing, $13, $25, $42, $59 and $79 per user a month across the five tiers, with monthly billing roughly 15% higher. Every tier includes unlimited contacts and full API access. Read on 17 August 2026.

What are the add-ons?

Two: Marketing from $49 a month and Engagement from $16 per user a month, both bought on top of a CRM tier. If email marketing is part of why you are looking at a CRM, price the Marketing extension in from the start rather than comparing bare seat prices.

Which tier is the honest starting point?

Foundation at $13 covers contact and pipeline management with the unlimited contacts and API that make it usable. The tiers above buy reporting depth and automation rather than capacity. Start low and move up on a specific missing report, not on the tier names.

Do Nutshell's own growth numbers mean anything?

They publish three of them on nutshell.com/customers, read 15 September 2026: 14.9% faster close times, 13.4% more leads won and 26.4% more new sales revenue. The method sits right beside them, and it is the part worth reading. Nutshell compared each customer's first year against their second, across customers active between 2011 and 2023, then stripped outliers at both ends and averaged what was left. So these are the vendor's own figures about businesses that stayed at least two years, which measures the customers who kept paying rather than the average buyer, and nothing in the method separates the CRM from everything else those companies changed in that second year. Take it as a direction of travel, not as a forecast for your pipeline.

What should I weigh before buying Growth & Revenue software?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Small teams get the most out of this category when one clear problem is already costing real hours or revenue; buying ahead of that just adds cost and another login. Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.
