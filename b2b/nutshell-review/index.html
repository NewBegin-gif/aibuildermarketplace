When the paid plan is worth it

The seat ladder is conventional — Foundation $13, Growth $25, Pro $42, Business $59 and Enterprise $79 per user a month on annual billing, with monthly running about 15% higher — but two things about it are unusual and both work in your favour. Unlimited contacts and full API access come with EVERY tier. Most CRMs ration one or both, and rationing either is how a cheap CRM becomes an expensive one, so integrations are genuinely not the trap here.

The add-ons are. Marketing starts at $49 a month and Engagement at $16 per user a month, and that second one changes the arithmetic most at the bottom of the ladder: $16 on top of Foundation's $13 is $29 a seat, more than double. On Enterprise the same $16 is a 20% increase. So the cheaper your CRM tier, the more the add-on distorts it — and a team that needs Engagement should compare Foundation-plus-Engagement against Growth or Pro on features rather than assuming the cheapest seat stays cheapest.

The marketing side has a second meter underneath the sticker: email and SMS are billed by contact count and message volume. That means the marketing spend scales with your list while the CRM scales with your headcount, and for a small sales team with a large list the marketing side can quietly outgrow the CRM itself. Model those separately; they do not move together.

Two capabilities to check against your requirements before picking a tier, because they sit at the top and nowhere else: SSO and SQL data access are Enterprise-only. If IT will require single sign-on, your tier is decided at $79 regardless of what the sales team needs.

How to actually use Nutshell

Budget the extensions rather than the seats, because that is where this one grows. Marketing starts at $49 a month and Engagement at $16 per user a month, both on top of the seat price, and email and SMS are billed by contact count and message volume — so the marketing side can quietly outgrow the CRM it was bolted onto. Price the whole thing at your list size before you compare Nutshell with anything.

Then take advantage of the unusual part while you evaluate: unlimited contacts and full API access come with every tier, including Foundation at $13. Most CRMs ration one or both, so integrations are not the cost trap here and you do not need to climb the ladder to connect your stack. Build the integrations you need on the cheapest tier and let the seat features decide the upgrade.

Check two Enterprise-only requirements early if they apply to you: SSO and SQL data access. Those are exactly the kind of thing that arrives from IT or a security review after the CRM is already chosen, and here they mean the $79 tier rather than a setting.

Frequently asked questions

What does Nutshell cost per user?

On annual billing, $13, $25, $42, $59 and $79 per user a month across the five tiers, with monthly billing roughly 15% higher. Every tier includes unlimited contacts and full API access. Read on 17 August 2026.

What are the add-ons?

Two: Marketing from $49 a month and Engagement from $16 per user a month, both bought on top of a CRM tier. If email marketing is part of why you are looking at a CRM, price the Marketing extension in from the start rather than comparing bare seat prices.

Which tier is the honest starting point?

Foundation at $13 covers contact and pipeline management with the unlimited contacts and API that make it usable. The tiers above buy reporting depth and automation rather than capacity. Start low and move up on a specific missing report, not on the tier names.

Is Nutshell worth it for a small team?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Small teams get the most out of this category when one clear problem is already costing real hours or revenue; buying ahead of that just adds cost and another login. Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.

Who should not use Nutshell?

Skip it if you need the marketing suite at scale — Marketing, Engagement, email and SMS are separate add-ons priced by volume, so price those before the seats. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
