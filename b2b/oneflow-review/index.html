Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 18 Sep 2026

Business is €50 per user per month, billed annually, for teams starting at five users, so €250 a month is the floor. Oneflow now states that floor itself rather than leaving it to be multiplied out: the page says the entry-level plan includes 5 seats and starts at €250 per month, billed annually. That is the whole public price list. Enterprise starts at ten users and goes through a sales call, and the trial runs 14 days without a card.

A free contract-template library is available without an account. Annual is not a discount option, it is the only option, and the page now says so in its own FAQ under “Why can you only pay on an annual basis?” — the answer cites long-term value, secure deployments and dedicated onboarding. Two features sit behind a ($) marker even on the plan that lists them, meaning a paid add-on rather than an unavailable feature: eID signature on Business and redirect-after-sign on Enterprise. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

Oneflow’s pricing page, as we read it

What Oneflow does

Digital contract platform for the full lifecycle — create, negotiate, e-sign and archive interactive contracts, with AI-assisted contract features and CRM/HR/ATS integrations (HubSpot, Salesforce), sold per user on annual billing.

What buyers report about Oneflow

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Oneflow ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • User-based tiered pricing instead of pay-per-envelope. For anyone who has been surprised by an envelope bill, that predictability is the reason to look. $17 to $40 per user per month.
  • • Quick to learn, and reviewers say the real-time collaboration on a contract in progress is what they would miss.
What they complain about
  • • Notifications and signature placement both draw complaints, as does user management.
  • • Little help with migrating existing contracts — plan that work yourself.
  • • Billing questions are reported as slow to resolve.
  • • Customisation runs out if your workflow is unusual.

When the paid plan is worth it

Oneflow's paid tier starts at €50/user/month billed annually with a 5-user minimum, so the entry cost floor is €250/month; there is no permanent free plan, only a 14-day trial with full platform access and no credit card required. Teams that only need core contract creation, e-signature, and lifecycle tracking — without AI-assisted contract review or single sign-on — can run through the trial and then stay on this Business plan without hitting real limits.

The push toward Enterprise (custom-quoted, 10-user minimum) is narrower than it looks. Account audit logging is already on Business, and single sign-on, SCIM, custom roles, user groups, shared templates and section rules all carry the paid add-on marker there rather than being withheld, buyable without changing plan. Only three rows are blank for Business: AI Review, AI Insights (both Beta) and white labelling. What upgrading does not solve is that add-on column.

eID signature, SMS signature, custom email domain, batch sending, limited users and organisational units are paid extras on Enterprise as on Business. So heavy eID users keep paying extra regardless of plan, and a custom quote does not fold those meters in.

How to actually use Oneflow

Getting real value starts with creating a trial account, then generating an API token from the Marketplace inside the app; that token goes in the x-oneflow-api-token header on every request, alongside an x-oneflow-user-email header identifying the acting user. Before sending anything through the API, a template has to already exist inside the target workspace. Contract-creation calls need both a workspace_id and a template_id. The documented failure mode is a 404 stating the template must be in the target workspace — which is what teams hit when templates were built in one workspace and the API call points at a different workspace_id.

For no-code setups, third-party integrations such as Salesforce, Zapier, and Power Automate ask for that same API token plus the account email during configuration. In practice the blocker is workspace/template alignment, not account signup.

Frequently asked questions

What is the real entry price?

€250 a month, billed annually — €3,000 committed before you send a contract. The rate is €50 per user per month and the Business plan starts at five users, and on 18 September 2026 oneflow.com/pricing/ states the floor directly: the entry-level plan includes 5 seats and starts at €250 per month, billed annually. Enterprise begins at ten users and is quote-only. If you read the €50 and stopped there, the real number is five times what you were budgeting.

Can I pay monthly, or start smaller than five seats?

Neither, and Oneflow is unusually direct about the first one. Its pricing FAQ carries the question “Why can you only pay on an annual basis?” and answers it with long-term value, secure deployments and dedicated onboarding rather than offering an alternative (read 18 September 2026). The seat floor is stated the same way: Business starts at five users, Enterprise at ten. A 14-day trial with no credit card is the only way to use the platform without the annual commitment.

Is there a free plan?

The page contradicts itself here, and it is worth getting the answer in writing before you plan around it. The plan cards on oneflow.com/pricing/ show exactly three things on 18 September 2026 — a 14-day trial, Business and Enterprise — and no free tier. The FAQ on the same page asks “Does Oneflow offer a freemium plan?” and answers “Yes, Oneflow offers a freemium plan”, then talks only about starting with a trial. What is unambiguously free is the contract-template library, which needs no account.

What should I weigh before buying Sales & CRM software?

Most tools in this category plug in rather than replace. Check which direction the data flows and whether the sync is genuinely two-way, because a one-way push creates a second source of truth that your reps will quietly stop trusting. If it does aim to replace your CRM, price the migration honestly: history, custom fields and reporting rarely move across cleanly.
