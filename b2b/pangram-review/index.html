We tested it · 2026-10-01

A new free account, with no payment method asked for. We ran nine texts whose authorship we knew before we started: three passages from public-domain books (Darwin and William James), a paragraph one of us typed for the test, typos included, three paragraphs a language model wrote for our own reviews, one paragraph Claude wrote on the spot, and one of those machine paragraphs with three of its five sentences rewritten by Claude. We read the credit meter after every check and ran it into the daily limit.

  • Nine for nine. All four human texts came back 'Human Written', including the 71-word paragraph typed for the test, and all five machine texts came back 'AI Generated', including the paragraph Claude wrote on the spot. Every result put the whole text on one side: the gauge read 100% each time. That figure is the share of the text it assigns to each side, not a confidence score.
  • A machine rewrite did not shake it. With three of its five sentences rewritten by a second model, the paragraph from our Hubstaff review still scored 100% AI. That measures a model paraphrasing a model; we did not test text a person had edited by hand.
  • Later the same day we gave it a tenth text: a deliberately clichéd AI paragraph after QuillBot's AI Humanizer had rewritten it (the Humanizer reported '0 AI signals'). Pangram still scored it 100% AI and added a note that the text 'appears to have been paraphrased or rewritten', pointing to one rewritten segment. Our own Claude rewrite of a review paragraph, earlier in the test, did not get that note.

Frequently asked questions

What does Pangram cost?

Free for up to 2,000 words a day. Individual is $20 a month, or $15 billed annually ($180 a year), for 300,000 words a month; Professional is $65 or $45 annually ($540) for 1.5 million words plus 500 image scans; Team is $20 per seat from two seats. Read from their own machine-readable plan list on 17 August 2026.

Is there a Pangram promo code?

We have found none, and it is one of the things people search for most about this tool. What exists instead is the annual discount, which is substantial: $15 against $20 on Individual and $45 against $65 on Professional, so a year up front saves 25% to 31%. There is also a free tier of 2,000 words a day, about ten checks of 150 words, that needs no payment method at all.

What does the API cost per document?

Watch the unit, because it changed with the model. Realtime checks on Pangram 4 are $0.05 per 100 words; the older Pangram 3 charged $0.05 per 1,000 words. Bulk document scans are 20% cheaper than realtime. Credit is prepaid, from $5 up to $2,000. A 1,000-word essay therefore costs about $0.50 on the current model, not $0.05: check which generation any quoted rate refers to.

Is there a free trial before I pay?

Yes on two of the three paid tiers, and it is worth noting which two. Individual offers a 7-day free trial and Team a 7-day team trial, read on their pricing page on 6 September 2026. Professional, the 1,500,000-word tier, does not: its button reads 'Upgrade to Professional', with '$65 billed now' underneath (1 October 2026). So the plan whose allowance you would most want to try before committing is the one without a trial. The free plan needs no payment method and covers 2,000 words a day, which is the way to calibrate first.

Does Pangram tell text a person polished with AI apart from text AI wrote from scratch?

Pangram says it tries to. Its homepage (pangram.com, read 2 November 2026) says it "will highlight segments that have been edited by AI rather than fully AI-generated". That describes the feature, not how accurate it is. Our own test above only ran machine rewrites through it, not text a person wrote and then edited with AI, so we have not checked that claim. If you plan to act on a flagged segment, for example in a student or freelancer dispute, treat the highlight as a reason to ask questions, not as proof.
