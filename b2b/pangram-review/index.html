What Pangram does

AI content detector. Identifies AI-generated text with a focus on minimizing false positives — used by educators, publishers and platforms to verify whether writing is human or machine, via web app and API. The vendor's Pangram 4.0 release (July 2026) claims a false-positive rate of 1 in 10,000, detection of output from the newest frontier models, resistance to "humanized" text written to evade detection, and improved boundary detection between human and AI passages. An early research preview adds AI image detection, flagging which segments of an image appear AI-generated and verifying C2PA content credentials where present.

What buyers report about Pangram

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Pangram ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • A 1-in-10,000 false positive rate independently verified by researchers at the University of Chicago and the University of Maryland — that verification is what separates it from every detector that only cites its own numbers.
  • • 99.8-100% accuracy on straightforwardly AI-generated text, with a free tier of 4 scans a day.
What they complain about
  • • On AI-edited text — a human draft polished by a model — published accuracy drops to 73%. That is the case most real writing now falls into, and it is where accusations go wrong.
  • • Users report false positives on older human-written work and on formal academic language, and reliability falls on short text (50 words minimum).
  • • Public sentiment reads worse than the metrics because the people who get falsely flagged say so loudly — which is fair, since being wrongly accused costs them more than a missed detection costs anyone.
  • • $20/month for 600 credits up to $65, and the credit system adds up if you also run plagiarism checks.

When the paid plan is worth it

The subscription ladder is straightforward. Free is $0 with no payment method, for up to 2,000 words a day — about twenty checks. Individual is $20 a month or $15 annually for up to 300,000 words a month. Professional is $65 or $45 annually for 1,500,000 words, plus 500 image scans and $200 of API usage. Team is $20 a seat ($15 annually) from two seats. Per thousand words that is about $0.05 on Individual and $0.03 on Professional — a real volume discount.

But the number that matters most is on the other side of the page. The Developer API is prepaid pay-as-you-go from $5 to $2,000, and realtime checks on Pangram 4 cost $0.05 per 100 words. That is $0.50 per thousand — ten times the Individual rate and seventeen times Professional's. Bulk document scans are 20% cheaper than realtime, which narrows it but does not close it. So the rule is simple and it is the opposite of what most tools suggest: if the volume can go through a subscription, put it there.

The API is for integration, not for saving money, and anyone routing steady volume through it is paying an order of magnitude more per word than the plan they already qualify for. One correction that applies to older write-ups, ours included: the billing unit changed with the model generation. Pangram 3 charged $0.05 per 1,000 words; Pangram 4 charges $0.05 per 100. Any estimate built on the older figure is off by a factor of ten.

And the limit no tier removes, which belongs in the buying decision rather than the footnotes: every AI detector is fallible. It will sometimes flag genuinely human writing and sometimes miss AI text. Pangram's pitch is fewer false positives than rivals and that is real progress, but fewer is not none — so use it as a signal, never as sole proof for an academic penalty or a hiring decision.

How to actually use Pangram

Calibrate on writing you already know the provenance of, using the free tier's 2,000 words a day. Run ten pieces you wrote yourself and ten you know were machine-generated, and record what it says about each. That gives you a false-positive rate on your own material and in your own subject area, which is the only number that should govern how you use it. Then decide the policy before the subscription, because this is the honest limit of every detector including the accurate ones: it will occasionally flag genuinely human writing and it will miss some AI text.

Never use a score as sole proof for a high-stakes call — an academic penalty, a hiring decision — and write down in advance what a flag actually triggers: a conversation, not a verdict. Size the plan on words a month rather than checks: Individual covers 300,000 words for $15 a month billed annually and Professional 1,500,000 for $45, which is $5.00 and $3.00 per hundred thousand words. Team is $15 a seat annually from two seats.

If you are wiring it into something, note that the billing unit changed with the model generation. Pangram 4 charges $0.05 per 100 words in realtime where Pangram 3 charged $0.05 per 1,000 — a tenfold difference that older write-ups, ours among them, still quote the old side of. Bulk document scans run 20% cheaper than realtime, so batch whatever you can.

The natural comparison is GPTZero or Originality.ai — close AI-detection peers — Pangram competes on lower false positives; compare accuracy claims and per-word credit cost for your volume.
