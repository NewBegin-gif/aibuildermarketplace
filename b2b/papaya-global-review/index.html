Which of my countries does Papaya run itself?

Ask that by name, country by country, before the rate comes up — it predicts your experience better than the price does. Outside the countries where Papaya owns entities, EOR is delivered through in-country partners; a third-party estimate puts the owned count near forty, and Papaya does not publish which countries are owned and which are partner-delivered. In a partner country you are one escalation removed from whoever actually runs your payroll, and the problems clients report follow from exactly that: invoicing errors, data that does not sync between Papaya and the partner, missed or late payroll runs, and support that decays from a named account manager into a rotating ticket queue. If your hiring map is one or two countries, get them named. If it is fifteen, the split is the deal.

What am I committing to if it does not work out?

A longer commitment than a monthly per-employee rate implies. Business.com documents a typical agreement of two years with a rolling 90-day termination-for-convenience clause, so your notice date is three months before the day you want to be gone, and the exit is a clause you exercise rather than a subscription you cancel. Put the signature date and the notice date in a calendar on the day you sign. Read the term and exit clauses before the rate, because you are committing for two years to a service whose price you cannot verify in advance: every figure on the pricing page is a starting-from under a demo booking, and two of the products carry no figure at all. The two questions belong together — what the fully landed cost is, and what it takes to leave.

What should I weigh before buying HR & People software?

Compliance is country by country, so the honest answer is: only where the vendor says it is, and only for the entity type you actually have. Ask which countries are covered by its own entities versus a local partner, who carries the liability if something is filed wrong, and how changes in local law reach you. Get those answers in the contract rather than the sales deck.
