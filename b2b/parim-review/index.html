Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 20 Aug 2026

(2026-08-15, read on their own pricing page) One number is published and the rest is not. The page says pricing starts from £47 per month with unlimited users on every plan, but each individual tier — Starter, Pro, Premium — carries a 'Request Pricing' button rather than a figure. Their own explanation: they price on the functionality you need and the number of shifts you schedule each month. So the £47 is a starting point, not a plan price. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

For a staffing agency or an events business, shift volume is the one number that swings hardest month to month, which makes this the least predictable pricing model in the category. Before you commit, ask for the price at your busiest month rather than your average, and ask what happens when you cross a threshold.

What Parim does

Workforce management for shift-heavy operations — staffing agencies, security, events — with unlimited users on every plan. Pricing starts from £47/month but every tier is quote-only: they price on functionality and the number of shifts you schedule.

What buyers report about Parim

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Parim ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Reviewers in security, healthcare and events keep naming the same field-facing details: staff see their own schedule with site maps, directions and site-specific instructions, and the system prompts workers who are running late or have not clocked in.
  • • Open shifts are the other repeated win -- schedule the core team, then let the rest apply -- which is why the reviews cluster around agencies and event caterers rather than fixed-rota employers.
  • • Support and the development team are described as responsive to feature requests, and several reviewers say training took almost no time.
What they complain about
  • • The sharpest review comes from a healthcare CEO who says what was demonstrated during the sale turned out not to be included: the capability was there, but only at considerably more money. On a quote-only product priced by functionality, that is the failure mode to guard against.
  • • Integrations are the practical limit: reviewers can export everything to CSV but want automatic synchronisation into invoicing and accounting instead of a manual import.
  • • Add-on modules such as SMS messaging are billed on top of the plan.
  • • Visibility between employees is described as all-or-nothing rather than configurable per team.

Where the ratings disagree. The Capterra gripes line up with the pricing model rather than the feature list. Unlimited users removes the seat arithmetic, so the levers left are shift volume and which modules are switched on -- and the one review that describes real regret is about exactly that: functionality that was scoped in the demo and priced later. Get the module list into the quote in writing.

When the paid plan is worth it

There is no tier comparison to make here, and saying so plainly is more use than inventing one. Parim publishes exactly one number -- from GBP 47 a month, with unlimited users on every plan -- and each of the three named tiers, Starter, Pro and Premium, carries a Request Pricing button instead of a figure. So the GBP 47 is a floor rather than a plan price, and nobody can tell you what the tiers cost relative to each other.

What that means practically: the quote you get is built around your shift volume, so go into the conversation with that number ready and ask for the price at your current volume AND at the volume you expect in a year. Unlimited users on every plan is the genuinely useful part of the model, because it removes the per-seat arithmetic that dominates most rota tools. But it also means shift count is the only lever they have, and that is the one to pin down in writing.

How to actually use Parim

Parim publishes a key-steps guide and the order in it is not cosmetic. Add employees first -- individually, by CSV import where you match the fields by dragging them onto the columns, or by email invite -- and grant access at the same time so staff can log in and see their own shifts. Then set up positions, under System Settings, People Settings, Positions. Give each a colour that becomes how it reads on the calendar, and assign them in bulk from the People page using 'With Selected'.

Two optional settings are worth doing before go-live rather than after. Pay and charge rules can be scoped to a person, position, employment type, location, client, event or subcontractor. Employment types carry pay rates, work and rest rules and pay schedules.

Frequently asked questions

What should I weigh before buying HR & People software?

Compliance is country by country, so the honest answer is: only where the vendor says it is, and only for the entity type you actually have. Ask which countries are covered by its own entities versus a local partner, who carries the liability if something is filed wrong, and how changes in local law reach you. Get those answers in the contract rather than the sales deck.
