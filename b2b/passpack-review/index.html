When the paid plan is worth it

Teams is $20 per user per year, paid annually, for up to twenty people, and Business is $4.50 per user per month. Re-read on 2 September 2026 on their own pricing page, unchanged from our August reading. Teams works out at about $1.67 a user a month, which is genuinely a fraction of the mainstream managers where $3 to $8 a seat is normal — for a twenty-person team that is on the order of a thousand dollars a year.

The saving sits almost entirely in that one plan, though, and it is worth knowing before you grow into the next one. Business at $4.50 a seat is inside the normal range rather than under it, so past twenty people the price stops being the reason to choose this and the trade-offs below start doing all the work. What you trade for the price is maturity rather than security, and that trade is worth taking seriously.

The interface is plain, and the mobile side is brand new: the iOS app appeared on the App Store on 10 August 2026, and when the vendor wrote to us on 2 September 2026 the Android version was still described as coming very soon. For a password manager that is not a cosmetic detail, because the tool only protects you if people actually use it, and friction on mobile is where team adoption quietly fails.

A cheaper licence that half the team routes around is more expensive than a polished one they use. So the honest test during the 28-day trial is not the feature list but adoption: put the iOS app on the phones of the two least patient people on your team and see whether they still use it in week four. If they do, the saving is real. If your team is on Android, wait until the app has actually shipped before you decide — an announced release is not a released one.

How to actually use Passpack

Work out first whether you are under or over twenty people, because that is where the economics change. Teams is $20 per user per year for up to twenty; Business is $4.50 per user per month. That is a step of roughly 2.7 times per seat, so a team of eighteen and a team of twenty-two are looking at quite different propositions. Then spend the 28-day trial on the phone, because that is the part of this product that changed most recently.

The iOS app was published on 10 August 2026 and was already on version 1.0.1 by the 15th, with a handful of ratings behind it; the Android version was still unreleased when we checked again on 6 September 2026, twenty-seven days later, with no Play Store listing and no Android build in Passpack's own Downloads menu. So the question is no longer whether mobile exists but whether a first-generation app holds up: give it to the least technical person on your team and have them do a week of real logins from their handset before you commit anyone else.

If your team runs on Android, that test cannot be run at all yet. Be clear about the trade you are making. You are buying solid zero-knowledge security at a fraction of the mainstream managers' price, and paying for it in maturity: a plain interface, mobile apps that are weeks rather than years old, and reports of slow support and billing hiccups. For a team where cost matters more than convenience that can be exactly right — but it is a trade rather than a bargain, and the trial is where you find out which side you are on.

Plan the migration and the offboarding before you start. Check the import path from wherever your passwords live now, and check what happens to shared vaults when someone leaves — that second one is the reason teams buy a password manager in the first place.

Frequently asked questions

What does Passpack cost?

$20 per user per year for Teams, up to twenty people, and $4.50 per user per month for Business — both paid annually, both with a 28-day trial, re-read on 3 September 2026 on their own plans page and unchanged since August. Enterprise is a custom quote.

Does Passpack have mobile apps?

On iPhone, yes, since 10 August 2026, when Passpack Inc published Passpack Password Manager on the App Store; it was on version 1.0.1 with a handful of ratings when we checked on 2 September 2026, and by 6 September the plans page lists 'Mobile app for iOS' as a feature on all three plans. Is it really that much cheaper than the big password managers?

On Teams, yes: about $1.67 a user a month against the $3 to $8 a seat the mainstream business managers charge. On Business at $4.50 that gap largely closes, so the answer depends on which plan your headcount puts you in. What you weigh against that is the smaller ecosystem: fewer integrations and a smaller support organisation. For a team that mainly needs shared credentials with proper access control, that trade is often fine.

What should I test in the trial?

Twenty-eight days is generous, so use it on the three things that decide adoption: how credentials are shared and revoked when someone leaves, whether the browser extension behaves on the sites your team actually uses, and whether the new iOS app survives a week of real logins. Password managers fail on those details rather than on features.

Does Passpack support TOTP codes, and are the browser extensions being improved?

Both are on the plans page now, and TOTP arrived there between our readings. Passpack told its partners on 2 September 2026 that time-based one-time passwords are supported on all plans, and that a next generation of browser extensions is on the way with faster autofill and warnings about unsafe login pages. On 3 September the plans page listed Browser Extensions but not TOTP; on 6 September 2026 it lists both TOTP and 'Mobile app for iOS', in the Admin & User Functionality section, which carries no plan exclusions and therefore covers all three plans. Note this is a different row from Two-factor authentication under Security, which sits beside emergency access codes and YubiKey support and covers signing in to Passpack rather than storing one-time codes for your own logins. The key-features page still lags: on 6 September it listed 'Mobile & Browser Extensions' under New Features Coming Soon. If stored TOTP codes are why you are buying, confirm the behaviour inside the 28-day trial rather than reading it off a table.

What should I weigh before buying IT & Productivity software?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.
