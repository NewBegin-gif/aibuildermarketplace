What Payoneer does

Global payment solutions and cross-border billing. Easily receive funds from international clients and marketplaces with low fees.

Pricing (2026)

(2026-08-02, read on their own fee pages) Free to open; the cost is in the fees. Receiving non-local currency 1% (minimum $1). Receiving by card up to 3.99% + $0.49. Withdrawal to your own bank in the same country and currency $1.50 flat. Currency conversion 1.2–4%. Annual account fee $29.95, waived in year one and waived in any 12-month period in which at least $6,000 arrives. Rates vary by country and account type — confirm yours in the app before planning around them. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The conversion and cross-currency withdrawal markups are where it quietly costs you — for pure currency conversion a specialist like Wise is often cheaper. Payoneer earns its keep on marketplace payouts and getting paid by international clients, less so as an FX tool.

What buyers report about Payoneer

Recurring themes from Trustpilot, Capterra, TrustRadius and NerdWallet, read in August 2026. We have not run Payoneer ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Transfers land quickly and reliably when nothing is flagged, and that is what most of the 59,000+ Trustpilot reviewers are rating. About 3.8 out of 5.
  • • Getting paid from marketplaces and platforms that will not do a normal bank transfer is the job it does well.
What they complain about
  • • Roughly 75% of negative reviewers name account freezes or blocked funds as their main complaint. Re-verification can freeze an account precisely while money is in transit, and escalations drag for weeks. Do not run your only account here.
  • • The fee stack: 1% on non-local currency (minimum $1), up to 3.99% + $0.49 on card, $1.50 flat to withdraw same-country same-currency, and 1.2-4% on conversion. Annual fee $29.95, waived if at least $6,000 arrives in twelve months.
  • • Early 2026 brought reports of hacked accounts and unauthorised transfers, one of $4,300. Enable every security control on offer.

When the paid plan is worth it

There is no plan to upgrade to — Payoneer is free to open and earns on fees, so "is it worth it" is a question about volume, not about tiers. The number that decides it is the annual account fee: $29.95, waived in the first year and waived in any 12 months where at least $6,000 arrives in the account. Below that threshold you are paying about $30 a year for the privilege of being able to receive money. Against that, the fee stack read on their own pages on 2 August 2026: 1% (minimum $1) to receive non-local currency, up to 3.99% + $0.49 to receive by card, $1.50 flat to withdraw in the same country and currency, and 1.2–4% on conversion. Run your own numbers before assuming it is cheap: on $2,000 a month of card-paid work the receiving fee alone is roughly $80. The honest test is not price but access. If a marketplace or platform will not send a normal bank transfer to your country, the 1% is the cost of getting paid at all and the maths is easy. If your payer can send SEPA or local ACH, an ordinary business account beats this on every line.

How to actually use Payoneer

Two things are worth doing in the first week, and both are about the day something goes wrong rather than the day you sign up. First, finish verification completely before money is in transit. Roughly 75% of Payoneer's negative reviewers name frozen accounts or blocked funds, and re-verification can land precisely while a payment is arriving. Upload every document they ask for while nothing is at stake. Second, never make Payoneer the only route to your own money. Keep a second account that can receive the same payments, so a review that runs for weeks is an inconvenience rather than a cashflow event. Early 2026 also brought reports of unauthorised transfers, one of $4,300, so turn on every security control the account offers on day one. Then check which receiving method your payer will actually use. Card payment costs up to 3.99% + $0.49 while local bank details cost 1%, and that choice is usually made by the payer, not by you — ask before you invoice. Frequently asked questions

Is Payoneer safe to run my finances through?

The bar here is higher than features. Check who actually holds the money and under what licence, whether client funds are segregated, what audit trail and permission controls you get, and how support behaves when something goes wrong mid-transaction. Anything touching payments should be able to answer those in writing; if it cannot, that is your answer.

Who should not use Payoneer?

Skip it if your main need is cheap currency conversion — a specialist FX account usually beats Payoneer's conversion markup. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
