What PDWare does

Resource and capacity planning for project teams. Plan who works on what, balance workloads and forecast capacity in real time so projects don't stall on people bottlenecks.

Pricing (2026)

Quote-based enterprise pricing (2026) — no public tiers, no self-serve trial; the entry point is a guided demo. Used by pharma/tech enterprises (Pfizer, Intel, Medtronic-class logos). Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

The honest knock

This is genuine enterprise software with enterprise buying: opaque pricing, demo-first sales and implementation effort — appropriate for portfolio offices planning hundreds of resources, overkill below that. The honest pre-purchase question is organizational: resource-first planning only works if your PMO will actually maintain forecasts; the tool formalizes discipline, it can't create it.

What actually drives the quote

There are no published tiers and no self-serve trial: the entry point is a guided demo and everything is quoted. That is consistent with what this is — resource and portfolio planning bought by PMOs at pharma and tech enterprises — and it means the useful preparation is not price research but scope definition. What moves an enterprise resource-planning quote is reliably three things: the number of resources under management, the number of named users who plan rather than merely view, and implementation. Fix all three in writing before you ask, and ask for the implementation figure separately from the licence — in this category implementation is routinely a significant fraction of year one, and a quote that blends them hides the part that does not repeat. But the question that decides whether to buy at all is organisational rather than commercial. Resource-first planning only works if your PMO will actually maintain the forecasts. The tool formalises discipline; it cannot create it. A portfolio office that already keeps allocation data current will get compounding value from making it queryable. One that intends to start keeping it current because the software arrived is buying a very expensive prompt, and the licence will renew long before the habit does. So the honest sizing test is headcount under management. Planning hundreds of resources across a portfolio is what this is built for. Below that, the machinery — opaque pricing, demo-first sales, real implementation effort — costs more in elapsed time than the problem costs you today.

How to actually use PDWare

Answer the organisational question before you book the demo, because it decides whether this works regardless of what it costs: will your PMO actually maintain the forecasts? Resource-first planning formalises discipline; it cannot create it. If your current resource plan is a spreadsheet nobody has updated in six weeks, the problem is not the spreadsheet and a tool will not fix it. Then bring the numbers the quote will be built from, since there are no public tiers and no self-serve trial: how many resources you plan against, how many concurrent projects, how many people will actively plan, and how many need to view rather than edit. That last split usually moves the price more than anything else. Ask about implementation explicitly, in hours and weeks rather than in principle. This is genuine enterprise software bought the enterprise way, and the configuration effort is routinely larger than the licence conversation implies — ask what a realistic go-live looks like for an organisation your size, who does that work, and what it costs. And be honest about scale. This is built for portfolio offices planning hundreds of resources. Below that it is more machine than the job needs, and the demo will not tell you so.

Best for: enterprise PMOs that plan portfolios around named-resource capacity and need feasibility answers before committing to projects.
Who should skip it: if teams under ~50 people (modern PM tools with workload views suffice), or organizations without a working PMO cadence.

Frequently asked questions

Does PDWare replace my current setup or sit on top of it?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.

Who should not use PDWare?

Skip it if teams under ~50 people (modern PM tools with workload views suffice), or organizations without a working PMO cadence. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
