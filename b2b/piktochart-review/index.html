What Piktochart does

Infographic, report and poster maker with template libraries and AI credits — the free plan allows 2 downloads, Pro is $10 per member/month billed annually, and PDF/PowerPoint export starts at Business ($17).

Pricing (2026)

(2026-08-06, read in a live browser on their own pricing page, US dollar view) Free is $0, Pro is $15 per member per month billed monthly or $10 billed annually, Business is $20 monthly or $17 billed annually, and Enterprise is quote-only from 101 members. Free stops at 2 PNG downloads, 1 GB of storage, 60 AI credits and free templates only, and is capped at 1-4 members. Pro adds unlimited PNG downloads, premium templates and icons, 500 AI credits and 100 GB, and the annual rate is what the page calls 33% off. Business, 15% off annually, adds PDF and PowerPoint export on top of PNG, the Brand Kit (brand colours, fonts and logos), 1,000 AI credits and 250 GB. Enterprise adds SSO/SAML, workspace management and a dedicated account manager. Two separate tabs on the same page price other audiences: Education is $39 per member billed annually with 250 AI credits and unlimited PNG, PDF and PowerPoint downloads, and requires a valid educational email address, with Campus quote-only above 3,000 seats; a Nonprofits tab also exists, which we did not read. The page can be switched between USD, GBP, EUR, SGD, MXN and INR. No free trial is offered on the paid tiers — the free plan is the trial — and the page states you can cancel anytime. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The ladder here is not about how much you make, it is about which file you are allowed to hand over. Pro — the tier the page marks most popular — exports PNG only. PDF and PowerPoint are Business features, which the full feature table confirms in a single row most people scroll past. So if the reason you want this is to email a PDF handout or drop slides into a deck, Pro at $10 a member a month is not a cheaper Piktochart, it is the wrong product, and your real entry price is $17 a member a month on annual billing, or $20 monthly. The free plan reads as generous and is not. Unlimited projects, two downloads — and not two a month: the comparison table lists the number of downloads as a flat 2. That is enough to find out whether you like the editor, which is a fair trial, but it is not the working free tier some competitors in this category offer, so do not plan around it. The third thing to price is the AI credits: 60 on Free, 500 on Pro, 1,000 on Business. Nowhere on the pricing page does it say what one credit buys, or what happens when you run out — there is no top-up price anywhere on the page. If AI generation is the reason you are buying, ask what a credit costs and what it covers before you commit to a year, because that is a meter that can decide your bill and it is not published. One last piece of arithmetic. The annual discount is 33% on Pro but only 15% on Business, so the saving shrinks exactly at the tier most teams that need PDF or PowerPoint end up on. And if you have an educational email address the picture changes completely: Education is $39 per member for a whole year, against $120 for a year of Pro, and it includes the PDF and PowerPoint exports that Pro does not have.

Best for: individuals and small teams who need infographics, data-heavy reports and posters to look designed without hiring a designer — and above all students and teachers, for whom $39 a member a year with PDF and PowerPoint export included is the best-value door on the whole page.
Who should skip it: if you mainly need slides, because tools built for presentations do that better for the money; you need PDF or PowerPoint export but were budgeting for Pro, since those formats only start at Business; you were counting on a real free tier, because two downloads is a demo rather than a plan; or your AI use is heavy, since the credit allowances are capped and the page never prices a top-up.

The natural comparison is Canva — the comparison nearly everybody actually makes, and it comes down to two questions rather than feature counts. First, check where each tool puts the file format you need, not what the headline costs: Piktochart keeps PDF and PowerPoint behind Business at $17 a member a month on annual billing, and a rival that includes your format one tier down is cheaper than its price list suggests. Second, be honest about what you are making — Piktochart is at its strongest on data-heavy infographics and reports and weaker as a general-purpose design tool, and if it is really slides you want then Beautiful.ai and Gamma are the closer peers. We have not read Canva's, Beautiful.ai's or Gamma's price lists ourselves, so verify theirs as well as ours; the Piktochart figures here were read on 2026-08-06.. Decide by which one fits the job above, not by the louder brand.

My ex-banker filter is simple: does Piktochart remove a real cost — time, errors, missed revenue — bigger than what it charges? If the job above is genuinely yours, it's worth a look. We never publish fake or “exclusive” prices, so always confirm the current plan on their site.

Frequently asked questions

Can Piktochart match my brand style?

Up to a point, and that point is usually set by your plan. Check whether brand kits, custom fonts, colour locking and template controls sit on the tier you would actually buy, since these are common upsells. Test it with your own assets during the trial rather than the demo content, which is always chosen to flatter the output.

Who should not use Piktochart?

Skip it if you mainly need slides, because tools built for presentations do that better for the money; you need PDF or PowerPoint export but were budgeting for Pro, since those formats only start at Business; you were counting on a real free tier, because two downloads is a demo rather than a plan; or your AI use is heavy, since the credit allowances are capped and the page never prices a top-up. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
