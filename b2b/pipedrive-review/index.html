Pipedrive Review (2026)From $14 a Seat, Billed a Year Up Front

Growth & RevenueResearched assessment · by Daniel HaketContent updated 2026-09-27Prices checked 2026-08-26View Pipedrive in the Atlas →

Pipedrive manages what happens after a lead enters your pipeline — it doesn't do outbound prospecting or marketing, so you'll pair it with a separate tool for that. As you scale and need sales, marketing and support data joined up, its sales-only focus starts to lean on add-ons.

Best for: sales teams that want a simple, visual pipeline they can use on day one, with a separate marketing tool already in place.
Who should skip it: if you want marketing automation in the same tool (HubSpot) or need built-in outbound prospecting.

Pricing (2026)

Four seat-based plans. The page advertises that annual billing saves up to 42%. Read from the Netherlands the same column reads €14, €39, €59 and €79: the numbers do not convert, they are simply reprinted with a different symbol, so a European pays materially more for the same seat.

Frequently asked questions

What does Pipedrive cost per seat in 2026?

Read on 16 August 2026 from an EU address, VAT exclusive: billed annually €14, €39, €59 and €79 per seat a month for Lite, Growth, Premium and Ultimate, charged up front as €168, €468, €708 or €948 per seat a year. Billed monthly the same plans are €24, €49, €79 and €99. The trial is 14 days and asks for no card, or 30 days from the affiliate link on this page, followed on 3 September 2026.

Which Pipedrive features are paid add-ons on top of the seat?

Five, and this is the single most common complaint about the pricing: LeadBooster from €32.50, Smart Docs from €32.50, Web Visitors from €41, Projects from €16 and Campaigns from €13.33, each per month on top of every seat you pay for. Lead capture and email marketing are therefore not in the seat price at any tier. Read on 16 August 2026.

Is Pipedrive good enough as the CRM you grow into?

The pattern across platforms is that teams stay for the ease of use and leave over reporting. Reviewers on TrustRadius who have run it for five to ten years still recommend it for small teams while naming the same two ceilings: reporting and dashboarding thin out as the company scales, and automation is trigger-action, so branching workflows do not fit. Two separate reviewers also name the same specific gap — you cannot link one person to more than one organisation.

What should I weigh before buying Growth & Revenue software?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Small teams get the most out of this category when one clear problem is already costing real hours or revenue; buying ahead of that just adds cost and another login. Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.
