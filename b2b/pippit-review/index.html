What the price did

We capture Pippit’s own pricing page every month and keep the figures, so this is our record of the page — not a forecast, and not someone else’s summary.

Real movement across three readings, all from a Netherlands exit and all in euros. On 5 August 2026 Starter was €9 a month against a struck-through €15, and Plus €30 against €50. On 6 September Starter was €14 against €22 and Plus €39 against €70 — a rise of 56% and 30%, with the list prices raised alongside the discounted ones. On 19 September the monthly headline fell again, to €8 and €19, but the allowances fell further: Starter went from 2,100 credits a month to 1,575 and Plus from 6,700 to 3,885. Priced per credit that is a worse deal than either earlier read, not a better one — 240 credits per euro on Starter in September became 217, and Plus went from 259 to 219. Pro moved the same way and in the open: the 35,500-credit step was €1,540 a year on 6 September and is €1,901 now. The struck-through discount was present on every read on all three dates, which is what makes it a price rather than a sale.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

Credit-based, with a free tier that needs no card. What buyers report about Pippit

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Pippit ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise

What they complain about

  • • Third-party AI models have been removed from the platform without notice, which reviewers cite as a trust issue.
  • • Some report charges after they believed they had cancelled.

When the paid plan is worth it

Credits are the meter, so compare on credits per euro rather than on the headline — and on 19 September 2026 the headline moves the opposite way to the value. Starter fell from €14 a month to €8 and Plus from €39 to €19, but the allowances fell further, from 2,100 to 1,575 credits and from 6,700 to 3,885. Work it out per credit and every tier got worse: Starter went from 240 credits per euro to 217, Plus from 259 to 219, and the 35,500-credit Pro step from 277 to 224 while its yearly price rose from €1,540 to €1,901.

The ladder still rewards going up — 217, 219 and 224 credits per euro — but the gaps are now small enough that the tier is rarely the decision. What no upgrade fixes is what a credit buys. Public buyer reviews describe generations that failed and still consumed credits, and a bigger allowance multiplies the successes and the failures at the same rate. Pro also buys 10 seats where Starter buys 1, so a team may be paying for the seats and taking the credits as change.

Pippit prices in the visitor's currency, and the euro ladder has been rewritten twice in six weeks: between 6 and 19 September 2026 every tier was repriced and every allowance cut. This is a page to open yourself, on the day. Then test the failure mode before you spend anything, using the free tier's recurring daily credits, which need no card.

The consistent complaint in public buyer reviews is that failed generations still consumed credits, so run your hardest prompt, let it fail, and check whether the credits came back. That single test tells you more about your real cost per usable output than the price list does. Size on credits rather than videos: 1,575 a month on Starter, 3,885 on Plus, and 8,645 to 59,000 across the five Pro steps.

And keep your own records from day one — dates, charges, what failed and what it cost — because refunds are reported as hard to obtain.

Frequently asked questions

Pippit just got cheaper — is it a better deal?

No. Read on pippit.ai/pricing on 19 September 2026: the monthly headline fell from €14 to €8 on Starter and from €39 to €19 on Plus since 6 September, but the credit allowances fell further, from 2,100 a month to 1,575 and from 6,700 to 3,885. Per credit that is 240 down to 217 on Starter and 259 down to 219 on Plus. The price you pay went down; the amount you get for it went down more. Pro did not even keep the appearance: the 35,500-credit step was €1,540 a year and is now €1,901.

Which Pro step should I buy?

There are five now, not four — a new bottom step at 8,645 credits a month was added by 19 September 2026 — and the rate barely moves across them. Yearly, they run €467, €948, €1,260, €1,901 and €3,117 for 8,645, 17,600, 23,500, 35,500 and 59,000 credits a month, which is 222 to 227 credits per euro from bottom to top. So buy the step that matches your actual monthly use and ignore the ladder logic: there is no meaningful volume discount to climb towards, and the page shows the €1,901 step by default rather than the one you need.

The page prints a credits-per-euro figure. Can I trust it?

Today, yes — and that is new. On 6 September 2026 the cards printed EUR1=2.4, 2.59 and 2.77 while their own amounts worked out at 240, 259 and 277 credits per euro: a decimal point in the wrong place, a hundredfold off. On 19 September the same line reads EUR1=217.24, 218.87 and 224.09, which checks out against the yearly prices and allowances beside it. The vendor fixed the arithmetic. Note what the corrected figures show, though: the ratio is lower than the 240/259/277 of two weeks earlier, so the repair and the price rise landed together.
