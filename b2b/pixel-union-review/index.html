Against a themed subscription at even $30 a month, it pays for itself before the first year is out. If you are choosing between a one-off premium theme and a recurring page-builder, run that comparison over three years rather than one. What the price does not buy is a finished store, and this is where the money actually goes. A $350 theme on a store with weak product pages and slow images converts weakly, and the hours spent fixing those are the real budget line. Buy it for speed-to-launch and a coherent design language, not as a conversion switch. Two ongoing costs that are easy to miss on a one-time purchase. How to actually use Pixel Union

Buy the theme for the design system and the speed to launch, not as a conversion switch. A $350 theme on a store with weak product photography, thin descriptions and slow images still converts weakly — the lift comes from how you configure, populate and optimise it, and that work happens after the purchase rather than instead of it. So plan that work before you buy: real photography, real copy, and a page-speed check once the theme is populated with your actual catalogue rather than demo content.

Heavily customised premium themes can add page weight if you switch on every feature, so turn features on deliberately and measure after each one. Establish how updates work before you customise heavily. A theme you have edited extensively is more work to keep current, and that cost arrives months later when you have forgotten what you changed — keep a written note of every customisation as you make it. And time the purchase.

These are one-time prices rather than subscriptions: roughly $300 to $340 for Pixel Union themes, and $350 to $495 for Out of the Sandbox's Turbo and Flex. Promotional discounts appear often, so if you are not launching this week it is worth checking the current price rather than paying list.
