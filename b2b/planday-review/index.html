Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 5 Oct 2026

Three tiers, two of them priced. On the euro page, planday.com/de/preise, Starter is €2.99 per user per month with a 5-user minimum, Plus is €4.99 per user with a 10-user minimum plus a €25.00 monthly subscription fee on top of the per-user cost, and Pro is custom-priced with a demo button rather than a figure. The English page at planday.com/pricing now serves the UK list in its own pricing data — £2.99 Starter, £3.99 Plus and a £30.00 monthly subscription fee, same 5- and 10-user minimums — and carries a separate US and Canada column of $2.99 and $4.49 per user.

Which price list applies to me — euros, pounds or dollars?

Check before you budget, because Planday publishes three columns and they are not conversions of each other. Read on 17 September and again on 5 October 2026: the euro page (planday.com/de/preise) prices Starter at €2.99 per user per month and Plus at €4.99 plus a €25.00 monthly subscription fee. The English page at planday.com/pricing carries the UK list in its own pricing data — £2.99 and £3.99, with a £30.00 monthly fee — and a separate United States and Canada column of $2.99 and $4.49 per user. So the Plus seat is €4.99, £3.99 or $4.49 depending on where you buy, and the flat fee is €25 in euros against £30 in pounds, with no separate US figure published for it. The tier structure is identical everywhere: 5-user minimum on Starter, 10 on Plus, Pro quoted. Quote the currency alongside the number in any internal comparison.

Why is the step from Starter to Plus so much bigger than it looks?

Because two things change at once and only one of them is in the headline. Read 17 September and 5 October 2026 on the euro page: Starter is €2.99 per user with a five-user minimum and no platform fee; Plus is €4.99 per user with a ten-user minimum and a €25.00 monthly subscription fee on top. Run it at the minimums and the difference is not the two euros a seat it appears to be: five people on Starter is €14.95 a month, ten on Plus is €49.90 plus €25, which is €74.90. The flat fee does not scale down, so it weighs heaviest on exactly the small team most likely to be choosing between the two — €7.49 a head at ten users against €5.49 at fifty. Planday’s own FAQ says the fee covers system access to advanced features, their development and maintenance. The 30-day Plus trial takes no card, which is long enough to run a real rota cycle.

What gets billed on top of the per-user price?

Three things, and the first one catches people out. Planday’s FAQ, read 17 September and again 5 October 2026, defines a user as anyone with a login to your account and says billing runs in bundles of five — their own example is 23 users billed as 25. So sitting just above a multiple of five is the expensive place to be, and every manager, every casual and every leaver who still has an account is counted. Second, every SMS sent from Planday carries a fee; the in-app messaging is included in the subscription. Third, setup help is a paid onboarding service rather than part of the plan. On the euro page the Plus tier also carries the €25.00 monthly subscription fee on top of the seats, which the FAQ says applies to Pro as well. Billing itself is monthly or quarterly, by major card or direct debit.

Can I cancel Planday halfway through my contract term?

Generally not. Planday's help article on cancelling a subscription, read on 23 October 2026, says subscriptions generally do not support mid-term cancellations, so you pay out the current term. Check the term length before you sign, especially if you are trialling Planday for one season.

How long is my scheduling data kept after I cancel?

Up to 120 days. The same help article, read on 23 October 2026, says workspace data is deleted within 120 days unless the subscription is resumed before then. Save anything you need to keep, such as timesheets for payroll records, before that window closes.
