Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 19 Sep 2026

One plan, Performance, free to install, at $0 a month plus usage charges. The usage table, read from the listing's own pricing-estimator data, has two tiers: 2.5% on the first $20,000 of upsell revenue in a month, and $0 from $20,001 up. In practice that is a commission with a ceiling — the app cannot charge more than $500 in a month, and a month in which it sells nothing costs nothing.

There is no paid tier above it: unlimited funnels and offers, one-click upsell and downsell, AI product recommendations, analytics and A/B testing and market translations all sit on that single plan. Charges are billed in USD every 30 days. The vendor's own site publishes no prices at all — its pricing link leads to the same single-plan listing — so the listing is the only place the meter is stated.

Neither page defined how 'upsell revenue' is measured, so we asked, and the vendor answered in writing on 21 September 2026 with permission to quote it. The figure is net: the amount the customer is actually charged for the accepted upsell item, after any discount set on that offer, and nothing from the original cart is included. Refunds and cancellations of an accepted upsell are reversed automatically through Shopify — proportionally for partial refunds, with the 2.5% coming off alongside, and no support ticket needed — while refunds of original cart items never move the figure, because they were never billed.

There is no multi-day attribution window either: the offer exists only on the Shopify post-purchase page between payment and the order confirmation, and the merchant sets how long that moment stays open, with a default of 20 minutes, any value from 1 to 59 minutes, or Shopify's own 60-minute default. The vendor says the full rule now sits in its help center under Pricing and billing.

That is the vendor's account of its own meter, dated and on the record, but it is not something we have audited against a live bill. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

On a $40 upsell carrying a 20% margin the app takes a dollar of the eight you actually made — fine. On discounted or thin-margin add-ons the same percentage is a far bigger share of the profit, and the meter reads revenue either way. There is also no flat option to escape to: a store doing $20,000 of upsell revenue a month pays the full $500 and cannot buy out of the percentage, while the free-above-$20,001 tier only rewards stores already past that line.

And the app is young — it launched on 19 June 2026, so at the end of August there were barely ten weeks of merchant experience behind it. Post-purchase offers also depend on your store's checkout supporting the post-purchase extension; check that before you plan around them.

So this is a young, single-market review set describing first impressions of setup, and it should not be read alongside a four-figure rating as though the two mean the same thing. What it does support well is the claim about configurability, because that is what merchants describe concretely; what it cannot yet support is durability.

What the money actually buys

There is no premium tier, which makes the sum unusually short: the app charges 2.5% of the first $20,000 of upsell revenue it generates in a month and nothing at all above $20,001. The most it can cost you is $500 in a month; the least is nothing. The question that replaces 'which tier' is whether the attribution is honest, because attribution is the entire invoice. Every feature is included — unlimited funnels and offers, one-click upsell and downsell, AI product recommendations, analytics and A/B testing, translations for markets — so you are not paying for capability at all.

You are paying a commission on what the app says it earned you. Verify that claim against your own numbers in the first month and the model is genuinely aligned with your interests. Skip the check and you are trusting the meter that writes the bill.

How to actually use Post Purchase Upsell Hero

There is nothing to weigh up before installing, which is unusual: the plan is $0 a month and a bill only exists once the app has already sold something. That makes the install itself the test rather than the commitment. Build one funnel on your best-selling product, let the AI recommendation pick the follow-up offer for a second funnel, and run both long enough to compare — A/B testing sits on the same free plan, so there is no tier to buy in order to run the comparison properly.

What to watch from week one is the number the billing rests on: the upsell revenue the app attributes to itself. Check it against your own order data early rather than at the end of the month, because 2.5% of that figure is the invoice, and attribution is exactly where upsell apps and merchants tend to disagree. The offers themselves appear after checkout, so test on a real order rather than a preview to see what your customers will actually be shown. Frequently asked questions

What is the most this can cost me in a month?

$500, and the structure that produces it is unusually easy to check. The listing's usage table has exactly two steps: 2.5% on upsell revenue up to $20,000 in a month, and nothing at all above $20,001. Two and a half per cent of $20,000 is $500, so that is the ceiling by construction — a store doing $80,000 of upsell revenue pays the same $500 as one doing $20,000, and an effective rate of 0.6% instead of 2.5%. A month in which the app sells nothing costs nothing. The bill is therefore worst, in percentage terms, for the store that is just getting going, and best for the one that is already winning.

The whole bill rests on 'upsell revenue'. Who counts it, and how?

The app does, and neither page we read on 19 September 2026 defines the term. The listing prices the meter and names it, the vendor's own site talks about tracking upsell revenue in a real-time dashboard, and nothing on either states whether the figure is gross or net of discounts, what happens to an upsell that is later refunded or returned, or how long after checkout an order still counts as attributed. For a plan with no feature tiers this is the only number that matters — it is not what you pay for capability, it is a commission on a figure the vendor calculates. Check the first month's attributed total against your own order data before you let it run, and get the refund treatment in writing if you sell anything that comes back.

Will it actually run on my store?

It depends on a Shopify mechanism rather than on your theme, and that is the thing to check first. Post-purchase offers appear in the window between payment and the thank-you page, which requires your checkout to support Shopify's post-purchase extension — no app can add that for you. Both pages are clear that no coding is needed and that any theme works, and the vendor's site says offers carry no additional shipping fees and appear in 130-plus languages. But if your checkout cannot host a post-purchase extension, the single feature you are installing this for does not render at all, and since the plan is free to install you will find that out without being billed. Verify it on a test order before you build offers.

What should I weigh before buying Growth & Revenue software?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Small teams get the most out of this category when one clear problem is already costing real hours or revenue; buying ahead of that just adds cost and another login. Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.
