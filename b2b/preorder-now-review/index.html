Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 13 Sep 2026

Five tiers, and they are named after your Shopify plan rather than after what the app does. Free is $0 and covers your first pre-order product, with mixed-cart warnings and email tech support. Then Shopify Basic is $19.95 a month, Shopify Standard $39.95, Shopify Advanced $59.95 and Shopify Plus $99.95 -- all with unlimited pre-order products. Read the feature lists side by side and the point becomes obvious: Basic and Standard list exactly the same five features (unlimited pre-order products, collection page badges, preorder discount, pre-order by product tag, Quickview support), and Advanced and Plus list exactly the same three (unlimited pre-order products, "ALL FEATURES of Basic Shopify & Shopify plans", dedicated success manager support).

So the ladder is not buying you capability; it is charging you according to the size of store Shopify says you run. A Plus merchant pays five times what a Basic merchant pays for the same badges, the same discounts and the same tagging. The one real step is Advanced, which adds a dedicated success manager. Note that partial payments, which the FAQ advertises as a feature, are named on none of the five plan cards, so the tier that carries them is unstated. All charges are in USD and recur every 30 days. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

For a store that runs one drop at a time that is not a trial, it is a plan. And if you are on Plus, price this against alternatives properly: at $99.95 a month you are paying a Plus surcharge for a feature set the app itself lists as identical to the $59.95 tier.

When the paid plan is worth it

Free to Basic at $19.95 is the only step that buys capability: it lifts the one-product limit and adds collection-page badges, pre-order discounts, tag targeting and Quickview support. Everything above that is priced by your Shopify plan, not by what you receive. Read the four paid cards side by side and the pattern is plain: Basic at $19.95 and Standard at $39.95 print the same five features, Advanced at $59.95 and Plus at $99.95 the same three.

The one genuine step up is the dedicated success manager at Advanced. What upgrading does not solve is the pre-order mechanics. Badges, discounts and tag targeting are all on the $19.95 card, and no higher tier adds a capability the cards distinguish: a Plus merchant pays five times a Basic merchant for a feature list the app prints as identical, plus that manager. Partial payments are worth asking about separately: the FAQ advertises them, but none of the five plan cards names them, so which tier carries them is unstated.

If you want help with the rollout, budget for Advanced; otherwise the honest comparison at $99.95 is against other pre-order apps, not lower tiers. Charges are in USD, recurring every 30 days.

How to actually use PreOrder Now

Start on free with one product and run a real pre-order through it end to end, including the mixed-cart case where a shopper puts an in-stock item and a pre-order item in the same basket -- that is where pre-order apps either save you support tickets or create them, and the mixed-cart warning is in the free plan. The vendor's own five steps are short: open the app and click "Pre-order products", enable pre-orders on the products you want, configure the settings including pricing and visibility, turn on Inventory Management so stock levels decide how pre-orders behave, then save.

Zenpire puts that at under ten minutes and says no coding or theme edits are needed, so treat a long setup as a sign something is wrong rather than normal. Two things to check before you pay. Which Shopify plan you are on, because that -- not your feature needs -- sets your rate here. And whether you actually want partial payments and confirmation emails switched on, since those shape what the shopper is promised at checkout.

Why are the plans named after Shopify plans?

Because your Shopify plan, not your pre-order volume or your feature needs, is what sets the rate. The cards say so outright -- each grants "ALL FEATURES of Basic Shopify & Shopify plans" and so on up the ladder. The consequence is blunt: a Plus merchant pays $99.95 a month where a Basic merchant pays $19.95 for a feature list the app itself prints as all but identical. Check which Shopify plan you are on before you read the price list, because that is the number that decides your bill.

Which upgrade actually buys me something?

Only the first one. Free to $19.95 lifts the one-product limit and adds collection-page badges, pre-order discounts, tag targeting and Quickview support -- that step is real capability. Above it, read the four paid cards side by side: $19.95 and $39.95 print the same five features, and $59.95 and $99.95 the same three. The one genuine addition higher up is a dedicated success manager at $59.95. If you are on Shopify Plus and pre-orders are a sideline, price this against alternatives properly before paying five times the Basic rate.

Is the free plan usable for a real store?

For a store that runs one drop at a time, yes. It is one pre-order product with no time limit and the mixed-cart warning included, which makes it a plan rather than a trial. Run a real pre-order through it end to end before you consider paying, including the case where a shopper puts an in-stock item and a pre-order item in the same basket -- that is where pre-order apps either save you support tickets or create them, and it is the one thing you cannot judge from a feature list.
