Prezi Review (2026)PDF Only, No Editable Export

Operations & Workflow✓ Hands-on tested · by Daniel HaketContent updated 2026-09-28Prices checked 2026-09-25View Prezi in the Atlas →

Prezi exports to PDF only — no editable .pptx — and the zooming format is polarising: some audiences find it engaging, others find it disorienting. Throughout this review we weigh it against PowerPoint.

Best for: presenters who want motion and zooming-canvas storytelling that stands out from slide decks — especially for talks, pitches and teaching.
Who should skip it: if your team just needs standard slides fast — conventional deck tools are quicker to learn and easier to hand off.
Price: Standard €5 a month, billed annually · full pricing as we read it on 25 Sep 2026 ↓
We tested it · 2026-09-28

On 28 September 2026 we gave Gamma, Beautiful.ai, Prezi and Canva the same brief for a ten-slide investor deck: eleven facts about a made-up bakery chain and the rule to add no other numbers. We checked every deck against 15 tests written down before the first one was generated. Prezi ran on a new Basic (free) account.

  • Prezi's AI editor opened in a pop-up window and built ten slides plus its zooming overview in under four minutes, using 150 of the 500 starting credits.
  • It added no numbers of its own and drew both charts to scale, but it made up a company website (kettlecrumb.nl), gave the CEO a gender the brief did not, and described 2025's actual revenue as a projection.
  • Asked once to 'Change the ask to €450,000 for 12%', it updated the ask slide but left 'Our €400,000 investment' on the use-of-funds slide, so the deck contradicted itself.

Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 25 Sep 2026

Three plans, re-read on prezi.com/pricing (which redirects to prezi.com/gts/pricing) on 25 September 2026, shown in euros from an EU exit: Standard €5 a month, Plus €15 a month and Premium €25 a month, all billed annually, each behind a 14-day free trial. The comparison table is where the gates actually are: Prezi AI is 500 credits on Standard and unlimited above it; presentation recording, smart branding, portable presentations, presenter view, voice-over, PDF export and live remote presenting all start at Plus; presentation analytics and priority technical support are Premium only.

The PowerPoint converter is ticked on all three tiers, including Standard, even though the Plus card advertises PowerPoint import as a Plus benefit. Business and Education sit on their own tabs and are quoted separately. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

The price is small; the exit is not

Paid plans start at €5 a month on annual billing, about €60 a year, and the top individual tier is Premium at €25 a month, roughly €300 a year (read 7 September 2026 from the EU). So the ladder spans five times end to end -- worth knowing before you assume the entry price is the price -- and it is still not the thing that should decide this purchase.

That number is not on the pricing page at all. Prezi exports to PDF only. There is no editable .pptx on the way out, which means every presentation you build here stays here in any form you could still work on. A PDF is a photograph of your deck: fine for sending, useless for the thing people actually do with old decks, which is open last year's and change four slides.

So price this as a commitment rather than a subscription, and note what upgrading does not fix: Premium buys analytics and priority support, not an editable export. Sixty euros a year is nothing; a library of eighty presentations you cannot take anywhere is a switching cost that grows every month you keep paying, and it does not shrink if you move up a tier or down one. The format is the other half of it.

The zooming canvas genuinely holds a room when the content suits it, and genuinely makes people seasick when it does not. That is an audience question, not a feature question, and it is worth answering on one real presentation before you build a habit around a tool whose output only leaves as a picture.

How to actually use Prezi

Test the format on one real audience before you build a habit around it. The zooming canvas genuinely holds a room when the content suits it and genuinely makes people seasick when it does not -- that is a question about your audience rather than about the software, and one presentation answers it definitively. Then plan for the exit before you build a library, because the export is the constraint nobody mentions until later.

Prezi exports to PDF only; there is no editable .pptx on the way out. So if your working habit is to open last year's deck and change four slides, that habit does not survive the move -- every presentation you build here stays here in any form you could still edit. Keep the content somewhere you control. Write the actual words in a document of your own and use Prezi as the presentation layer, so that if you leave, the switching cost is redoing a design rather than rewriting the substance.

That single discipline defuses most of the lock-in. And read the comparison table, not the cards, before you pick a tier. Frequently asked questions

Can I pay for Prezi month to month?

Not on the individual plans as the pricing page shows them. Read on 25 September 2026 from a Netherlands exit, all three cards — Standard €5, Plus €15 and Premium €25 a month — say billed annually, and the page prints no monthly rate beside them. So the real commitment is €60, €180 or €300 for a year. Each plan starts with a 14-day free trial, which is the month-to-month test you get; use it on one real presentation before the annual charge.

Do I need Plus to import my PowerPoint slides?

Probably not. The Plus card advertises importing existing PowerPoint slides, but the comparison table (read 25 September 2026) ticks the PowerPoint converter on Standard too. What Plus does gate is the output side: PDF export, presentation recording, presenter view, voice-over, portable presentations, smart branding and Live Prezi remote presenting all start there. Try the import on the Standard trial; pay for Plus if you need one of those seven.

What does Premium add over Plus?

Two rows in the comparison table (read 25 September 2026): presentation analytics and priority technical support, both marked Premium only. The Premium card also mentions advanced online training. That is €10 a month more, €120 a year, and it does not change the export: Premium still exports to PDF, not to an editable .pptx. Pay for it if you need to see how viewers engage with a shared prezi.

What should I weigh before buying Operations & Workflow software?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.
