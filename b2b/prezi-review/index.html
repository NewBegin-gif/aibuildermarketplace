Operations & WorkflowResearched assessment · by Daniel HaketUpdated 2026-09-05

What buyers report about Prezi

What they praise
  • • The zooming canvas still does something no slide tool does, and 4.2 on G2 across 5,719 reviews shows active users like it.
  • • For a presentation that needs to feel different from a deck, it remains the distinctive option.
What they complain about
  • • An F rating at the BBB, and the negative reviews are overwhelmingly billing rather than product.
  • • Auto-renewal after a free trial with no reminder email — reviewers describe finding out from the receipt.
  • • Refunds are refused as a matter of policy, with reviewers reporting generic replies stating no exceptions are possible.
  • • Monthly equivalents are advertised but most paid tiers bill annually. Check what you are actually signing up for.

Where the ratings disagree. 4.2 on G2 across 5,719 reviews against 2.9-3.2 on Trustpilot across roughly 600. G2 captures people using it; Trustpilot captures people trying to stop paying for it.

The price is small; the exit is not

Paid plans start at around $5 a month on annual billing, which is roughly $60 a year — cheap enough that the tier ladder is not really the decision. Premium sits far above that, and we will be straight about the gap: our dossier has no verified figure for it, so treat the distance between the entry price and Premium as unknown until you see it on their own checkout in your own currency.

The number that should decide this purchase is not on the pricing page at all. Prezi exports to PDF only. There is no editable .pptx on the way out, which means every presentation you build here stays here in any form you could still work on. A PDF is a photograph of your deck: fine for sending, useless for the thing people actually do with old decks, which is open last year's and change four slides.

So price this as a commitment rather than a subscription. Sixty dollars a year is nothing; a library of eighty presentations you cannot take anywhere is a switching cost that grows every month you keep paying, and it does not shrink if you downgrade. The format is the other half of it. The zooming canvas genuinely holds a room when the content suits it, and genuinely makes people seasick when it does not.

That is an audience question, not a feature question. It is worth answering on one real presentation before you build a habit around a tool whose output only leaves as a picture.

How to actually use Prezi

The zooming canvas genuinely holds a room when the content suits it and genuinely makes people seasick when it does not — that is a question about your audience rather than about the software, and one presentation answers it definitively. Then plan for the exit before you build a library, because the export is the constraint nobody mentions until later.

Prezi exports to PDF only; there is no editable .pptx on the way out. So if your working habit is to open last year's deck and change four slides, that habit does not survive the move — every presentation you build here stays here in any form you could still edit. Keep the content somewhere you control. Write the actual words in a document of your own and use Prezi as the presentation layer, so that if you leave, the switching cost is redoing a design rather than rewriting the substance.

That single discipline defuses most of the lock-in. And check what Premium actually costs in your own currency at checkout.
