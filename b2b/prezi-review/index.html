We tested it · 2026-09-28

On 28 September 2026 we gave Gamma, Beautiful.ai, Prezi and Canva the same brief for a ten-slide investor deck: eleven facts about a made-up bakery chain and the rule to add no other numbers. We checked every deck against 15 tests written down before the first one was generated. Prezi ran on a new Basic (free) account.

  • Prezi's AI editor opened in a pop-up window and built ten slides plus its zooming overview in under four minutes, using 150 of the 500 starting credits.
  • It added no numbers of its own and drew both charts to scale, but it made up a company website (kettlecrumb.nl), gave the CEO a gender the brief did not, and described 2025's actual revenue as a projection.
  • Asked once to 'Change the ask to €450,000 for 12%', it updated the ask slide but left 'Our €400,000 investment' on the use-of-funds slide, so the deck contradicted itself.

Read on 28 September 2026 on prezi.com/pricing (which redirects to prezi.com/gts/pricing), in euros from a Netherlands exit, with every section of the comparison table opened; the three paid plans sit on the Individual tab, and Basic has a tab of its own, where it is shown next to Plus.

Full pricing notes (3 more paragraphs)

Its presentation exports, Export to PDF and portable presentations, start at Plus, as do video download and downloading as files in the Videos and Infographics sections; Standard is ticked on none of them, nor on presentation recording, smart branding, presenter view, voice-over or live remote presenting. On the Individual tab, presentation analytics and priority technical support are Premium only, as are five infographic rows: SQL connectors, iFrame import integrations, callouts, page linking and custom map import.

The table has no PowerPoint-export row, and Prezi's help-centre article on downloading a presentation as PowerPoint names no plan either; the PowerPoint converter the table ticks on all three paid tiers, including Standard, is an import tool, even though the Plus card advertises PowerPoint import as a Plus benefit. The Business tab lists Plus €15 a month, Premium €25 a month and Teams €39 a month per user, all billed annually, and a Students & Educators tab has its own EDU plans that need a valid school email address.

When we signed up the same day, Prezi's plan screen offered Plus €15, Premium €25 and Teams €39 a month (billed annually) with a trial, and an option to continue with Basic, but not Standard: the same lineup as the Business tab.

The price is small; check the exit first

Read on 28 September 2026 on prezi.com/pricing from a Netherlands exit, the ladder is a free Basic plan, then Standard at €5 a month, Plus at €15 and Premium at €25, all billed annually -- €60, €180 or €300 a year. So the paid ladder spans five times end to end -- worth knowing before you assume the entry price is the price -- and it is still not the thing that should decide this purchase.

What decides it is the way out, and that is where the tiers split. On the comparison table the presentation exports, Export to PDF and portable presentations, start at Plus, as do video download and downloading as files in the Videos and Infographics sections; Standard is ticked on none of them, and the table has no PowerPoint-export row at all. Prezi's help centre does document a Download PPTX from the dashboard, added in February 2026, but describes the result as static slides, one per frame; the article names no plan for it, and Prezi's other pages disagree: the help centre's credits article lists 'Download and PDF export' as a Plus perk, while a June 2026 Prezi blog lists PPT export on Standard.

That route is still unchecked on every plan. In the newer AI editor, the Download tab on our free account listed PDF, SCORM and a PowerPoint option labelled editable ('Bewerkbare PPTX' in the Dutch interface we used), all three locked to Plus. The public room page showed PDF and PowerPoint download buttons, both to us on the free account and to logged-out viewers, who were asked to sign in first; we tried neither.

We downloaded no file, so whether any Prezi .pptx gives you text you can still change is unproven. So price this at Plus rather than Standard if your decks will need to leave Prezi as a PDF or an offline file, the only deck exports the pricing table lists, with one caveat we could not settle: the room page's PDF and PowerPoint buttons showed up even on our free account, and those buttons remain untested.

If a PowerPoint file would do, first check on a Standard trial whether the dashboard's Download PPTX works there. Note what moving up again does not change: Premium adds presentation analytics, priority support and five infographic features, and its export rows are the same as Plus. Before you build a library of eighty presentations here, export one on a Plus trial, if the trial unlocks the download, which we have not checked, and try to change four slides in PowerPoint; if they come back as pictures, the switching cost grows every month you keep paying.

The format is the other half of it. The zooming canvas genuinely holds a room when the content suits it, and genuinely makes people seasick when it does not. That is an audience question, not a feature question, and it is worth answering on one real presentation before you build a habit around it.

How to actually use Prezi

Test the format on one real audience before you build a habit around it. The zooming canvas genuinely holds a room when the content suits it and genuinely makes people seasick when it does not -- that is a question about your audience rather than about the software, and one presentation answers it definitively. Then plan for the exit before you build a library, because export is where the tiers split and not every route is documented.

On the comparison table the presentation exports, PDF and portable presentations, start at Plus, as do video download and downloading as files in the Videos and Infographics sections, and Standard has none of them; the table has no PowerPoint-export row. Prezi's help centre documents a Download PPTX from the dashboard and describes the result as static slides, one per frame, but the article names no plan for it, and Prezi's other pages disagree: the help centre's credits article lists 'Download and PDF export' as a Plus perk, while a June 2026 Prezi blog lists PPT export on Standard.

That download remains untested on every plan. In the newer AI editor, our free account's Download tab locked PDF, SCORM and a PowerPoint option labelled editable ('Bewerkbare PPTX' in the Dutch interface we used) to Plus. We have not opened an exported .pptx, so do that on a Plus trial, if the trial unlocks the download, which we have not checked: export one deck, open it in PowerPoint and try to change four slides before you commit a library to Prezi.

Keep the content somewhere you control. Write the actual words in a document of your own and use Prezi as the presentation layer, so that if you leave, the switching cost is redoing a design rather than rewriting the substance. That single discipline defuses most of the lock-in. And read the comparison table, not the cards, before you pick a tier. From a Netherlands exit on 28 September 2026 the individual ladder is Standard €5, Plus €15 and Premium €25 a month, all billed annually, and PDF export, portable presentations and presenter view only start at Plus -- so the tier you actually need is usually one above the one the entry price suggests.

Frequently asked questions

Can I pay for Prezi month to month?

Not as the pricing page shows it. Read on 28 September 2026 from a Netherlands exit, the page has no monthly-billing switch, and every paid card -- Standard €5, Plus €15 and Premium €25 a month on the Individual tab, and the Business and EDU plans too -- says billed annually, with no monthly-billed price anywhere on the page. So the real commitment is €60, €180 or €300 for a year. The page advertises a 14-day free trial, which is the closest you get to a month-to-month test: use it on one real presentation before you pay for a year. We have not checked whether the trial needs a card or renews automatically.

Do I need Plus to import my PowerPoint slides?

Probably not. The Plus card advertises importing existing PowerPoint slides, but the comparison table (read 28 September 2026) ticks the PowerPoint converter on Standard too; only the free Basic plan lacks that converter. Importing a PowerPoint through Prezi AI is a separate route, which Prezi's plan guide lists for Basic as well: the help centre says it spends AI credits, and the pricing table gives Basic and Standard 500 of them while Plus and above are unlimited. Beyond lifting that credit cap, Plus is where the presenting, branding and output features start: on the comparison table PDF export, portable presentations, presentation recording, presenter view, voice-over, smart branding and Live Prezi remote presenting are all ticked from Plus up, and on our free account the AI editor's PowerPoint download was locked to Plus as well. Try the import on the Standard trial; pay for Plus if you need one of those.

What does Premium add over Plus?

Read on 28 September 2026 with every section of the comparison table opened: presentation analytics and priority technical support, plus five infographic rows -- SQL connectors, iFrame import integrations, callouts, page linking and custom map import -- are marked Premium only on the Individual tab. The Premium card also mentions advanced online training. That is €10 a month more, €120 a year billed annually, and it does not change the export: the export rows on the table are the same on Plus and Premium. Pay for it if you need to see how viewers engage with a shared prezi or connect SQL data sources to infographics.

What should I weigh before buying Operations & Workflow software?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.
