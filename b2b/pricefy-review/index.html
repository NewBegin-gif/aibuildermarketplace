Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 28 Sep 2026

A genuinely free tier: $0 for up to 50 SKUs, 5 competitors, daily price updates and AI automatch. Paid, with yearly billing saving 25%: Starter $49/mo ($37 annually), Pro $99/mo ($74), Business $189/mo ($142) and Enterprise $499/mo ($374). Starter covers 100 SKUs, Pro 2,000, Business 15,000 and Enterprise 25,000. Every paid plan adds unlimited competitors, twice-daily updates, email alerts and Excel/CSV reports; dynamic repricing starts on Pro, and autopilot repricing with MAP/MSRP monitoring on Business.

Every plan, free included, carries AI product matching, competitor monitoring and daily price updates — what you buy going up is SKU volume and automation, not the core. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

When the paid plan is worth it

Pricefy's Free plan covers up to 50 SKUs, 5 tracked competitors, and one price check per day, which suits a small store that just wants basic competitor visibility without repricing automation. The concrete push toward paid comes fast: Starter, at $49/month ($37/month billed annually, a 25% discount), only raises the catalog cap to 100 SKUs, though it does unlock unlimited competitors and doubles price checks to two per day. Merchants who actually need automated repricing have to go further still — Dynamic Repricing sits on Pro ($99/month, up to 2,000 SKUs), and Autopilot Repricing with MAP/MSRP monitoring only appears on Business ($189/month, up to 15,000 SKUs).

What upgrading does not solve is match quality. The AI Automatch engine that pairs your SKUs to competitor listings is the same across every tier. Variants, bundles, refurbished listings and multipacks still need manual confirmation in the dashboard before repricing rules are safe to activate, regardless of how much you pay.

How to actually use Pricefy

Setup starts with a free signup that needs no credit card, then connecting a store — Pricefy offers one-click integrations for Shopify, WooCommerce, Magento, BigCommerce, PrestaShop and Wix — after which the catalog imports automatically through the connection or via API. Pricefy's marketing claims you can be live within 2 minutes of signup, but that refers to account creation, not full catalog matching. AI Automatch pairs SKUs to competitor listings first by EAN, GTIN, UPC or SKU codes, falling back to an AI/ML comparison of names, brands, descriptions and images when codes are missing.

The documentation cites 95%+ match accuracy and one case of 8,000 SKUs matched against six competitors overnight. Where users actually get stuck is after matching: variants, bundles, refurbished listings and multipacks are prone to mismatches and need manual confirmation in the dashboard before any repricing rule is activated, and catalogs above 50 SKUs will hit the Free plan's cap mid-setup.

Frequently asked questions

What does Pricefy cost?

Free for up to 50 SKUs and 5 competitors. Paid runs $49, $99, $189 and $499 a month billed monthly, or $37, $74, $142 and $374 billed yearly — a 25% saving. Read on 17 August 2026 and unchanged on 28 September 2026. Note the $49 Starter tier, which our earlier write-up skipped entirely.

Which tier do I need for my catalogue?

SKU count is the meter: 50 free, 100 on Starter, 2,000 on Pro, 15,000 on Business and 25,000 on Enterprise. Competitors become unlimited from Starter up, together with twice-daily updates, email alerts and Excel/CSV reports; the free tier stays at 5 competitors and one daily update. If you are under 50 SKUs the free tier is a real plan rather than a trial, which is unusual in this category.

What is the difference between dynamic and autopilot repricing?

Dynamic repricing arrives on Pro and lets you set rules that adjust your prices against competitors. Autopilot, on Business, runs those adjustments without you approving each one, and that tier is also where MAP and MSRP monitoring lives — the compliance side, if you sell brands that police minimum pricing.

What should I weigh before buying E-commerce software?

Check your platform and version specifically. Shopify support is close to universal in this category, while WooCommerce, BigCommerce, Magento and headless setups vary a lot, and 'supported' sometimes means a generic API rather than a maintained app. A listing in your own platform's app store, with recent reviews there, is a better signal than the vendor's logo wall.
