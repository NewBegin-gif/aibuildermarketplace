Premium pays for itself at 14 items, not 17

This is one of the few decisions on this site that is pure arithmetic, so here is the arithmetic -- including a correction to our own earlier note. The free plan is $0 a month; you pay the product base cost plus shipping per order and nothing else. Premium is $39 a month, or $24.99 a month billed yearly, which the page states as 36% off. What Premium buys is printed as two separate lines: up to 33% off new products and up to 20% off all products, plus custom branding and mentorship.

Enterprise is quoted. Take a typical item at roughly $8.77 of fulfilment cost. A 33% discount saves $2.89 on it. Divide the monthly fee by that saving and Premium breaks even at 13.5 items a month -- call it 14. On annual billing the fee is $24.99, so break-even falls to 8.6 items, call it 9. Our earlier note put it at 16-17 orders a month; that was wrong on both counts and this replaces it.

Two details in that correction matter more than the numbers themselves. First, the discount applies per item, not per order, so an order containing three shirts counts as three -- a store with a two-item average basket hits the monthly break-even at seven orders, not fourteen. Second, the two discount lines are not one offer. The 33% is quoted on new products and the 20% on all products, so 20% is the realistic figure across an established catalogue: at 20% the saving is $1.75 an item and break-even moves out to 23 items monthly, or 15 on the annual plan.

The honest answer is a range -- somewhere between 14 and 23 items a month on monthly billing -- and the way to narrow it is to look up the discount on the specific products you sell. Below that volume the free plan is genuinely cheaper, and Printify's model is on your side there: they make nothing until you sell, so there is no pressure in staying free while you find out.

The variable the spreadsheet cannot hold is quality, and it is the thing upgrading does not fix. This is a middleman marketplace, and print and shipping vary by the provider you pick, not by the plan you are on -- no tier fixes a bad print partner. Order samples first, from the specific provider, before either the free plan or Premium is the question.

How to actually use Printify

Start on the free plan, and start by ordering samples -- of your own design, from the specific print provider you intend to use, to your own address. Nothing on the subscription side of this decision matters if the print is wrong, and provider quality is the one variable no plan changes. Then count items, not orders, for a full month. That is the unit the Premium discount works on and therefore the unit your break-even is in: roughly 14 items a month on monthly billing at the 33% ceiling, 9 on annual, and 23 and 15 respectively if your catalogue only carries the 20% line.

Before you upgrade, look up the actual Premium discount on your top five products rather than accepting the headline. The page quotes up to 33% on new products and up to 20% on all products, and the difference between those two moves your break-even from 14 items to 23 -- which is the difference between Premium paying for itself and Premium being a monthly fee you are carrying for the branding.

Frequently asked questions

What does Printify cost?

Nothing to start: the free plan is $0 a month and you pay only product cost plus shipping. Premium is $39 a month, or $24.99 billed yearly (36% off). Read on 7 September 2026.

When is Premium worth paying for?

When your item volume clears the break-even. Printify prints two discount lines -- up to 33% off new products and up to 20% off all products -- so at roughly $8.77 of fulfilment cost the fee pays for itself somewhere between 14 items a month and 23, or 9 to 15 on annual billing. The discount counts items, not orders, so a two-item basket halves the order count you need. Run it on your actual bestseller rather than on the average.

How does it compare with a premium-blank supplier?

On margin, Printify usually wins: lower product and shipping costs mean more profit per sale. What you trade is the garment itself, since suppliers built around heavyweight blanks charge more for the same shirt. If your brand competes on quality rather than price, that trade may go the other way.

What should I weigh before buying Growth & Revenue software?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Small teams get the most out of this category when one clear problem is already costing real hours or revenue; buying ahead of that just adds cost and another login. Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.
