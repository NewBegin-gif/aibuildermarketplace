What buyers report about Proton Drive

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Proton Drive ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • End-to-end encrypted storage from a Swiss provider with 5 GB free — for privacy-sensitive files it is the reference point.
  • • Download speeds are excellent, and it bundles into the Proton Unlimited subscription alongside Mail, VPN and Pass.
What they complain about
  • • Upload speed is the recurring complaint. On a 400 Mbps line reviewers measure sustained uploads of 30-50 Mbps, and small files are worse because encryption overhead is per file. One reviewer on symmetrical gigabit reports 93.6 KB/s.
  • • There is no search function in the Android or iOS apps — only on the web. For a storage product that is a real gap.
  • • Mobile app bugs are reported.
  • • The privacy costs money, and reviewers who do not need it say so.

Two people should never buy two accounts

Proton publishes six plans and the per-gigabyte rate is not the story in any of them — the seat count is. Read on their own site in euros from a Dutch address, with annual billing on. Drive Plus is 200 GB at €3.99 a month, and Proton Unlimited 500 GB at €9.99. Duo is 2 TB for up to two users at €14.99, and Family 3 TB for up to six at €23.99.

Drive Professional is €7.99 per user for 1 TB each. Free is 5 GB. The sharpest finding sits between Unlimited and Duo. Two people buying an Unlimited account each pay €19.98 a month and get 500 GB apiece — one terabyte between them. Duo is €14.99 for the pair and gives them two terabytes. That is 25% less money for twice the storage, and there is no scenario in which two Unlimited accounts is the right purchase.

If you are a couple, a co-founder pair or two people sharing a household bill, this is the entire decision. The same shape repeats one step up. Three separate Unlimited accounts cost €29.97; Family covers up to six people for €23.99. So from three users onward Family is cheaper than buying individually, and it stays cheaper all the way to six. At that point it works out at €4.00 per person, close to what Drive Plus alone costs, except that everyone also gets VPN, Mail, Calendar and Pass.

That makes Drive Professional the plan to look at hardest. At €7.99 per user it is exactly double Family's per-head cost at six people, and what the doubling buys is the business wrapper — team administration — rather than storage or features. For a six-person company that is €47.94 a month against €23.99. Worth it if you need central control of accounts and offboarding; a straightforward overpayment if you are six people who trust each other.

One last thing the pricing page will not point out. The monthly penalty is not uniform. Drive Plus, Family and Professional all cost almost exactly 25% more billed monthly. Unlimited costs 30% more, and Duo 33% more — and those are the two plans most people end up on. If you are going to pay monthly, know that you are paying the steepest surcharge precisely where the surcharge is worst.

Every plan carries a 30-day money-back guarantee, so the annual commitment is less binding than it looks.

How to actually use Proton Drive

Set up account recovery before you upload anything. Proton cannot read your files, which is the product, and the direct consequence is that losing your credentials without a recovery method means losing the data — by design, with no support ticket that fixes it. This is the one step that is genuinely irreversible if skipped. Then test the trade before you commit. Because encryption is end-to-end there is no server-side search inside your documents, previews are more limited than Google Drive or Dropbox, and third-party integrations are thinner.

Put a realistic working folder in the free 5 GB and try to find something in it the way you normally would. If that works for you, everything else here is arithmetic; if it does not, no tier fixes it. Count the people before you pick the plan, because that is what the ladder is built on: one person on Plus or Unlimited, two on Duo (never two Unlimited accounts), three to six on Family, and Professional only if you need central administration.

Check the currency switch too — the page serves CHF, EUR and USD, and the figures above are the euro ones. Every plan has 30 days' money back, so the annual rate is the sensible default once the plan is right.

Frequently asked questions

What does Proton Drive cost?

Read on 17 August 2026 from a Dutch address, so in euros: Free gives 5 GB; Drive Plus is €3.99 a month on annual billing (€47.88 a year) for 200 GB; Proton Unlimited is €9.99 (€119.88) for 500 GB plus VPN, Mail, Calendar and Pass. The page switches between CHF, EUR and USD, so your figure depends on where you buy.

Which plan makes sense if two of us need the storage?

Proton Duo, which our previous write-up missed entirely: 2 TB shared across up to 2 users at €14.99 a month on annual billing (€179.88 a year), against €9.99 for Unlimited's 500 GB for one. For a household of more, Family gives 3 TB across up to 6 users at €23.99. For a business it is Drive Professional at €7.99 per user for 1 TB each.

Is the free tier usable?

It is 5 GB, which is enough to try the encryption and the document editor and not much else — a phone's photo roll will fill it. What you do get free is the full end-to-end encryption and the online document and spreadsheet editor, so it works as a genuine trial of the privacy model rather than a crippled demo.

What should I weigh before buying IT & Productivity software?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.
