What Proton does

Privacy-first suite from the makers of ProtonMail — encrypted mail, calendar, drive, VPN and password manager under one account, with a free tier and paid bundles.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Storage is the axis, and the jump from Drive Plus to Unlimited is not really about storage at all: you go from 200 GB to 500 GB, but you also start paying for Mail, Calendar, Pass and VPN whether or not you wanted them. If all you need is encrypted file storage, the bundle is where the money leaks. The other thing to price honestly is the encryption itself — end-to-end encryption is the reason to be here, and it is also why per-gigabyte this costs more than the mainstream drives.

That is a fair trade if privacy is the requirement, and a poor one if you just want cheap space.

When the paid plan is worth it

Proton Free is 1 GB of mail storage, one address and no custom domain — that last part is the whole decision. If you want mail on your own domain, the free plan cannot do it at any volume, so the question is only which paid tier. Read on their own plans page on 2 August 2026, in euros: Mail Plus €4.99 a month or €47.88 a year (€3.99/mo) gives 15 GB, 10 addresses and 1 custom domain. Proton Unlimited €12.99 or €119.88 a year (€9.99/mo) gives 500 GB, 15 extra addresses, 3 domains, and folds in VPN, Pass and Drive. Duo €19.99 or €14.99/mo annual covers 2 users and 2 TB; Family €29.99 or €23.99/mo covers 6 users and 3 TB; Visionary €39.99 or €29.99/mo raises the domain limit to 10.

The upgrade that actually pays is Unlimited rather than Mail Plus, and not for the mailbox: at €9.99 a month annual it replaces a separate VPN and password manager subscription. If you already pay for either of those, price the bundle against your current stack rather than against Mail Plus. Storage on every tier is shared with Drive, so a photo backup eats your mail allowance.

If you are buying this for a company rather than yourself, the consumer ladder above is the wrong one to read. Proton for Business is priced per user, and the entry tier is the answer to the question most people arrive with — mail on your own domain. Read on their business pricing page on 18 August 2026, in euros: Mail Essentials is €6.99 per user per month on yearly billing, which is €83.88 per user per year, and carries 15 GB per user, 3 custom domains and 10 addresses per user. Workspace Standard is €12.99 per user per month yearly (€155.88 per user per year) and raises that to 1 TB per user and 15 domains, adds VPN with 140 countries instead of 10, team password vaults and meetings up to 100 people. Workspace Premium is €19.99 per user per month yearly (€239.88 per user per year) for 3 TB per user, 20 domains, 250-participant meetings, data-retention policies and unlimited Lumo AI.

Two things worth doing the arithmetic on before you choose. First, the headline is the yearly rate. Monthly billing is €7.99, €14.99 and €24.99 for the same three tiers, so paying month to month costs roughly 14%, 15% and 25% more — the gap widens as you go up, which is the opposite of what most people assume. Second, the domain limits are the real tier boundary for a small company, not the storage: three custom domains on Mail Essentials is generous for one brand and immediately tight for an agency or a holding structure with several. Count your domains first, then look at the gigabytes.

How to actually use Proton

The order of the setup steps is not cosmetic — get it wrong and you lose mail. Proton documents five: verify the domain with a TXT record (protonmail-verification=…), create the addresses, then change the MX records, then SPF, DKIM and DMARC.

Their own warning is the one to read twice: "Please create corresponding email addresses on Proton Mail before changing the MX records." Once the MX records point at Proton, anything sent to an address that does not yet exist there is not delivered — it does not queue at your old host, it fails. So make every address first, including the aliases nobody uses, and only then touch MX.

Three details catch people out. Two MX records are needed, and your old ones must be deleted or given a higher priority number. There must be exactly one SPF record on the domain, so merge rather than add if you already have one. And all three DKIM records are required, not the first one. Proton recommends p=quarantine for DMARC. Propagation is "a few minutes or a few hours, depending on your previous TTL settings" — lower the TTL a day ahead if you can.
