Proton Review (2026)

The IT and productivity layer is where small, reliable tools quietly give you hours back — or, done wrong, add another login to babysit. So where does Proton actually fit?

What Proton does

Privacy-first suite from the makers of ProtonMail — encrypted mail, calendar, drive, VPN and password manager under one account, with a free tier and paid bundles.

Pricing (2026)

Read in a live browser on their own pricing page, 31 Jul 2026, in euros — the page has a CHF/EUR/USD switch, so the currency matters. Free gives 5 GB. Drive Plus (200 GB) lists at €4.99 a month and was showing €3.99 with a 20% discount, billed €47.88 a year. Proton Unlimited (500 GB plus VPN, Mail, Calendar and Pass) lists at €12.99 and was showing €9.99, billed €119.88 a year. Above that sit Duo (2 TB, up to 2 users) at €19.99 list, Family (3 TB, up to 6 users) at €29.99, and Drive Professional at €9.99 per user for 1 TB each. There is a 30-day money-back guarantee. The discounts shown are promotional — check the list price, because that is what renewal tends to return to. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

The honest knock

Storage is the axis, and the jump from Drive Plus to Unlimited is not really about storage at all: you go from 200 GB to 500 GB, but you also start paying for Mail, Calendar, Pass and VPN whether or not you wanted them. If all you need is encrypted file storage, the bundle is where the money leaks. The other thing to price honestly is the encryption itself — end-to-end encryption is the reason to be here, and it is also why per-gigabyte this costs more than the mainstream drives. That is a fair trade if privacy is the requirement, and a poor one if you just want cheap space.

What buyers report about Proton

Recurring themes from Capterra, Cloudwards, CyberInsider and long-term user tests, read in August 2026. We have not run Proton ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • End-to-end encryption that is actually the default, and Proton Sentinel catching real phishing — one six-month tester reports it flagging a fake Proton renewal email. 93% positive sentiment across 121 reviews.
  • • The Unlimited bundle covers Mail, VPN, Drive, Pass and Calendar with 500 GB for $9.99/month on annual, $7.99 on two-year. Prices have come down since earlier years.
What they complain about
  • • Still more expensive than mainstream mail, and you are paying for the privacy rather than the features.
  • • Attachments do not display inline — you open and download them one at a time, which reviewers find tedious.
  • • The cheapest rates need a one- or two-year commitment; monthly costs noticeably more.
Best for: teams and individuals for whom encryption is an actual requirement rather than a preference — handling client material, legal or medical files, or anything where a provider being able to read your data is itself the problem.
Who should skip it: if you mainly need cheap capacity or deep collaboration features, where the mainstream drives give more space and better co-editing per euro, or you would be paying for the Mail and VPN half of the bundle without using it.

The natural comparison is Internxt — the other privacy-first storage we cover — Proton's advantage is the surrounding suite and a long track record; compare on whether you want the bundle or only the drive, because that is what you are really choosing between. Decide by which one fits the job above, not by the louder brand.

Frequently asked questions

Is Proton secure and reliable enough to depend on?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.

Who should not use Proton?

Skip it if you mainly need cheap capacity or deep collaboration features, where the mainstream drives give more space and better co-editing per euro, or you would be paying for the Mail and VPN half of the bundle without using it. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
