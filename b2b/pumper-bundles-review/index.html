What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The bill therefore rises exactly when the app is working, and it rises in steps: pass $300 and you owe $9.99, pass $1,000 and you owe $29.99, pass $5,000 and you owe $49.99. Read that in both directions. It makes the free plan unusually generous — the whole app, not a stripped demo — and it makes every upgrade buy you nothing new, only permission to keep going. The one number the pricing table never defines is how that attributed extra revenue is measured, and it is the number the entire ladder rests on.

What Pumper Bundles does

Shopify bundle app by Profit Pumper: unlimited product bundles, quantity breaks, volume and cart discounts, BOGO and free-gift offers, with cart drawer and page builder integrations. The plans are a revenue ceiling rather than a feature ladder — all four carry identical features.

When the paid plan is worth it

Read the ladder as a take rate and the decision gets simple. Starter is $9.99 once the app has generated $300, Growth $29.99 past $1,000 and Unlimited $49.99 past $5,000, so the share of attributed revenue you hand back falls from about 3% at the Growth threshold to roughly 1% at the Unlimited one. Every upgrade costs less than the revenue that triggered it, which is the whole design. Annual billing takes another slice: $94.68 against $119.88 on Starter, $269.88 against $359.88 on Growth and $449.88 against $599.88 on Unlimited — $25.20, $90 and $150 saved.

What paying more does not buy is a single extra feature, since all four tiers ship the same four. Nor does it buy a definition: the attributed-revenue figure that decides your tier is never explained on the listing, so an upgrade settles the invoice without settling the question of how the number was reached.

How to actually use Pumper Bundles

Start on Free and treat it as the real product, because it is — the whole app up to $300 of attributed revenue. Build one offer rather than a catalogue: pick the product where a second unit is plausible and put a quantity break on it using a single discount type, Each Unit, Percentage or Flat, instead of testing all three at once. Then check where it renders. The listing names integrations for page builders (GemPages, PageFly) and cart drawers (Upcart, Monster Cart, AMP Slider cart, Opus Cart, qikify), which is another way of saying a themed store with a custom drawer needs verifying rather than assuming — look at the product page and the drawer on a phone before you announce the offer.

Write down your own revenue on that product first. Since the ceiling is measured on revenue the app claims to have created, your own before-and-after figure is the only check you hold on it.

Frequently asked questions

Is the trial seven days or fourteen?

Their two pages say different things, and we could not settle it on 19 September 2026. The App Store listing prints a 7-day free trial on each of the three paid tiers. The vendor's own site answers its FAQ question 'Is Profit Pumper free to start?' with a free 14-day trial, full access to every feature, no credit card required. Both were read the same day. It is a week either way rather than a fortune, but it is the week in which you are meant to prove the app lifts your average order value, and a bundle test on a slow product needs the longer one. Assume seven, since that is what the page that actually bills you says.

What is the most this can cost, $39.99 or $49.99?

The listing says $49.99 and the vendor's own FAQ says $39.99, which is the second thing their two pages disagree about. Under 'Are there any hidden charges?' their site answers that Pumper charges a maximum of $39.99 a month and only when it generates meaningful extra revenue. The App Store listing, which is where the charge is actually raised, tops out at the Unlimited tier at $49.99 a month or $449.88 a year. A ten-dollar gap on the ceiling of a four-step ladder suggests one page was updated and the other was not — and the one you should believe is the listing, because Shopify bills from it.

Every tier has the same features. So what am I actually buying?

Permission for the app to keep earning. All four tiers ship the identical four items — unlimited bundles and quantity breaks, chat support seven days a week, bundles on page builders, cart drawer integrations — and what changes is a ceiling on the extra revenue the app is allowed to generate: $300 on Free, $1,000 on Starter at $9.99, $5,000 on Growth at $29.99, and no ceiling on Unlimited at $49.99. Read as a take rate that is good news going up: about 3% of the revenue that triggers the Growth step, roughly 1% at the Unlimited one, and every upgrade costs less than the revenue that caused it. Read as a buyer it means the free plan is the whole product rather than a stripped demo, and that no upgrade will ever add a feature.

How is that 'extra revenue generated by Pumper' measured?

Neither page says, and it is the number the entire ladder rests on. Both the listing and the vendor's site describe the tiers in terms of extra revenue generated by the app, and the vendor's site adds that its analytics attribute revenue to each offer type so you can see which ones move average order value. What neither states is the rule: whether an order that would have happened anyway counts when a bundle was merely shown, how a partly-returned bundle is treated, or over what window an order stays attributed. Since crossing a threshold is what moves you from $9.99 to $29.99 to $49.99, ask for the definition before you rely on the free tier's $300 as a real allowance.

What should I weigh before buying Growth & Revenue software?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Small teams get the most out of this category when one clear problem is already costing real hours or revenue; buying ahead of that just adds cost and another login. Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.
