What Pylon does

B2B customer support platform built for Slack and Microsoft Teams. Manage tickets, track issues, and deliver white-glove support directly where your clients chat.

Pricing (2026)

(2026) Per-seat with minimums: Starter about $59/seat/mo (3-seat min ≈ $177/mo), Professional ~$89 (3-seat min), Enterprise ~$139 (7-seat min ≈ $973/mo), billed annually. AI modules are separate: AI agents from ~$100/mo scaling with volume, premium AI assistant ~+$50/seat, phone ~+$35/seat. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

That said, its B2B-native design (Slack/Teams-first support, account-based views) is a genuine fit gap that classic ticket tools fake with workarounds.

The $139 tier costs $324 a seat

Pylon's tiers are per seat with minimums attached, and the minimums are doing more work than the rates. Starter is about $59 a seat with a three-seat floor, so $177 a month. Professional is around $89, also three seats, so $267. Enterprise is roughly $139 — but the floor jumps to seven seats, which makes the real entry $973 a month.

That last step is the one to look at twice. A team of three that needs something from the Enterprise feature set cannot buy three Enterprise seats; it buys seven. Divide $973 by the three people who will actually use it and the advertised $139 becomes $324 a seat. Going from Professional to Enterprise is not a 56% increase in the rate, it is a 3.6× increase in the bill, and four of those seats are colleagues you do not have.

Then there are the modules, which are where a realistic deployment stops resembling the pricing page. AI agents start around $100 a month and scale with volume; the premium AI assistant adds about $50 a seat; phone adds about $35 a seat. Put a three-person team on Professional with all three and the bill is $622 a month against a $267 sticker — 2.3× the tier price. Do the same on Starter and it is $532 against $177, which is 3.0×. On Enterprise at seven seats it is $1,668 against $973, or 1.71×.

Notice the direction of that. The modules cost the same whatever tier you are on, so the cheaper the tier, the more badly it misrepresents your actual bill — Starter's sticker is off by a factor of three. Anyone sizing this from the entry price is sizing it from the most misleading number on the page.

What you are buying for that is a genuine fit rather than a feature list: Pylon is built B2B-native, Slack and Teams first, with account-based views that classic ticket tools reproduce through workarounds. That is a real difference and it is worth money. Just model the whole basket — seats, AI agent, assistant, phone — before you compare it to anything, because the tier price alone is not a number you will ever pay.

How to actually use Pylon

Model the full basket before you compare Pylon to anything, because the tier price is not a number you will ever pay. A three-person team on Professional with the AI agent, the premium assistant and phone comes to about $622 a month against a $267 sticker. The same basket on Starter is roughly $532 against $177 — three times the headline, because the modules cost the same whatever tier you are on.

Check the seat minimums, which matter more than the rates. Starter and Professional both have a three-seat floor. Enterprise jumps to seven, which turns an advertised $139 a seat into $324 for a team of three — so if you need one Enterprise feature, price it as $973 a month rather than as a per-seat increase.

Then evaluate the thing that actually makes Pylon different rather than working through the feature list. Connect it to the Slack or Teams channels where your customers already talk to you, and see whether an account-based view of those conversations changes how your team works. That B2B-native design is a genuine fit gap classic ticket tools paper over with workarounds — and it is either how your support runs or it is not, which is a question the trial answers in a week.
