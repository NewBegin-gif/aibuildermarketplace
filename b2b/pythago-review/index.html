Your third store is the cheapest one you will add

Pythago charges per store, and the published ladder is $59 a month for one, $49 per store for two, and $39 per store from three up. Every tier carries the tracking itself — full Google Ads ROI tracking, product research and competitor monitoring — so the core is not what climbing the ladder buys. Three things are: priority support and advanced translations arrive at two stores, and automation tools and API access only at three.

If you need the API, your tier is decided for you and the volume decision is moot. Otherwise it is purely a volume decision, and it behaves oddly. Read it as marginal cost rather than as a rate card and the shape appears. One store is $59. Two stores is $98, so the second store costs you $39. Three stores is $117, so the third store costs $19 — less than half the advertised per-store price and the cheapest addition on the ladder.

From the fourth store on it is flat $39 each. If you run two stores and have been putting off a third, the pricing is not the reason to wait: the step from two to three is the smallest one Pythago ever asks you to take. Past three stores the model is honest and unforgiving in equal measure: it is linear, with no further breaks. Ten stores is $390 a month, twenty is $780. How to actually use Pythago

Start with one store on the month-to-month billing, but read the feature ladder first, because it is not flat. Every tier carries full Google Ads ROI tracking, product research and competitor monitoring. What the single-store tier leaves out is priority support and advanced translations, which start at two stores, and automation tools and API access, which appear only at three. If you intend to pull the data into your own stack, the API sets your tier rather than your store count — and you can cancel any time.

Know the marginal cost before you add one. Store one is $59, the second adds $39 for a $98 total, and the third adds only $19 for $117 — the cheapest store you will ever add. Every store after that is a flat $39. If you have been putting off a third store, the pricing is not the reason to wait. Then reconcile it against a source you already trust.

Connect the store you know best and check whether Pythago's ROAS figure matches what your ad platform reports, and where it differs — attribution windows, returns and shipping costs are the usual reasons. A dashboard you cannot reconcile is not worth $59 a month, and finding the discrepancy in month one is far cheaper than trusting it for a quarter.

Frequently asked questions

What can Pythago actually change inside my Google Ads and Shopify accounts?

It asks for write access to both, and on 22 September 2026 it spells out the limits itself in a section called What Pythago can and cannot do in your accounts. On Google Ads it connects through Google's official OAuth flow, so it never sees your password, and it states that write access is part of the job: it can pause campaigns and exclude a product or variant from an asset group. It says it never touches budgets or bids, and that automated actions are off by default and configured per campaign — so the destructive-sounding half is opt-in, campaign by campaign. On Shopify it reads and writes orders, products, markets and publications, and states it cannot edit your storefront, change your listings, contact your customers or issue refunds. That combination is consistent with the product: excluding a losing product from an asset group is the action the dashboard is there to justify. If you are not comfortable with pause-and-exclude rights, the tool still reports, but you are paying $59 a month for a read-only view of numbers you will act on by hand.

Where does my store data live, and what happens to it if I cancel?

Pythago publishes both answers on its homepage, read 22 September 2026. The data is hosted in Europe (the page names the CJ2 region), encrypted with TLS 1.2 or higher in transit and AES-256 at rest. It is kept while your account is active and deleted within 30 days of cancelling — or immediately, if you ask. Access is revocable without going through support: you can disconnect from your Pythago settings, or revoke it directly from your Google and Shopify account pages, and the page says so in those words — no email, no support ticket. For a tool that holds order-level revenue and cost data for every product you sell, those are the three things worth knowing before you connect, and they are unusually plainly stated. What the page does not publish is a data processing agreement or a subprocessor list, so if your own customers' order data crossing into an analytics vendor needs paperwork on your side, that is a question for support rather than something the site answers.

What should I weigh before buying E-commerce software?

Check your platform and version specifically. Shopify support is close to universal in this category, while WooCommerce, BigCommerce, Magento and headless setups vary a lot, and 'supported' sometimes means a generic API rather than a maintained app. A listing in your own platform's app store, with recent reviews there, is a better signal than the vendor's logo wall.
