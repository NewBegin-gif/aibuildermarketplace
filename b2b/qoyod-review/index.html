Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 5 Sep 2026

Billed in Saudi riyal, which is pegged to the dollar at 3.75, so the conversion is exact rather than approximate. Annual pricing runs four tiers. Basic is 1,200 SAR (~$320) for 1 user and 1 location, covering ZATCA Phase 1, invoices, payments and credit notes. Pro is 1,800 SAR (~$480) for 3 users and 3 locations, adding ZATCA Phase 2, quotations and the full procurement cycle.

Advanced is 2,211 SAR (~$590) for 5 users and 5 locations, adding fixed-asset management, depreciation and accounting dimensions; that price is shown struck through from 3,300 SAR (~$880) under a “33% off for a 3-year term” banner. Enterprise is quote-only, with unlimited users, custom API integrations and a dedicated account manager. A free trial is offered, and the monthly toggle costs more — the site advertises “save up to 20%” on annual.

Add-ons are priced per year on top: payroll 120 SAR per active employee (~$32), POS 600 SAR per user (~$160), extra system users 240 SAR each (~$64) and extra locations 480 SAR each (~$128). Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

The honest knock

Qoyod's pitch is surviving Saudi e-invoicing, but Basic covers ZATCA Phase 1 only — Phase 2 is the integration phase, where invoices are cleared with the tax authority in real time, and that starts at Pro. If Phase 2 applies to you, your actual floor is 1,800 SAR and not 1,200. The second thing to price before you sign: Basic is genuinely one user and one location, and every extra seat is 240 SAR a year, which is a fifth of the whole Basic plan per person.

A four-person shop on Basic pays 1,200 + 3×240 = 1,920 SAR, more than Pro costs while still sitting on Phase 1. Add a single POS user at 600 SAR and the arithmetic tips further. Work out your seat and location count first, then pick the tier — doing it the other way round is how the add-on line becomes the biggest number on the invoice.

What Qoyod does

Saudi accounting and e-invoicing platform built around ZATCA compliance, used by 25,000+ businesses. From 1,200 SAR a year (~$320) for one user and one location — but ZATCA Phase 2, the part most businesses actually need, starts on the Pro tier.

What buyers report about Qoyod

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Qoyod ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Customer service comes up more than any feature. Reviewers in both Arabic and English describe support as excellent and reachable, and a March 2025 reviewer says the platform keeps getting better and fits a small business well.
  • • Ease of use for people without an accounting background — reviewers who describe themselves as non-accountants say invoicing and daily bookkeeping are manageable.
What they complain about
  • • The mobile app's stability is the dominant complaint. Reviewers report freezing on the first screen, the app working for two or three days and then needing a reinstall, and login or password-reset failures. A February 2026 reviewer says it mostly does not work and is not useful when it does.
  • • Updates break things: a July 2025 reviewer reports that after some updates parts of the system stop working.
  • • Parts of the product are browser-only. The March 2025 reviewer who otherwise praises it asks for product creation inside the app instead of the browser, and for a fuller HR module.
  • • Support silence in specific cases: a July 2025 reviewer says they cannot download the journal ledger report from the site and got no response from technical support.
  • • One December 2025 reviewer says they could not cancel their subscription or stop the card charges and resorted to cancelling the card. That is a single account and the vendor has not replied publicly to it, so treat it as one merchant's experience — but check the cancellation path before you enter card details.

Where the ratings disagree. The public record for Qoyod is thin in an unusual way. Capterra lists the product with zero reviews, and its G2 page is not readable without an account, so the only sizeable body of public buyer writing is the Google Play listing: 3.6 stars across roughly 200 reviews. That number rates the mobile app, not the accounting platform — almost every one-star there is about crashes, logins and updates rather than about bookkeeping, ZATCA filing or reporting. So the low score is evidence about the phone client and says nothing either way about the web product where the actual accounting happens.

When the paid plan is worth it

The tiers are cheap and the add-ons are where the money is, so build your total before comparing anything. These are annual rates. Basic is 1,200 SAR (about $320) for one user and one location with ZATCA Phase 1. Pro is 1,800 SAR (about $480) for three users and three locations, adding ZATCA Phase 2, quotations and the full procurement cycle. Advanced is 2,211 SAR (about $590) for five users and five locations with fixed assets, depreciation and accounting dimensions.

Because the riyal is pegged to the dollar at 3.75, those conversions are exact rather than approximate. The step that actually matters is Basic to Pro, and it is not about seats: ZATCA Phase 2 is the e-invoicing integration phase, so if your business falls under it, Pro is not an upgrade but the minimum. Check that before you price anything else. Then the add-ons, all per year on top: payroll at 120 SAR per active employee (about $32), point of sale at 600 SAR per user (about $160), extra system users at 240 SAR each (about $64) and extra locations at 480 SAR each (about $128).

Payroll for twenty staff is 2,400 SAR -- twice the entire Basic plan. A twenty-person shop on Pro with payroll and two POS users is 1,800 + 2,400 + 1,200 SAR, about $1,440 a year, against the $480 the tier suggests. One caution on the Advanced price: the 2,211 SAR is shown struck through from 3,300 SAR under a banner reading 33% off for a three-year term. That is a three-year commitment, not a discount on the annual plan, so read which one you are agreeing to.

How to actually use Qoyod

Establish which ZATCA phase applies to you before you look at a single price, because that decision picks your tier for you: Phase 1 fits Basic, Phase 2 puts you on Pro at minimum. Then count three numbers — users, locations, POS terminals — and add them up at 240, 480 and 600 SAR a year respectively, since those add-ons are what separate the headline from the invoice. Use the free trial to test the part that is hardest to undo: import your existing data early rather than at the end, because migration is the step where accounting switches actually fail.

The 33% Advanced discount is tied to a three-year commitment, so treat it as a three-year decision and not a discount.
