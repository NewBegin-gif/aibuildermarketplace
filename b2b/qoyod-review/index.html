The honest knock

The entry tier does not do the thing the product is sold on. Qoyod's pitch is surviving Saudi e-invoicing, but Basic covers ZATCA Phase 1 only — Phase 2 is the integration phase, where invoices are cleared with the tax authority in real time, and that starts at Pro. If Phase 2 applies to you, your actual floor is 1,800 SAR and not 1,200. The second thing to price before you sign: Basic is genuinely one user and one location, and every extra seat is 240 SAR a year, which is a fifth of the whole Basic plan per person. A four-person shop on Basic pays 1,200 + 3×240 = 1,920 SAR, more than Pro costs while still sitting on Phase 1. Add a single POS user at 600 SAR and the arithmetic tips further. Work out your seat and location count first, then pick the tier — doing it the other way round is how the add-on line becomes the biggest number on the invoice.

When the paid plan is worth it

The tiers are cheap and the add-ons are where the money is, so build your total before comparing anything. Annual: Basic 1,200 SAR (about $320) for one user and one location with ZATCA Phase 1; Pro 1,800 SAR (about $480) for three users and three locations, adding ZATCA Phase 2, quotations and the full procurement cycle; Advanced 2,211 SAR (about $590) for five users and five locations with fixed assets, depreciation and accounting dimensions. Because the riyal is pegged to the dollar at 3.75, those conversions are exact rather than approximate. The step that actually matters is Basic to Pro, and it is not about seats: ZATCA Phase 2 is the e-invoicing integration phase, so if your business falls under it, Pro is not an upgrade but the minimum. Check that before you price anything else. Then the add-ons, all per year on top: payroll at 120 SAR per active employee (about $32), point of sale at 600 SAR per user (about $160), extra system users at 240 SAR each (about $64) and extra locations at 480 SAR each (about $128). Payroll for twenty staff is 2,400 SAR -- twice the entire Basic plan. A twenty-person shop on Pro with payroll and two POS users is 1,800 + 2,400 + 1,200 SAR, about $1,440 a year, against the $480 the tier suggests. One caution on the Advanced price: the 2,211 SAR is shown struck through from 3,300 SAR under a banner reading 33% off for a three-year term. That is a three-year commitment, not a discount on the annual plan, so read which one you are agreeing to.

How to actually use Qoyod

Establish which ZATCA phase applies to you before you look at a single price, because that decision picks your tier for you: Phase 1 fits Basic, Phase 2 puts you on Pro at minimum. Then count three numbers — users, locations, POS terminals — and add them up at 240, 480 and 600 SAR a year respectively, since those add-ons are what separate the headline from the invoice. Use the free trial to test the part that is hardest to undo: import your existing data early rather than at the end, because migration is the step where accounting switches actually fail. The 33% Advanced discount is tied to a three-year commitment, so treat it as a three-year decision and not a discount.
