The honest knock

The product is sold as a platform plus a dedicated account team, and there is no self-serve entry: the only route in is Request Demo. That carries a cost which never appears on the page — you cannot size this against your ad budget before you talk to someone, and you cannot put it next to a competitor on paper. It matters more than usual in this category, because the direct peers do publish.

Against those two, Quartile is an unknown until you have a quote in hand. The introductory offer that used to soften this has been withdrawn. So there is no published discount left to negotiate against, and the quote is the only figure in the entire transaction. What buyers report about Quartile

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Quartile ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Nearly all the praise is about people rather than software: named account managers who communicate well and bring the strategy. That is worth knowing, because the account team is what you are actually buying here.
  • • The single-ASIN, single-keyword and waterfall campaign structures, plus bulk campaign management across several marketplaces at once, are what analysts running European Amazon accounts credit for the time saved.
What they complain about
  • • The granular structure cuts both ways: an analyst managing hundreds of campaigns (October 2025) says oversight and decision-making get harder at that scale, and another reviewer wants a simple metrics view for someone who is not in the tool daily.
  • • The people implementing the changes are usually not the people you speak to, so reviewers cannot find out why a given change was made.
  • • The one detailed long-term negative (November 2023, a consumer-goods CEO after eighteen months) is severe: campaign structures that de-ranked them on top keywords and bloated campaigns leaking spend, which the reviewer blames on the company scaling headcount faster than account quality.

Where the ratings disagree. 4.5 across 94 reviews, and the shape matters more than the number: the five-star reviews tend to be short and about one named account manager, while the low ratings are long and about media results. When the review is really a review of a person, the service is only as good as staffing continuity — ask who will run your account and what happens when they move on.

There is no plan to weigh, so weigh the quote

There is nothing to compare here, and that is the finding. quartile.com/pricing serves the same page as the homepage: no tiers, no rate card, no entry figure, and one call to action, which is Request Demo. The dollar amounts the site does show are volume claims about their own book, not prices: over $2B in annual retail ad spend managed and over $25B in managed revenue across 5,300+ customers in 32 countries.

Those claims are still useful, because you can divide them. $2B of managed ad spend across 5,300 customers is an average of about $377,000 a year per customer, or roughly $31,000 a month. That is the size of business Quartile is built around, and if your ad spend is an order of magnitude below it you are not their median customer and the quote will tell you so. It also lets you build a yardstick from peers who do publish.

Our own dossiers have Teikametrics at $149 a month billed annually for up to $10K of monthly ad spend, plus 3% of everything above that (read 2026-08-01), and BidX at €495 a month for its self-service Amazon tier (read 2026-08-06). Run Quartile's own average customer through Teikametrics' published formula — $149 plus 3% of the $21,446 above the threshold — and you get about $792 a month. At $100K of monthly spend the same formula gives $2,849, and at $500K it gives $14,849.

That is not what Quartile charges; it is the order of magnitude a percentage-of-spend model produces at your size, and it is the number to have in your head when the quote arrives. Handle the introductory offer carefully, because it is gone. In August 2026 the referral route carried 40% off a new customer's first three months; on 11 September 2026 quartile.com/partners publishes no customer discount at all. It is now a referrer-reward scheme — you introduce a brand by email, and the reward is described only as one that may vary.

The single published number a buyer could have negotiated against has been withdrawn, and nothing quantified replaced it. One more figure to hold at arm's length: the 41% average ROAS increase on their homepage is Quartile's own number, measured by Quartile on Quartile's customers. Nobody independent has checked it and neither have we. Ask what the sample was and over what period before you let it weigh on the decision.

How to actually use Quartile

There is no self-serve route in, so the first step is the demo — which means the preparation matters more than usual. Go in with your monthly ad spend written down, because every plausible pricing model in this category is anchored to it, and with the peer figures beside it: Teikametrics at $149 a month plus 3% of spend above $10K, BidX at €495 a month self-serve. Those are the only published comparisons you will have.

Ask for four things in writing. The pricing basis — flat fee, percentage of spend, or both, and what happens when spend rises mid-contract. The contract term and notice period. What the dedicated account team actually includes and what is billable on top of it. And the sample behind the 41% ROAS claim: how many accounts, over what period, measured against what baseline. Do not count on the referral discount that was there in August 2026: the referral page now rewards the referrer, not you, and names no amount. If one is offered, convert it to a full-year figure before it moves anything.
