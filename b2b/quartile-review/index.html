The honest knock

Everything about the sales motion here is a conversation. The product is sold as a platform plus a dedicated account team, and there is no self-serve entry: the only route in is Request Demo. That carries a cost which never appears on the page — you cannot size this against your ad budget before you talk to someone, and you cannot put it next to a competitor on paper. It matters more than usual in this category, because the direct peers do publish. Our own dossiers have Teikametrics at $149 a month billed annually for up to $10K in monthly ad spend, plus 3% of spend above that (read 2026-08-01), and BidX at €495 a month for its self-service Amazon tier (read 2026-08-06). Against those two, Quartile is an unknown until you have a quote in hand. The introductory offer runs into the same wall. New customers arriving through a referral link get 40% off their first three months, which is a real discount — but it is forty per cent of a number nobody publishes, on the first three invoices only, with the normal rate from month four. Treat it as a reason to ask for the quote, not as a saving you can put in a spreadsheet. One more figure to handle carefully: the 41% average increase in ROAS on their homepage is Quartile's own number, measured by Quartile on Quartile's customers. Nobody independent has checked it and neither have we, so ask what the sample was and over what period before you let it weigh on the decision.

There is no plan to weigh, so weigh the quote

There is nothing to compare here, and that is the finding. quartile.com/pricing serves the same page as the homepage: no tiers, no rate card, no entry figure, and one call to action, which is Request Demo. Two audiences are named — Small Business and Mid-Market/Enterprise — with no rate attached to either. The dollar amounts the site does show are volume claims about their own book, not prices: over $2B in annual retail ad spend managed and over $25B in managed revenue across 5,300+ customers in 32 countries. Those claims are still useful, because you can divide them. $2B of managed ad spend across 5,300 customers is an average of about $377,000 a year per customer, or roughly $31,000 a month. That is the size of business Quartile is built around, and if your ad spend is an order of magnitude below it you are not their median customer and the quote will tell you so. It also lets you build a yardstick from peers who do publish. Our own dossiers have Teikametrics at $149 a month billed annually for up to $10K of monthly ad spend, plus 3% of everything above that (read 2026-08-01), and BidX at €495 a month for its self-service Amazon tier (read 2026-08-06). Run Quartile's own average customer through Teikametrics' published formula — $149 plus 3% of the $21,446 above the threshold — and you get about $792 a month. At $100K of monthly spend the same formula gives $2,849, and at $500K it gives $14,849. That is not what Quartile charges; it is the order of magnitude a percentage-of-spend model produces at your size, and it is the number to have in your head when the quote arrives. Handle the introductory offer carefully. New customers arriving through a referral link get 40% off their first three months. Three months at 40% off is 1.2 months of value, which across a first year is a 10% discount, not a 40% one — and it is 10% of a number nobody publishes, with the full rate from month four. Treat it as a reason to ask for the quote, not as a saving you can put in a spreadsheet. One more figure to hold at arm's length: the 41% average ROAS increase on their homepage is Quartile's own number, measured by Quartile on Quartile's customers. Nobody independent has checked it and neither have we. Ask what the sample was and over what period before you let it weigh on the decision.

How to actually use Quartile

There is no self-serve route in, so the first step is the demo — which means the preparation matters more than usual. Go in with your monthly ad spend written down, because every plausible pricing model in this category is anchored to it, and with the peer figures beside it: Teikametrics at $149 a month plus 3% of spend above $10K, BidX at €495 a month self-serve. Those are the only published comparisons you will have. Ask for four things in writing. The pricing basis — flat fee, percentage of spend, or both, and what happens when spend rises mid-contract. The contract term and notice period. What the dedicated account team actually includes and what is billable on top of it. And the sample behind the 41% ROAS claim: how many accounts, over what period, measured against what baseline. If a referral discount comes up, price it correctly before it influences anything: 40% off three months is 10% off the first year, and the full rate applies from month four. Budget from the month-four number, not the month-one number.

Best for: brands and sellers already running paid media across several retail networks at once — Amazon Sponsored Ads and Amazon DSP, Walmart, Instacart, Criteo, Google, Meta and Microsoft — who would rather hand the whole set to one team than wire together a tool per channel. The fixed account team is the actual product here, not a support tier, and if you were going to hire an agency anyway then a platform with people attached is a fair shape for the money.
Who should skip it: if you want to know what something costs before you take a sales call — nothing on this site will tell you, and two of its direct competitors will. Skip it as well if your spend sits on one marketplace and you are comfortable running the bids yourself: the multi-network reach and the account team are what you would be paying for, and neither does much for a single-channel seller with a routine that already works.
