What QuickBooks UK does

Intuit's accounting platform for the UK market — bookkeeping, VAT and Making Tax Digital submissions, invoicing and payroll as a paid add-on. Annual plans run from £108 for sole traders to £604.80 for the five-user tier, excluding VAT, with a 30-day trial.

Plus is cheaper per user than Essentials

Read on their own UK pricing page in a live browser on 31 July 2026, annual toggle on, in pounds and excluding VAT: Sole Trader Plus £108 a year, Simple Start £172, Essentials £410.40 for three users and Plus £604.80 for five. Divide the top two by their seat counts and the ladder inverts. Essentials works out at £136.80 per user per year; Plus, despite costing £194.40 more, works out at £120.96 — 12% cheaper per head.

And the step itself is cheaper still: £194.40 buys two extra users, so each of them costs £97.20, below both published per-seat rates. If you are on Essentials and a fourth person needs access, the upgrade is not a penalty, it is the cheapest seat on the page. Now the number that should actually decide this purchase, which is not a tier at all. At the time of reading, a 90% first-year discount was running, showing those same plans as £10.80, £17.20, £41.04 and £60.48.

The page says plainly that the full rate returns after one year. So Sole Trader Plus goes from £10.80 to £108, and Plus from £60.48 to £604.80 — a tenfold step in year two, arriving at the exact moment your entire bookkeeping lives inside the product and switching is hardest. That is not a trick, it is written on the page; it is just a number almost nobody budgets for.

Two things to add on top before you compare Intuit to anything. VAT at 20% applies to all of it, which turns Plus's real year-two cost into £725.76. And payroll is a paid add-on rather than part of any tier, so if you employ anyone, the tier price is not your bill. There is a 30-day free trial, and it is worth being clear about what it can and cannot tell you.

Thirty days is enough to judge whether the bookkeeping fits how you work. It is not capable of telling you anything about a price change eleven months out. Budget from the list price, treat the 90% as a one-off rebate on year one, and make the decision on the £108-to-£604.80 range rather than on the £10.80-to-£60.48 one.

How to actually use QuickBooks UK

Write down the year-two price before you start the trial, because it is the only number that will still matter in twelve months. It is £108 for Sole Trader Plus, £172 Simple Start, £410.40 Essentials and £604.80 Plus, all excluding VAT and all roughly ten times what the discounted first year shows. Add 20% and add payroll if you employ anyone. Count your users next, not your features. Essentials caps at three and Plus at five, and because Plus is cheaper per head, the seat count settles the tier more cleanly than the feature list does.

If you are at three users and growing, buying Plus straight away costs £97.20 per extra seat, which is less than either tier's per-user rate. Use the 30 days on the work you actually dread — the VAT return, the bank reconciliation, the invoice chase — rather than on the setup wizard. And do the import during the trial, not after: the cost of this decision is not the subscription, it is that your books end up inside it, which is exactly what makes the year-two step so hard to walk away from.

What should I weigh before buying Finance & Accounting software?

Ask your accountant before you buy; it is the cheapest step in this whole decision. What matters is whether it exports in the format they already work in, whether the ledger mapping survives that export, and whether they can access it directly. A tool your accountant has to re-key by hand costs more than it saves.
