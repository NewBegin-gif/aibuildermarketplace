QuickSigner Review (2026)

By Daan Haket · Researched and written with an AI assistant · who does what

The price is the argument, and the legal ceiling is the limit. Throughout this review we weigh it against DocuSign.

Best for

cost-conscious teams that want certified e-signing (ISO 27001, AATL) with unlimited documents from ~$5/mo and a real free tier.

Skip it if

you need advanced workflow features and integrations of the big suites — this wins on price, not on ecosystem depth.

What QuickSigner does

Legally-binding e-signatures with unlimited documents and no per-envelope caps: send a secure link and recipients sign from any device without creating an account. ISO/IEC 27001-certified, AATL signatures, valid across the US, UK and EU — a fair-priced DocuSign alternative.

When the paid plan is worth it

Occasional signers fit comfortably on the free Personal plan: the pricing table and llms.txt both list 5 documents per month (read 20 October 2026), up to 3 signers per request, one document per request, and a 5 MB file cap. The concrete push toward paying comes from hitting any of those caps — running out of the monthly document quota, needing more than 3 signers on a single send, or needing to batch more than one document at once.

Business, at $5 per user/month ($48/year), removes the document quota, raises the signer limit to 10, and allows 10 saved templates. QuickSigner's pricing table and llms.txt both list Word support as a Business addition, but the FAQ describes signatures added to 'your PDFs and Word files' without naming a plan, so which claim governs the free plan is unresolved. What upgrading to Business does not solve: the per-request document cap only moves from 1 to 3, and there is still no API access.

Both require Professional ($15/user/month, $144/year) — and even there, each signed document sent through the API costs an additional $0.30, so embedding signatures into another system carries a running per-document cost that no tier eliminates.

How to actually use QuickSigner

Getting a first document signed starts with a free account — no credit card required — then uploading a document, adding signer emails, and placing signature, date, or text fields before sending a secure link; recipients sign from any device without an account. Which file formats the free plan accepts is unclear: the pricing table and llms.txt list the free tier as PDF-only, with Word added at Business, while the FAQ describes signing PDFs and Word files without naming a plan.

The monthly cap is now settled at 5: llms.txt, which said 3 in August, matched the pricing table when re-read on 20 October 2026. Either way the free plan allows one document per send, up to 3 signers, and a 5 MB file size limit, so anyone testing a multi-document or multi-signer workflow hits a wall quickly. For API integration, a developer sandbox is free, but only with watermarked test documents; moving to production requires the Professional plan ($15/user/month) plus $0.30 per signed document, so teams building an integration cannot test the real, unwatermarked flow before committing to the paid tier.

Before choosing a tier, count the three numbers the plans cap per send rather than per month: signers on one request (3, 10 or 50), documents bundled into one request (1, 3 or 10) and file size (5, 10 or 15 MB). A scanned multi-page lease can pass 5 MB long before the free monthly quota runs out. The FAQ says signed files are stored in Google Cloud with encryption; check that against your own data-residency rules before uploading client documents, because no region choice is published.

Frequently asked questions

What does QuickSigner cost?

Personal is free for 5 documents a month. Business is $5 per user a month, or $48 a year, and that is where documents become unlimited. Professional is $15 per user a month, or $144 a year, and adds bulk send, teams, OTP verification and the API. Annual billing saves 20%. Read on 17 August 2026 and again, unchanged, on 27 September 2026.

Is the free plan really free, or capped?

Capped, and the cap is documents rather than time: five a month, one document and three signers per request, PDF only. Crucially it still includes AATL sealing, so what you sign on the free tier is as legally solid as on the paid ones — you simply cannot sign much.

What does the API cost?

The developer account is free with unlimited test requests; production signing costs from $0.30 per signed document and requires the Professional plan. For a product that otherwise sells unlimited documents per seat, that per-document rate is the line to model if you are embedding signing in your own software.

Can QuickSigner produce a Qualified Electronic Signature?

No. Its own llms.txt, read on 20 October 2026, says QuickSigner does not issue Qualified Electronic Signatures, which the EU requires for a small set of transactions and for filings with some government portals. What it applies is an Advanced Electronic Signature in the PAdES format, sealed with an AATL certificate and a qualified timestamp, with an audit trail of signer identity, IP address and timestamps. The same file reminds US users that the ESIGN Act excludes wills and testamentary trusts, family-law matters, court orders and filings, foreclosure and eviction notices and certain insurance cancellations.

Does QuickSigner come with contract templates?

Two different things carry that name. The FAQ (read 20 October 2026) says there is no in-app gallery of pre-written legal documents: a template is a document you upload and save with signature fields and recipient roles in place, ten on Business and more above, also callable through the API by templateId. Separately, the site publishes a free download library — an NDA, a master service agreement, an LLC operating agreement, a residential lease, W-9 and I-9 forms among them — in PDF and Word without an account. Those are starting documents to edit, not saved in-app templates.

What does wiring up the QuickSigner API involve?

Per llms.txt (read 20 October 2026): two HTTP calls — upload the file to /api/v1/files, then create the request at /api/v1/requests with recipients, signing order and field coordinates — authenticated with a single API key in the Authorization header, with no OAuth flow and no SDK. Webhooks fire on RECIPIENT_SIGNED and REQUEST_COMPLETED, and embedded signing opens the recipient's signUrl in an iframe with a signedRedirectUrl back to your app. The interactive reference is at quicksigner.stoplight.io.

How does QuickSigner's price compare with Signeasy and signNow?

On each vendor's own page: Signeasy has no free plan, and its Personal tier at $10 a month billed yearly sends 5 documents a month with no templates (read 4 October 2026); signNow's Business is EUR 8 a month billed annually with 100 signature invites a year (read 26 September 2026). QuickSigner's free plan already sends 5 documents a month, and Business at $48 a year per user has no document cap. For a small team sending a few dozen agreements a month the per-seat price is lower and the quota question disappears — check the per-request caps above before relying on that.
