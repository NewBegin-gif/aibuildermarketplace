What QuickSigner does

Legally-binding e-signatures with unlimited documents and no per-envelope caps: send a secure link and recipients sign from any device without creating an account. ISO/IEC 27001-certified, AATL signatures, valid across the US, UK and EU — a fair-priced DocuSign alternative.

Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 17 Aug 2026

(re-verified 2026-08-17 against QuickSigner's own machine-readable pricing page) Personal is free: 5 documents a month, 3 signers and 1 document per request, 5 MB maximum, PDF only, with signature and stamp fields and AATL sealing. Business is $5 per user a month ($48 a year) and is where documents become unlimited — 10 signers and 3 documents per request, 10 MB, Word files, 10 saved templates, reminders and signing order.

Professional is $15 per user a month ($144 a year): 50 signers and 10 documents per request, 15 MB, OTP phone verification, bulk send, teams and the eSign API. Annual billing saves 20% and paid plans carry a 14-day trial. The API has a free developer account with unlimited test requests; production costs from $0.30 per signed document and requires Professional. ISO/IEC 27001:2022-certified and AATL, legally binding in the US, UK and EU. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

When the paid plan is worth it

Occasional signers fit comfortably on the free Personal plan: the pricing table lists 5 documents per month (llms.txt says 3, unreconciled), up to 3 signers per request, one document per request, and a 5 MB file cap. The concrete push toward paying comes from hitting any of those caps — running out of the monthly document quota, needing more than 3 signers on a single send, or needing to batch more than one document at once. Business, at $5 per user/month ($48/year), removes the document quota, raises the signer limit to 10, and allows 10 saved templates. QuickSigner's pricing table and llms.txt both list Word support as a Business addition, but the FAQ on the site's own PDF-signing page describes the free flow as already accepting both PDF and Word, so which claim governs the free plan is unresolved. What upgrading to Business does not solve: the per-request document cap only moves from 1 to 3, and there is still no API access. Both require Professional ($15/user/month, $144/year) — and even there, each signed document sent through the API costs an additional $0.30, so embedding signatures into another system carries a running per-document cost that no tier eliminates.

How to actually use QuickSigner

Getting a first document signed starts with a free account — no credit card required — then uploading a document, adding signer emails, and placing signature, date, or text fields before sending a secure link; recipients sign from any device without an account. Which file formats the free plan accepts is unclear: the pricing table and llms.txt list the free tier as PDF-only, with Word added at Business, while the FAQ on QuickSigner's own PDF-signing page describes the same free flow as accepting both PDF and Word. The monthly document cap is unsettled too — pricing table says 5, llms.txt says 3 — either way the free plan allows one document per send, up to 3 signers, and a 5 MB file size limit, so anyone testing a multi-document or multi-signer workflow hits a wall quickly. For API integration, a developer sandbox is free, but only with watermarked test documents; moving to production requires the Professional plan ($15/user/month) plus $0.30 per signed document, so teams building an integration cannot test the real, unwatermarked flow before committing to the paid tier. Frequently asked questions

What does QuickSigner cost?

Personal is free for 5 documents a month. Business is $5 per user a month, or $48 a year, and that is where documents become unlimited. Professional is $15 per user a month, or $144 a year, and adds bulk send, teams, OTP verification and the API. Annual billing saves 20%. Read on 17 August 2026.

Is the free plan really free, or capped?

Capped, and the cap is documents rather than time: five a month, one document and three signers per request, PDF only. Crucially it still includes AATL sealing, so what you sign on the free tier is as legally solid as on the paid ones — you simply cannot sign much.

What does the API cost?

The developer account is free with unlimited test requests; production signing costs from $0.30 per signed document and requires the Professional plan. For a product that otherwise sells unlimited documents per seat, that per-document rate is the line to model if you are embedding signing in your own software.

Is QuickSigner secure and reliable enough to depend on?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.

Who should not use QuickSigner?

Skip it if you need advanced workflow features and integrations of the big suites — this wins on price, not on ecosystem depth. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
