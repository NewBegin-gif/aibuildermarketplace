We tested it · 2026-10-01

A free account, used on QuillBot's AI Detector, AI Humanizer and Paraphraser. We ran the detector over the texts of known authorship we used for our Pangram test the same day: three passages from public-domain books, a paragraph one of us typed for the test, three paragraphs a language model wrote for our own reviews, and one Claude wrote on the spot. We added a control paragraph written in stock chatbot phrasing, sent it through the Humanizer, and checked the rewrite in QuillBot's detector and in Pangram.

  • The detector scored all eight texts 0% AI, the four machine-written ones included. Our three review paragraphs and the paragraph Claude wrote on the spot each came back '100% Human-written'. Pangram, given the same eight texts the same day, labelled every one correctly.
  • It does catch AI text that sounds like AI. The control paragraph, which opens “In today's fast-paced digital landscape”, scored 100% AI, with the stock phrases underlined and the opening sentence listed as 'High'. In our test it picked up the style, not the authorship: plainly written machine text passed as human.
  • The Humanizer and the detector disagree. The Humanizer rewrote the control paragraph and reported '0 AI signals' on its own output. QuillBot's detector scored that same output 100% AI. Pangram scored it 100% AI too and added that it 'appears to have been paraphrased or rewritten'. The rewrite mostly swapped words: 'seamless' became 'smooth', 'In conclusion' became 'Simply put'.

The honest knock

The honest framing is that this is a box of writing utilities -- paraphraser, grammar, plagiarism, AI detection -- rather than somewhere you actually write. General AI chat tools now overlap much of it. And treat the free AI detector as weak: in our test of 1 October 2026 it rated four plainly written machine texts 100% human and only caught a paragraph full of stock chatbot phrases. Note the sticker too: the advertised €8.33 a month assumes you pay for the year, and month-to-month is €19.95, about 2.4 times as much.

Monthly costs 2.4 times the annual rate

There are two real prices, and the gap between them is the decision. Read on quillbot.com/premium from the EU on 7 September 2026 and re-read unchanged on 26 September: Premium is €19.95 a month if you pay monthly, or €8.33 a month if you pay for the year. That is 2.4 times, and the page itself labels the annual route 58% off. So a monthly plan does exist -- it is simply priced to push you onto the annual one.

Team is an enquiry rather than a published price. That changes the shape of the decision rather than removing it. Over twelve months the annual route is €99.96 and the monthly route €239.40, so the right to leave costs about €139 a year. You are weighing that against a twelve-month commitment to a category moving underneath you: general AI chat tools now overlap most of what QuillBot does, and twelve months is exactly the period over which that overlap has been widening.

Judge the free tier on what it is, which is a demo rather than a smaller version of the product. Paraphrasing is capped at 125 words and two modes. A typical paragraph runs 100 to 150 words, so the free plan handles roughly one paragraph at a time -- a 1,000-word article is eight separate passes. That is enough to see whether the output reads well and nowhere near enough to do a day's work, which is precisely the design.

What you are buying is honest enough on its own terms: a box of writing utilities -- paraphraser, grammar checker, plagiarism check, AI detection -- rather than a place you actually write. If those four are jobs you do repeatedly, €100 for a year is a fair price for a tool that does them properly. What upgrading does not fix is the shape of the product: Premium removes the word caps, not the fact that this is a utility belt rather than a writing environment. If you are hoping to replace a writing workflow, no tier here is that.

What we found running QuillBot ourselves

Hands-on · 2026-10-01

The first findings are at the top of this page. The rest:

  • Every result came with the prompt 'Want your text to sound more authentic? Refine with Paraphraser', including on texts the detector had just rated 100% human.
  • Signed out, the detector ran four checks (689 words) and then asked us to sign up. It will not run below 40 words, and under 80 it shows 'For more accurate results, add at least 80 words'; it still scored our 71-word paragraph.
  • The free Paraphraser's 125-word cap is real, and stricter than it sounds. Given 219 words, it reported '219/125 words. Limit exceeded' and showed only the first sentence of the rewrite, with the rest blurred behind an upgrade button, not the first 125 words.
  • Not tested: Premium, the plagiarism and grammar checkers, the Humanizer's daily use limit, languages other than English and texts longer than 219 words. The detector page showed model version v7.1.0.

How to actually use QuillBot

Run the free tier against the hardest thing you have, not the easiest. The cap is 125 words per paraphrase and two modes, so pick one dense paragraph -- jargon, a long sentence, something you would genuinely struggle to rewrite -- and see whether the output is better than your own second draft. That is the whole test. Do the same with the other three utilities while you are there: run the grammar checker over something you have already proofread, the plagiarism check over a piece you know the sources of, and the AI detector over text you wrote yourself.

Each one tells you more about the tool's false-positive rate than any review will. Then decide with the real price in front of you. Premium is €19.95 a month month-to-month or €8.33 a month billed annually -- about €139 more over a year to keep the right to cancel. Since the free tier does not expire, take the monthly plan for one working month first if you are unsure; waiting costs you nothing except the paraphrases you do a paragraph at a time.

Frequently asked questions

Can I get my money back if QuillBot Premium isn't for me?

Within 14 days, with conditions. The FAQ on quillbot.com/premium (read 26 September 2026) offers a 14-day money-back guarantee if you are a new user, have not had a refund from QuillBot before, and ask within 14 days of being charged; it reserves the right to refuse in cases of suspected fraud, shared accounts or abuse. On the annual plan, EUR 99.96 up front, that fortnight is the only exit, so test the paraphraser on real work in the first days rather than the last.

Can I pause QuillBot Premium instead of cancelling?

Yes. The same FAQ says that if you will not use Premium for a while you can pause it from your Account page and you are not billed for that time, with no need to cancel. That matters most on the monthly plan at EUR 19.95: a student who writes in term time can pause over the summer rather than paying for months without essays, which narrows the gap with the EUR 8.33 annual rate.

Can I use one Premium account on several devices or for a team?

On your own devices, yes: the FAQ allows Premium on all of them, but on no more than 10 at a time. Sharing one account across people is a different matter - shared accounts are one of the grounds on which QuillBot says it can refuse the money-back guarantee. For a group, the FAQ offers a volume discount on a single invoice from 5 members, and the Team plan carries no public price; it routes to an enquiry (read 26 September 2026).

Does a workflow tool replace my current setup or sit on top of it?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.
