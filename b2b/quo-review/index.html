What Quo does

The business phone system formerly known as OpenPhone. Shared numbers, AI call summaries and a clean app — professional calling and texting for startups and small teams.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Call reliability is the recurring complaint — G2 logs hundreds of connection and disconnection reports, and calls get shaky the moment you're off Wi-Fi. There's also no video conferencing, no 24/7 support on lower tiers, international calling costs extra, and the CRM integrations are shallow (the HubSpot app scores ~3.3/5).

What buyers report about Quo

Recurring themes from G2, Capterra and practitioner reviews, read in August 2026. We have not run Quo ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • 4.7 across 3,200+ reviews with over 90,000 businesses on it — the interface is what everyone names, and teams say having calls, texts and voicemail in one shared inbox genuinely organises them.
  • • The September 2025 rebrand from OpenPhone came with $105M in growth funding, so the company is not going anywhere.
What they complain about
  • • The $19 per user headline understates it. A five-person Starter team on annual billing lands around $97-$108 a month rather than the $75 the page implies — 29-44% above sticker.
  • • Sona costs extra past 10 free calls a month, and the AI credit pricing frustrates reviewers at scale.
  • • Support breaks down exactly when it matters: slow responses, unexpected account flags and unresolved tickets during technical problems.
  • • Audio instability and dropped calls on unstable Wi-Fi, which matters if your team works from home.
  • • It suits freelancers and micro-teams. Larger teams report hitting scalability and reliability limits.
Best for: micro-teams of 2–5 people working mostly on Wi-Fi who want a clean, modern phone app built around a shared inbox, with AI call summaries (Sona).

Frequently asked questions

Will Quo work for a remote or distributed team?

Generally yes, since this category is built for it, but the details that bite are timezone handling, how per-seat cost scales as the team spreads, and whether numbers, routing or coverage exist in the countries your people actually sit in. Availability varies far more by country than the marketing pages suggest, so check your specific regions before committing.

Who should not use Quo?

Skip it if you're scaling past ~10 users and need rock-solid call infrastructure, you make lots of calls off Wi-Fi, or you need video — Nextiva or RingCentral fit better. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
