Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 19 Sep 2026

The plans
  • Free — $0 a month
    100 export credits a month (1,200 a year on annual billing), 2,400 email views a year, unlimited contact and company views, 1 seat, no card required
  • Starter — $39 a month, or $29 on annual billing
    5,000 export credits a month (60,000 a year), unlimited email views, data enrichment, Chrome extension, ABM list builder, advanced filtering, 1 seat
  • Pro — $59 a month, or $49 on annual billing
    10,000 export credits a month (120,000 a year), everything in Starter, 1 seat
  • Business — $99 a month, or $79 on annual billing
    20,000 export credits a month, credits granted upfront, 3 seats
  • Enterprise — custom — Talk to Sales
    custom credit grant and user count

Five tiers in USD. Free is $0 with 100 export credits and 200 email views a month (1,200 and 2,400 a year). Starter is $39 a month, or $29 on annual billing, for 5,000 export credits a month (60,000 a year). Pro is $59 or $49 for 10,000 a month. Business is $99 or $79 for 20,000 a month (240,000 a year) and, for the first time, up to three seats instead of one.

Enterprise is quote-only. The annual discount is a flat 20% on the price and nothing else: the credit allowance is identical either way. Two meters run separately — export credits are spent downloading to CSV or syncing to a CRM, email credits are spent revealing an address in the platform or the Chrome extension — while contact and company insight views are unlimited on every tier including Free.

Email views are unlimited on all paid tiers. The FAQ, re-read on 19 September 2026, states that unused credits do not roll over and that they reset at the start of each new billing cycle -- on 10 September that same question answered the other way round, so size the plan for an ordinary month rather than a busy one and confirm the rule before you buy.

It also states that direct-dial and mobile numbers are included on Starter, Pro and Business. Note that the main /pricing/ page carries no figures at all — it only routes you to Prospect or Email Verifier — and that the site answers a plain HTTP request with 403, so the figures above come from a browser reading of the Prospect pricing page one level down. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Contact and company views are unlimited on every plan including the free one, so the demo will feel generous; what you actually pay for is pulling those records out, and separately for revealing an email address, which is a second meter the plan cards do not put next to the first. Free gives 100 exports and 200 email views a month, which is a sample rather than a workflow. The second thing to check is seats: Starter and Pro both include exactly one, so a two-person sales team is on Business at $99 a month before it needs the extra credits.

Credits do not roll over: the FAQ, re-read on 19 September 2026, says unused credits reset at the start of each new billing cycle, so a plan sized for a busy month is simply wasted in a quiet one. That same question answered the other way on 10 September, which is reason enough to check it yourself before you commit. Underbuying is no clearer either: two figures on the page are not explained anywhere — Starter and Pro promise to "unlock an extra $250 / $500 worth of data", which is priced in dollars rather than in credits or records — and the cost of a credit once you run out is not published at all.

When the paid plan is worth it

Two meters run here and the pricing page keeps them apart. Export credits are spent downloading a list to CSV or syncing it to a CRM; email credits are spent revealing a verified address inside the platform or the Chrome extension. Contact and company insight views cost neither and are unlimited on every tier, free included, which is why the trial feels generous. Free meters email views at 200 a month; every paid tier makes them unlimited.

The vendor's FAQ said on 10 September 2026 that unused credits on paid plans roll over to the next billing cycle. Re-read on 19 September 2026, the same question answers the opposite: credits do not roll over and reset at the start of each new billing cycle. Take the live answer as the one that binds -- an underspent month is money gone -- and get it in writing if a rep tells you otherwise.

Upgrading fixes neither direction of a bad estimate. Buy too much and the surplus expires with the billing cycle; buy too little and nothing on the page says what a credit costs once the allowance is gone. Nor is the 'extra $250 worth of data' on Starter and '$500' on Pro explained anywhere — it is denominated in dollars rather than credits or records. And Starter and Pro carry one seat each, so a second user means Business at $79 whatever your volume.

How to actually use ReachStream

Use the free plan as the test it is built to be. It costs nothing, needs no card, and carries 100 export credits and 200 email views a month with the advanced filters and the Chrome extension — enough to pull a sample against your own ideal-customer profile and check it before any money moves. The vendor claims 95% data accuracy and 90% email deliverability; a sample is how you find out what those mean for your market.

Size yourself in exports rather than searches. Browsing and viewing company records burn nothing, so the only number that matters is how many contacts you actually download or push to a CRM in a month. Settle two things in writing before you commit: what an extra credit costs once the allowance runs out, which is published nowhere, and how many seats you get — the plan card says three on Business while the comparison table and the FAQ both say up to three.

Frequently asked questions

What does ReachStream cost?

Five tiers in USD, re-read on reachstream.com/prospect/pricing/ on 19 September 2026 and unchanged since 10 September. Free is $0 with 100 export credits and 200 email views a month. Starter is $39 a month, or $29 if you pay annually. Pro is $59 or $49. Business is $99 or $79. Enterprise is quote-only. The annual discount is a flat 20% off the price and nothing else: the credit allowance is identical either way, so paying yearly buys a discount rather than headroom.

Do unused credits roll over to the next month?

No -- and the vendor’s own FAQ has answered this both ways inside a fortnight. Read on 19 September 2026 it says unused credits do not roll over and reset at the start of each new billing cycle; on 10 September the same question on the same page said they roll over on paid plans. The live answer is the one to plan around, so size the tier for an ordinary month rather than your busiest, and if a sales rep tells you otherwise, get it in writing.

How many seats does each plan include?

One on Free, Starter and Pro. Business is the first tier with more, at up to three, and Enterprise is custom. For a lot of teams that is what picks the plan, not the volume: a two-person sales team is on Business at $99 a month ($79 on annual billing) however few records it exports. Worth settling early, because it is the one line on the pricing page that an upgrade cannot work around.

What happens when I run out of credits?

The page does not say, and that gap is wider now that rollover is off the table. No overage rate is published anywhere on the pricing page, so a busy month means negotiating mid-cycle with no reference price. Two related figures are unexplained in the same way: Starter and Pro advertise an extra $250 and $500 "worth of data", denominated in dollars rather than in credits or records. Ask what a credit costs and what that dollar figure actually buys before you commit.

What should I weigh before buying Sales & CRM software?

Most tools in this category plug in rather than replace. Check which direction the data flows and whether the sync is genuinely two-way, because a one-way push creates a second source of truth that your reps will quietly stop trusting. If it does aim to replace your CRM, price the migration honestly: history, custom fields and reporting rarely move across cleanly.
