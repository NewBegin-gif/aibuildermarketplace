Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 14 Sep 2026

Four levels, and the page keeps both billing terms in the markup rather than recomputing them. Lite is free forever for a single user. Billed yearly the seats are Starter $10, Business $15 and Enterprise $22 per seat a month; billed monthly Starter is $12 and Business $18, and the monthly column prints no Enterprise figure at all. Enterprise carrying a list price is new here — we described it as quote-only until this reading, and Business had no number in our dossier at all.

Seats are capped by tier rather than open: 1 user on Lite, up to 10 on Starter, up to 100 on Business and over 100 on Enterprise, with the AI-agent allowance per seat stepping 5, 10, 100 and unlimited beside them. The mechanism most reviews miss is Attendee Units, and it survives this redesign even though the plan cards no longer mention it.

AUs are attendees without a paid Reclaim seat in Smart Meetings of 3 or more people (two-person meetings do not need them), and they accumulate across a team account. Extra AUs are bought in packs on top of the seat, and the packs follow the same two terms. On yearly billing Starter packs run $8 for 3, $16 for 6, $24 for 9 and $32 for 12, and Business $12 for 6, $24 for 12, $36 for 18 and $48 for 24; billed monthly those become $10/$20/$30/$40 and $15/$30/$45/$60.

Enterprise packs are $18 for 10, $36 for 20, $54 for 30 and $72 for 40 on either term. If you schedule across people who do not have seats, the AU packs rather than the seat price are what determines your bill. One caveat on that arithmetic: the page still carries a launch-promo line saying AUs are 100% free through July 31st, a date that has passed, so confirm at checkout whether the packs are billing yet.

Discount codes appear periodically; on the day we read it a banner offered 30% off Starter and Business to new users. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

Correction after re-checking: Lite is free forever; Starter is $10 per seat/month billed yearly ($12 billed monthly), Business is $15 per seat/month yearly ($18 monthly), and Enterprise is $22 per seat/month on yearly billing only (not custom-quoted, and unavailable on monthly). The annual toggle is labelled SAVE 20%, not roughly 29%. Priced per seat. The vendor's own machine-readable pricing file carries the same four prices. (re-verified 2026-08-11)

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Google Calendar was first, but Microsoft 365, Outlook.com and Exchange-backed accounts are supported too (reclaim.ai/integrations/outlook-calendar, read 15 September 2026); Reclaim's own help centre still lists hosted and on-premise Exchange as unsupported. The free tier is quite limited. A genuine time-saver for the right setup, useless for the wrong one.

What Reclaim.ai does

Auto-schedules tasks, habits and meetings around your stated priorities. Google Calendar-first, so Outlook-heavy teams are out, and the free tier is limited. Paid seats run about $8–10/month on Starter and Plus, roughly 29% cheaper on annual billing.

What buyers report about Reclaim.ai

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Reclaim.ai ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • It defends focus time on your calendar automatically, and that is what earns 4.8 on G2 across 120+ reviews.
  • • There is a real free tier, and paid starts at $10 per seat.
What they complain about
  • • No native iOS app. It is on the public roadmap as In Planning and had not shipped as of July 2026 — the most-cited gap in G2 feedback.
  • • The free Lite tier has shrunk over time, from 3 calendars and 16 habits down to 1 and 3. Long-time free users describe that as being squeezed toward paying.
  • • Trustpilot sits at 2.7 across 14 reviews, mostly billing. Small sample, but the theme is consistent.
  • • Several users report Reclaim being hard to fully remove from Google Calendar after deleting the account, with notifications persisting.
  • • The team features that justify it organisationally — analytics, team OOO, delegated access — are all on Business or Enterprise, so the free tier does not show you what you would be buying.

When the paid plan is worth it

Lite includes 5 AI Agents, Focus Time, Habits, Buffer Time and Smart Meetings, but the pricing page marks it 'Limited integrations', 'Limited' AI Memories, 'Limited' AI Assistant (LLM chat) and 'Default settings' for time blocking customization, against 'Full customization' on paid tiers. Lite caps you at 2 connected calendars, 1 scheduling link, 5 AI agents, a 1 week scheduling range and 1 user team. Starter, $10 per seat/month on yearly billing ($12 monthly), lists an 8 week range, unlimited connected calendars, unlimited integrations, 3 scheduling links and 10 AI agents per seat.

Business at $15 yearly ($18 monthly) shows 12 weeks, unlimited scheduling links, 100 agents per seat, webhooks support, and up to 100 seats against Starter's 10. The vendor's machine-readable pricing file lists Team OOO Calendar and Delegated Access as 'No' on Lite and Starter, 'Yes' from Business. Enterprise is $22 per seat/month on yearly billing only. Upgrading does not close the documented Outlook gaps. Hosted or on-premise Exchange account holders 'will not be able to create accounts or add calendars', and the vendor says it won't add that support 'for the foreseeable future'.

Reclaim's own hashtags also do not work in the RSVP note of a Microsoft event.

How to actually use Reclaim.ai

The 2.0 FAQ argues for restraint. The vendor files it under 'Beta features > Reclaim 2.0' and sends 1.0 users to support for 'early access'. A good starting setup is your working hours, one Focus goal, 1-3 Habits, one Scheduling Link and any task or collaboration integrations you use regularly. On Agents it says 'Start small'. The 2.0 overview calls it 'currently in private beta and actively evolving.' Focus best practices: 'Start with a modest weekly target, then tune it after a week' and 'Protect 2+ hour blocks when possible'.

The FAQ lists five reasons 'An Agent may not schedule'. On a Scheduling Link where you're a co-organizer or main organizer, the booking page carries a 'Troubleshoot availability' toggle, which requires being logged in to Reclaim and shows why a given time isn't available. After the 14-day trial the account 'will be automatically switched to our free forever Lite plan' unless you upgrade; only 'if you're using features outside of the free Lite plan limit' does it 'roll into a free overage period for 28 days'.

Frequently asked questions

What does Reclaim.ai cost per seat?

Starter is $10 per seat a month with Business above it, yearly billing saving 20%, and a free tier below. But the seat is only half the price — see the attendee units below. Read on 17 August 2026.

What are Attendee Units and will I need them?

AUs cover attendees who do not have a paid Reclaim seat in Smart Meetings of three or more people; two-person meetings do not consume them, and they accumulate over the month. Extra packs cost $8 for 3 up to $32 for 12 on Starter, and $12 for 6 up to $48 for 24 on Business. If you schedule mostly with people outside your subscription, budget the packs alongside the seats.

Are there discount codes?

Reclaim runs them openly rather than hiding them: when we read the page it carried both a 30% new-user offer and a 20% code. Because those rotate, it is worth loading the pricing page before signing up rather than going straight to checkout from an app link.

What should I weigh before buying Operations & Workflow software?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.
