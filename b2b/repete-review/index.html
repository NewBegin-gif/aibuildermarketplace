rePete Review (2026)No Subscription, 3% of What It Brings

E-commerceResearched assessment · by Daniel HaketUpdated 2026-09-21

The pricing is honest and the arithmetic still deserves care, because this is a percentage with no ceiling on it.

Best for: Shopify stores selling consumables or anything with a natural replenishment rhythm — coffee, supplements, pet food, skincare — whose customers refuse subscriptions but do come back, and who would rather pay for results than carry another monthly app fee.
Who should skip it: if your repeat revenue is already large, because an uncapped 3% overtakes any subscription app well before six figures; your customers buy once and rarely return, where there is no reorder moment to predict; or you cannot get comfortable with an app that can edit orders, discounts and the storefront.

Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 19 Sep 2026

There is no subscription and no tier. rePete is free to install and takes a percentage of the sales it claims to have caused, billed in USD, on three rates that are defined by a clock rather than by judgement. Direct Sales is 3%, charged when rePete nudges a customer to reorder a product and they complete the purchase within 24 hours. Assisted Sales is 1%, charged on the same nudge when the purchase lands within 7 days.

Frequently asked questions

What will rePete actually charge me?

Nothing to install, and nothing at all if it produces nothing. Read on repete.me/pricing and on the App Store listing at apps.shopify.com/reorder-service on 19 September 2026: three commission rates in USD, with no subscription, no tier, no contract and cancellation at any time. Direct revenue is 3%, charged when rePete's own nudge drives the reorder and the purchase lands within 24 hours of the message. Influenced or assisted revenue is 1%, the same nudge when the purchase arrives within seven days. Upsells and cross-sells are 5%, charged on anything extra the customer adds beyond the reorder product itself. No cap, no volume discount and no enterprise rate is published anywhere, so the bill tracks the result the whole way up.

Do the texts and emails cost extra on top?

No, and that is the part that changes the comparison. The pricing page rules out SMS fees, email fees, per-contact fees, push fees, app fees and minimums by name, so email, SMS, WhatsApp and on-site reminders all sit inside the commission. In a category where SMS is normally metered on its own line, a store already paying a messaging platform to send reorder reminders should take that cost out of its stack before comparing rather than set it alongside. Compare what a reorder costs you in total, not the headline percentage.

Does it replace my subscriptions app, and how fast does it do anything?

rePete's own framing is that it does not replace a subscription programme but picks up the repeat revenue subscriptions structurally cannot reach — customers who reorder on their own rhythm rather than on a billing cycle. On speed, the page claims setup in three minutes and first predictions in under 24 hours, which is plausible because it learns from order history you already have rather than from campaigns you have to build. The honest flip side is that a store without a repeat-purchase history gives it very little to predict on. Treat the case-study figures on the same page — 4× repeat revenue against subscriptions, 36× ROI — as the named customer's claim rather than a measured benchmark.

What should I weigh before buying E-commerce software?

Check your platform and version specifically. Shopify support is close to universal in this category, while WooCommerce, BigCommerce, Magento and headless setups vary a lot, and 'supported' sometimes means a generic API rather than a maintained app. A listing in your own platform's app store, with recent reviews there, is a better signal than the vendor's logo wall.
