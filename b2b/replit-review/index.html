What Replit does

Build, deploy and scale software with AI as your co-pilot. Cloud IDE, instant deployments and Replit Agent that ships features for you.

Pricing (2026)

(2026-08-02, read on their own pricing page) Starter free: limited daily Agent credits, publish 1 project. Core $20/month including $25 of credits, up to 5 collaborators, 2 parallel agents. Pro $95/month billed annually including $100 of credits, up to 15 collaborators, 10 parallel agents — launched February 2026, replacing Teams. Enterprise custom, with SSO and single-tenant options. Billing is effort-based, and it reached existing Core and Teams subscribers on 1 July 2026. Documented bills show $0.25 per Agent checkpoint and $0.05 per Assistant checkpoint on top of the plan fee; credits do not roll over. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The credit model on AI-heavy building is unpredictable — a complex agent session can burn credits fast, and they expire monthly, so heavy AI users hit surprise costs. Great for learning and prototyping; watch the meter for production work.

When the paid plan is worth it

Starter is free with limited daily Agent credits and one published project, and it is enough to learn whether the Agent suits how you work. Beyond that the plan fee is the smaller half of the bill, so "is the paid plan worth it" is really "do I understand what the Agent costs". Read on their own pricing page on 2 August 2026: Core is $20 a month and includes $25 of credits, up to 5 collaborators and 2 parallel agents. Pro is $95 a month billed annually with $100 of credits, 15 collaborators and 10 parallel agents; it launched in February 2026 and replaced the old Teams plan. Enterprise is custom. Then the part nobody quotes. Documented bills show $0.25 per Agent checkpoint and $0.05 per Assistant checkpoint — one recorded period ran 632 Agent checkpoints and 965 Assistant checkpoints, $206.25 in checkpoints on top of the plan. At $0.25 a checkpoint your $25 of Core credits is about 100 Agent checkpoints a month. Budget against checkpoints, not against the $20, and note that effort-based billing reached existing Core and Teams subscribers on 1 July 2026 — check which basis you are on.

How to actually use Replit

Watch the checkpoint counter from the first prompt. It is the meter, and three things drive it: vague prompts, because the Agent iterates until it matches what you asked; debug loops, because it keeps spending on each failed attempt when it gets stuck on its own mistake; and broad scope, because a large instruction that breaks a working feature costs twice. So work in small, verifiable steps and stop it yourself when it starts going in circles. A precise brief costs fewer rounds than a conversation, and that is a difference measured in dollars here rather than in patience. Use the free Starter tier to find out how many checkpoints your kind of task actually takes before you pick a plan. If a single feature runs to twenty checkpoints, Core's $25 of credits is five features a month — which may be plenty, or may tell you Pro is the honest answer. Frequently asked questions

Is Replit secure and reliable enough to depend on?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.

Who should not use Replit?

Skip it if you need a stable, cost-predictable production environment — the expiring-credit model makes AI-heavy usage hard to budget. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
