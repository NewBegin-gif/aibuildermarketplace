Restream Review (2026)Each Extra Seat Costs $25

Growth & RevenueResearched assessment · by Daniel HaketUpdated 2026-08-31

The channel caps are the business model — each tier buys a few more destinations, and every extra teammate is a separate $25/mo seat on top of the plan. Price it at your real destination count and team size, not the sticker.

Best for: creators, podcasters and marketing teams who broadcast one show to many platforms at once, without extra encoding hardware.
Who should skip it: if you stream to a single platform (its native studio is free) or you need broadcast-grade production control — an OBS-style tool fits better.

Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 17 Aug 2026

(2026) A free plan (multistream to 2 channels, with Restream branding); Standard about $16/mo billed annually ($19 monthly, 3 channels), Professional $39/mo annual ($49 monthly, 5 channels, Full HD), Business around $199/mo annual (up to 8 channels). Extra team seats cost about $25/seat/mo on every paid tier. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

The per-channel price rises as you climb

Restream's ladder is sold on destination count, and destination count gets more expensive per unit the higher you go — the reverse of how volume pricing usually works. Free covers 2 channels with Restream branding. Standard is about $16 a month billed annually ($19 monthly) for 3 channels, which is $5.33 a channel. Professional is $39 annually ($49 monthly) for 5 channels and Full HD, or $7.80 a channel. Business is around $199 for up to 8, which is $24.88 a channel — 4.7 times Standard's rate.

Read it as marginal cost and the shape is clearer still. Going from Standard to Professional buys two more channels for $23, or $11.50 each. Going from Professional to Business buys three more for $160, or $53.33 each. Nobody pays $53 a channel for channels, which means Business is bought for something else — and our dossier does not record what that something is. Ask before you assume the jump is about destinations.

Then the line that catches teams out: extra seats are about $25 per seat per month on every paid tier, which is more than the entire Standard plan. Your second teammate costs 1.6× what the plan does. A three-person team on Standard is $16 plus two seats at $25, so $66 a month — more than Professional costs for one person, without Full HD. If more than one person touches the stream, the seat price is the number that decides your plan, not the channel count.

Monthly billing costs 19% more on Standard and 26% more on Professional. Price this at your real destination count and your real headcount, in that order, and the sticker will rarely be the answer.

How to actually use Restream

Count destinations and people separately, because both are metered and the seat is the one that catches teams out. Extra team seats run about $25 a month each on every paid tier — more than the entire Standard plan — so a three-person team on Standard is $66 a month, which is more than Professional costs for one person and without Full HD. Then test the thing that actually breaks, which is your upload.

Multistreaming takes one feed and pushes it to several platforms, so your bandwidth and encoder settings decide the quality on all of them at once. Run a full-length test stream to your real destinations, at your real bitrate, from the venue you will broadcast from — not a two-minute check from your desk. Use the free plan for that rehearsal, but know its limits: two channels and Restream branding on the output.

Fine for a test, wrong for a launch. And check where Full HD begins, because it arrives at Professional. If your platforms and your audience expect 1080p, Standard is not a cheaper version of the product — it is a different output quality, and no add-on fixes it.

The natural comparison is StreamYard or OBS Studio — StreamYard wins on beginner-friendly guest shows, OBS on free pro-grade control; Restream wins on multistream breadth and scheduling.
