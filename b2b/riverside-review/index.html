What Riverside does

Records podcasts and video locally at each end, so guests on poor connections still come out broadcast-quality. The meter is separate-track recording time: Pro is $29/month for 15 hours, and hitting the cap stops new separate-track downloads until the next cycle rather than billing you more.

Pricing (2026)

(2026-08-01, read on their own pricing page) Free $0: one 2-hour recording, 720p, single track, 44.1 kHz. Pro $29/month ($24 billed annually): 15 hours a month of separate tracks, up to 4K, 48 kHz, 1 studio. Grow $39/month ($34 annually): 20 hours, 2 studios. Webinar $99/month ($79 annually): 25 hours, 3 studios. Business is quote-only with unlimited hours. Pro and Grow include 20 AI credits, Business starts at 60; more are bought separately. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The meter is not recording time, it is separate-track recording time — and separate tracks are the entire reason to use this rather than a video call. Their own wording on hitting the cap: you can still reach your content but cannot download new separate tracks until the next billing cycle or you upgrade. So a busy interview month does not cost you more money, it quietly stops you delivering, which is worse. Fifteen hours on Pro is about seven or eight hour-long interviews once you count retakes and the recording that runs while you set up. The free tier is a demonstration rather than a plan: one 2-hour session, 720p, single track.

What buyers report about Riverside

Recurring themes from G2, SelectHub and creator reviews, read in August 2026. We have not run Riverside ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Each participant records locally, so a guest's bad connection does not ruin the take. That is the core reason to use it, and it works. 4.8 on G2 across 1,657 reviews.
  • • Guests join without friction, and the AI editing that strips filler words and pauses saves real time.
What they complain about
  • • Sync problems, missing files and glitches are reported often enough to matter — always check your files are all there before you let a guest go.
  • • Support is described as inconsistent and not transparent when something has gone wrong.
  • • $19/month Standard for 1080p, $29 Pro for 4K, both on annual billing; monthly costs up to 35% more.
  • • Integrations with other platforms are thinner than reviewers want.
Best for: podcasters and interviewers who need broadcast-quality audio and video from guests on bad connections — local recording at each end is genuinely the thing that solves it.
Who should skip it: if you record alone, or your guests are always in the room with you, because then a local recorder does the same job once; also skip Pro if you interview more than about eight hours a month, since the track cap is what will stop you.

The natural comparison is Zencastr or SquadCast — the direct local-recording peers — compare the monthly hour allowance for separate tracks, not the headline price. Weigh it against Zencastr on the job you actually need done.
