What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

On the same card serverless runs roughly 1.7x the pod rate per compute-hour: $4.79 against $2.89 for an H100, $2.72 against $1.59 for an A100 PCIe. That is brilliant for spiky inference and wasteful for steady training, so pick the mode per workload, not per habit. And like all GPU clouds, the meter never sleeps: an idle pod you forgot is the cloud version of the subscription graveyard. Set spend alerts before the first experiment, not after the first invoice.

What buyers report about Runpod

Hands-on: not tested by usReported by buyers: G2 and GPU-cloud comparisons, read August 2026

Themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Runpod ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • 60-80% below AWS, GCP and Azure — RTX 4090 from $0.34/hr on Community Cloud, A100 80GB from $0.89. Billed by the second with no monthly subscription.
  • • Affordability against the hyperscalers is the top reason reviewers name for choosing it for experiments and small workloads.
What they complain about
  • • Reliability is the weakest point and the most common complaint. Runpod tracked 227+ outages over nine months, and reviewers report pods failing to start, crashing mid-job, or showing as available when they are not — while billing continues.
  • • Cold starts of 15-30 seconds after idle, which rules it out for anything latency-sensitive.
  • • Storage costs double when idle: $0.10/GB/month active, $0.20 stopped. A 200 GB volume is $20 while you work and $40 after you stop. Delete volumes you are not using.
  • • Secure Cloud costs roughly double Community Cloud for the same card.

The crossover is 58-60% utilisation

Runpod's two modes are not tiers, they are different bets on how busy your GPU will be — and there is a number where the bet flips, which nobody publishes. A pod bills for wall-clock time whether or not it computes. Serverless bills only while it runs, at a premium. Divide one by the other and you get the utilisation at which they cost the same: an H100 at $2.89 against $4.79 crosses at 60%, an A100 PCIe at $1.59 against $2.72 at 58%.

Below roughly 58-60% busy, serverless is cheaper despite the higher headline; above it, a pod is. Spiky inference sits well under that line, steady training well over it. Every rate here was re-verified on 24 September 2026 against runpod.io/pricing. Two smaller things. The H100 SXM is $3.49 against the PCIe's $2.89 — 20.8% more, up from 13.8% when the SXM was $3.29 on 5 September. What that buys is the interconnect, which pays off when a job spans several GPUs; for single-card inference the PCIe is now the cheaper default.

And a multi-node A100 SXM cluster is $1.79 an hour per GPU against $1.59 for the same card in a single pod: a 12.6% premium for the networking, not the silicon. What none of that protects you from is the meter, which never sleeps. One forgotten H100 pod is $2,081 a month; even a forgotten A5000 is $194.

How to actually use Runpod

Set a spend alert before you start a single pod. That is the urgent step: an idle H100 you forgot bills $2,081 over a month, and nothing about picking the right card protects you from it. Then pick the mode from your duty cycle rather than the headline rate. Estimate what fraction of the hour the GPU actually computes. Under roughly 58-60% — which is most inference — serverless is cheaper despite billing at a premium, because it stops charging between requests.

Over it, which is most training and batch work, take a pod. Benchmark on the cheap cards first. An A5000 at $0.27 an hour or an L4 at $0.49 will tell you whether your container builds and your weights load, and none of that needs an H100. When you move up, pay for the H100 SXM only if the job spans several GPUs: at $3.49 against $2.89 for the PCIe it is 20.8% more, and the interconnect is what that buys.

And compare a cluster against the same card rather than a cheaper one — $1.79 per A100 SXM is 12.6% over the single-pod A100 SXM at $1.59, worth paying only if your job genuinely spans nodes. If you are integrating rather than clicking, write against REST API v2 from the start: v1 stops serving traffic on 15 November 2026 and returns 410 Gone.

Frequently asked questions

What does one H100 cost me for a month?

Between about $1,000 and $2,500, depending on how you rent it. Read 24 September 2026 on runpod.io/pricing: an H100 PCIe pod on Secure Cloud is $2.89 an hour, so a pod left running all month (720 hours) is $2,080.80; the H100 SXM at $3.49 is $2,512.80. The same PCIe card on Community Cloud is $1.99, or $1,432.80 a month. Serverless bills only while it computes, at $4.79 an hour for the 80GB H100 tier, so a worker that is busy 30% of the time comes to about $1,034.64. Storage is billed on top of all of these.

How much cheaper is Community Cloud, and is there a catch?

Between about 10% and 55% per card, and the pricing page does not say what else changes. The page's structured data lists both rates for every GPU (read 24 September 2026): RTX 4090 $0.34 against $0.74, A100 PCIe $1.19 against $1.59, H100 PCIe $1.99 against $2.89, H100 SXM $2.69 against $3.49, and at the narrow end an L4 at $0.44 against $0.49. The discount is largest on consumer cards and smallest on the L4. The pricing page describes no difference in hosting, security or uptime between the two columns, so if your data has compliance rules, ask Runpod where Community machines run before moving it there.

Do I still pay when my pod is stopped?

For storage, yes, and the idle rate is double. Runpod's storage table (read 24 September 2026) bills volume disk at $0.10 per GB a month while the pod runs and $0.20 while it sits idle, so a 100GB volume you keep between experiments costs $20 a month even when no GPU is attached to it. Network storage is $0.07 per GB under 1TB and $0.05 above it, $0.14 for the high-performance tier, and container disk is $0.10 per GB. The GPU rates are per hour, billed by the second, and the page lists no GPU charge for an idle pod; the disk charge is the one that keeps running until you delete the volume.

Is an IT or productivity tool secure and reliable enough to depend on?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.
