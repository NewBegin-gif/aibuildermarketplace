Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 26 Aug 2026

The plans
  • Pods — Secure Cloud — $0.27–$7.89 per hour
    dedicated GPU instances from RTX A5000 to B300, billed per second, in vetted data centres
  • Pods — Community Cloud — $0.16–$6.94 per hour
    the same GPU models on community hosts; roughly 10–55% cheaper per card
  • Serverless — $0.58–$9.98 per hour
    per-second billing, sub-200ms cold starts, autoscaling workers from 16GB to B300
  • Clusters — $1.79/hr per A100 SXM, $4.31/hr per H200 SXM
    multi-node jobs up to 64 GPUs; H100 SXM, L40S and B200 are quote-only
  • Reserved clusters — quote — talk to an engineer
    1, 3, 6, 12 and 12+ month commitments with SLA-backed uptime

Pay-per-use GPU cloud. Secure Cloud on-demand pods run from $0.27/hr (RTX A5000) and $0.49/hr (L4) up to $2.89/hr for an H100 PCIe, $3.29/hr H100 SXM, $4.59/hr H200, $6.79/hr B200 and $7.89/hr B300. Four of those moved up since July — L4 from $0.39, H100 SXM from $2.99, H200 from $4.39 and B300 from $7.39 — while the A5000 and the H100 PCIe did not move at all.

The page's own structured data publishes the Community Cloud column beside every card, and that is where the real discount sits: RTX 4090 $0.34 against $0.74 on Secure, RTX 3090 $0.22 against $0.50, RTX A6000 $0.33 against $0.53, H100 PCIe $1.99 against $2.89. Serverless bills per second at a premium over pods in exchange for sub-200ms cold starts and autoscaling: $0.58/hr for the 16GB tier, $2.72/hr for an A100, $4.79/hr for an 80GB H100 (was $4.55) and $9.98/hr at the top for a B300.

Multi-node Clusters now publish two rates — $1.79/hr per A100 SXM and $4.31/hr per H200 SXM — while H100 SXM, L40S and B200 clusters plus all reserved capacity stay quote-only. Storage is billed on top and is easy to miss: container disk $0.10/GB/month, volume disk $0.10 while running and $0.20 while idle, network storage $0.07/GB under 1TB and $0.05 over it, $0.14 for the high-performance tier. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

On the same card serverless runs roughly 1.6-2x the pod rate per compute-hour: $4.79 against $2.89 for an H100, $2.72 against $1.39 for an A100 PCIe. That is brilliant for spiky inference and wasteful for steady training, so pick the mode per workload, not per habit. And like all GPU clouds, the meter never sleeps: an idle pod you forgot is the cloud version of the subscription graveyard. Set spend alerts before the first experiment, not after the first invoice.

What they praise

  • • Affordability against the hyperscalers is the top reason reviewers name for choosing it for experiments and small workloads.
What they complain about

  • • Cold starts of 15-30 seconds after idle, which rules it out for anything latency-sensitive.
  • • Storage costs double when idle: $0.10/GB/month active, $0.20 stopped. A 200 GB volume is $20 while you work and $40 after you stop. Delete volumes you are not using.
  • • Secure Cloud costs roughly double Community Cloud for the same card.

The crossover is 51-60% utilisation

Runpod's two modes are not tiers, they are different bets on how busy your GPU is going to be — and there is a number where the bet flips, which nobody publishes. Here it is. On-demand pods run from $0.27 an hour for an RTX A5000 and $0.49 for an L4, up to $2.89 for an H100 PCIe, $3.29 H100 SXM, $4.59 H200 and $7.89 for a B300. Serverless bills per second, at a premium, in exchange for sub-200ms cold starts and autoscaling: $0.58 an hour for the 16GB tier, $2.72 for an A100 and $4.79 for an 80GB H100.

Multi-node clusters start at $1.79 an hour per A100 SXM; H100 SXM, L40S and B200 clusters and all reserved capacity are quote-only. A pod bills for wall-clock time whether or not it is computing. Serverless bills only while it runs. So divide the pod rate by the serverless rate and you get the utilisation at which they cost the same: $2.89 against $4.79 puts the H100 crossover at 60%, and $1.39 against $2.72 puts the A100 at 51%.

Below about half to two-thirds busy, serverless is cheaper despite the higher headline. Above it, a pod is. That single calculation settles most of the mode question, and it is worth redoing per workload rather than picking a habit: spiky inference sits well under the line, steady training sits well over it. Two smaller things worth pocketing. The H100 SXM is $3.29 against the PCIe's $2.89 — 14% more for a materially better interconnect.

That gap widened in August when the SXM rose and the PCIe did not, and it is close enough to free that there is rarely a reason to pick the PCIe card. And the multi-node A100 rate of $1.79 is a 29% premium over the single-pod $1.39, which is the price of the networking rather than the silicon. Then the failure mode that costs more than any of the above.

The meter never sleeps. One H100 pod left running and forgotten is $2,081 a month; even a forgotten A5000 is $194. That is the cloud version of the subscription graveyard, and it is not caught by choosing the right rate.

How to actually use Runpod

Set a spend alert before you start a single pod. This is the one step that is genuinely urgent: an idle H100 you forgot about bills $2,081 over a month, and nothing about picking the right card or the right mode protects you from it. Then pick the mode from your duty cycle rather than from the headline rate. Estimate what fraction of the hour your GPU will actually be computing.

Under roughly half to two-thirds — which is most inference — serverless is cheaper despite billing at a premium, because it stops charging between requests. Over it, which is most training and most batch work, take a pod. Benchmark on the cheap cards first. An A5000 at $0.27 an hour or an L4 at $0.49 will tell you whether your container builds, your weights load and your pipeline runs, and none of that needs an H100 to find out.

When you do move up, take the H100 SXM rather than the PCIe — it is 3.5% more for a better interconnect. And do the cluster arithmetic before committing to multi-node: $1.79 per A100 SXM is a 29% premium over a single pod, which is worth paying only if your job genuinely spans nodes. If you are integrating rather than clicking, write against REST API v2 from the start. Runpod made v2 generally available on 31 August 2026 and published the sunset dates with it: staged rate limits on v1 and GraphQL from 17 September 2026, two short brown-outs during November, and on 15 November 2026 v1 stops serving traffic and returns 410 Gone.

GraphQL follows in early 2027. Every v1 response now carries a Sunset header with its decommission date, so a quick check of your own client tells you whether you are already on v2.

The natural comparison is Lambda or Vast.ai — close GPU-cloud rivals — compare per-GPU-hour on the exact card you need and whether serverless cold-start speed matters for your workload.
