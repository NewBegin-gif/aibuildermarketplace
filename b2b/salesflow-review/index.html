When the paid plan is worth it

There is no free tier, so the question is which seat count you are buying into — and on Salesflow that changes the price per seat more than any feature does. Their own published rates: Basic $99 per seat from one seat, Starter $70 from five, Pro $39.95 from twenty, Agency $29.98 from fifty and Enterprise $24.99 from a hundred, with Agency and Enterprise requiring an annual commitment billed monthly.

Longer terms take a further 15% off at six months and 30% off at twelve. Read that ladder by the monthly bill rather than by the seat rate, because the two come apart at every threshold. Nineteen seats on Starter is $1,330 a month; twenty seats on Pro is $799. The twentieth seat makes the bill smaller, and the same holds at forty-nine to fifty and at ninety-nine to a hundred.

If you are sitting one seat below a boundary you are paying more for less. The quotas are what you are actually renting: around 400 LinkedIn invites, up to 2,000 follow-ups and 800 Open InMails a month. Those figures come from independent reviews rather than from the pricing page, which does not publish them, so check them against your real weekly send volume before you commit. The trial is seven days and takes no card — enough to test one sequence, not a month of sending.

How to actually use Salesflow

Start with your LinkedIn account limits, not with the software. Salesflow automates invites and messages from your own profile, and LinkedIn enforces its own weekly connection ceiling regardless of what you have paid for. A tool quota of 400 invites a month is only reachable if your account is in good standing. Warm the account up before you run a full sequence. Outreach tools get accounts restricted when volume jumps overnight, and the cost of that is your profile, not your subscription.

Build one sequence end to end before adding email steps. The multichannel piece is what separates this from a plain connection-request tool, but a broken LinkedIn step multiplied across channels is just a faster way to burn a list. If you are an agency, set up the admin panel, seat management and the shared blacklist on day one — the blacklist in particular, because it is what stops two of your people contacting the same prospect.

Frequently asked questions

Which plan is cheapest for the size of team I have?

Not the one your seat count lands on — Salesflow's per-seat rate drops so sharply at each tier boundary that buying a seat you do not need is often cheaper. On the ladder read 9 September 2026 and unchanged on 1 October (Basic $99 per seat from 1, Starter $70 from 5, Pro $39.95 from 20, Agency $29.98 from 50, Enterprise $24.99 from 100), nineteen seats on Starter costs $1,330 a month while twenty seats on Pro costs $799 — one extra seat saves $531 every month. The same cliff repeats: 49 seats on Pro is $1,957.55 against $1,499 for 50 on Agency, and 99 on Agency is $2,968.02 against $2,499 for 100 on Enterprise. One catch before you jump: Agency and Enterprise require an annual commitment billed monthly, so the seat that saves you $458 a month also ties you in for a year, and past fifty the comparison table adds seats in batches of 5 on Agency and 10 on Enterprise. The arithmetic is ours, on their published rates.

Do the longer-term discounts stack on top of the tier price?

Yes, by their own pricing page: 15% off at six months and 30% off at twelve, applied on top of whatever your seat count already earns. On Pro at twenty seats that turns $799 a month into about $559 at the twelve-month rate. Two things to hold onto. That is our arithmetic on their published percentages, not a quoted price, so confirm it before signing. And there is no free tier to fall back on if the fit is wrong — the trial is seven days without a card, which is the whole window you get to find out.

What are the monthly sending limits?

Salesflow does not publish them, and that matters more than the price. The figures in circulation — around 400 LinkedIn invites, 2,000 follow-up messages and 800 Open InMails a month — come from independent reviews, not from Salesflow's own pricing page, which lists seats and rates and nothing about volume. Treat them as unconfirmed. For a sequencing tool those caps, not the per-seat rate, decide whether a plan fits your outreach, so ask for them in writing and test them inside the seven-day trial. A headless-browser re-read of salesflow.io/pricing on 1 October 2026 again showed seats, rates and features and no volume caps.

What should I weigh before buying Sales & CRM software?

Most tools in this category plug in rather than replace. Check which direction the data flows and whether the sync is genuinely two-way, because a one-way push creates a second source of truth that your reps will quietly stop trusting. If it does aim to replace your CRM, price the migration honestly: history, custom fields and reporting rarely move across cleanly.
