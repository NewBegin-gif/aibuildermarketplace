What Salesmsg does

Business texting and SMS marketing in one shared inbox. Reach customers where they actually reply — two-way texting, automation and team collaboration.

Pricing (2026)

(2026-08-08, re-verified on their own pricing page) Repackaged since our last check into two named plans plus Enterprise, metered in message credits. Monthly billing: Basic is $25 a month for 500 credits a month; Pro is $329 a month for 10,000 credits a month; Enterprise is contact-for-pricing with custom credits. Both plans carry a credits dropdown that raises the allowance for a fixed add-on: on Basic, 1,000 credits is +$24, 2,500 is +$74, 5,000 is +$154 and 7,500 is +$224 a month; on Pro the ladder runs from 15,000 credits at +$150 up to 50,000 at +$1,170 a month, in 5,000-credit steps.

Annual billing is shown as two months free: Basic $21 a month billed yearly for 6,000 credits a year (12,000 credits +$252, 30,000 +$756, 60,000 +$1,560, 90,000 +$2,268) and Pro $277 a month billed yearly for 120,000 credits a year (180,000 +$1,512, rising to 600,000 at +$11,796). Seats and numbers are priced apart from the plan: the first seat is free and each additional seat is $10.00 a month or $100.00 a year; the first number is free and each additional number is $5.00 a month or $50.00 a year.

The page defines the meter itself: SMS is 1 credit per 160 characters, MMS is 2 credits, calling is 1 credit a minute and call forwarding is 2 credits a minute. A 14-day free trial runs, and a 30-day money-back guarantee is offered, refunded less the message credits used. The page no longer publishes a per-credit overage rate, nor terms for taxes, carrier fees or country availability, so we quote none. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Two things changed here and both cost money. First, calling now runs on the same meter as texting — 1 credit a minute, 2 a minute when forwarding — so voice minutes eat the allowance you budgeted for messages. Second, the plan ladder is a canyon: $25 to $329 a month with nothing between them, and the credits dropdown raises your allowance without adding a single seat. Size the credits, not the headcount. An MMS is 2 credits, so one picture message to 1,000 contacts is 2,000 credits — four times the entire monthly allowance on Basic. Seats and numbers bill on top of every plan beyond the first of each: a five-person team on one number is $40 a month over the sticker, and the annual headline of $21 and $277 is a per-month display of a bill charged yearly. Note what the page has stopped saying since our previous check: there is no published per-credit overage rate anymore, so what happens when you run out mid-cycle is not stated where you buy — ask before you commit. And US business texting carries a regulatory layer of its own: 10DLC registration and carrier surcharges bill separately from anything quoted here, and opt-in and opt-out handling stays your legal responsibility — the platform enables compliance, it does not absolve you.

What buyers report about Salesmsg

Recurring themes from G2, Capterra, GetApp and Trustpilot, read in August 2026. We have not run Salesmsg ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • The shared inbox is easy enough that teams are running the same day — the most common praise across B2B platforms, where it sits around 4.6 to 4.7.
  • • CRM sync with HubSpot and Salesforce is the single most-praised feature, with voice calling in the same place as texting.
What they complain about
  • • Credit-based pricing: $25 a month for 500 credits at the bottom, $329 a month for 10,000 on Pro. Reviewers report surprise costs once real volume starts.
  • • Value for money scores lower than the other categories, and the gap widens as teams scale.
  • • Mobile app bugs come up occasionally.

Where the ratings disagree. Around 4.6 on the B2B platforms against 2.8 on Trustpilot. Trustpilot collects the unfiltered end of the experience, and for a credit-metered product that usually means billing.

Best for: sales and service teams that want two-way texting and calling in one CRM-linked inbox, and can estimate their monthly credit burn — messages plus call minutes — before they sign.
Who should skip it: if your audience doesn't respond to SMS, or your volume is low enough that the fixed 10DLC overhead outweighs the sending; also skip it if you need a lot of seats cheaply, or if your real usage lands in the gap between the $25 and $329 plans, since the only bridge on offer is a bigger credit bundle on the small plan.
