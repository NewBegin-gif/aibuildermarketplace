When the paid plan is worth it

SaneBox has no free tier — a 7-day trial and then a paid plan — and the tiers split on two axes that are easy to miss. Read in a live browser on their pricing page on 18 August 2026, because the figures are rendered client-side and a plain fetch of that page returns nothing: Snack covers 1 email account, Lunch 2 and Dinner 4. The second axis is the one that catches people.

Lunch does not give you every feature — it lets you pick 6 from a list of eleven: SaneLater, SaneNoReplies, SaneDoNotDisturb, SaneBlackHole, SaneNews, SaneCC, Reminders, Attachments, SaneSnooze, SaneNotSpam and the filtering extras. Snack gives you 2 of the eleven. Only Dinner includes all of them. So if SaneBlackHole and SaneReminders are the two you came for, on Snack that is your entire allowance. The allowance is per connected account, which is the one piece of good news in the arrangement.

The pricing itself has a sharper edge than most. The number on the pricing page is the every-two-years rate: Snack reads $4.54 a month, but that is $109 charged once for two years. The same plan is $5.42 a month billed annually and $9.49 month to month — so the advertised figure is less than half what a month-to-month buyer actually pays, and Dinner runs from $22.04 to $42.99 across the same three columns.

A 20-30% annual discount is normal in this market; a 109% gap between the headline and the flexible option is not. And it is worth knowing that the cheap number on the page asks for a two-year commitment to a tool you are meant to be trialling for seven days.

How to actually use SaneBox

SaneBox works by moving mail between folders on your existing account, so the first decision is which account to connect — and on Snack you only get one. Connect the inbox that actually hurts. Then leave it alone for a few days. The filtering learns from where you move things, so the useful signal is you dragging a message out of SaneLater back into the inbox, not you reading a settings page.

Judge it at the end of the trial, not on day two. Pick your feature set deliberately if you land on Lunch. Six of eleven is a real constraint, and the two most people end up wanting are SaneBlackHole, which permanently kills a sender, and SaneNoReplies, which tracks mail you sent that nobody answered. Both are on the list you choose from, not included by default.

Frequently asked questions

Is the two-year SaneBox plan really the cheapest way to pay?

Yes, if you are sure you will keep it for two years. Read 24 September 2026 from the plan data on sanebox.com/pricing, Snack costs $109 once for two years, $65 a year on annual billing or $9.49 month to month. Over 24 months that is $109, $130 or $227.76. For Lunch the same comparison is $179, $218 or $383.76, and for Dinner $529, $638 or $1,031.76. The two-year price saves about a sixth against annual, but month to month costs roughly twice as much. The trial is 7 days, so the cheapest price asks for a two-year commitment after one week of use. Annual is the middle ground if you are unsure.

I have two email accounts. Which SaneBox plan do I need?

Lunch at the least. The plan data on the pricing page, read 24 September 2026, gives Snack 1 email account, Lunch 2 and Dinner 4, and 4 is the most any listed plan connects. Lunch is $15.99 month to month, $109 a year or $179 for two years, and comes with 6 features against Snack's 2. If both inboxes need every feature, including SaneSnooze and SaneCC, that is Dinner at $42.99 a month or $319 a year, which also covers two more accounts.

Which SaneBox features come with Snack and Lunch?

A short list, set by the plan's feature count. Read 24 September 2026, the plan data gives Snack 2 features and Lunch 6, and the selection the page shows a new visitor is SaneLater and SaneBlackHole on Snack, then SaneNews, SaneNoReplies, SaneNotSpam and DIY Folders added on Lunch, and SaneSnooze and SaneCC added on Dinner. So a Snack buyer gets the core filter (unimportant mail to SaneLater) and the one-click sender block, but no reply tracking, newsletter folder or snooze. If SaneNoReplies is why you are buying, Lunch is the entry point, not Snack.

Does a workflow tool replace my current setup or sit on top of it?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.
