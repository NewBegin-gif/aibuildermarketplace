What SaneBox does

Email triage that moves low-priority mail out of the inbox and works with any provider. From $4.54/month for one account on a two-year commitment — $9.49 month to month — up to $22.04 for four accounts with every feature. The honest catch: you are paying monthly to filter mail that modern email clients increasingly sort for free.

Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 18 Aug 2026

Three plans, and the advertised price is the two-year rate. Snack $4.54/mo billed $109 every 2 years, $5.42/mo billed $65 annually, or $9.49 month to month: 1 email account and 2 of the Sane features. Lunch $7.46 / $9.08 ($109 a year) / $15.99: 2 accounts and 6 features. Dinner $22.04 / $26.58 ($319 a year) / $42.99: 4 accounts and every feature, plus phone support.

The free trial is 7 days with all features on up to 4 accounts. Education, non-profit and government get another 25% off on request during the trial. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

When the paid plan is worth it

SaneBox has no free tier — a 7-day trial and then a paid plan — and the tiers split on two axes that are easy to miss. Read in a live browser on their pricing page on 18 August 2026, because the figures are rendered client-side and a plain fetch of that page returns nothing: Snack covers 1 email account, Lunch 2 and Dinner 4.

The second axis is the one that catches people. Lunch does not give you every feature — it lets you pick 6 from a list of eleven: SaneLater, SaneNoReplies, SaneDoNotDisturb, SaneBlackHole, SaneNews, SaneCC, Reminders, Attachments, SaneSnooze, SaneNotSpam and the filtering extras. Snack gives you 2 of the eleven. Only Dinner includes all of them. So if SaneBlackHole and SaneReminders are the two you came for, on Snack that is your entire allowance. The allowance is per connected account, which is the one piece of good news in the arrangement.

The pricing itself has a sharper edge than most. The number on the pricing page is the every-two-years rate: Snack reads $4.54 a month, but that is $109 charged once for two years. The same plan is $5.42 a month billed annually and $9.49 month to month — so the advertised figure is less than half what a month-to-month buyer actually pays, and Dinner runs from $22.04 to $42.99 across the same three columns. A 20-30% annual discount is normal in this market; a 109% gap between the headline and the flexible option is not. And it is worth knowing that the cheap number on the page asks for a two-year commitment to a tool you are meant to be trialling for seven days.

How to actually use SaneBox

SaneBox works by moving mail between folders on your existing account, so the first decision is which account to connect — and on Snack you only get one. Connect the inbox that actually hurts.

Then leave it alone for a few days. The filtering learns from where you move things, so the useful signal is you dragging a message out of SaneLater back into the inbox, not you reading a settings page. Judge it at the end of the trial, not on day two.

Pick your feature set deliberately if you land on Lunch. Six of eleven is a real constraint, and the two most people end up wanting are SaneBlackHole, which permanently kills a sender, and SaneNoReplies, which tracks mail you sent that nobody answered. Both are on the list you choose from, not included by default.
