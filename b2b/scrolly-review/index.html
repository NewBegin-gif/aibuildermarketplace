Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 19 Sep 2026

Two plans now, where there was one. On 25 August 2026 the listing carried no paid tier, no usage meter and no surcharge — the pricing block most App Store listings use to sell an upgrade was simply absent. On 19 September 2026 it carries a Free Plan and an Unlimited Plan at $2.99 a month, or $25.11 a year with a 30% saving and a 7-day free trial.

The split does not fall where the app’s name suggests. Free keeps the scroll-to-top button and its styling: multiple presets, button colour and hover colour, and the option to hide it on the mobile storefront. Unlimited adds infinite scrolling and the load-more button, plus uploading your own SVG icon for the button. So the feature the app is named after, and the one the first three of the listing’s five selling points describe, is now the paid half.

All charges are billed in USD every 30 days. The app has been on the App Store since March 2020, carries Shopify’s Built for Shopify badge — the store’s own bar for performance, design and integration — and the listing names eleven interface languages. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Infinite scroll replaces the paginated collection URLs a crawler uses to walk a large catalogue, pushes your footer permanently out of reach, and on some themes collides with the load-more or filtering logic already in the theme — that is a merchandising decision, not a styling toggle. The app also edits Online Store script tags and the theme, so it adds a third-party script to every page and an uninstall can leave remnants.

What changed is which half you pay for. Read on 19 September 2026, the free plan keeps the scroll-to-top button and infinite scroll sits on the $2.99 Unlimited plan, where on 25 August the whole app was free. The amount is trivial; the shape is not. The half that can quietly alter how your collection pages are crawled is now the half with a billing relationship behind it, and the seven-day trial is the only window in which you will find out what it does to your store before the charge starts.

Anything written about this app before September — including a free-forever recommendation — describes an install that no longer exists.

What Scrolly does

Free Shopify app from Effectify that adds a customizable floating back-to-top button and, optionally, infinite scroll on collection pages, with no code and no paid tier. The button is a safe convenience; the infinite scroll replaces the paginated collection URLs crawlers use, so it is a merchandising decision rather than a styling one.

There is something to buy now, and it is the half that carries the risk

Until late August 2026 there was nothing to buy here. Now there is: $2.99 a month, or $25.11 for a year at a 30% saving, with a seven-day free trial. That is not enough money to argue about, which is lucky, because the interesting question was never the price. Scrolly does two separate things, they carry very different risk, and the paywall now runs exactly between them. The back-to-top button is the low-risk half, and it stayed free.

It is a floating control with the styling you would expect to pay for — presets, colour and hover colour, and the option to hide it on mobile — and you can scope it to the whole store or to specific pages. On a long collection or a long product page it is a small, genuine convenience, and if you dislike it you turn it off and nothing about your store has changed.

Uploading your own SVG icon for that button is the one cosmetic thing that moved behind the paywall. Infinite scroll is the half that deserves a decision, and it is the half you now pay for. Loading more products as the shopper scrolls replaces the paginated collection URLs underneath, and those paginated URLs are how a crawler walks a large catalogue. It also puts your footer out of reach, because the page keeps growing beneath it, and on some themes it interferes with the load-more or filtering logic the theme already ships.

None of that makes it wrong — plenty of stores run it deliberately — but it is a merchandising and SEO choice dressed up as a display setting, and it should be made on purpose. The practical effect of the new plan is that the seven-day trial is your window to make it on purpose: switch it on, watch whether Search Console keeps crawling your paginated collection URLs, and decide before day seven rather than after.

One cost applies either way. The listing’s data-access panel says the app edits Online Store script tags and the theme, so switching it on adds a third-party script to your storefront and switching it off can leave remnants behind. The Built for Shopify badge means it cleared Shopify’s own performance review, which is a real counterweight, but it is still one more thing running on every page.

How to actually use Scrolly

The vendor's own walkthrough is three steps: open Scrolly's dashboard from the Shopify admin, pick the button style under Scroller Type, then set behaviour and appearance — colour, shape, size, hover effect, position, padding, animation — under Appearance Settings, and save. The button can run storewide or on selected pages only, which is the setting to reach for when it collides with a sticky menu or a chat bubble. Turn the button on and leave infinite scroll off for the first week.

The two features are independent and carry different risk: the button is a convenience, while infinite scroll changes how your collection pages are built and crawled. Ship both at once and you will not know which one caused whatever you see afterwards. One thing to know before you switch infinite scroll on: the help centre documents the back-to-top button and nothing else — there is no article on infinite scroll at all, even though it leads the listing, so plan on testing it rather than reading up on it.

Watch two numbers afterwards: whether Search Console keeps crawling the paginated collection URLs, and whether products viewed per session actually rises in your analytics. Infinite scroll is sold on the second number, and it is the one merchants almost never check.

What do the 236 reviews actually tell me?

Less than the 4.9 suggests, for two reasons.
