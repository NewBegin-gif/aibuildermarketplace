What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The app launched 10 October 2023 and, as of 5 October 2026, exactly one merchant has written about it: a five-star note from October 2023 by someone who had it installed for four minutes. That is not evidence of quality and not evidence of trouble: it is an absence of evidence, and on an app that writes sections into your live theme, that absence is the main thing to weigh.

On the same listing page Shopify shows Section Store with 2,967 reviews (read 5 October 2026), also free to install, which is what a track record looks like in this category. The old site, sectionlibrary.com, still answers with Shopify’s “Store unavailable” page (17 September, 5 October and 12 November 2026); the product now lives at optifyapps.com/mason under its new name, Mason. Second, the credit is defined only inside the app.

Every plan is priced in credits and nothing on the listing says what one buys; the app's own price list says a full AI page is 5 credits, an AI section 1, an AI edit 0.5 and a library section 5 credits once, so you only learn the rates after you have installed it. Third, the AI half of the product is Google's. The October listing named Gemini 3.1 Pro, and the Mason privacy policy (effective 24 October 2026) says prompts, the detected brand kit and the relevant theme content go to third-party AI model providers such as Google's Gemini models, so output quality and consistency shift when Google ships a new model, which is outside the vendor's hands as much as yours.

The library half, the 200+ pre-built sections, is the part that depends on nobody else, and it is the part worth judging the app on.

When the paid plan is worth it

The ladder is one number repeated four times: credits. 8 free, 25 at $9, 55 at $19, 90 at $29. Divide it out and there is a mild volume discount: about 36 cents a credit on Starter, 34.5 on Pro, 32 on Business, which is roughly a tenth off for triple the commitment, and not on its own a reason to climb. The one real gate sits between Free and Starter, and it has nothing to do with credits: the listing says the free plan reaches only the sections marked free, and every paid tier includes the full library.

In our own test of 17 November 2026 the app's Free plan showed library access too, but each library section cost 5 of the 8 free credits and we saw none marked free. If you want the 200+ sections that are the point of the app, the entry price is $9 and everything above it is allowance plus a support queue (email on Starter, priority on Pro and Business, identical to each other on paper).

The listing still never defines a credit, so you cannot price a plan from it alone; the app does. In our own test of 17 November 2026 its price list read: a full AI page 5 credits, an AI section 1 credit, an AI edit 0.5 credits and a library section 5 credits once. On those rates the free 8 credits buy one library section and a few AI edits, Starter's 25 credits about five AI pages (the app's own estimate), Pro's 55 about eleven and Business's 90 about eighteen.

Install the free plan and spend the 8 credits deliberately before you pick a tier.

    How to actually use Section Library

    Spend the free plan's 8 credits before you pay for anything, and write down what each one bought: the listing does not say what a credit is, but the app's own price list does (a library section 5 credits once, an AI section 1, an AI page 5). In our test the install dialog preselected the live theme and we did not open the dropdown, so choose your copy deliberately.

    Install into a duplicated theme rather than your live one: these are theme sections, so the safe test is a copy you can throw away. Check what happens to a page built on these sections if you stop paying or uninstall, before you build a landing page on one. And judge the library first: the 200+ ready-made sections are the part that does not depend on Google's model, while anything you generate with Google's Gemini models can come out different next month.

    Since the rename to Mason the app can also install a generated page as a new template on a draft theme, which is the safe place to try the full-page generator.

    What happens to my pages if I uninstall Mason?

    They stay in your theme. The Mason privacy policy (optifyapps.com/mason/privacy, effective 24 October 2026, read 12 November 2026) says the sections, templates and pages it installs are written to your Shopify theme, are stored in your store rather than on Optify's servers, and remain until you remove them. After uninstall Shopify sends an erase request and Optify deletes your store data, apart from records it must keep for legal, tax or accounting reasons. The policy does not say whether every section keeps working without the app installed, so check that on a duplicated theme before you build a live page on it.

    What store data does Mason send to an AI model?

    Per the privacy policy effective 24 October 2026: your prompt, the brand kit it detects from your theme (colours, fonts, store description) and the store or theme content relevant to that request, sent to third-party AI providers such as Google's Gemini models. The app reads and writes your themes, theme files, templates and Online Store pages, and reads products and collections. It does not request access to your customers' names, contact details or orders. From Shopify it receives the store owner's name, email, phone number and address.
