The App Store listing shows one ladder, Starter, and it is priced by your store's total monthly order count rather than by anything the app does: free up to 50 orders a month, $9 from 51 to 500, $19 from 501 to 1,000, and $29 above 1,000. All four steps carry the same 18 upsell and cross-sell strategies, 50 free AI recommendations, free setup on a call and chat, email and Zoom support, with a 30-day free trial on the paid steps.

Logbase's own pricing page adds two tiers the listing does not show. Pro is $49 a month or $499 a year — $42 a month, which the site says saves $89 — and lifts the order limit entirely while adding 1,000 AI recommendations a month, an AI campaign optimizer, checkout upsells for Shopify Plus, advanced campaign targeting and metafield bulk recommendations. Max is $99 a month or $1,009 a year — $84 a month, saving $179 — adding 2,500 AI recommendations a month, advanced customization, a rules engine, an AOV advisor and priority support. An Enterprise tier is quoted on request. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

The honest knock

Every Starter tier counts total store orders, so the free plan's 50-order ceiling is reached by any shop doing two orders a day whether or not a single upsell ever fired — and, read the other way, a 5,000-order store running one quiet bundle pays the top step of the ladder all the same. That cuts both ways and it is cheap in either direction, but it is not the meter most people assume they are signing up to.

Second, the App Store listing shows only the Starter ladder, so it reads as though $29 is the ceiling. It is not. Pro at $49 and Max at $99 exist only on logbase.io, and that is where AI recommendations at volume and checkout upsells for Shopify Plus sit — the two upgrades a growing store is most likely to want. Third, every Starter tier says '50 free AI recommendations' with no period attached, while Pro says '1,000 AI recommendations/month' and Max says '2,500 AI recommendations/month'.

The contrast in wording suggests the 50 is a one-off allowance rather than a monthly one, but neither page says so outright. Ask before you plan around it. And treat the vendor's headline claims as claims: the 20% AOV increase and the 40,000+ brands are Logbase's own figures on its own site, with no methodology attached to either.

What Selleasy does

Shopify upsell and cross-sell app (by Logbase): bundles and product add-ons on the product page, add-on pop-ups and response-based funnels on the cart, one-click post-purchase offers and thank-you page add-ons. Priced by your store's total order count rather than by upsell revenue, and free under 50 orders a month.

What the money actually buys

On the Starter ladder the money buys headroom and nothing else. Tiers I, II, III and IV carry an identical feature list — 18 upsell and cross-sell strategies, 50 free AI recommendations, free setup on a call, and chat, email and Zoom support — and differ only in how many orders your store is allowed to process: 50, 500, 1,000, then no limit above $29. That is unusual in this category and it is the main reason to look at Selleasy at all.

A store doing 1,200 orders a month pays $29 for exactly the same product a store doing 40 orders gets for nothing. The real feature paywall sits above that ladder, and it is on Logbase's own site rather than on the App Store listing. Pro at $49 a month is where the AI layer opens: 1,000 recommendations a month instead of 50, an AI campaign optimizer, advanced campaign targeting and metafield bulk recommendations — and it is also where checkout upsells for Shopify Plus live.

Max at $99 raises recommendations to 2,500 and adds advanced customization, a rules engine, an AOV advisor and priority support. Annual billing brings both down: $499 a year for Pro, $1,009 for Max, which the site puts at $42 and $84 a month. Above that an Enterprise tier is quoted on request.

How to actually use Selleasy

The free tier is a genuine trial, but read what it counts. Tier I is free up to 50 orders a month and that is your store's total order count, not the orders the app creates — so if you are already shipping more than a couple of orders a day you are on a paid step from the first month. The step is $9, and it opens the same 18 strategies as every other Starter tier, so this is a cheap thing to be wrong about.

Before you install, decide which of the six offer types you actually want: bundles and product add-ons on the product page, add-on pop-ups and response-based funnels on the cart, one-click post-purchase offers on the same order, and thank-you page add-ons. Set up one of them rather than all six and let it run a fortnight — a frequently-bought-together bundle on your best seller is the usual first test, and Logbase will do the setup with you on a call at every tier including the free one.

Two things push you off the Starter ladder entirely: wanting AI recommendations at volume, and being on Shopify Plus and wanting checkout upsells.
