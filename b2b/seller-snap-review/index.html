What Seller Snap does

AI repricer and seller-analytics platform for Amazon and Walmart — what the vendor calls game-theory repricing, aimed at Buy Box share rather than racing to the bottom, plus min/max profit calculation, 80+ analytics data points and imports from tools like InventoryLab and SkuVault. Starter is $100/month billed annually (annual only); month-to-month starts at $250. 15-day free trial, no credit card.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Read the caps before you read the prices, because the caps are what you are actually buying. Two of them deserve scrutiny. First, the revenue limits — $15,000 on Starter, $30,000 on Accelerator — are printed with no period attached on the pricing page. We asked, and Seller Snap confirmed to us in writing on 24 Jul 2026 that both caps are calculated per month. That is the reading that costs you money: $15,000 a month is roughly $500 a day, so a seller who already clears that sits above Starter before signing up, and $30,000 a month puts the ceiling of Accelerator within reach of a shop doing $1,000 a day. Measure your own monthly revenue against the tier first, because the page will not do it for you. Second, the SKU limit sits at 1,000 on both of the lower tiers, so catalog growth alone — not revenue, not users — can push you up a step. And the steps are steep: leaving Accelerator for Standard means $425/month annually or $500 monthly, roughly 2.4 times what you were paying, for capacity rather than for any feature you did not already have. The cheapest door is also not quite $100: Starter is annual-billing only, so the lowest month-to-month entry into the product is $250/month. Finally, 'Starting at' appears above every monthly price on the vendor's own page — treat all of these as floors and confirm your own quote. Worth checking first, though: Amazon already includes a rule-based repricer. Automate Pricing comes with the Professional selling plan and supports rules such as Competitive Featured Offer and Competitive Lowest Price with a seller-set price floor, so if your problem is simply matching the competitive price without going below cost, test that before paying for anything. And note what sets your tier: their FAQ says pricing follows the total number of SKUs in your Seller Central account, not the active ones, so a dormant catalogue costs you money.

When the paid plan is worth it

Work out which cap binds you before you look at the prices, because the tiers move different levers. Starter at $100/month buys one marketplace, one user and 1,000 SKUs — and only on annual billing, so the cheapest month-to-month door into the product is Accelerator at $250. Accelerator adds two users and lifts the revenue cap from $15,000 to $30,000 — both figures are monthly, which Seller Snap confirmed to us in writing on 24 Jul 2026 — but keeps the same 1,000-SKU ceiling, so it buys you nothing if catalogue size is what you keep hitting; for that, Standard is the tier that moves the wall, to 15,000 SKUs with no revenue cap. Above that, or with a second Seller ID, no price is published at all — that is Custom, and only sales knows the number. One thing no tier fixes: their FAQ states pricing follows the total number of SKUs in your Seller Central account rather than the active ones, so a large dormant catalogue is billed as if you were selling it.

How to actually use Seller Snap

Start on the 15-day trial — it needs no credit card — and stay on monthly billing until the tool has proven itself on your own listings, because Seller Snap states that annual plans cannot be cancelled during the 12-month period. Before you sign up, count two things in Seller Central: your total SKUs, all of them rather than the active ones, and your Seller IDs. Those two numbers decide your tier, so an estimate built from the headline price will be wrong if your catalogue is bigger than it feels. Set your minimum prices before you let any repricer touch a listing; that floor is the safety rail the whole category depends on. Two limits the vendor states plainly: no eBay support, and no repricing of used items. Billing runs in advance and renews automatically, and cancelling takes effect at the end of the paid period with no refund for the unused part, so put the renewal date in your own calendar. Best for: Amazon and Walmart sellers past the hobby stage who compete for the Buy Box on a catalog of up to a few thousand SKUs and want AI repricing and deep seller analytics in one place — the 15-day no-card trial makes it cheap to test against your own listings before any commitment.
Who should skip it: if you sell mainly outside Amazon and Walmart (those are the only two marketplaces supported), your catalog and revenue are small enough that a rules-based repricer at a fraction of the price does the same job, or what you actually need is multi-channel competitor price monitoring rather than marketplace Buy Box repricing.
