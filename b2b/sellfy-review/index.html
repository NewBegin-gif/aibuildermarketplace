Going over the cap costs 2%, not a tier

Sellfy's plans cap by your annual sales, which makes this one of the few pricing pages where growing your business is what raises your bill. Starter is $29 a month billed monthly or $22 billed yearly, up to $10k of sales a year; Business is $79 or $59, up to $50k; Premium is $159 or $119, up to $200k; above that you are quoted. Note the summer's detour: in August 2026 the entry plan read $39 monthly and $29 yearly, and by 1 September it was back at $29 and $22.

The argument for it is real and it is the 0% transaction fee. Every plan takes nothing per sale, which against percentage-taking marketplaces is decisive at volume. Your payment processor still charges you, though: PayPal or Stripe take roughly 2.9% + 30c, and that is not Sellfy's fee to waive. Express each plan as an effective take rate at the top of its band. $10,000 of sales for $264 a year is 2.64%; $50,000 for $708 is 1.42%; $200,000 for $1,428 is 0.71%.

It gets better the closer you sit to a ceiling. But the ceilings are softer than they look, and this is the part that changes the decision. Exceeding your limit does not automatically move you up a tier and does not suspend your store. Sellfy's own pricing document calls the limits soft caps; the page's FAQ says you are expected to upgrade, and that if you have not, they may charge a 2% overage fee on the revenue above the limit.

Take that at face value and the cliff most write-ups describe disappears. Staying on Starter at $264 and paying 2% on the excess works out cheaper than Business's $708 until about $32,200 of annual sales, well inside Business's band; staying on Business beats Premium until roughly $86,000. The catch is the word may. The overage is Sellfy's discretion rather than a published entitlement, so it is a question to put to support in writing before you plan a year around it.

How to actually use Sellfy

Forecast your annual sales before you pick a plan, because that - not your feature list - is what the tiers are built on: $10k on Starter, $50k on Business, $200k on Premium. Then ask support one question in writing before you upgrade: what actually happens when you go over. The pricing page's FAQ says you are expected to move up a tier, but that if you have not, Sellfy may charge 2% on the revenue above the limit - and their own pricing document calls the caps soft and says the store stays live.

If that 2% is genuinely available to you, staying on Starter and paying it beats Business's $708 until about $32,200 of sales. Since the page says may, get it confirmed rather than assumed. Use the 14-day trial to check the storefront and the payout flow, and remember the processor fee is separate: PayPal or Stripe still take about 2.9% + 30c, which no plan removes.
