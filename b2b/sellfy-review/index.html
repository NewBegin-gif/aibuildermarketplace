The worst price on this ladder is $10,001 in sales

Sellfy's plans cap by your annual sales, which makes this one of the few pricing pages where growing your business is what raises your bill. Starter is $39 a month billed monthly or $29 billed yearly, up to $10k of sales a year; Business is $79 or $59, up to $50k; Premium is $159 or $119, up to $200k; above that you are quoted. Note the change since our last reading: what our earlier note called '$29 a month' is now the yearly rate, and the monthly rate is $39.

The argument for it is real and it is the 0% transaction fee. Every plan takes nothing per sale, which against percentage-taking marketplaces is decisive at volume. But a flat subscription against a revenue cap produces a sawtooth, and it is worth seeing where the teeth are. Take the yearly rates and express each plan as an effective take rate. At the top of Starter's band — $10,000 of sales for $348 a year — you are paying 3.48%.

At the top of Business, $50,000 for $708, it is 1.42%. At the top of Premium, $200,000 for $1,428, it is 0.71%. That is a genuinely good deal and it gets better the closer you sit to a ceiling. Now look just past one. At $10,001 of annual sales you are on Business, paying $708 to sell essentially $10,000 of goods — an effective 7.08%, double what you were paying the day before.

At $50,001 you are on Premium at $1,428, or 2.86%, again double. The worst value on this entire price list is the first dollar past each threshold, and the best is the last dollar before the next one. If your year is heading for $10,500, it is worth knowing that the last $500 of it costs you $360. The other end deserves the same honesty. Below a few hundred dollars a month in sales, the flat subscription is the tax: $348 a year against $1,000 of sales is 34.8%, which is worse than any percentage-taking platform would charge.

The 0% transaction fee only becomes an argument once there are transactions. Work out which side of that line you are on before the 14 days are up.

How to actually use Sellfy

Forecast your annual sales before you pick a plan, because that — not your feature list — is what the tiers are built on. Starter caps at $10k a year, Business at $50k and Premium at $200k, and crossing a ceiling roughly doubles your effective take rate overnight. If your projection lands near a threshold, model both sides of it. The last dollars before a ceiling are the best value on the page and the first ones past it are the worst: $10,000 of sales costs 3.48% on Starter, and $10,001 costs 7.08% on Business.

That is worth knowing in November, when a push either way is still a choice. Use the 14-day trial to check the storefront and the payout flow, then decide on billing period rather than plan. Yearly saves 25%, but it also commits you before you know which revenue band your first full year lands in — and the monthly rate is $39, not the $29 the marketing figure suggests.

The natural comparison is Gumroad or Payhip — the creator-store trio — Gumroad takes ~10% but no subscription; the crossover math is simple: past ~$300-400/mo in sales, Sellfy's flat fee wins. Weigh it against Gumroad on the job you actually need done.

Did Sellfy just get more expensive?

Yes, on the entry plan. Our monthly captures show Starter at $22 yearly and $29 monthly in July 2026, and $29 yearly and $39 monthly in August — an increase of about 32%. What do the sales limits actually mean?

Each tier caps the sales volume it covers per year: $10k on Starter, $50k on Business, $200k on Premium, with a bespoke arrangement above that. Since transaction fees are 0% on all plans, the tier is effectively how Sellfy prices your revenue — so the honest comparison against a percentage-fee marketplace is your expected annual sales against the tier price.
