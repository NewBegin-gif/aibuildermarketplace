What Sendcloud does

European e-commerce shipping platform: connect 160+ carriers, print labels in batches, show delivery options at checkout, send branded tracking (email, WhatsApp, SMS) and run a self-service returns portal — with rules-based shipping automation. Available to online retailers in 8 countries (Netherlands, Belgium, UK, France, Germany, Austria, Italy, Spain). Free plan available.

What they praise

What they complain about
  • • The bill has three layers: subscription, per-label fee (€0.12 on Lite down to €0.08 on Pro, plus €0.15 for each label over your included volume), and the actual postage. Carrier surcharges can land weeks later.

  • • Bugs are reported, notably in Royal Mail rate selection.
  • • A merchant spending over €12,000 a month describes 18 months of DPD pick-up failures needing weekly support contact. Support consistency is the recurring theme.

Lite is the answer until 5,900 labels a month

Sendcloud runs a double meter — a subscription and a per-label fee on every paid plan — which makes the tiers impossible to compare without doing the algebra. So here it is. Lite is €28 a month plus €0.10 a label. Growth is €87 plus €0.09. Premium is €175 plus €0.08. Pro is €639 plus €0.07. Enterprise is bespoke, and there is a genuine free plan at €0 with no courier contract, where you pay only carrier costs within a limited scope.

Each step up trades €0.01 off the label fee for a higher subscription, so the crossover is simply the extra subscription divided by one cent. Lite and Growth cost exactly the same at 5,900 labels a month — €618 each. Growth and Premium meet at 8,800 (€879). Premium and Pro meet at 46,400 (€3,887). Which gives a table you can act on: Lite is the cheapest plan up to 5,900 labels a month, Growth only between 5,900 and 8,800, Premium from there to 46,400, and Pro above that.

Two things fall out of that. Growth's window is 2,900 labels wide — a narrow band in the middle of an otherwise wide ladder — so the merchant who upgrades to Growth because the name sounds like the right stage is very often paying more than Lite would have cost. A store shipping 300 parcels a month pays €58 on Lite and €114 on Growth; at 1,000 parcels it is €128 against €177.

Lite stays ahead for a long time. And yearly billing takes 20% off, which moves every crossover down by roughly the same proportion — confirm whether the discount applies to the label fee as well as the subscription before you rely on the exact figures. There is a third meter neither number includes. Carrier surcharges can land on invoices weeks after the parcel moved, so the monthly total you model here is a floor rather than a bill.

Beyond price, the fit is honest: this is built out-of-the-box, so deep customisation is limited, there is no inventory management, and both the very high-volume shippers and the pickup-reliability complaints point the same way — it is happiest with small-to-mid EU stores.

How to actually use Sendcloud

Count your labels a month before you look at the plans, because that single number decides everything. Under 5,900 labels a month, Lite at €28 plus €0.10 a label is cheaper than Growth — and most stores are a long way under it. At 300 parcels a month the gap is €58 against €114. Start on the free plan if you can. It carries no subscription and no courier contract, so you pay carrier costs only, and it is enough to see how the label flow and the returns portal fit your process before any commitment.

The scope is limited, which is the point of it. Then model the full cost rather than the subscription. There are three meters here: the plan, the per-label fee, and carrier surcharges that can arrive on invoices weeks after the parcel shipped. Ask your carrier which surcharges apply to your lanes and add them to the figure before you compare Sendcloud with anything else. And check the gaps deliberately — there is no inventory management here, so if you need that, it stays in another system.

The natural comparison is ShipStation — the US-focused fulfilment platform with inventory management. Weigh it against ShipStation on the job you actually need done.
