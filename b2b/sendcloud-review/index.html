What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

It's built out-of-the-box, so deep customisation is limited, there's no inventory management (ShipStation has it), and high-volume shippers (€12k+/mo) plus pickup-reliability complaints suggest it's happiest with small-to-mid EU stores.

What Sendcloud does

European e-commerce shipping platform: connect 160+ carriers, print labels in batches, show delivery options at checkout, send branded tracking (email, WhatsApp, SMS) and run a self-service returns portal — with rules-based shipping automation. Available to online retailers in 8 countries (Netherlands, Belgium, UK, France, Germany, Austria, Italy, Spain). Free plan available.

The label cap decides your tier, not the arithmetic

Sendcloud runs a double meter — a subscription and a per-label fee — but the thing that actually picks your tier is a cap most comparisons miss (price re-verified 2026-08-31 against the vendor's own pricing page). Lite is €28 a month plus €0.10 a label. Growth is €87 plus €0.09. Premium is €175 plus €0.08. Pro is €639 plus €0.07. There is a genuine free plan at €0 for up to 20 parcels a month, with no courier contract.

Each plan also carries a monthly label limit — 400 on Lite, 1,000 on Growth, 10,000 on Premium, 30,000 on Pro — and past it every extra label costs €0.15 on top of the plan rate. That changes the sums completely. Divide the subscription gap by the one-cent difference in label fees and Lite looks cheapest until 5,900 labels a month; put the cap and the overage back in and Lite stops winning at about 744.

Growth beats Premium only to about 1,488, and Premium beats Pro only to about 12,275. Size a plan on the label fee alone and you will overshoot the real crossover by a factor of four to eight. What is left is a ladder you climb on volume rather than on price. At 300 parcels a month Lite is €58 against Growth's €114 and is clearly right. At 600 you are over Lite's cap: €28 plus €40 plus €50 of overage is €118, against €141 on Growth — still ahead, but the gap is closing, and by about 744 it is gone.

Beyond price the fit is a hard constraint rather than a preference. Sendcloud ships from eight European countries — Austria, Belgium, France, Germany, Italy, the Netherlands, Spain and the UK — so stock outside them cannot use it at all. There is no inventory management, the build is out-of-the-box so deep customisation is limited, and carrier surcharges can land on invoices weeks after the parcel moved, which makes any figure here a floor rather than a bill.

How to actually use Sendcloud

Count your parcels a month before you look at the plans, because that number hits a cap before it hits a price. Lite allows 400 labels a month, Growth 1,000, Premium 10,000 and Pro 30,000, and past the cap each extra label costs €0.15 on top of the plan rate. Lite stays the cheapest option to roughly 744 labels a month — not the 5,900 you get from comparing label fees alone.

Check you can ship from your country at all before anything else. Sendcloud supports shipping from eight European nations, so if your stock sits outside them this is not a candidate. Start on the free plan. It carries no subscription and no courier contract, covers 20 parcels a month, and is enough to see how the label flow and the returns portal fit your process. There is also a 14-day trial of the paid tiers without a card.

Then model the full cost rather than the subscription: the plan, the per-label fee, the overage past the cap, and carrier surcharges that can arrive weeks after the parcel shipped. And note the gap — there is no inventory management here, so that stays in another system.

Frequently asked questions

Is Lite really the cheapest plan until I hit its 400-label cap?

Only if you ignore the carrier rates, and they are the bigger number. Read 22 September 2026, the pricing page publishes a starting rate per plan per carrier, and it drops as you climb: PostNL Standaard is €7.20 on the free plan, €6.95 on Lite, €6.75 on Growth and €6.60 on Premium. That 20-cent step between Lite and Growth is twenty times the one-cent step in the label fee everyone compares. Put all three meters together for a shop sending PostNL Standaard - subscription, label fee and carrier rate - and Lite costs €28 plus €7.05 a parcel while Growth costs €87 plus €6.84, so Growth overtakes Lite at about 281 parcels a month, well before Lite's 400-label cap ever bites. Growth gives way to Premium at about 550 on the same arithmetic. Those are starting rates for one service before surcharges, so run them against your own carrier mix - but size your plan on the rate column, not the label fee.

What does Pro actually buy for €639 a month?

Everything except a number you can check. Read 22 September 2026, Pro is the only tier with no published carrier rate: PostNL, DHL and DPD all read 'On request' in its column while the four cheaper plans show figures, and the three UK carriers are priced on the free column alone. Its label fee is one cent below Premium's, at €0.07, and its cap is 360,000 labels a year against Premium's 120,000. So the €464 a month over Premium is not bought on the published meters at all - it is bought on cross-border shipping partnerships, dynamic delivery options in checkout, the marketplace shipping solution, a Customer Success Manager, guided onboarding and a shipping strategy health check, plus a carrier discount the feature table grades '++++' and never prices. It is also the only plan you cannot start with a trial: the button says talk to an expert. Ask for the carrier rate card in writing before you sign, because that is the line that multiplies by every parcel you send.

We ship from the UK. Which rates apply to us?

None that this page will show you on a paid plan. Read 22 September 2026, the rate table lists Royal Mail Tracked 48 at £2.38, Evri Standard Two Day at £3.37 and FedEx Priority at £5.15 - and every one of those figures sits in the Free column, with 'On request' across Lite, Growth, Premium and Pro. The Dutch, German and French carriers get four published numbers each. That inverts the normal reading of a pricing page: on the UK carriers, the only rate you can verify before paying is the one on the plan with a 20-parcel monthly ceiling. The UK is one of the eight countries Sendcloud fully supports shipping from, alongside Austria, Belgium, France, Germany, Italy, the Netherlands and Spain, so this is not a coverage gap - it is a disclosure gap. Open a free account, pull the rates for your own services and postcodes, and only then decide which subscription the discount ladder justifies.

What should I weigh before buying Operations & Workflow software?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.
