How to actually use Sentaro

Connect one mailbox first, then run it alongside what you already have. Sentaro connects over the Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 admin APIs in about four minutes, with no agents and no MX changes, so your existing gateway stays in place and both see the same mail. Sentaro's own FAQ says you do not have to remove a secure email gateway; most customers retire it later, once the engine has proven itself on live traffic.

Keep score for two full weeks in two columns. Real catches: messages your current filtering delivered that this one labels Threat or Suspicious, and whether a person would plausibly have fallen for each. False positives: legitimate mail it held, and how long release took. Sentaro also inventories the OAuth grants in your tenant, so read that list in week one; an abandoned integration that still reads mail is a finding in its own right.

Price it on mailboxes, not people. At $6 per protected account after the first, 50 mailboxes is $294 a month, or 49 x $68 = $3,332 a year on annual. Start monthly: annual saves only $4 per account, and a Paddle purchase has a 14-day refund window. Before you sign, confirm SSO or SCIM if you need them, since the pricing page mentions neither, and ask for the SOC 2 and ISO 27001 dates.

Frequently asked questions

Do shared mailboxes and distribution lists count as paid accounts?

No. Sentaro's pricing FAQ, read 24 September 2026, defines a protected account as one mailbox it watches and says shared aliases and distribution lists do not count, so most organisations pay for fewer accounts than they have people. The first protected account is free, and each one after it is $6 a month on Pro, or $68 a year. A team of ten people with a shared sales@ and support@ address therefore pays for nine accounts: $54 a month.

Where is our mail processed, and does anyone at Sentaro read it?

In the EU, and by software. The pricing FAQ and homepage, read 24 September 2026, say training and inference run on EU-sovereign compute hosted in Sweden, that your signals never leave Europe and are never used to train third-party models, and that nobody at Sentaro reads your mail in the course of providing the service. The one exception it names: a slice of anonymised data can be reviewed for forensic analysis, and the DPA says when and by whom, so read that clause. Data is encrypted at rest with per-tenant keys and with TLS 1.2 or later in transit. SOC 2 and ISO 27001 are still listed as in progress.

Can we get our money back if it does not work out?

Yes, within 14 days of paying. Sentaro's refund policy, read 24 September 2026, gives a full refund within 14 days of a purchase made through Paddle, with no reason required, if you email legal@sentaro.com inside that window, and it applies to renewals as well as first purchases. Separately, the pricing FAQ says you can cancel any time: monthly plans stop at the end of the period, and annual plans follow the refund policy. So on annual, the 14 days after each yearly charge are your only way back.

What should I weigh before buying IT & Productivity software?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.
