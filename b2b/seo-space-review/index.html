SEO Space Review (2026)

MarketingResearched assessment · by Daniel HaketUpdated 2026-09-01

Squarespace has been quietly absorbing the job this tool was invented for.

Best for: Squarespace freelancers and studios billing for several client sites. Pricing by number of websites rather than per seat, multi-site dashboards and white-label PDF reports are things the big per-seat SEO suites never bothered to build for Squarespace. And the extension tells you where the setting actually lives in the editor.
Who should skip it: if you run a single Squarespace 7.1 site and have no clients. Squarespace's own free SEO panel, AI-generated descriptions and AI Visibility now cover much of what you would be paying for. And the free plan here will tell you whether the paid layer adds anything for your site before you spend.

Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 17 Aug 2026

(2026-08-17, re-verified on their own homepage pricing block) One figure is published: Pro, the default position on their website slider, is $33.33/mo billed annually, labelled MOST POPULAR and covering 5 websites. The slider runs 1, 3, 5, 10, 20 and unlimited websites, but the amounts for the other positions are rendered per selection and do not appear in the delivered page, so we do not quote them.

Yearly billing is sold as "4 Months Free", which means the equivalent monthly rate is higher than the headline — the page does not print it next to the plan. Pricing is by number of websites, not per seat, and their own line "All Plans Include Every Feature" means a higher position buys site slots rather than extra capability. Free entry is real: a scan of up to 50 pages with no card, plus a 7-day trial of Pro, also with no card.

An earlier reading (21 Jul 2026) recorded named Starter/Pro/Expert tiers at 1/3/5 sites with their own amounts; the page no longer shows that structure, so those figures have been withdrawn rather than carried forward. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The platform's own SEO panel, checklist, sitemaps and structured data are free, and its AI-suggested titles, descriptions and alt text are available on Fluid Engine 7.1. On 7 July 2026 it announced AI Visibility, brand tracking across AI search, included on 7.1 plans with no extra subscription. It is metered against plan-varying AI credits rather than unlimited. If you own a single Squarespace site, a large share of what you would pay for here is now a friendlier wrapper around something the platform already does.

The tool earns its keep on volume and client work, not on one site. Note too what their own pricing block says out loud — 'All Plans Include Every Feature' — so moving up the ladder buys site slots, not a better tool.

What SEO Space does

SEO tool built exclusively for Squarespace, delivered as a Chrome extension paired with a web dashboard. The extension runs inside the Squarespace editor and on live pages, scoring each page and saying in plain language what to change and where the setting lives; the dashboard adds site-wide audits, keyword rank tracking, backlink and competitor data, local SEO tools and scheduled white-label PDF client reports. Pricing is keyed to the number of websites rather than seats, which is why it suits freelancers and studios managing client sites. There is a genuinely usable free plan — a scan of up to 50 pages without a card; paid pricing is keyed to the number of websites, with the most-popular position (5 sites) at $33.33/month billed annually, read 17 Aug 2026.

What buyers report about SEO Space

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run SEO Space ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • It is built specifically for Squarespace rather than adapted to it, which is why designers use it — the site audit is the feature people name.
  • • Designers use the free audit as a lead magnet with their own clients, which is a use case the product supports deliberately.
What they complain about
  • • The step from the DIY plan to the designer plan is a real jump — roughly $14 to $39 a month — and keyword scanning sits on the paid side.
  • • The trial is 7 days.
  • • Reviews are thin and overwhelmingly positive, largely from the Squarespace designer community. Treat that as a small, friendly sample rather than a broad verdict.

When the paid plan is worth it

A single Squarespace site that just needs a one-off technical check-up is fine on the free option: the SEOSpace homepage offers a scan of up to 50 pages with no card required, alongside a 7-day trial of the paid plan. The concrete push toward paying is anything ongoing: tracking rankings week over week, auditing more than one site, or covering a site larger than a 50-page snapshot. The paid Pro plan is sold via a slider running from 1 site up to unlimited, priced at $33.33/mo billed annually at the 5-site level shown by default on the pricing page.

What upgrading does not solve: the tool only works on Squarespace, so it is no help for any other platform, and it does not generate backlinks or off-page authority. Its ranking recommendations are also billed as "personalized" only once a separate Google Search Console account is connected, a step SEOSpace does not perform for you, so a paid subscription on its own can still leave you with generic guidance.

How to actually use SEO Space

The first real step is creating a SEOSpace account, installing the Chrome extension, and opening a Squarespace site either through its live URL or inside the Squarespace editor. From there the extension icon in the bottom corner opens a scan of the current page, or a URL can be added from the dashboard directly. A Squarespace site and a Chrome browser are the baseline requirements. Ranking guidance is described as "personalized" only after a Google Search Console account is separately connected, and that linkup is a distinct step users can skip, leaving recommendations generic.

The free scan caps out at 50 pages, so a larger site will not get a full picture until scanning continues under a paid plan. The FAQ also notes that scanning draft or password-protected pages sits behind the paid plan rather than the free scan. That trips up anyone auditing a site before it goes live.

Distilled from SEOSpace homepage and pricing block, https://seospace.co/ (vendor, read 2026-08-06 and re-verified 2026-08-17); SEOSpace FAQ on the same site (vendor, read 2026-08-06). Vendor-published pages, not independent reviews.

The natural comparison is Ahrefs or Semrush — SEO Space wins on translation, not data: it knows where each setting lives in the Squarespace editor, while Ahrefs and Semrush have vastly deeper indexes and cost several times more. Pick by whether your bottleneck is knowing what to fix or knowing where to fix it.

Frequently asked questions

Will SEO Space integrate with my existing stack?

Only if you are on Squarespace — that is the whole point of it. SEO Space is a Chrome extension that runs inside the Squarespace editor, so there is nothing to connect to WordPress, Shopify, Webflow or Wix. If your site is on any of those, this is the wrong tool and no integration or workaround changes that. If you are on Squarespace, the flip side is real: because it only targets one platform, it works inside the editor you already use instead of asking you to copy findings between tools.
