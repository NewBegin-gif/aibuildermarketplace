SEOmatic Review (2026)

SEO & MarketingResearched assessment · by Daniel HaketUpdated 2026-08-25

This app is eleven years old and has 114 reviews.

Best for: Shopify stores under 5,000 products that want image compression, alt text, auto-generated meta tags, schema and sitemaps from one app on a flat monthly fee, and would rather not pay per AI credit.
Who should skip it: if your catalogue is larger than 5,000 products, or you want the safest bet in a category where three rivals on the same page carry 850 to 2,400 reviews at 4.9-5.0 against this app's 4.4 over 114.

What SEOmatic does

SEO suite for Shopify from Aheadworks: unlimited image compression with alt text, AI-generated meta titles and descriptions, SEO audit, schema markup and both XML and HTML sitemaps. Free up to 10 products; the paid ladder is priced purely by catalogue size — $15, $25 or $40 a month for 500, 2,000 or 5,000 products, with a 14-day trial. Live since 2015, rated 4.4 over 114 App Store reviews.

Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 19 Aug 2026

Four tiers, and the only thing that changes between the three paid ones is how many products they cover. Free: 10 products, 50 image optimisations, AI SEO audit, auto-pilot AI meta title and description, schema markup, HTML sitemap. Starter $15 a month or $144 a year (save 20%): 500 products, unlimited image optimiser, broken links, XML and HTML sitemaps, 14-day free trial.

Basic $25 a month or $240 a year: 2,000 products. Advanced $40 a month or $384 a year: 5,000 products. All charges are billed in USD and recur every 30 days. The vendor's own pricing page at seomatic.io publishes the identical ladder, which is worth noting because the two often disagree — here they do not. There is no plan above 5,000 products on either page, so a larger catalogue has no listed price at all.

Developer Aheadworks, 12495 S Parkland Bay Trail, Parkland, Florida; the app launched 18 August 2015 and carries a 4.4 average over 114 App Store reviews, and it holds Shopify's 'Built for Shopify' badge. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

That is the number to sit with. On the same listing page Shopify shows three direct rivals. SEOLab is 5.0 over 2,368 reviews and free, Tiny SEO 5.0 over 2,261 and free to install, and Smart SEO 4.9 over 851 with a free plan. All three carry between seven and twenty times the review volume, all are rated higher, and none of them charges by product count. SEOmatic's 4.4 is a real rating from real merchants, but it is the weakest track record on its own page.

The distribution behind that 4.4 matters too: 81% five stars against 8% one star, with almost nothing in the middle. The most substantial recent review, from March 2025, is a merchant of six months saying it appears to work on images and nothing else, and that support is difficult and slow. The vendor replied that the reports do exist, and asked them to get in touch. An older one-star from a merchant who lasted seventeen minutes reads 'Paid for it to tell me 0% savings'.

Both are about the same thing — what the app actually did after installation was not visible to the person paying for it. And the product cap is a hard ceiling, not a soft one. There is no tier above 5,000 products on the listing or on seomatic.io, so if your catalogue is larger than that, the published ladder simply does not have a row for you.

What buyers report about SEOmatic

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run SEOmatic ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Recent reviewers rate the AI store audit: a Spanish merchant in October 2024 says the whole audit ran in a short sitting and read professionally.
  • • Support is praised in the newer reviews, including by merchants on the free plan — a February 2024 reviewer notes they were helped despite paying nothing.
  • • The original image-compression job is what the long-tenured five-stars are about, and they are short and consistent: it did what it said, it was cheap, it was simple.
What they complain about
  • • The savings promise is the oldest and sharpest complaint. Multiple reviewers paid for compression advertised around 30% and measured about 12% afterwards — one spent over $110 for that result, another was told 0% savings after paying. Those reviews date from 2016-2017.
  • • Damage that cannot be undone: a December 2021 reviewer paid $18.21 and describes hundreds of product photos coming back blurry, pixelated and discoloured with no reversal, and having to re-upload the originals. The vendor's reply points to a 30-day backup.
  • • A December 2015 reviewer says the app removed all 725 images from their catalogue and broke the site.
  • • Support latency recurs across years: a 2017 reviewer waited over a week with no reply, a 2020 reviewer got no help at all, and a March 2025 reviewer — six months into using it — calls support difficult and slow.
  • • A March 2025 reviewer says it appears to work on images and nothing else, and that they see no reporting beyond what was done to the images. That is worth weighing against the app's current positioning as a full SEO optimizer.
  • • A 2018 reviewer found the auto-generated alt text worse than their own and had to override each proposal manually.

Where the ratings disagree. The 4.4 across 114 reviews averages two different products. The listing began life as Minifier, an image-compression app, and nearly all of the nine one-stars come from that era and are about compression falling short of the advertised savings or damaging photos. The reviews from 2024 onward are about an AI SEO audit and are largely positive. Read the old reviews for how the vendor handles a merchant whose images came back worse, and the new ones for what the current product does — averaging them tells you about neither.

When the paid plan is worth it

Read the four feature lists side by side and the ladder collapses into one number: 500, 2,000, 5,000 products. Unlimited image optimisation, the SEO audit, auto-pilot meta titles and descriptions, broken-link detection, schema markup and both sitemap generators are on Starter and stay exactly the same on Basic and Advanced. You are not buying capability at $25 or $40, you are buying catalogue size.

So the tier is picked for you by your product count, and the only real decision is Free versus Starter. Free stops at 10 products and 50 image optimisations, which is a demonstration rather than a plan — enough to see what the audit flags on a handful of products, not enough to run a store on. The moment you have a real catalogue you are at $15.

The annual option is the one place there is money on the table: $144 against $180 for twelve months of Starter, $240 against $300, $384 against $480. The vendor's site frames the same discount as 'two months free with annual billing'; on the numbers it is closer to two and a half. Either way it is a fifth off for committing a year to an app whose paid trial is fourteen days.

How to actually use SEOmatic

Install on Free first and point it at ten products you know well — that is exactly what the free tier is for, and it is the only way to see whether the audit tells you anything you did not already know. Look specifically for the reporting. The recurring complaint in the reviews is that merchants could not see what the app had done, so before you pay, find the screen that lists the changes it made and check that it names products and fields, not just image counts.

Turn auto-pilot meta titles and descriptions on for a small collection rather than the whole catalogue, then look at those pages in Google Search Console a fortnight later; auto-generated meta descriptions are the setting most likely to be rewritten by Google anyway. Count your products before you pick a tier, because the tier is nothing but the product cap. Take the 14-day trial on Starter before committing to the annual price: a fifth off is only a saving if the app is still installed in month three.
