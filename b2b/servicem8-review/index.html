What they complain about

Unlimited users, metered jobs

Say the good part first, because it is genuinely unusual: every paid tier carries unlimited users. Your whole crew goes in without the bill moving, which in field-service software is close to unheard of (2026-08-30, re-read on their own pricing page from a US exit, USD). The meter is jobs instead, and jobs are the thing you control least. Free is $0 for one user and 30 jobs a month.

Starter is $29 for 50, Growing $79 for 150, Premium $149 for 500 and Premium Plus $349 for 1,500 or more. A second, quieter meter sits on the free tier only: ten AI uses a day, where every paid tier is unlimited. Fifty jobs a month is about two and a half a working day, which a two-van operation passes in any decent month — and the next step is not gentle: $29 to $79.

Per job the ladder does improve, from $0.58 on Starter to $0.53 on Growing, $0.30 on Premium and $0.23 on Premium Plus, so volume is rewarded. But the marginal jobs are what matter when you are choosing: the hundred extra jobs Growing buys cost $0.50 each, while the 350 that Premium adds cost $0.20 each. The middle of this ladder is the expensive part. So count your jobs in your best month rather than your average one, because that is the month that trips the tier.

And if your trade is seasonal, accept that you will spend part of the year on a plan you do not need — or plan to move between tiers deliberately rather than being moved. The 14-day trial on the paid tiers is where you should be testing the thing that actually matters: whether your people will use it on a phone in a van.

    How to actually use ServiceM8

    Count your jobs in your busiest month, not your average one, because that is the month the tier is set by. Fifty jobs on Starter is about two and a half a working day — a two-van operation clears that easily — and the step from Starter to Growing is $29 to $79. Then put the whole crew in from day one, because you can. Unlimited users on every paid tier means there is no reason to ration logins, and the value of field software comes entirely from everyone actually using it rather than one person retyping job cards.

    Use the 14-day trial on a real job, end to end: quote, schedule, dispatch, complete, invoice, take payment — from a phone, in a van, with poor signal. That is where this class of software fails, and never in the office. If your trade is seasonal, decide in advance how you will handle the quiet half of the year. The meter is jobs, so a winter on a summer tier is money for nothing — plan to step down deliberately rather than noticing in March.

    What happens when I run out of jobs mid-month?

    You keep working. Their FAQ says you can create new jobs as normal for another seven days after you hit the ceiling; if your monthly reset falls inside that window your job credits refresh and nothing happens, and if it does not you are prompted to upgrade. So the meter is a prompt rather than a wall, and there is no overage fee on Starter, Growing or Premium — the comparison table prices additional jobs at 20c only on Premium Plus. That seven-day grace is worth knowing before you buy a tier above the one you need for the two busy months a year.

    Will it work if my crew is on Android?

    Partly, and this is the thing to settle before the price. The full-featured app is built for iOS; Android gets ServiceM8 Lite, which the FAQ describes as being for technicians, subcontractors and temporary workers who need core job management — view schedules and job details, complete checklists. ServiceM8 still recommends iPhones for your main field technicians, and runs both an Upgrade to iPhone offer and a Small Business Fund to help buy them. A $29 subscription that assumes your crew carries iPhones is not a $29 decision.

    Is the free plan really capped at 30 jobs?

    That is the printed number, and the page names two ways past it. The FAQ says free-plan users can earn unlimited bonus jobs simply by accepting card payments, and a separate Promotions answer says working through their Learning Roadmap unlocks higher job and SMS limits, plan upgrades and higher-tier add-ons. Neither route is quantified anywhere on the page, and both are theirs to change, so treat 30 as what you are entitled to and anything above it as a bonus you have not been promised in writing.

    Text messaging is listed as free on paid plans — is it unlimited?

    No. The comparison table shows 10c per SMS on Free and Free on all four paid tiers, but the FAQ says there is a standard overage fee per SMS sent over your plan's monthly SMS cap. The caps themselves are not printed on the pricing page, and the fee is not either. If your plan is to lean on on-the-way texts, booking reminders, quote follow-ups and two-way SMS — which is most of what this product automates — ask for the per-plan cap and the overage rate before you commit.
