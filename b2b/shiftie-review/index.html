Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 24 Sep 2026

£2.75 per active employee per month for the first 30, then £1.50 for each employee above 30 — the reduced rate is marginal, not retroactive. Measured on the vendor's own pricing slider: 1 employee £2.75, 5 £13.75, 10 £27.50, 15 £41.25, 30 £82.50, 31 £84.00, 50 £112.50, 100 £187.50, 150 £262.50, 199 £336.00. At 200 employees the slider stops quoting and switches to Custom Pricing.

There is no minimum and no entry-plan floor: one employee costs £2.75. Monthly billing only — no annual option is offered anywhere on the page. Billing is in arrears: at the start of each month you are charged for users marked active during the previous month. 28-day free trial, no card details required, and 7-day UK-based support included. Cancelling is a button in the billing section; access runs to the end of the current billing period, and the cancellation can be reversed until then. Note the domain move: shiftie.co.uk now redirects to shiftie.co. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Pricing is genuinely low, but the per-active-employee model means a large seasonal team in a busy month costs more than the headline suggests. For shift-based scheduling specifically, it's a clean, cheap fit.

What Shiftie does

Rota building, clock-ins, leave and shift swaps for shift-based businesses. Charged per active employee — £2.75 a month for the first 30 and £1.50 for each one above that, with no minimum and a custom quote past 200 staff. Not an HR or payroll platform, so those stay separate purchases.

The thirty-first employee costs half the thirtieth

Shiftie charges per active employee per month, and the ladder is short enough to price exactly. The first 30 employees cost £2.75 each; every employee above 30 costs £1.50. At 200 the published pricing stops and becomes a custom quote. There is no minimum and no entry plan: one employee is £2.75, five is £13.75, fifteen is £41.25 (2026). The important detail is that the £1.50 is marginal rather than retroactive, and reading it the other way doubles your estimate.

A 50-person team does not pay 50 × £1.50 = £75. It pays £82.50 for the first 30 plus £30 for the other twenty — £112.50 a month. Checked against the vendor's own slider on 5 September 2026, the numbers land exactly there: 31 employees £84.00, 100 £187.50, 199 £336.00. So the blended rate falls as you grow but never reaches £1.50 — at 100 staff it is £1.88 a head, at 199 it is £1.69.

What paying for more staff does not fix is the shape of the commitment. There is no annual plan to discount and no volume contract to negotiate below 200 employees — the slider is the price, and above 200 it stops being published at all, so the largest deployments are the ones with no number to look up. Nor does spending more widen the product: this is scheduling, clock-ins, leave and swaps, and no amount of headcount turns it into payroll or a full HR platform.

Where the model genuinely helps is a seasonal payroll, because you pay only for active employees. A pub running twelve staff in January and thirty-five in July pays £33 in winter and £90 in summer, so budget from your busiest month rather than the average. And use the full 28-day trial: most software gives you fourteen days, but rota software only fails at the edges — the sick call, the swap approved on a phone at seven in the morning, the shift that crosses midnight — and a four-week cycle is long enough to hit them.

How to actually use Shiftie

Use all 28 days and build a real rota, not a sample one. The trial is twice the usual length and needs no card details, and the reason to use the extra fortnight is that scheduling software fails at the edges. Someone calls in sick. A swap has to be approved on a phone at seven in the morning, a shift crosses midnight, somebody's leave overlaps a busy weekend. None of that happens in week one.

Price your own headcount before the trial ends, and do it marginally. The first 30 employees are £2.75 each and everyone above 30 is £1.50, so a 50-person team is £112.50 a month rather than the £75 you get by applying the lower rate across the board. There is no minimum to catch you out at the small end — five staff really is £13.75 — and above 200 employees the published pricing stops and you are into a custom quote.

Understand the billing before the first invoice, because it runs in arrears. At the start of each month you are charged for whoever was marked active in the previous month, and your first bill lands on the 1st of the month after the trial ends. That makes marking leavers inactive a billing task, not just an admin one — and if your headcount swings with the season, model your busiest month rather than the average.

Frequently asked questions

Does Shiftie run payroll, or connect to our payroll software?

It does neither out of the box. Shiftie's plan covers scheduling, availability, leave, documents, time clock and timesheets, team messaging and reports. Its integrations page, read 24 September 2026, lists DigiTickets, TrekkSoft, eola, ICRTouch, Drawbridge and InfoVetted plus calendar feeds for staff, and no payroll connector. For anything else it points to its API, which it says was built API-first, with access arranged on request. So budget for a payroll tool alongside it, and during the trial check how you would get approved timesheets from Shiftie into that tool.

Do staff need to install an app on their phones?

No. Shiftie's FAQ, read 24 September 2026, describes it as a progressive web app: anyone with an internet connection and an up-to-date browser logs in at shiftie.app, and staff can save it to their home screen when prompted instead of installing anything from an app store. That makes it easy to roll out to casual staff on older phones. It also means you should test during the 28-day trial how well it copes with patchy signal in your venue.

What happens when we cancel?

You keep access until the end of the billing period. The FAQ, read 24 September 2026, says cancelling is a 'Cancel Account' button in the billing section, that access runs to the end of the current billing period, and that you can reverse the cancellation until then. Billing runs in arrears: at the start of each month you are charged for users marked active in the previous month. By that rule the month you cancel in is billed at the start of the following one, so expect that last charge. There is no annual contract to exit, because the page offers monthly billing only.

What should I weigh before buying Operations & Workflow software?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.
