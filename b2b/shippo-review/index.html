Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 25 Sep 2026

Read on their own pricing page, re-verified 25 September 2026. Starter is free: up to 30 labels a month, discounted USPS, UPS, FedEx and DHL Express rates, 1 user login, and 5¢ per label if you connect your own carrier account. Pro is banded by monthly label volume, priced monthly or annually: 1-200 labels $19/mo or $205/yr; 201-500 $29 or $313; 501-1,000 $54 or $583; 1,001-2,500 $79 or $853; 2,501-5,000 $139 or $1,501; 5,001-10,000 $199 or $2,149.

Annual billing saves about 10% at every band. Labels beyond 10,000 a month cost $0.08 each. Pro makes your own carrier accounts free and adds branded tracking, chat and phone support. Premier is contact-us: unlimited shipments, 15+ logins, a dedicated account manager. Non-US address validation is metered separately at 9¢, 8¢ and 6¢ per validation on Starter, Pro and Premier. API calls not tied to a Shippo label, and the Estimate API, bill at API Starter rates. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The free tier stops at 30 labels a month, and its 5¢ per label on your own carrier accounts quietly adds up. Pro looks like a flat $17 a month until you read the comparison table: it covers up to 10,000 shipments and then bills $0.08 for every label beyond that, so a good sales month adds a variable line to a subscription that reads as fixed. Address validation outside the US is metered too, at 9¢, 8¢ or 6¢ depending on plan.

The real money is elsewhere anyway — in the carrier rate discounts, which vary by lane and weight. Run your five most common shipments through it against your current rates; that comparison decides, not the subscription price.

The headline is the annual rate, and Pro has six bands

The headline is the annual rate, and Shippo's own page admits it the moment you flip the billing toggle (read 6 September 2026). Pro reads 'Starts at $17/mo' on the annual view and 'Starts at $19/mo' on the monthly one. So it is $19 month to month, or $205 for the year — $17.08 a month, rounded down for the card, and the ~10% saving the page advertises. An earlier reading of this review set $17 x 12 = $204 against the $205 annual price and called it a contradiction.

It is not one; the $204 was never a price Shippo charges. The volume figures are not a contradiction either. Pro is banded, and the band comes from a dropdown most readers never open. Past 10,000 labels in a month, $0.08 each. So your label count is the meter, and it moves the price whether you watch it or not. User seats ride along, with one odd jump: the page's own band data lists 5 logins, then 7, then 10, then 15 at the 2,500 band — and then 300 at both bands above it.

If you need more than fifteen people in the account, the cheapest route to them is a volume band you may not otherwise need. What upgrading does not solve is the metered work beside the labels. API calls not tied to a Shippo label — tracking, rating, address validation — and the Estimate API bill at API Starter rates on every plan, and non-US address validation stays metered at 8¢ on Pro, so a thousand international lookups a month is $80 against a $19 subscription.

No band includes them. Nor does any band change the carrier rate discounts, which vary by lane and by weight and are the reason to use a platform like this at all. Price five real shipments against what you pay today; the subscription is rounding.

How to actually use Shippo

Price five real shipments before you look at a plan. Take your five most common parcels — the actual weights, the actual destinations — and compare Shippo's quoted rates against what you pay your carriers today. Discounts vary by lane and weight, so this is the only comparison that predicts your bill, and it dwarfs the subscription either way. Use the free Starter plan to do it. Thirty labels a month is enough to ship real orders and see real rates, though note the 5¢ per label if you connect your own carrier account rather than using Shippo's negotiated ones.

Then pick your band deliberately, because Pro is six of them and the pricing card only shows the smallest. Count your labels in a busy month, not an average one, and open the dropdown to find the band that covers it — 1-200 at $19 a month, up to 5,001-10,000 at $199, with about 10% off for paying annually. Budget $19 rather than the advertised $17 unless you are paying for the year up front.

If you ship internationally, add address validation at 8¢ a lookup on Pro. A thousand a month is $80 — several times the plan itself.

Frequently asked questions

If I move up a Pro band mid-month, am I charged straight away?

No, according to the pricing-page FAQ (read 25 September 2026): you can cancel or change your plan at any time, and if you upgrade you are not billed the higher price until the next billing cycle, which runs from the day you signed up. So a busy month that pushes you from 1-200 labels ($19) into 201-500 ($29) does not cost more that month. Past 10,000 labels it is different: the table bills $0.08 per extra label.

Are there fees beyond the subscription and postage?

Shippo's FAQ (read 25 September 2026) says no hidden fees: you pay postage, the plan fee if you have one, optional insurance and an optional global address validation fee per lookup — 9¢ on Starter, 8¢ on Pro, 6¢ on Premier. Two more lines are on the same page: 5¢ per label on Starter if you use your own carrier account, and API calls not tied to a Shippo label, plus the Estimate API, billed separately at API Starter rates.

How many people can log in to one Shippo account?

It depends on the band. Starter has 1 user login. Pro's volume dropdown (read 25 September 2026) gives 5 logins at 1-200 labels, 7 at 201-500, 10 at 501-1,000, 15 at 1,001-2,500 and 300 at both 2,501-5,000 and 5,001-10,000; Premier says 15+. If you need more than fifteen people in the account, the cheapest route on Pro is the 2,501-5,000 band at $139 a month or $1,501 a year.

What should I weigh before buying Operations & Workflow software?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.
