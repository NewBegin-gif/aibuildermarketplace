Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 6 Sep 2026

Three paid plans, no free plan on the table. Rocket $15/mo, or $12/mo billed annually ($36 off the year); Pro $29/mo, or $24/mo annually ($60 off); Agency $99/mo, or $82/mo annually ($204 off). Pages included: 5 on Rocket, 50 on Pro, 250 on Agency, with Agency adding Projects and Teams from three up. Direct messenger buttons, Click Insights and Shor Rewards are on all three; Google Analytics, custom domains (per project) and Dynamic Feeds (5 per page) start at Pro.

The table states all prices are in USD and all sales are final. Note that the marketing site's own /pricing/ page renders nothing — this table is only reachable at dash.shorby.com. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Its case rests on two genuinely differentiated features: messenger deep-links, one tap into WhatsApp/Telegram chats, and retargeting pixels firing on every click. If you use neither, Linktree's free tier wins; if messenger-first selling or ad retargeting is your funnel, the $15 pays for itself.

What Shorby does

Link-in-bio pages and short links, with messenger deep-links into WhatsApp or Telegram and retargeting pixels firing on every click. It charges from about $15/month where Linktree does not charge at all, so the gap only pays off if messenger selling or ad retargeting is genuinely your funnel.

What each tier buys, and where Rocket actually stops

Rocket is $15 a month, or $12 if you pay for the year. Pro is $29, or $24 annually. Agency is $99, or $82 annually. Those are the amounts on Shorby's own table on 6 September 2026, in USD, with a note that all sales are final. The entry plan is less crippled than it looks. Rocket includes the messenger deep-links and Click Insights — Shorby's own click analytics — so at $15 you can both build the page and see what happens on it.

What it does not include is Google Analytics, a custom domain, Dynamic Feeds, and more than five pages. That is a real ladder rather than a hostage-taking, and it means the cheapest plan is a coherent product for a single creator. The competitive problem is elsewhere, and it is not about tiers. This is a category whose market leader gives the link page away, so Shorby has to earn the gap from day one on two genuinely differentiated things: deep-links that drop a visitor straight into a WhatsApp or Telegram conversation, and retargeting pixels that fire on every click.

If your funnel is neither messenger-first selling nor paid ads, you are paying $180 a year for a page a free tool would host. If retargeting is the reason you are here, the arithmetic is not close. Fifteen dollars a month is less than a day's ad spend for most advertisers, and a pixel firing on every bio-link click builds an audience you otherwise have no way to collect. What pushes you up to Pro is a custom domain, Google Analytics alongside Shorby's own numbers, or more than five pages — not the ability to measure at all.

Agency at $99 is straightforward: it beats separate Pro accounts from the fourth client onward. Three clients on Pro is $87 and stays cheaper; four is $116 and does not. Paying annually changes that crossover, because $82 against three annual Pro seats at $72 does not pay off until the fourth client either. One thing to know before you pay, because it is not about features. The prices above are on dash.shorby.com, the application.

The marketing site's own /pricing/ page returns HTTP 200 and then renders nothing — its markup still carries unreplaced build placeholders — and the login and help subdomains, app.shorby.com and help.shorby.com, serve a certificate issued for dash.shorby.com, so a browser blocks both outright. None of that changes what the product does, and dash.shorby.com works fine once you know to go there. But a link-in-bio tool is infrastructure for your traffic, and the state of this vendor's own front door is worth one minute of your attention before you move a bio link onto it.

How to actually use Shorby

Go to dash.shorby.com, not to the pricing link on the marketing site — that page loads and then shows nothing, and app.shorby.com serves a certificate your browser will reject. The dashboard is where both the plan table and the sign-up live. Decide in advance which of the two differentiated features you are buying, because free alternatives cover everything else. If it is messenger deep-links, set one up in the first hour and click it from a phone that is not yours — the test is whether it opens the conversation cleanly on iOS and Android, not whether the page looks right.

If it is retargeting, install the pixel on day one so the audience starts building immediately. Rocket at $15 is a real starting point rather than a trap: it carries the messenger buttons and Click Insights, so you can measure from the first plan. Move up to Pro at $29 when you need a custom domain, Google Analytics next to Shorby's own numbers, Dynamic Feeds, or more than five pages.

If you are buying for clients, count them first. Agency at $99 only beats separate Pro accounts from the fourth client: three clients cost $87 on Pro, four cost $116.

The natural comparison is Linktree or Beacons — the free-tier giants — Shorby's paid-only stance is justified only by its messenger and pixel features; decide on those. Weigh it against Linktree on the job you actually need done.
