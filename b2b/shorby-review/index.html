What Shorby does

Link-in-bio pages and short links, with messenger deep-links into WhatsApp or Telegram and retargeting pixels firing on every click. It charges from about $15/month where Linktree does not charge at all, so the gap only pays off if messenger selling or ad retargeting is genuinely your funnel.

Pricing (2026)

(2026) Rocket about $15/mo, Pro ~$29/mo (analytics, custom domains), Agency ~$99/mo (multi-client); 7-day trial, no permanent free tier. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

The honest knock

Its case rests on two genuinely differentiated features: messenger deep-links, one tap into WhatsApp/Telegram chats, and retargeting pixels firing on every click. If you use neither, Linktree's free tier wins; if messenger-first selling or ad retargeting is your funnel, the $15 pays for itself.

Rocket has no analytics, which is the odd part

Rocket is about $15 a month, Pro about $29 with analytics and custom domains, and Agency about $99 for multi-client work. There is a 7-day trial and no permanent free tier (2026). That last fact is the whole competitive problem, because the market leader in this category gives the link page away. Shorby charges from day one, so it has to earn the gap — and its case rests on two genuinely differentiated things: messenger deep-links that drop a visitor straight into a WhatsApp or Telegram conversation, and retargeting pixels that fire on every click.

Which makes the shape of the entry plan strange. Rocket costs $15 and does not include analytics — those start at Pro. So on the cheapest paid plan you are paying for a link page you cannot measure, when a free alternative gives you the page and some measurement. Rocket only makes sense for one buyer: someone whose funnel is specifically messenger-first, who wants the deep-links and genuinely does not care about click data.

For everyone else the real entry price is Pro at $29, which is where the product becomes coherent. If retargeting is the reason you are here, the arithmetic is not close. Fifteen dollars a month is less than a day's ad spend for most advertisers, and a pixel that fires on every click of every link in your bio builds an audience you otherwise have no way to collect. If you are not running ads, that feature is worth nothing and $180 a year is a lot for a link page.

Agency at $99 is straightforward: it beats buying separate Pro accounts from the fourth client onward. Three clients on Pro is $87 and stays cheaper; four is $116 and does not. The 7-day trial is short for this — a week of link clicks is a thin sample — so decide on the two differentiated features rather than on the data you collect in that window.

How to actually use Shorby

Decide in advance which of the two differentiated features you are buying, because the free alternatives cover everything else. If it is messenger deep-links, set one up in the first hour and click it from a phone that is not yours — the test is whether it opens the conversation cleanly on iOS and Android, not whether the page looks right. If it is retargeting, install the pixel on day one, because seven days is a thin sample and you want the audience building from the start.

Skip Rocket unless messenger is genuinely your funnel. It has no analytics — those begin on Pro — so at $15 you are paying for a link page you cannot measure while free tools elsewhere give you both. For most buyers the real entry plan is Pro at $29. If you are buying for clients, count them first. Agency at $99 only beats separate Pro accounts from the fourth client: three clients cost $87 on Pro, four cost $116.

The natural comparison is Linktree or Beacons — the free-tier giants — Shorby's paid-only stance is justified only by its messenger and pixel features; decide on those. Weigh it against Linktree on the job you actually need done.
