Shore Review (2026)1.35% Card Fee Beats the Tier Price

Operations & WorkflowResearched assessment · by Daniel HaketUpdated 2026-08-29

Shore is built for local appointment businesses, and that's also the boundary: the integration list is short (calendar, Google, Instagram, Meta), and once you add POS and payments the monthly total climbs well past the entry price. If you only need a booking link, this is more machine than you need. Throughout this review we weigh it against Fresha.

Best for: salons, barbers, spas, wellness studios, physical therapists, craftspeople and local retailers that want bookings via Google and Instagram, no-show-reducing reminders, marketing and (optionally) POS in one fixed-price system.
Who should skip it: if you just need a simple scheduling link (a Calendly-class tool is lighter) or you run a business without appointments at all.

(2026-08-15, read on their own pricing page, in euros) There are three packages, each per month and each currently marked down from a struck-through list price. What Shore does

Booking and business software for local service businesses (salons, barbers, wellness, coaches): 24/7 online appointments via your website, Google and Instagram, calendar and staff scheduling, client profiles, automated reminders that cut no-shows, plus email marketing and POS. Fixed monthly price with unlimited bookings — no per-booking commission.

The card fee outgrows the subscription

There are three packages, each per month and each currently struck through with a lower price beside it. Starter is €59.90 marked down to €49.90 with two employees included, and Growth €89.90 down to €79.90 with four. The top tier is €129.90 down to €119.90 with unlimited employees (2026-08-15, read on their own pricing page, in euros). Notice that the discount is a flat €10 on all three, which makes it 17% off Starter and 8% off the top tier — and that a permanently struck price is a list price nobody pays.

The employee caps set the shape. Per head, Starter is €24.95 and Growth €19.98, so Growth is the better rate the moment you have four people. But Growth stops at four, so a fifth employee forces you to the top tier — €40 more for one more person, and at five staff that works out at €23.98 a head, worse than Growth. The top tier's per-head cost only draws level with Growth at exactly six employees and beats it from seven.

Between five and six staff you are paying for headroom you have not reached yet. None of which is the biggest number on your bill. Card payments run at up to 1.35% on every tier, and a salon taking €10,000 a month on cards pays €135 in processing — more than the top subscription costs. At €20,000 it is €270, more than double. So the tier choice is a rounding error against the payment rate, and the question to put to Shore is what your actual card rate would be, in writing, against your real monthly card volume.

POS hardware is billed separately again on top. One caution about comparing anything here. Shore reorganised this grid during August 2026: on 4 August the same page carried five packages split across Booking, Marketing and POS, and by 15 August it carried the three above. Any quote, comparison or screenshot from earlier in the month describes a structure that no longer exists. The page is also region-specific, so verify your own country's rather than these euro tiers.

What you get for it is genuinely built for local appointment businesses. Starter carries the booking calendar, online booking through your own site plus Google and Instagram, reminders, deposits and the iPad register; Growth adds the marketing layer; shift planning, per-employee revenue reporting and a DATEV connection sit at the top. That focus is also the boundary: the integration list is short, and if all you need is a booking link, this is a great deal more machine than the job requires.

How to actually use Shore

Ask for your card processing rate in writing before you look at the packages, because it is the larger number. Up to 1.35% on €10,000 of monthly card takings is €135 — more than the top subscription — so the software tier is close to a rounding error against it. Get the rate quoted against your real monthly volume, not in the abstract, and ask what POS hardware costs on top.

Count employees next, because that is what the packages meter: two on Starter, four on Growth, unlimited above. If you have five or six staff you are in the awkward band where the top tier costs more per head than Growth did — it only pays off from seven. And check the page for your own country rather than working from any comparison you have read. Shore reorganised this grid during August 2026, from five packages split by Booking, Marketing and POS down to three, so anything written earlier describes a structure that has since been replaced.

The natural comparison is Fresha or Calendly — commission-based salon platforms or lighter pure-scheduling tools. Weigh it against Fresha on the job you actually need done.

Frequently asked questions

What should I weigh before buying Operations & Workflow software?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.
