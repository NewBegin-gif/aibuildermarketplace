Signal House Review (2026)500 Numbers Cost Less Than 499

Communication & VoiceResearched assessment · by Daniel HaketUpdated 2026-08-22

Communication tools win on reliability and coverage — every missed call or message is a customer who quietly moves on. So where does Signal House actually fit?

The honest bottom line. Worth it for teams sending real SMS volume in the US who already know what an A2P 10DLC campaign is and want a cheaper per-segment rate than Twilio’s list without giving up published pricing. The volume ladder is genuinely steep — from $0.0065 down to $0.0030 — so the case gets stronger the more you send, and the number discounts above 500 numbers suit anyone running many local presences. Skip it once you are mainly buying voice, because there is no published rate to compare and no way to model the cost. Skip it too if your volume is small: at a few thousand messages a month the per-segment saving against Twilio is a few dollars, and you would be trading a mature ecosystem and documentation for that. And if you are not already registered for A2P 10DLC, budget the brand and campaign fees plus the approval wait before you compare anything — those apply wherever you send from.

What Signal House does

SMS and Voice API positioned as a Twilio alternative. Usage-based with no platform fee: $0.0065 per SMS segment with no minimum, down to $0.0030 above 10m, and $1.00 a month per local number. A2P 10DLC fees are published; voice rates are not.

Pricing (2026)

(2026-08-07, read on their own pricing page) Usage-based, no platform subscription. SMS on a 10DLC local number costs $0.0065 per segment with no minimum, falling on volume: $0.0050 from 500k segments a month, $0.0045 from 1m, $0.0040 from 2m, $0.0035 from 5m and $0.0030 from 10m. Numbers are $1.00 a month, dropping to $0.50 between 500 and 1,000 numbers and $0.25 above 1,000.

MMS is $0.0200 per segment, $0.0170 above 500k. Toll-free numbers are $1.00 a month with SMS from $0.0079 down to $0.0050. Number lookup is $0.005. A2P 10DLC registration is billed separately: $4.50 for the brand, $15 one-time per campaign, then $1.50 a month for a low-volume campaign or $10 a month for a standard one. Carrier fees are passed through on top: outbound AT&T $0.0035, Verizon $0.0045, T-Mobile $0.0045.

A2P approval is quoted at 72 hours or less, dropping to 48 hours above 500k segments a month. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Two things are missing from a page that is otherwise unusually specific. The first is voice. The product is sold as an SMS and Voice API and the pricing page publishes no voice rate at all — nor short code pricing. If the voice half is why you are looking, you cannot budget it from public information and will have to ask. The second is the arithmetic behind the Twilio comparison, which is the whole pitch. Take the entry rate with no minimum. Signal House is $0.0065 a segment against Twilio’s $0.0083, which reads as a 22% saving. But carrier fees are a pass-through and identical either way: add T-Mobile’s $0.0045 to both and you get $0.0110 against $0.0128 — about 14%, not 22%. Numbers narrow it further, $1.00 against $1.15. So the saving is real and it is worth having at volume, but it is a discount on the platform margin rather than on your bill, and the headline does not make that distinction. On a million segments a month that difference is roughly $1,800 a month against Twilio’s list — worth switching for, provided the voice side does not turn out to be where the money goes. Worth noting on the other side: Signal House prints its A2P 10DLC registration fees on the pricing page, and Twilio’s equivalent page says only that fees apply and points elsewhere. Being able to see the whole bill before you sign is not nothing.
