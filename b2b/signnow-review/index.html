A signed PDF is a signed PDF

The published ladder is Business at $8 per user a month on annual billing, Business Premium at $15 and Enterprise at $30 - a clean doubling at each step, annualising to $96, $180 and $360 a seat. Month-to-month is priced higher. Above them sit a Site License billed at $1.50 per signature invite rather than per seat, aimed at organisations sending 1,000 or more documents a year, and airSlate Business Cloud at $50 per user a month, which bundles signNow with airSlate, pdfFiller and US Legal Forms.

The per-tier split is published, in signNow's own machine-readable pricing document. Business carries the legally binding signature itself with audit trails, templates, mobile apps and the ISO 27001, SOC 2 Type II and GDPR posture. Premium adds bulk send, kiosk mode, payment requests and quick invite links. Enterprise adds formula fields, conditional fields and advanced signer authentication. SSO, full API access and phone support are not on the seat ladder at all - they arrive with the Site License.

One contradiction survives, and it is the vendor's own. signNow states in the same document that pricing is charged per user and that all paid plans include unlimited users at no extra cost; its companion help page explains the model as usage-based, where you pay for documents sent rather than seats. Those cannot both describe a seat, so confirm at checkout what your quote actually meters, because the difference is the entire bill.

The structural point stands either way. Electronic signature is commoditised at the output: a legally valid signed document from an $8 tool is exactly as valid as one from a $30 tool. Nothing about the signature improves as you climb, so every step has to be justified by workflow - bulk send, conditional routing, authentication - and never by the signature itself. And if you send at volume, price the Site License against the seats before assuming Enterprise is the top of the range: at $1.50 an invite it undercuts a $96 seat for any user sending fewer than about 64 invites a year.

How to actually use SignNow

Confirm one thing at checkout that signNow's own documents cannot settle between them. Its pricing document states a per-user rate and, in the same breath, that all paid plans include unlimited users at no extra cost; the companion help page describes the model as usage-based, billed on documents sent rather than seats. Ask which of those your quote actually meters, because the difference is your entire bill. Then match the tier to a workflow rather than to the signature.

Business covers legally binding signing with audit trails and templates; Premium adds bulk send, kiosk mode and payment requests; Enterprise adds conditional and formula fields and stronger signer authentication; SSO and full API access only arrive with the Site License. If you send at volume, price that Site License at $1.50 an invite against $96 a seat a year - it wins below roughly 64 invites per user. Test with your real documents and real signers on the 7-day trial rather than a sample.

How a template behaves when a field moves, how a two-signer sequence routes and chases, what the audit trail records - and above all what it is like for a signer who is not technical and is doing it on a phone in a car park. And decide which problem you have first. This is built for everyday signing, not contract lifecycle management: if you need clause libraries, negotiation tracking and renewal alerts, no tier here is the answer.

Frequently asked questions

What should I weigh before buying Operations & Workflow software?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.
