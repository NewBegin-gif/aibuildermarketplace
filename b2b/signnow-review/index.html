Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 26 Sep 2026

Three plans on signNow's live checkout page, read in a browser on 26 September 2026 from the EU: Business EUR 8, Business Premium EUR 15 and Enterprise EUR 30 a month billed annually, against EUR 20, EUR 30 and EUR 50 month-to-month (the cards state the annual saving as EUR 144, EUR 180 and EUR 240); signnow.com/pricing.md, read the same day, gives the annual rates as $8, $15 and $30 per user a month.

Every card says 'Unlimited users' and 'Get 100 invites per year': the FAQ puts annual plans at up to 100 signature invites per account per year and monthly plans at up to 10 a month, after which the plan moves to pay-as-you-go for the extra invites, at a rate the page does not state. A Site License is billed at $1.50 per signature invite instead, with custom invite volumes, aimed at organisations sending 1,000+ documents a year, and is where SSO, full API access and phone support appear; HIPAA, 21 CFR Part 11 and QES are add-ons on top. airSlate Business Cloud is $50 per user a month and bundles signNow with airSlate, pdfFiller and US Legal Forms.

Free 7-day trial, no credit card required; refunds can be requested within 30 days of the invoice. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

A signed PDF is a signed PDF

The published ladder is Business at $8 per user a month on annual billing, Business Premium at $15 and Enterprise at $30 - a clean doubling at each step, annualising to $96, $180 and $360 a year. Month-to-month costs far more: from the EU on 26 September 2026 the cards read EUR 20, EUR 30 and EUR 50 against EUR 8, EUR 15 and EUR 30. Above them sit a Site License billed at $1.50 per signature invite rather than per seat, aimed at organisations sending 1,000 or more documents a year, and airSlate Business Cloud at $50 per user a month, which bundles signNow with airSlate, pdfFiller and US Legal Forms.

The per-tier split is published, in signNow's own machine-readable pricing document. Business carries the legally binding signature itself with audit trails, templates, mobile apps and the ISO 27001, SOC 2 Type II and GDPR posture. Premium adds bulk send, kiosk mode, payment requests and quick invite links. Enterprise adds formula fields, conditional fields and advanced signer authentication. SSO, full API access and phone support are not on the seat ladder at all - they arrive with the Site License.

The live page settles the meter, and it is not the seat. Every card reads 'Unlimited users' and 'Get 100 invites per year'; the FAQ (read 26 September 2026) counts 100 signature invites per account per year on annual plans and 10 a month on monthly ones, then switches to pay-as-you-go at a rate it does not print. signNow's pricing.md still says 'per user' and 'no envelope cap', so ask at checkout what the 101st invite costs.

The structural point stands. Electronic signature is commoditised at the output: a legally valid signed document from an $8 tool is exactly as valid as one from a $30 tool. Nothing about the signature improves as you climb, so every step has to be justified by workflow - bulk send, conditional routing, authentication - and never by the signature itself. What no tier raises is the allowance: Business, Premium and Enterprise all carry the same 100 invites a year, so climbing the ladder buys workflow, never volume. Volume is the Site License's job, at $1.50 an invite with custom volumes.

How to actually use SignNow

Count your signature invites before you count people. Users are unlimited on every paid plan, but annual plans include up to 100 invites per account per year and monthly plans 10 a month; past that the account moves to pay-as-you-go at a rate the pricing page does not print. An invite is one signing request, even to several recipients, and a declined or cancelled request still counts. Ask what the 101st costs before you sign up.

Then match the tier to a workflow rather than to the signature. Business covers legally binding signing with audit trails and templates; Premium adds bulk send, kiosk mode and payment requests; Enterprise adds conditional and formula fields and stronger signer authentication; SSO and full API access only arrive with the Site License. If you send well past 100 a year, price the Site License, at $1.50 an invite with custom volumes, against that overage rate.

Test with your real documents and real signers on the 7-day trial rather than a sample. How a template behaves when a field moves, how a two-signer sequence routes and chases, what the audit trail records - and above all what it is like for a signer who is not technical and is doing it on a phone in a car park. And decide which problem you have first. This is built for everyday signing, not contract lifecycle management: if you need clause libraries, negotiation tracking and renewal alerts, no tier here is the answer.

Frequently asked questions

How many documents can I send on a signNow plan?

Fewer than 'unlimited' suggests. Users are unlimited, but the pricing FAQ (read 26 September 2026) includes up to 100 signature invites per account per year on annual Business, Business Premium and Enterprise plans, and up to 10 a month on monthly plans; beyond that the plan moves to pay-as-you-go for the extra invites, at a rate the page does not state. The FAQ counts a request sent to several recipients at once as a single invite, and still counts it if a signer declines or you cancel it.

How much more does monthly billing cost?

A lot on the entry plan. From the EU on 26 September 2026 the cards read Business EUR 8 a month billed annually against EUR 20 month-to-month, Business Premium EUR 15 against EUR 30 and Enterprise EUR 30 against EUR 50 - the page states the annual saving as EUR 144, EUR 180 and EUR 240 a year. Monthly plans also include 10 invites a month rather than 100 a year. Use the 7-day free trial, which needs no card, before committing to either.

Can I get a refund if signNow doesn't work out?

Within 30 days. The pricing page (read 26 September 2026) advertises a 30-day money-back guarantee, and its FAQ says new customers who are dissatisfied, or anyone whose invoice was wrong, can ask support for a refund within 30 days of receiving the invoice. On annual billing that is the window to find out whether 100 invites a year is enough; after it, the year is paid.

What should I weigh before buying Operations & Workflow software?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.
