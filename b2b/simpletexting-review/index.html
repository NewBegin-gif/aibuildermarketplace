Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 17 Aug 2026

(re-verified 2026-08-17) Credit-based by monthly volume, from $39/mo (500 credits) up to about $909/mo (50,000), with SMS = 1 credit and MMS = 3. Yearly billing saves 20% — the entry plan is $39 a month billed yearly at $398.40 — and unused credits roll over. Extra credits beyond your plan run 5.5 cents each. Every paid plan includes three user seats, with additional seats at $20/mo each and additional numbers at $10/mo; a local number is $10/mo plus a $4 activation fee, and there is a one-off $4 carrier (10DLC) registration.

Toll-free and short-code options sit above that, with short codes beginning at $1,000/mo. Nonprofits, churches and other tax-exempt organisations get 15% off any monthly plan. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

When the paid plan is worth it

There is no permanent free plan, so the paid floor has to earn itself back — and the vendor quotes that floor two ways. A solution page advertises "$29/month for 500 outgoing SMS messages," while the pricing page charges $39.00 for the same 500 credits, or $398.40 billed yearly; budget against the pricing page. Tiers scale on volume beside one shared feature list — two-way messaging, auto-replies and drip campaigns, unlimited contacts — so paying more buys volume, not capability.

The spend justifies itself for a business with an opted-in list that texts on a predictable rhythm: incoming SMS replies cost nothing (incoming MMS still costs 1 credit each), and a bigger bundle beats overage. Sporadic sending is the bad fit: unused credits roll over only until the end of the following month, and overage runs 5.5 cents per credit, so guessing high and guessing low both leak money. Annual billing saves 20% but freezes that guess for a year. Upgrading does not remove the carrier layer: a local number is still $10 monthly plus a $4 registration, extra numbers $10 each, extra seats $20 monthly, and a dedicated short code begins at $1,000/mo. A 30-day refund covers a bad guess.

How to actually use SimpleTexting

Signup takes no credit card, and the 14-day trial is the whole evaluation window, so the number decision comes first. A local 10DLC number costs $10 per month plus a $4 one-time carrier registration, and the form asks for a legal company name, an EIN, a website, a customer support email and number, an opt-in method and two sample text messages. The usual stall is a mismatch between the stated use case and the submitted business details; the vendor says registration is often done in a day or less, and once the registry accepts it, activation takes minutes. A toll-free number carries no additional monthly fee, but verification typically takes three to five business days and carriers cap unverified toll-free traffic until it clears.

Once a number is live, the fastest route to something real is a keyword opt-in with a welcome message and a compliance auto-reply, then the Campaigns tab, New campaign, name it for internal reference, paste the message, choose a contact list, and send now, schedule it, or set it to repeat.

Frequently asked questions

What does SimpleTexting cost?

From $39 a month for 500 credits, billed yearly at $398.40, up to about $909 a month at 50,000 credits. An SMS spends one credit and an MMS three, extra credits beyond the plan are 5.5 cents each, and unused credits roll over. Read on 17 August 2026.

What does it cost for a team, and for extra numbers?

Every paid plan already includes three user seats, which is more generous than most; beyond that it is $20 a month per seat. Numbers are separate: $10 a month each, with a local number carrying a $4 activation fee on top and a one-off $4 carrier registration for 10DLC compliance. Short codes are a different product entirely, starting at $1,000 a month.

Is there a discount for nonprofits?

Yes, and it is stated on the page: nonprofits, churches and other tax-exempt organisations receive 15% off any monthly plan. That stacks against the 20% yearly discount as an either/or rather than both, so ask which applies before committing to a year.

Will SimpleTexting work for a remote or distributed team?

Generally yes, since this category is built for it, but the details that bite are timezone handling, how per-seat cost scales as the team spreads, and whether numbers, routing or coverage exist in the countries your people actually sit in. Availability varies far more by country than the marketing pages suggest, so check your specific regions before committing.

Who should not use SimpleTexting?

Skip it if you don't have a real opted-in list or a compelling offer — texting a cold audience burns money and trust. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
