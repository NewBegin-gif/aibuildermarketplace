When the paid plan is worth it

The free plan is genuinely usable and that makes the decision unusually clear: 50 bookings a month, one provider, one custom feature, no card. If you take fewer than 50 appointments a month and work alone, you may never need to pay. What pushes you up is rarely the booking count — it is the custom features. Free gives you one, Basic three, Standard eight, and only Premium makes them unlimited.

Reminders, intake forms, coupons, memberships and payment integrations each count as one, so a business wanting reminders plus online payment plus an intake form is already past Basic at EUR 11.90 and into Standard at EUR 24.90. The other trigger is your own branding. Free and Basic show SimplyBook.me on your booking page; the white-label footer starts at Premium (EUR 49.90 annual). If you are sending clients to that page from your own site, price that in before you compare with the cheaper tiers.

How to actually use SimplyBook.me

Count two numbers before you pick a plan, because they cap you independently: appointments a month, and how many custom features you actually need switched on. Most people size on bookings, hit the feature ceiling first, and upgrade twice. Start on Free rather than the trial. The 14-day trial runs on premium features, so it tells you what the top plan feels like and not whether the tier you intend to buy is enough.

Free is permanent and renews monthly — build the real booking page there. Set up the reminders before anything else. Reducing no-shows is what buyers on G2 and Capterra name first, and it is the one thing that pays for the subscription on its own. Connect your payment processor after that, remembering their fees are charged by Stripe, PayPal or Square directly and not by SimplyBook.me.
