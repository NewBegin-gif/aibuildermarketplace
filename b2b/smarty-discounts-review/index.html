Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 4 Sep 2026

Three tiers, all billed in USD and recurring every 30 days. Forever Free costs nothing, Lite is $9.99 a month or $99.99 a year, and Pro is $25.99 a month or $259.99 a year; the annual price on both paid tiers saves 17%, and both carry a 7-day free trial. What you are buying is capability, not volume: there is no order cap, no revenue meter and no surcharge tied to your Shopify plan anywhere in the block.

Free covers product and order discounts with basic targeting and what the listing itself calls low support. Lite adds shipping and mix-and-match discounts, enhanced targeting and priority support. Pro unlocks every discount type, every targeting rule, active time periods and free shipping on a single rule. One detail in the plan table is worth reading twice, because it is unusual and it is in your favour: each tier gives unlimited discounts at its own level plus up to two at the level above.

On Free you can build two Lite-grade discounts, on Lite two Pro-grade ones. That is a working trial of the next tier rather than a locked door, and it means you can prove the rule you actually need before you pay for it. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The app launched on 14 January 2025 and carries six reviews as of 4 September 2026 — all five stars, but six is not a track record, and the vendor, Neezon, is a small developer in Ho Chi Minh City rather than a company with a support contract behind it. The listing is unusually candid about what that means at the bottom of the ladder: the free plan's own feature list says low support.

Add to that what the app needs to do its job. The data-access panel says it reads customer data flagged as sensitive and can view and edit customers, products, discounts and the Online Store, which is the access any discount engine needs but is still broad. And because the ladder gates discount types rather than volume, the plan you land on is decided by the one rule you cannot live without: if the campaign you have in mind needs time-period scheduling or free shipping on a single rule, you are on Pro at $25.99 regardless of how few discounts you run.

How to actually use Smarty Discounts

Two lines in the vendor's documentation will save you a support ticket. If you are running a fixed-amount discount — the reason most stores pick this over Shopify's native engine — add every currency under Settings, Display currencies before you build the rule, because the editor expects that list to exist first. And leave the theme app embed alone unless you want storefront widgets: the docs state discounts still apply at checkout without it, so switching it on is a design decision, not a setup step.

Then build in the editor's own order instead of jumping around it — discount method (automatic, code or bulk codes), title, value, the apply and exclude rules, strategy, eligibility, maximum uses, combinations, and active dates last. Run the test the docs ask for and most merchants skip: one cart that should qualify and one that should not, on the live storefront. Export the rule set once it works, since the export is encrypted and reusable across drafts and stores.

Distilled from Smarty Discounts vendor documentation — the Install and open page and the Create a discount walkthrough (neezon.com/docs/smarty-discounts), which docs.neezon.com redirects to — plus the Shopify App Store listing (apps.shopify.com/smarty-discount), read 4 September 2026: editor section order and required fields, the display-currency prerequisite for fixed-amount discounts, the optional theme app embed, the positive and negative storefront test, encrypted rule import and export, and the plan gates.

The natural comparison is Shopify's own native discounts — the honest comparison is not another app but the discount engine already sitting in your admin. Shopify does percentage, fixed-amount, buy-X-get-Y and free-shipping discounts at no cost, and for most stores that is enough. Smarty Discounts starts earning its $9.99 or $25.99 at the point where you need a cap on the discount, a fixed amount that converts properly across markets, tiers built on weight or subtotal rather than quantity, or targeting by company or market — the rules the native engine cannot express at all.
