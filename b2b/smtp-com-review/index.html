Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 5 Sep 2026

Fixed-volume plans, all billed monthly: Essential $25 (50,000 emails, shared IP), Starter $80 (100,000, dedicated IP begins here), Growth $300 (500,000), Business $500 (1,000,000). Above that, High Volume Senders is quote-only and starts at a minimum of 250 million emails a month, with a dedicated platform instance, dedicated IP and MTA and no shared mail queue. Reputation Defender and Insight Engine are optional add-ons at 20% of plan cost each, available on all plans.

No free tier or published trial. Payment by card or PayPal. The site is operated by J2 Global Canada, a Ziff Davis company. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Pricing is volume-tiered, so a shared IP on the cheapest plan is fine for low volume while serious senders need the dedicated-IP tiers (and the IP warm-up discipline that comes with them). And the value-add features — reputation and verification — are metered on top at ~20% of plan cost, so the real bill is higher than the base tier. It's infrastructure, not a marketing suite; you bring the campaigns.

What SMTP.com does

Reliable transactional and bulk email delivery via SMTP relay and API: high-deliverability sending for app notifications, receipts and campaigns, with real-time analytics and dedicated IPs — built for volume senders.

The same price per email at both ends

Fixed-volume plans: Essential at $25 a month for 50,000 emails on a shared IP, Starter at $80 where dedicated IPs begin, Growth at $300, Business at $500 for roughly a million emails, plus custom high-volume tiers above that. Do the division on the two ends of that range and something odd falls out. Essential is $0.50 per thousand emails. Business is also $0.50 per thousand. Across a twentyfold increase in volume the unit price does not move at all — there is no volume discount here in the sense that word usually carries.

Whatever the higher plans are buying you, it is not cheaper email. What they are buying is the dedicated IP, and that is a real distinction rather than a packaging one. A shared IP means your deliverability is partly a function of other people's sending; a dedicated IP means it is entirely yours, for better and worse, and it comes with the warm-up discipline that any serious sender already knows about.

Dedicated IPs start at Starter, so the practical entry price for a serious sender is $80 rather than $25 — 3.2× the headline. Then add the meter on top. Reputation Defender and Insight Engine are each an optional add-on at 20% of your plan cost, available on every plan, so a Business deployment running both is $700 rather than $500 — and the honest way to compare this against anything is to price the whole stack rather than the tier.

What buying a bigger plan does not fix is the top of the ladder. The published tiers stop at Business and one million emails; the next published step, High Volume Senders, starts at a minimum of 250 million emails a month and carries no price at all, only a quote form. Between one million and 250 million there is nothing posted, so the moment you outgrow Business the price stops being something you can look up.

Nor does any tier come with a free allowance or a published trial: the cheapest way in is $25 a month, billed from the start. One useful cross-reference from our own dossiers, since both sit in the same category: at 50,000 emails a month, SMTP.com's Essential is $25, while SMTP2GO would be its $15 Starter plus 40,000 emails of overage at about $1 per thousand — $55, or 2.2× more.

At the low end of the volume range SMTP.com is materially the cheaper relay. It is a veteran and it is reliable, but be clear about what it is: infrastructure, not a marketing suite. You bring the campaigns, the list hygiene and the authentication records; it brings the pipe.

How to actually use SMTP.com

Decide whether you need a dedicated IP, because that is the real tier boundary rather than volume. Dedicated IPs begin at Starter at $80, so a serious sender's entry price is $80 rather than $25 — and the unit cost per thousand emails is identical at both ends of the range anyway, so you are not buying cheaper mail as you climb. If you do take a dedicated IP, plan the warm-up before you migrate.

A new IP has no reputation, so volume has to ramp gradually over weeks; moving your whole send to a fresh dedicated IP on day one is the classic way to make deliverability worse by paying more for it. Set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC before the first send regardless of tier. A relay delivers what your domain has earned, and switching provider does not reset that — half-configured authentication is the most common reason a change of relay disappoints.

Then budget the meters on top: Reputation Defender and Insight Engine are each 20% of your plan cost and available on any plan, so a Business deployment running both is $700 rather than $500. And be clear what you are buying — this is infrastructure rather than a marketing suite. You bring the campaigns, the list hygiene and the content.

The natural comparison is SendGrid or Postmark — close relay/API peers — Postmark leans transactional-fast, SendGrid broader; compare on IP options and deliverability at your volume.
