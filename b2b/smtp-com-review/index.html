What SMTP.com does

Reliable transactional and bulk email delivery via SMTP relay and API: high-deliverability sending for app notifications, receipts and campaigns, with real-time analytics and dedicated IPs — built for volume senders.

Pricing (2026)

(2026) Fixed-volume plans: Essential $25/mo (shared IP, 50k emails), Starter $80/mo (dedicated IP begins here), Growth $300/mo, Business $500/mo (~1M emails), plus custom high-volume tiers. Add-ons (Reputation Defender, Insight Engine) run ~20% of plan cost; verification ~$0.01/address. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

It's a veteran, reliable relay, but two honest notes. Pricing is volume-tiered, so a shared IP on the cheapest plan is fine for low volume while serious senders need the dedicated-IP tiers (and the IP warm-up discipline that comes with them). And the value-add features — reputation and verification — are metered on top at ~20% of plan cost, so the real bill is higher than the base tier. It's infrastructure, not a marketing suite; you bring the campaigns.

What they complain about
  • • Cancellation needs 30 days' notice and there are no refunds for unused service in that cycle. That is the consistent complaint.

  • • The interface is dated against Mailtrap or Postmark.
Best for: businesses and developers needing dependable high-deliverability transactional or bulk email via SMTP relay and API, with dedicated IPs at scale.
Who should skip it: if you send low volume and a free/cheap SMTP tier covers you, or you want a full marketing-email platform with templates and automation built in.

The natural comparison is SendGrid or Postmark — close relay/API peers — Postmark leans transactional-fast, SendGrid broader; compare on IP options and deliverability at your volume. Decide by which one fits the job above, not by the louder brand.

Frequently asked questions

Is SMTP.com secure and reliable enough to depend on?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.

Who should not use SMTP.com?

Skip it if you send low volume and a free/cheap SMTP tier covers you, or you want a full marketing-email platform with templates and automation built in. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
