On Starter, overage is cheaper than the plan

The ladder: free for 1,000 emails a month capped at 200 a day; Starter around $15 for 10,000 with overage at about $1 per thousand; Professional around $75 for 100,000 with dedicated IPs and overage at about $0.85 per thousand; custom above roughly 3 million. Annual billing is two months free (price re-verified 2026-08-17 against smtp2go.com).

The interesting part is the relationship between each plan and its own overage. Starter's 10,000 emails for $15 works out at $1.50 per thousand — but its overage is $1.00 per thousand, a third cheaper than the plan itself. Every email past your allowance is better value than the ones inside it. On Professional the relationship flips: $75 for 100,000 is $0.75 per thousand and overage is $0.85, 13% dearer. So the right strategy is opposite on the two plans — on Starter, let it run over; on Professional, size up.

That gives a crossover you can act on. Starter plus overage costs the same $75 as Professional at exactly 70,000 emails a month ($15 plus 60,000 emails at $1 per thousand). Below 70,000, staying on Starter and paying overage is cheaper than upgrading. Above it, Professional wins — and Professional is also where the dedicated IPs live, which for a sender who needs one moves the decision regardless of the arithmetic.

Annual billing takes 17% off whichever you land on, and the free tier's real constraint is the 200-a-day cap rather than the monthly 1,000 — a single send to 500 people will not go out.

What none of it buys is reputation. This is the pragmatic middle of email infrastructure: simpler and better supported than the hyperscale APIs, cheaper than most, with genuinely useful reporting. But a relay delivers what your domain deserves. Without proper SPF, DKIM and DMARC records and honest list hygiene, no provider on this site saves you, and the plan you pick will not be the reason your mail lands.

How to actually use SMTP2GO

Set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC before you send a single message through it. A relay delivers what your domain has earned, and the most common reason a switch of provider disappoints is that the authentication records came across half-configured. This is the step that determines whether anything else matters.

Then use the free tier to test shape rather than volume. A thousand emails a month sounds like room to experiment, but the real constraint is 200 a day — enough to verify that your application connects, your templates render and your bounces are being reported, and not enough to test a campaign send.

When you size a plan, do the overage arithmetic first. Starter's overage at about $1 per thousand is a third cheaper than Starter's own included rate, so running over is not a mistake to avoid — it is cheaper than the plan. Staying on Starter beats upgrading all the way up to 70,000 emails a month, at which point Professional costs the same and includes dedicated IPs. If you need a dedicated IP sooner than that, take Professional early and warm it up properly.
