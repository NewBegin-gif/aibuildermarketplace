Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 7 Sep 2026

Free: 1,000 emails a month, 200 a day, 5 days of reporting, ticket support only (toll-free phone and live chat limited to 14 days), extra emails not permitted. Starter: $15/mo or $150/year for 10,000 (overage $1.20 per thousand); $30/mo or $300/year for 50,000 ($1 per thousand). Professional: $75/$750 for 100,000 ($0.85), $170/$1,700 for 250,000 ($0.70), $280/$2,800 for 500,000 ($0.60), $520/$5,200 for 1,000,000 ($0.50), and it adds a dedicated IP address and email-testing tools.

Premier: custom, over 3,000,000 a month. Annual is exactly ten months' money at every step (their 'two months free'). All invoices are billed in US dollars; EUR, GBP, AUD and NZD are shown as approximations. 30-day money-back promise. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

The 50,000 tier beats Professional per email

Seven paid steps, and the price per email does not fall in a straight line down them (read on their pricing page on 7 September 2026, with the volume slider walked through every stop). Free is 1,000 emails a month capped at 200 a day, five days of reporting, and no overage permitted — hit the cap and sending stops. Starter is $15 a month for 10,000 or $30 for 50,000.

Professional is $75 for 100,000, $170 for 250,000, $280 for 500,000 and $520 for a million; Premier is quote-only above three million. Annual billing is exactly ten months' money at every step — $150 rather than $180 on the smallest paid plan. Per thousand emails at each plan's own ceiling that reads $1.50, $0.60, $0.75, $0.68, $0.56 and $0.52. The step you would expect to be a discount is the one that is not: moving from Starter's 50,000 to Professional's 100,000 raises the rate per email by a quarter.

What the upgrade buys is the dedicated IP address and the email-testing tools, not cheaper email. The overage line decides the rest. Each tier prices extra thousands at its own rate — $1.20, $1.00, $0.85, $0.70, $0.60, $0.50 — so on the 10,000 plan an extra thousand at $1.20 costs less than the thousands you already bought at $1.50. Run the same sum upward and the $30 plan plus overage stays cheaper than Professional until about 95,000 emails a month.

What none of the tiers buys is reputation. Without SPF, DKIM and DMARC configured properly and honest list hygiene, no relay rescues your delivery, and the plan you pick will not be the reason your mail lands.

How to actually use SMTP2GO

Set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC before you send a single message through it. A relay delivers what your domain has earned, and the most common reason a switch of provider disappoints is that the authentication records came across half-configured. This is the step that decides whether anything else matters. Then use the free tier to test shape rather than volume. A thousand emails a month sounds like room to experiment, but the real constraint is 200 a day and the free plan permits no overage at all — enough to verify that your application connects, your templates render and your bounces are reported, and nothing like enough to test a campaign send.

Size the plan from the overage rates rather than the headline. Every tier prices extra thousands at its own rate, which makes the arithmetic short: the 10,000 plan plus overage costs the same as the 50,000 plan at about 22,500 emails a month, and the 50,000 plan plus overage stays cheaper than Professional until about 95,000. Move to Professional earlier than that only if you need the dedicated IP address — and if you do, warm it up properly.
