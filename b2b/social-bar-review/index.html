Social Bar Review (2026)The Free Plan Is Not For Live Stores

This is a styling subscription, and the listing's own framing oversells it: social icons and share buttons build no trust on their own, and 'boost sales with social proof' is a claim the app has no way to support.

Best for: Shopify merchants who want branded social icons and a WhatsApp chat button on product pages without touching theme code, and who will use the built-in click counter to check whether anyone taps them.

What Social Bar does

Shopify app from Effective Apps that adds a bar of social media icons and share buttons to storefront pages, covering more than 80 platforms, with a per-platform Follow Us bar, shape and colour options, an animation, a one-click hide for shoppers and a click counter. The free plan is limited to development and partner stores, so a live store pays $4.99 a month or $41.88 a year after a 7-day trial. Built for Shopify, on the App Store since April 2020, 5.0 stars over 39 reviews.

What buyers report about Social Bar

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in September 2026. We have not run Social Bar ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • It solves the problem it names. A US store (January 2025, almost three years on the app) installed it because Shopify's default theme buried the social icons, and says they have not had to touch it since.
  • • Installation is genuinely minutes, without theme code. Reviewers from the United States, the United Kingdom, Canada, South Korea and Germany all say some version of it, and a South Korean store calls it the easiest install of any app it has used.
  • • The customisation people actually want: colour, shape, size and position, plus a bar that a visitor can click away and that can be switched off on mobile.
  • • The developer acts on requests. A US store (May 2020) says a TikTok button was added on their suggestion; a German store (June 2024) asked for the number assignment to be improved and reports it was.
What they complain about
  • • Placement is the recurring complaint. A Canadian store (July 2025) calls it a bit limited in where the icons can go and tricky to work out at first; merchants in Canada (May 2020) and the United States (December 2020) describe the same trial and error before it sat right.
  • • No hover text on desktop. The German store that praised the support asked for a mouseover label over the icons and it was not there (June 2024).
  • • Several of the warmest reviews are from an era when a live store paid nothing. Two May 2020 reviewers call the app free; today the free plan is limited to development and partner stores and a live store pays $4.99 a month.
  • • Nobody reports a result. The app logs visitor clicks, but not one review cites a number from that counter, or a follower gained, or a sale. The reviews are overwhelmingly about the first quarter of an hour: 9, 11, 13 and 14 minutes of use are all in there.

Where the ratings disagree. 5.0 across 39 reviews, 38 of them five-star and one four-star, with about ten ratings carrying no text. There is no negative body of evidence to weigh against the praise here — but there is also almost no evidence about the thing you would be paying for. The one four-star review, the Canadian store on placement, is the most informative review on the page.

The question is not which plan, it is whether icons are worth a subscription

There is effectively one plan for anyone with a real store, so the upgrade question is simple and the value question is the whole review. What $4.99 a month buys is a bar of social icons covering more than 80 platforms, a Follow Us bar per platform, round or square shapes, colours you can match to your brand, an animation, the option to hide the bar on mobile, a one-click hide for shoppers who do not want it, and a count of how many visitors clicked.

The click counter is the part worth having, because it is the only feature here that answers a question rather than decorating a page: it tells you whether anyone actually uses the icons. The honest way to think about the price is as a recurring charge for something a theme edit can often do once. Most Shopify themes already place social links in the footer. What you are paying for is placement on product pages, the animation, and not having to touch theme code.

That is a real convenience and a modest price, but it is a styling subscription, and at $41.88 a year it should clear the bar of actually being clicked. The counter will tell you within a month. The permissions are narrow, which counts in its favour: the listing asks to view the store owner's staff record and to view and edit the online store, and nothing about customers or orders.

One caution about the rating. The 5.0 average rests on 39 reviews, and the usage times attached to them are unusually short. Of the three shown on the listing, two were written by merchants who had the app installed for 11 and 13 minutes, and both praise setup speed and support rather than results. The one review from someone who used it for 11 days is the most useful of the three, and it is the mildly critical one: placement options are limited and the app is tricky to work out at first, though it came good after some experimenting. Read the 5.0 as a verdict on installation, not on outcome.

How to actually use Social Bar

Setup runs through the theme, not the app. In the Shopify theme editor open App Embeds, find the Social Bar app embed and toggle it on, then save at the top right. Then open the app dashboard and check that the status reads Active; if it does not, use the Activate Social Bar button at the top right and save. If the bar still does not show up, clear your browser cache before you contact anyone — the vendor's troubleshooting article puts that third for a reason.

Connect only the accounts you actually maintain. Eighty platforms is an invitation to add icons to profiles you posted on twice in 2024, and a dead link in a Follow Us bar costs more trust than a missing one. Switch the click counter on from day one and give it a month. This is the rare styling app that reports whether it is being used, and the number it produces is what tells you whether $4.99 a month is buying anything.

Also decide early whether to hide the bar on mobile: it competes for screen space with the sticky elements most stores already run there. Support is chat from the app dashboard, or support@effectify.io. The vendor does not offer support in Dutch, per the listing.

Frequently asked questions

What does $4.99 a month actually buy?

One plan, because the other one is not for you. Read on apps.shopify.com/sticky-social-media-icons-bar on 19 September 2026: the Development Stores plan is free with unlimited access to every feature, and its own feature line says it is for development and partner stores only, with payment activated on upgrade. A live store is on the Monthly Plan at $4.99 a month, or $41.88 a year, with a 7-day free trial. What the paid card names: 80-plus social icons, a Follow Us bar for any platform, a dance animation, round or square icon shapes, a bar that visitors can click away, the option to hide it on mobile, a log of visitor clicks, and online support. That is the whole product. Sixty dollars a year monthly, or forty-two on annual, for icons that your theme places and a counter that says how often they were clicked.

Reviewers keep calling it free. Did the price change?

Some of the warmest reviews describe an app that cost a live store nothing. A Canadian store in May 2020: easy to install, perfect for my website, and it is FREE. A US store in the same month calls them incredible features for a free app. Today's listing reserves the free plan for development and partner stores. We have not found a vendor statement saying when that changed or whether early installs were grandfathered, so the safest reading is that the old reviews describe a different deal than the one you would sign. It also explains part of the five-star wall: a fair number of those stars were given for something that was free at the time.

Will I be able to tell whether it works?

Barely. The only measurement the app offers is its own click counter, named on the pricing card as a log of the number of visitor clicks, and not one reviewer on the listing cites a number from it — not a follower count, not a click rate, not a sale. The praise is about installation: reviews written after 9, 11, 13 and 14 minutes of use, saying it went in quickly and looks good. The two reviewers who had the app for years say the same thing they said on day one, that it looks right and they never had to touch it. So judge this the way you would judge a font licence rather than a marketing tool: you are buying the look of the thing, and the counter will tell you it was clicked, not what the click was worth.
