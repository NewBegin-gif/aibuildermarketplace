Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 7 Sep 2026

A free plan with unlimited apps, up to 3 builders, 5 app users, 5,000 database records, 500 workflow actions and 5 AI credits a month. Paid on yearly billing: Basic $19/month, Pro $99/month and Business $329/month, with Enterprise on custom limits; the pricing page opens on the Yearly tab and advertises two months off, so month-to-month costs more. App-user allowances are published per tier: Team 5 + Client 5 on Basic, Team 10 + Client 50 on Pro, Team 30 + Client 100 on Business, where Team users share a business email domain with one of your builders and everyone else counts as a Client user.

Extra app users beyond the allowance start at $1/month on Pro and $2/month on Business. Extra builder seats are $5/month on Basic, $10 on Pro and $20 on Business, and builders can be added as app users free. Records run 50K / 500K / 1M and workflow actions 2.5K / 10K / 25K a month. Non-profits and education get a stated 50% discount.

Refunds: in full within 14 days of a first monthly payment, 30 days of a first yearly one. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

The dearer plan charges double per extra user

The free plan is unusually usable: unlimited apps, up to 3 builders, 5 app users, 5,000 database records, 500 workflow actions and 5 AI credits a month. Paid tiers on yearly billing are Basic $19 a month, Pro $99 and Business $329, with Enterprise on custom limits. Extra builder seats are priced per tier at $5, $10 and $20 a month. Note what the page does with billing periods: it opens on the Yearly tab - the markup carries that selection, not just the styling - and advertises two months off, so every figure you see is already the discounted one and paying monthly costs about 20% more.

The number that decides your bill is the app-user allowance, and it is published on the comparison table rather than being something you have to ask for. Softr counts two populations. Builders make the app; app users log into it. App users then split again: Team users share a business email domain with one of your builders, and everyone else - including every Gmail address - is a Client user.

The allowances run Team 5 plus Client 5 on Basic, Team 10 plus Client 50 on Pro, and Team 30 plus Client 100 on Business. Builders can be added as app users at no charge. Beyond the allowance, extra app users start at $1 a month on Pro and $2 a month on Business. That is worth reading twice: the plan costing three and a half times more charges double per additional user.

If you expect to run well past your allowance, the cheaper plan is also the cheaper meter, and the reason to buy Business becomes its other limits - 1M records against Pro's 500K, 25K workflow actions against 10K - rather than its user economics. The ladder itself is steep: $19 to $99 is more than five times, $99 to $329 more than three. Builder seats quadruple across it too, though as a share of the plan they get cheaper, from 26% of Basic to 6% of Business.

That distinction settles what this tool is for. With a known, counted audience - an internal tool, a client portal, a members' area - the meter is predictable. With an open signup it is your growth curve. Start on the free plan; at 5,000 records and three builders it is enough to build something real. And if you are a non-profit or in education, claim the stated 50% discount, which takes Business from $329 to $164.50.

How to actually use Softr

Count your two user populations before you build, because Softr meters them separately and the split is not obvious. Builders make the app; app users log into it. App users then divide into Team users, who share a business email domain with one of your builders, and Client users, which is everyone else - every Gmail address included. Check the overage rate against the plan before you climb. Additional app users start at $1 a month on Pro but $2 on Business, so if you expect to run past your allowance, moving up costs you twice as much per user.

Buy Business for its record and workflow-action limits, not for its user economics. Then build the real thing on the free plan. Unlimited apps, three builders and 5,000 records is enough for a working internal tool or client portal, and the five-app-user cap is the only thing that stops you. Two last things. The page opens on the Yearly tab, so $19, $99 and $329 already assume annual billing - monthly is about 20% more.

And non-profits and education should claim the stated 50% discount rather than assuming it is applied automatically.

Frequently asked questions

What does Softr cost?

Free for a small app (3 builders, 5 app users, 5,000 records), then $19, $99 and $329 a month on yearly billing for Basic, Pro and Business. Monthly billing costs more - the page advertises two months off for paying yearly. Read on 7 September 2026.

What counts as a billable user?

Two different things, which is the most common budgeting mistake here. Builders log into Softr Studio to build, and cost $5, $10 or $20 a month each beyond the one, two or three included. App users are the end users who log into what you built, and they split again: Team users share a business email domain with one of your builders, everyone else (Gmail included) is a Client user. The allowances are Team 5 + Client 5 on Basic, Team 10 + Client 50 on Pro and Team 30 + Client 100 on Business. Builders can be added as app users free.

Which plan is cheapest if my app grows?

Not necessarily the bigger one. Extra app users beyond the allowance start at $1 a month on Pro but $2 a month on Business, so the dearer plan meters growth at double the rate. Buy Business for its record limit (1M against Pro's 500K) and workflow actions (25K against 10K) rather than for its user economics. Non-profits and education can claim a stated 50% discount, which takes Business from $329 to $164.50.

What should I weigh before buying IT & Productivity software?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.
