What Spocket does

Dropshipping platform with vetted US and EU suppliers. Find fast-shipping products, add them to your store in a click and automate order fulfillment.

Pricing (2026)

(2026) Four plans about $39.99–$299.99/mo: Starter $39.99 (25 products), Pro $59.99 ($24 annual, 250 products), Empire $99.99 (10,000), Unicorn $299.99 (25,000); 7-day trial, no per-order platform fee on top of the subscription. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Spocket's pitch is faster-shipping US and EU suppliers, and that's real — but you still pay twice: the monthly subscription and the wholesale product cost, so margins depend on picking items with room to mark up. Catalog breadth is narrower than giant AliExpress-based tools (that's the trade for vetting and shipping speed), and the low Starter product cap pushes serious stores up to Pro quickly. It reduces, not removes, the usual dropshipping risks.

What buyers report about Spocket

Recurring themes from Capterra, G2 and dropshipping practitioner reviews, read in August 2026. We have not run Spocket ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Suppliers are vetted and mostly US and EU based, so shipping runs 2-5 days rather than weeks — that is the whole reason to pay for it over the free directories.
  • • Branded invoices are included, which matters if you are building a store rather than flipping products.
What they complain about
  • • Billing is the serious complaint. Reviewers report being upgraded to the $300+/month Unicorn plan without consent, including one charged $314.99 after uninstalling the app. Watch your statement from day one.
  • • The 14-day trial converts without a warning email, and reviewers report charges continuing after cancellation.
  • • 2.6 out of 5 on Capterra, dragged down by support: robotic automated replies and slow responses when a billing error needs fixing.
  • • Starter at $39.99 gives you 25 unique products, and practitioners argue much of the catalogue is not viable to dropship profitably anyway. Test margins on real products before committing.
Best for: dropshippers who want vetted US and EU suppliers with faster shipping and one-click store integration, prioritizing delivery speed over raw catalog size.
Who should skip it: if you want the widest possible catalog at the lowest sourcing cost (AliExpress-based tools fit), or your margins can't absorb both subscription and wholesale cost.

The natural comparison is DSers or Zendrop — AliExpress-oriented alternatives with bigger catalogs but often slower shipping — Spocket trades breadth for US/EU speed. Decide by which one fits the job above, not by the louder brand.

Frequently asked questions

Is Spocket worth it for a small team?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Small teams get the most out of this category when one clear problem is already costing real hours or revenue; buying ahead of that just adds cost and another login. Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.

Who should not use Spocket?

Skip it if you want the widest possible catalog at the lowest sourcing cost (AliExpress-based tools fit), or your margins can't absorb both subscription and wholesale cost. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
