What Spocket does

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Catalog breadth is narrower than giant AliExpress-based tools (that's the trade for vetting and shipping speed), and the low Starter product cap pushes serious stores up to Pro quickly. It reduces, not removes, the usual dropshipping risks.

What buyers report about Spocket

What they praise
  • • Suppliers are vetted and mostly US and EU based, so shipping runs 2-5 days rather than weeks — that is the whole reason to pay for it over the free directories.

  • • The 14-day trial converts without a warning email, and reviewers report charges continuing after cancellation.

  • • Starter at $39.99 gives you 25 unique products, and practitioners argue much of the catalogue is not viable to dropship profitably anyway. Test margins on real products before committing.

Eight months free is true of none of the plans

Four monthly plans, metered on unique products: Starter $39.99 for 25, Pro $59.99 for 250, Empire $99.99 for 10,000 and Unicorn $299.99 for 25,000. There is a 7-day trial and 0% transaction fee on every tier — no per-order platform fee on top of the subscription. Annual billing is advertised as eight months free, and the page quantifies the savings as $432, $516 and $2,563 on the three upper plans.

Run those against the monthly rates and the headline does not survive. Pro at $59.99 for twelve months is $719.88; take $432 off and you are at $287.88, which is 60% off, or 7.2 months free — and the page's own '$24 a month' figure agrees with that. Empire at $1,199.88 less $516 is a 43% discount, or 5.2 months free. Unicorn at $3,599.88 less $2,563 is 71%, or 8.5 months.

Three plans, three quite different offers, one headline that fits none of them and is furthest out on Empire. Whatever plan you pick, read the annual total at checkout rather than the months-free claim. The product ladder has an inversion in it too. Per product slot, Starter is $1.60 a month, Pro $0.24, Empire $0.010 and Unicorn $0.012 — so Unicorn is 20% worse per product than Empire, offering 2.5 times the catalogue for three times the price.

Nothing in the public information explains what else Unicorn buys, so if you are being pointed at it, ask what the extra $200 a month is for. At the other end, Starter's 25-product cap is the one to take seriously. Twenty-five products is not a store, and at $1.60 a slot it is 6.7 times Pro's rate. The real entry plan for anyone trading seriously is Pro. The thing the subscription does not cover is the margin.

Spocket's pitch is vetted US and EU suppliers with faster shipping, and that is genuinely the trade — you get better delivery times and a narrower catalogue than the giant AliExpress-based tools. But you pay twice regardless: the subscription and the wholesale cost of every item. So the arithmetic that decides this is not the tier, it is whether the products you can find here have enough room between wholesale and retail to carry a $60-a-month fixed cost on top.

Spend the 7-day trial finding ten items you would actually list and working out that margin, not browsing the catalogue.

Frequently asked questions

What does Spocket cost?

$39.99 a month for Starter, $59.99 for Pro, $99.99 for Empire and $299.99 for Unicorn. Annual billing is presented as eight months free — a saving of $432 on Pro, bringing it to about $24 a month. Read on 17 August 2026.

What separates the tiers?

Catalogue size, almost entirely: 25 unique products on Starter, 250 on Pro, 10,000 on Empire and 25,000 on Unicorn. Transaction fees are 0% on all of them, so you are buying how many products you may list rather than better terms on what you sell.

Is the annual discount as good as it sounds?

It is unusually steep — eight months free is a two-thirds discount, far beyond the 20-25% typical in this category. The trade is the commitment: dropshipping catalogues change and suppliers churn, so weigh a year up front against a seven-day trial that is short for proving a product line.

What should I weigh before buying a tool like this?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Small teams get the most out of this category when one clear problem is already costing real hours or revenue; buying ahead of that just adds cost and another login. Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.
