What Stamped does

Reviews, ratings and loyalty platform for e-commerce: collect product reviews and user-generated content, run rewards and loyalty programs, and show social proof to lift conversions on Shopify and other stores.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The $798/month figure that circulates for the full suite is the 5,000-order step, not an entry price — at 200 orders the same three-product bundle came to about $18/month when we read it. Read the order band, not the headline. What does bite is the shape: each product is its own subscription and the price steps hard with volume, so a good month raises the bill in several places at once, and a second storefront is another $100/month. Start with one product and price the step above the volume you actually expect.

When the paid plan is worth it

Stamped bills on monthly order volume rather than on a flat plan fee, and the single most useful thing we found is that the bundle undercuts the single product. Reading their pricing page on 11 August 2026 with the bundle toggle on and all three products ticked, the three-product bundle came to about $18 a month at the 200-order step, while Reviews on its own was about $23 at the same step. At 1,000 orders it is $79 bundled against $99 for Reviews alone. In other words, at the volumes most stores actually run, buying all three products costs less than buying one — so if you were planning to start with Reviews and add the others later, check the bundle price before you do, because later may cost more than now.

The second finding is where the ladder turns against you. Per order, the bundle works out at about $0.090 at 200 orders, $0.079 at 1,000 and $0.160 at 5,000 — so the rate improves once and then roughly doubles. Look at the marginal cost and it is starker still: going from 200 to 1,000 orders costs about $0.076 for each extra order, while going from 1,000 to 5,000 costs about $0.180 — more than twice as much per order to grow. The $798 figure that circulates for the suite is that 5,000-order step, not an entry price. Read the order band, not the headline.

What that shape means in practice: a good month raises the bill in several places at once, because each product steps with the same volume. Price the tier ABOVE the volume you actually expect rather than the one you are in, and know that seasonality costs you at both ends. Two structural details to add to the sum: plans cover up to two domains, and each additional store is $100 a month after a first free month, so a second storefront is a fixed addition on top of the volume steps. There is no free plan. Overage charges have been dropped, which removes one nasty surprise.

How to actually use Stamped

Install the app from the Shopify app store, then import first: existing reviews go in as a CSV through Settings, Importer. Then set store name, store email, the From address reviews are sent from, and the deliverability settings beside them. Display is a separate job. On Shopify 2.0 the main widget and star-rating badge are added from the theme customiser as a section and a block; on vintage themes you paste Stamped's liquid snippets into product.liquid or main-product.liquid, and into product-card.liquid for collection pages.

The tracking script goes into theme.liquid before the closing body tag, with your own public API key and store hash.

Distilled from Stamped's own help centre, chiefly 'Shopify: Getting Started' and 'Shopify: Installing the app, main widget and script' at stampedsupport.stamped.io, read 20 August 2026.

Common Stamped setup mistakes

Where Stamped setups actually go wrong, and what it costs you when they do. Same sources as the setup notes above — not from a walkthrough we ran ourselves.

The step teams underestimate is the theme code.

  • Review requests only generate once the Enable Review Requests toggle is saved; installing the app starts nothing.
  • Stamped documents that any change or update of your theme may remove its widget codes, so a redesign can silently end review display.
  • On non-product pages the main widget loses its Write a Review functionality.
  • Support for this installation is limited and, in Stamped's wording, offered to paid users only.

Frequently asked questions

Is Stamped really around $800 a month?

Only at volume, and the number gets quoted badly — including by us until we re-read it on 11 August 2026. $798/month is the figure at the 5,000-order step. With the bundle toggle on and all three products ticked, the same suite came to about $18/month at 200 orders and $79/month at 1,000. The bill tracks your monthly order volume, so the honest question is not what Stamped costs but what it costs at your order band.

Is there still a free plan?

No. The free tier is gone and each product — Reviews, Loyalty and Lifecycle — is its own subscription, so an early-stage store is choosing which one to start with rather than growing into the suite. Standalone, Reviews was around $23/month at 200 orders and $99 at 1,000 when we read it. Overage charges have been dropped.

What does a second storefront cost?

$100 a month per additional domain. Their pricing page states that plans include up to two domains and that each further store gets a first month free before that charge starts — wording that has been on the page unchanged in every capture we hold since 7 July 2026. Worth pricing in early if you run regional storefronts, because it lands on top of the per-product subscriptions rather than inside them.

Is Stamped worth it for a small team?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Small teams get the most out of this category when one clear problem is already costing real hours or revenue; buying ahead of that just adds cost and another login. Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.

Who should not use Stamped?

Skip it if you're an early-stage store — the removed free plan and $199+/product pricing are steep before you have the volume to justify them. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
