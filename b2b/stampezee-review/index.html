What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The deeper truth of loyalty programs: the mechanics are 10% of the outcome; a reward worth returning for is the other 90%.

What Stampezee does

Digital stamp cards for local businesses, replacing paper punch cards. Stamp Start is free for one branch, one card and up to 100 stamps; Stamp Lite is $29.99/month for two branches and three cards. Public pricing stays thin, so re-check the tiers before committing.

What buyers report about Stampezee

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Stampezee ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • A customer joins by scanning a QR code and starts earning immediately — reviewers name that frictionlessness, since no app install is the barrier that kills most loyalty schemes.
  • • Free-forever plan with paid upgrades, plus automation for lapsed customers, birthdays and referrals, and gamification through levels and badges.
What they complain about
  • • The independent review base is small and the available quotes read as testimonials. There is no complaint pattern to report yet — and no track record to lean on either.
  • • Loyalty programmes live or die on staff remembering to prompt the scan. That is your operational problem, not the software's, and no tool solves it.

When the paid plan is worth it

The paid tiers mostly buy scale, not capability. The comparison table has four columns, and what changes across them is branches (1, 2, 3, 10), active stamp cards (1, 3, 6, 12) and push notifications (0, 1, 4, 8). The whole ladder is now published: Stamp Start free, Stamp Lite $29.99 a month, Stamp Pro $49.99 and Stamp Elite $99.99, with 17% off on the annual term. Lite pays back in two cases: you run more than one location, or you want to send a re-engagement push at all, since the entry column allows zero.

It does not pay back if you want a better-looking card: custom stamp card designs appear only in the fourth column, and even there the allowance is 1. What upgrading does not solve is the ambiguity at the bottom. The plan card calls Stamp Start a 14-day free trial; the comparison table on the same page prices it as Free with no time limit. Get that answered in writing before you build a programme on the free column, because the two readings differ by everything.

How to actually use Stampezee

First value is a completed stamp card, not a created one. The vendor's route is short: build your first stamp card, assign staff, start rewarding customers, which the how-it-works page frames as launching "in minutes".

Distilled from vendor pages only — stampezee.com/en/pricing, /en/how-it-works, /en/for-customers, /en/faq and /en/help-center, read 30 August 2026; the help centre is the source for the setup steps and the pricing page for the tier limits. We looked for an independent walkthrough and found none, so nothing third-party is used here.

Common Stampezee setup mistakes

Where Stampezee setups actually go wrong, and what it costs you when they do. Same sources as the setup notes above — not from a walkthrough we ran ourselves.

The friction sits on the other side of the counter.

  • StampEzee's own customer page describes a four-step flow that starts with the customer downloading the app and creating an account; wallet passes come after joining, not instead of it.
  • So your first weeks are an install-rate problem, and staff have to ask at checkout every time.
  • Second snag: scanning is a separate staff role, the Loyalty Executive, with its own app, so nothing moves until someone is assigned.
  • Third, the documentation has caught up.
  • On 24 July 2026 the help centre returned no articles at all; by 30 August it runs separate tracks for retailers, customers and loyalty executives, with step-by-step pieces on creating the account, publishing a first card and adding staff.

The natural comparison is Stamp Me or Loopy Loyalty — the digital-stamp peer set — compare per-location pricing and whether Apple/Google Wallet passes are included.
