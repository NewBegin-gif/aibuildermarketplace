What they praise
  • • A customer joins by scanning a QR code and starts earning immediately — reviewers name that frictionlessness, since no app install is the barrier that kills most loyalty schemes.
  • • Free-forever plan with paid upgrades, plus automation for lapsed customers, birthdays and referrals, and gamification through levels and badges.
What they complain about
  • • The independent review base is small and the available quotes read as testimonials. There is no complaint pattern to report yet — and no track record to lean on either.
  • • Loyalty programmes live or die on staff remembering to prompt the scan. That is your operational problem, not the software's, and no tool solves it.

When the paid plan is worth it

The paid tiers mostly buy scale, not capability. The comparison table has four columns, and what changes across them is branches (1, 2, 3, 10), active stamp cards (1, 3, 6, 12) and push notifications (0, 1, 4, 8). The whole ladder is now published: Stamp Start free, Stamp Lite $29.99 a month, Stamp Pro $49.99 and Stamp Elite $99.99, with 17% off on the annual term. Lite pays back in two cases: you run more than one location, or you want to send a re-engagement push at all, since the entry column allows zero.

It does not pay back if you want a better-looking card: custom stamp card designs appear only in the fourth column, and even there the allowance is 1. What upgrading does not solve is the ambiguity at the bottom. The plan card calls Stamp Start a 14-day free trial; the comparison table on the same page prices it as Free with no time limit. Get that answered in writing before you build a programme on the free column, because the two readings differ by everything.

How to actually use Stampezee

Frequently asked questions

What does the annual term actually cost?

The visible page advertises the annual saving without printing a single annual amount, but the plan data behind that same page does, and the arithmetic is clean: Stamp Lite $299.99 a year, Stamp Pro $499.99, Stamp Elite $999.99. That is exactly ten months' money for twelve months of service on all three — 16.7% off, which is the 17% the page advertises. So the annual term is a straightforward two-months-free deal rather than a negotiated rate, and you can price it yourself by multiplying the monthly figure by ten.

Is the free tier free forever, or free for 14 days?

Still unanswered on 19 September 2026, and the contradiction has moved inside a single plan. Stamp Start's own feature list on the pricing page says both “Free — no card details needed!” and, two lines later, “Free — 14 days — No Card Required!”. The plan carries no price entry of any kind in the page's data and is flagged as free, which reads like a permanent tier, while the 14-day line reads like a trial. Its ceiling is firm either way: one branch, one stamp card, and 100 customers or 100 stamps, whichever comes first. Get the answer in writing before you build a programme on it.

Why does the price change when I open the page from another country?

Because StampEzee prices by region rather than converting one list price, across roughly 140 currencies. Stamp Lite is $29.99 a month in USD, €19.99 in EUR and £14.99 in GBP; Stamp Pro is $49.99, €39.99 and £29.99; Stamp Elite is $99.99, €69.99 and £59.99. Those are not the same amount in different clothes — the sterling entry price is roughly half the dollar one at any plausible rate. Which list you are shown follows the region the page decides you are in, so check the currency and the amount on the payment screen against the one that persuaded you.

Is a growth tool worth it for a small team?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Small teams get the most out of this category when one clear problem is already costing real hours or revenue; buying ahead of that just adds cost and another login. Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.
