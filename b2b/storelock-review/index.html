What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The copy protection blocks right-click, text selection, keyboard shortcuts and image dragging, and the vendor's own FAQ says what it cannot stop: screenshots, browser developer tools, visitors with JavaScript switched off and automated scrapers, which is how stores actually get cloned. It is a deterrent against casual copying. The part that does real work is detection and takedown: clone detection, a nightly scan of hundreds of lookalike variants of your domain that a person at StoreLock reviews before you get an alert, reverse image searches, and DMCA and abuse notices pre-filled with the evidence.

Even then, the FAQ says StoreLock cannot make a registrar respond, and a typical notice takes days to weeks. The gap between the plans is large. On Premium at $19 you file the notices yourself; having StoreLock's team watch the alerts and file for you is the Enterprise plan at $399 a month, about twenty times the price. Most of the reviews thank 'Adam and his team' by name for chasing fakes down, so check before you install which plan the help you are reading about came with.

Today image monitoring is $0.20 per monitored image on the Shopify listing: ten images scanned weekly is about 40 scans, or $8 a month. The FAQ we read on 27 October 2026 already quotes the StoreLock 2 terms instead (100 scans included on Premium, then $0.05 each), so check in the app which rate your plan is billed at. The one critical review is a warning about putting a script in front of your storefront.

On 28 April 2026 a Canadian store that had used the app for about a year wrote that after an update everyone was blocked from accessing their store, and they removed it rather than wait a day for support. StoreLock replied that it had released an update to its content protection, had no other such reports, had received no support request and had contacted the store within 30 minutes of the uninstall.

The other 9 of 10 reviews are five stars, two of them written within 25 minutes of installing. Also note that after a theme update you must switch the app embed back on, or the protection is silently off. We have not installed the app. What changed since our first reading. On 1 August 2026 Premium stopped including personal case handling: StoreLock's notice says it no longer handles case escalations and follow-ups on Premium, which stays a self-serve toolkit, while it still detects impersonation sites and generates and submits the initial abuse report when a case starts; cases opened before that date are finished at no charge.

Enterprise now promises daily verification, filing within 24 hours of verification, unlimited takedowns and a weekly case summary. The FAQ says the new version no longer identifies or blocks individual visitors by IP address (country blocking stays), and since September 2026 lookalike-domain scans run only for the store's primary domain, so extra authorised domains are no longer scanned. On the plus side, detections can be exported to CSV with their takedown status, and a detection can be reported to Google Safe Browsing, Microsoft, Netcraft and OpenPhish from its details page.

What StoreLock does

Shopify app (by Williams Web Services) that finds cloned storefronts, lookalike domains and stolen product photos, packages the evidence and sends DMCA and abuse reports from the app, with on-site copy protection and country blocking.

Premium is enough unless you want it done for you

Premium at $19 a month includes every detection and reporting feature: clone detection, nightly lookalike-domain scans, alerts, copy protection, country blocking and the DMCA tools. The only metered part is Image Monitoring at $0.20 per image scan. Choose a handful of your most-copied photos, scan them weekly or monthly rather than daily, and check the spending limit in the app: the 25 free scans and default $20 monthly cap that the FAQ described on 1 October 2026 were no longer in it on 27 October.

Enterprise at $399 is a different product: StoreLock's team watches the alerts and files the abuse reports, follows up and escalates, with unlimited image monitoring. That is worth it only for a brand that is cloned repeatedly and whose own time on takedowns costs more than $380 a month. Start on Premium; you will know after one incident whether you want to do the next one yourself. If StoreLock 2 launches as previewed, the sums change.

The $19 Premium would include 100 image scans and 5 automated reports a month; a store that is hit often would look at Plus at $149 for up to 5 domains or brand terms and subdomain monitoring; and the managed service would cost $499 instead of $399. Current subscribers keep their plan for 6 months after the launch and then choose a plan or move to Free, so today's $399 Enterprise price is guaranteed only until six months after the new plans arrive.

How to actually use StoreLock

Install the app, then click 'Enable StoreLock' on the settings page and save the app embed in the theme customiser. Check that it works in a private browser window while logged out of Shopify, because the app does not block anything while you are logged in and viewing your own store. Add any extra domains your store uses under the authorised domains, along with speed or accessibility testing tools you want to let through.

Add the team members who should receive alert emails, and allow mail from hello@ and legal@storelock.app, which the FAQ says strict filters sometimes block. If you sell in only a few countries, think carefully before blocking whole regions: a mistake there locks out real customers, and a store that locked out every visitor after an update is exactly what the one critical review describes. For Image Monitoring, pick your most valuable images in the gallery and start with a one-time scan before you schedule daily, weekly or monthly jobs; the FAQ says each image checked counts as one scan.

Mark authorised retailers and review sites as ignored so they stop showing up. After every theme change, open StoreLock and switch the app embed back on. To cancel, uninstall the app from your Shopify admin; protection stops immediately and your data is deleted within 30 days. Before StoreLock sends takedown notices in your name, the app asks you to review and accept a Brand Protection Authorization (added in June 2026), so it has your consent on file.

Lookalike domains appear on the settings page under Domain Squatter Detection: approve one to move it to your detections and start a takedown, or reject it to keep it out of future scans, which is the right choice for domains you own. Abuse emails go to every alert recipient in your settings. Marketplace listings and social media profiles are outside the automatic filing: StoreLock captures the evidence, but you report those on the platforms' own forms.

Frequently asked questions

What does StoreLock cost?

Today, Premium is $19 a month with a 3-day free trial plus image monitoring at $0.20 per monitored image (Shopify listing, read 27 October 2026), and Enterprise is $399 a month, fully managed. StoreLock's pricing page previews StoreLock 2 plans without a date: Free $0, Premium $19, Plus $149 and Enterprise $499; current subscribers keep their price for 6 months after that launch.

Can StoreLock stop people copying my Shopify store?

Only casual copying. It blocks right-click, text selection and shortcuts, but the vendor says screenshots, developer tools, scrapers and visitors without JavaScript get through. Its main use is detecting clones and lookalike domains and helping you get them taken down.

Does StoreLock take down fake stores for me?

Partly on Premium, fully on Enterprise. Since 1 August 2026 Premium still generates and submits the initial abuse report when a case starts, but you drive follow-up and escalation yourself. On Enterprise, at $399 a month, the team verifies matches, files within 24 hours of verification and escalates until the site is down. StoreLock cannot make a registrar act.

Can StoreLock still block individual IP addresses?

No. The FAQ (read 27 October 2026) says the new version of StoreLock no longer identifies or blocks individual visitors by IP address, because IP addresses change; blocking whole countries remains on every plan. StoreLock offers to help stores that relied on IP blocking move it to another tool.

What data does StoreLock share with third parties?

Only the images you choose to monitor go to its image-monitoring partner, Google Cloud, for reverse image search; abuse data goes to hosts, registrars and partners such as Google, Microsoft and Netcraft. The FAQ says StoreLock does not access customer details, payment information or passwords, never changes your products or settings, and deletes your data within 30 days of uninstalling.

Which of my domains does StoreLock monitor?

Your store's primary domain. Since September 2026 squatter scans ignore extra authorised domains, and the FAQ says your myshopify.com address is not scanned. The StoreLock 2 preview raises this to up to 5 domains or brand terms on Plus and up to 10 on Enterprise.
