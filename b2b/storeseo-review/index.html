The catch

This one fixes the mechanical layer well: meta titles and descriptions in bulk, alt text, compressed images, JSON-LD, sitemaps, indexing requests, and none of that creates demand for products nobody is searching for. It is maintenance, not marketing, and on a catalogue of 25 products the free tier already does most of it. Two specifics worth a raised eyebrow. The pricing gap between Essential at $39.99 for 250 products and Advanced at $249.99 for 10,000 is filled by a $99.99 Growth plan.

That plan lives on the vendor's own site but not on the App Store listing our link points to, which is a strange place to hide a tier. And the AEO/GEO features: the LLMs.txt generator, the Agents.md editor, the separate “agentic” ranking: are a bet, not a channel. No major search or AI vendor has committed to reading llms.txt, so treat those numbers as a hypothesis you are testing rather than traffic you are buying.

What buyers report about StoreSEO

Hands-on: not tested by usReported by buyers: the Shopify App Store listing, positive and low-rated reviews read sep, read August 2026

Themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run StoreSEO ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Support is the most-named strength, with merchants crediting individual agents by name for walking them through the whole setup.
  • • Bulk work is what merchants say they bought it for: product titles, descriptions and image alt text handled across the catalogue at once.
  • • The app shows exactly what it intends to change before anything is applied, which reviewers call out as the reason they trusted it on a live store.
  • • Scoring and recommendations put the mechanical SEO problems on one screen, including meta title length and missing tags, which merchants without a technical background say they could act on.
What they complain about
  • • The sharpest complaint is that paid AI credits were spent on meta descriptions that ran past the length limit and pushed the store's own SEO score down.
  • • One merchant reports a $68 charge they did not expect and did not feel they had agreed to, and describes the recurring monthly billing as unclear.
  • • Merchants raising credit-consumption problems say support did not respond to them, which is the opposite of the setup experience the positive reviews describe.
  • • Despite the automation promise, some reviewers say they still ended up optimising products one at a time.

Where the ratings disagree. Both sides of this come from the same platform, and that is the point: the listing surfaces the praise first, and the complaints only appear once you filter the reviews down to the low ratings. The two groups are also describing different phases: the praise is about setup and support, the complaints are about what the paid AI credits produced afterwards. Worth filtering yourself before you buy credits rather than after.

When the paid plan is worth it

The ladder is a product counter, and that is the whole decision. Free stops at 25 products, Lite at 100, Essential at 250 and Advanced at 10,000. Almost every feature that matters arrives at Lite: schema markup, instant indexing, Search Console and Analytics, automatic sitemap submission, and the only capability added higher up is multilingual SEO on Advanced. Everything else you are buying is allowance. Which makes the gap in the middle the thing to notice.

On the App Store listing the step from Essential to Advanced is 250 products to 10,000 products and $39.99 to $249.99: six times the money. On StoreSEO's own site there is a Growth plan at $99.99 covering 1,000 products that fills exactly that gap, and it is not on the listing we link to. If your catalogue is between 250 and 1,000 products, that missing tier is the difference between paying $99.99 and paying $249.99, so ask for it by name.

The AI credits are the second meter and they behave differently: they top up separately at $10 for 5,000 or $50 for 25,000, so running out of credits does not force a plan upgrade. Price the top-up against the next tier before you climb. At the bottom, the free tier is unusually complete for 25 products (real SEO scoring, alt text, the migrator) so a small catalogue can stay there.

The 7-day trial on paid plans is the right length to bulk-optimise once and see whether anything moves.

How to actually use StoreSEO

Run the free tier on your 25 best-selling products first and read the SEO score before you change anything, if it is already clean, the paid tiers are buying you scale you do not need. When you do upgrade, do the bulk pass once and then leave it alone: the value here is the first sweep across meta titles, alt text and image weight, and re-running it weekly changes nothing except your credit balance.

Connect Search Console on day one, because the only honest measure of whether any of this worked is impressions on the pages you touched, not the app's own score. Watch the two meters separately (products decide your plan, AI credits top up on their own at $10 for 5,000) so do not upgrade a tier because you ran out of credits. And if your catalogue sits between 250 and 1,000 products, ask them about the $99.99 Growth plan on their site before you accept the $249.99 tier the listing offers.

Frequently asked questions

What does StoreSEO cost?

Re-read on apps.shopify.com/storeseo on 12 November 2026. Lite went up from $14.99 to $19.99 a month after our September readings; every other amount and allowance is unchanged since 19 August. Free Forever covers 25 products, 200 AI credits, 25 image optimisations and up to 5 tracked keywords. Lite is $19.99 a month for 100 products, 500 images and 1,000 credits, and is where schema markup, instant indexing, Google Analytics and Search Console and automatic sitemap submission arrive. Essential is $39.99 for 250 products, 2,000 images and 5,000 credits. Advanced is $249.99 for 10,000 products, 50,000 images and 50,000 credits, and adds multilingual SEO. The paid tiers carry a 7-day trial and bill in USD every 30 days. Until 1 December 2026 the first month of any paid plan is 80% off ($3.99 for Lite).

Why is there such a jump from $39.99 to $249.99?

Because the tier that fills the gap is not on the Shopify listing. storeseo.com sells a Growth plan at $99.99 a month covering 1,000 products, 5,000 images, 12,500 AI credits and 200 keywords, and on 28 September 2026 it still appears nowhere on the App Store page. If your catalogue sits between 250 and 1,000 products, that omission is the difference between $99.99 and $249.99 a month, so ask for the plan by name rather than accepting the step the listing offers you.

What do I do when the AI credits run out?

Top them up rather than upgrading. Credits are a second meter that behaves differently from the product count: the vendor sells 5,000 for $10, 25,000 for $50 and 50,000 for $100 on its own site, so an empty credit balance is a $10 problem and not a reason to move up a tier. Keep the two apart when you size a plan -- your product count decides which rung you are on, and credits are consumable on top of it.

Will this actually get me more traffic?

It fixes the mechanical layer and nothing beyond it: meta titles and descriptions in bulk, alt text, compressed images, JSON-LD, sitemaps and indexing requests. That is maintenance rather than marketing, and it cannot create demand for products nobody searches for. Connect Search Console on day one and judge the app on impressions for the pages you touched, not on the score it gives itself. One caveat on the newest features: the LLMs.txt generator and the Agents.md editor are a bet on a standard no major search or AI vendor has committed to reading, so treat them as a hypothesis you are testing rather than a channel you are buying.

Can I get a refund if StoreSEO does not work for me?

Possibly, and it depends on how many AI credits you used. The refund policy (storeseo.com/refund-policy, effective 7 October 2025, read 12 November 2026) says requests must be made within 14 days of the original purchase and are calculated on the AI credits consumed, because those are a cost StoreSEO carries: if you used half or more of the plan's credits, it may give a pro-rated or partial refund. Credit top-ups are not refundable once used. Refunds go back through Shopify billing and can take 5 to 10 business days. If you are still deciding, leave the bulk AI rewrite until after the decision.
