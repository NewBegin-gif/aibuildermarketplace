Streak Review 2026 — A CRM That Lives Inside Gmail (and Gets Used)

The best CRM is the one your team actually updates. Most don't, because switching to a separate app breaks the workflow. Streak's answer is to put the CRM where the work already happens: inside Gmail. For Google Workspace teams, that's a genuinely smart bet.

This is a researched assessment based on the product and its market fit, not a multi-month hands-on test. I've noted where its inbox-native approach helps and where it limits you.

Verdict: The CRM most likely to get used by a Gmail-native team. That's high praise, because adoption is everything. Start free, turn one of your real processes into a pipeline, and see if updating it feels effortless — for the right team, it does.

What Streak is

CRM, pipelines and email power tools built directly into Gmail.

  • Pipelines in Gmail — track deals, hiring, support or any process as boxes and stages, without leaving your inbox.
  • Email power tools — open tracking, send-later, mail merge, snippets and threads splitting.
  • Shared views — collaborate on pipelines and see team activity.
  • Works where you do — desktop Gmail plus mobile, tied to Google Workspace.

What makes it stand out

Zero context-switching. Because Streak is inside Gmail, adoption — the thing that kills most CRM rollouts — is almost free for a team that already lives in their inbox. The email tools (tracking, mail merge, snippets) are useful on their own, so people keep it open even before they treat it as a CRM.

Pros & cons

What I like

  • Lives inside Gmail — near-zero learning curve for Workspace teams
  • Genuinely useful email tools (tracking, mail merge, snippets)
  • Flexible pipelines for sales, hiring, support or deal-flow
  • Free tier to start; quick to set up

Watch-outs

  • Tied to Gmail/Google Workspace — not for Outlook-based teams
  • Less powerful than full platforms (HubSpot, Salesforce) at scale
  • Heavy reporting and automation needs may outgrow it

Who it's for

Perfect for solopreneurs and small teams that run on Google Workspace and want a CRM they'll actually maintain — recruiters, agencies, founders managing deal-flow. If your company is on Outlook, or you need enterprise reporting and automation, look at a standalone CRM instead.

Why switch from a standalone CRM

For a small team, the problem with Salesforce or HubSpot usually isn't features — it's adoption. People won't leave their inbox to update a separate app, so the CRM quietly rots. Streak's whole bet is to put the pipeline where the work already happens: inside Gmail. For a Google Workspace team, that's the difference between a CRM that gets used and one that doesn't. The honest caveat: if you're on Outlook, or you need deep enterprise reporting and automation, a standalone platform is the better tool.

How hard is it to switch?

Near-zero for Gmail-native teams. Streak installs as a Gmail extension, you import existing contacts or deals by CSV, and there's effectively no new app to learn — the interface is your inbox. Start by turning one real process (a sales pipeline, hiring, deal-flow) into boxes and stages and see whether updating it feels effortless. For the right team, it does.

My verdict

The CRM most likely to get used by a Gmail-native team. That's high praise, because adoption is everything. Start free, turn one of your real processes into a pipeline, and see if updating it feels effortless — for the right team, it does.
