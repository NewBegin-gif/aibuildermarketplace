Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 8 Sep 2026

There is a Free Forever plan at $0, and the paid product is tiered per user with the rate falling as the team grows. Monthly billing: $12 per user a month for 1-25 users, $11 for 26-75, $9 for 76-125, $7 for 126-250 and $6 for 251-1,000. Annual billing takes roughly a dollar to two off each step: $11, $10, $8, $6 and $5 per user a month paid annually.

Two enterprise add-ons sit outside that ladder and are quoted on the page itself at $250 a month each: Single Sign-On (Okta, Microsoft Entra ID, Google Workspace or any SAML/OIDC provider) and Directory Sync via SCIM for automatic provisioning and de-provisioning. A 30-day free trial needs no credit card, and 501(c)(3) nonprofits get 50% off. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

So the number that decides your bill is headcount, not billing term — and at the small end, where most teams start, $12 a user is the least favourable rate on the whole page. The two capabilities a security team will ask for first, SSO and SCIM, are not in any plan: they are $250 a month each, which on a 20-person account doubles the bill.

When the paid plan is worth it

There is a Free Forever plan at $0, so the first question is not whether to pay but when. The paid product is priced per user with the rate falling in five bands, and reading those bands as a ladder misses the most useful thing about them: the band rate applies to every seat, not just the seats above the threshold. Work out the total and the arithmetic inverts at every boundary. Twenty-five users at $12 is $300 a month; twenty-six at $11 is $286. Seventy-five at $11 is $825; seventy-six at $9 is $684. A hundred and twenty-five at $9 is $1,125; a hundred and twenty-six at $7 is $882 — a saving of $243 a month for adding one person. So if you are sitting on 25, 75, 125 or 250 users, you are at the most expensive point on the whole page, and one more seat makes your bill smaller. That is worth knowing before you trim a licence to save money, because trimming is exactly what costs you here. (This reads the published bands the way the page states them — a rate per user per month for a team of that size. Confirm it against your own quote before planning around it.)

The annual discount is the second lever and it works the opposite way round from most software: it is worth proportionally MORE the bigger you are. At 1-25 users annual billing takes $12 to $11, about 8%. At 251-1,000 it takes $6 to $5, nearly 17%. A small team therefore gains little by committing for a year, while a large one gains twice as much. The real cost jump is not in the ladder at all. Single Sign-On and Directory Sync via SCIM — the two capabilities a security team asks for first — sit in no plan and are quoted on the page at $250 a month each. On a twenty-person account paying $240 a month, adding SSO more than doubles the bill, and adding both triples it. Price those in at the start if your IT department will require them, because no amount of climbing the user ladder includes them. There is a 30-day free trial with no credit card, and 501(c)(3) nonprofits get 50% off.

How to actually use Success.co

Signing up runs through a social login — Google, Microsoft, or Success.co's own SimpleLogin — and on first sign-in the platform asks for your company name and a Success.co company URL. That URL matters more than it looks: afterwards you can log in either at success.co/login, which sends you to the company you belong to, or directly at success.co/your-company-name. SimpleLogin is the documented route when your email is not hosted by Google or Microsoft, or when you want to sign in with a different address than your Google or Microsoft one.

First value is a Level 10 meeting rather than a filled-in dashboard, because L10s are the weekly rhythm the whole EOS framework hangs on. The documented path is Start a Meeting, pick the team (Leadership Team, for instance), then choose the meeting type: Weekly L10, Annual Planning Day 1 or Day 2, Focus Day, Quarterly Pulsing or Vision Building. The Schedule tab does the same thing for a future date, with description, date, time and a repeat rule.

A facilitator and a scribe are assigned per meeting, and joining is prompted by a top banner alert and an indicator on the Meetings page. What to set up before that first meeting is your team and your EOS artefacts: the guides cover Join your team, the customisable Dashboard, Agendas (EOS templates or your own), and the core tools — V/TO, Rocks, Scorecard, Accountability Chart, Issues, Headlines and People Analyzer.

Integrations exist for Asana, ClickUp, Monday, Teamwork, Slack, Zapier and both Google and Microsoft Calendar, there is an AI Connector, and there are desktop and mobile apps. EOS implementers get their own section for adding clients and tracking progress, plus an Integrator Hub for team health.
