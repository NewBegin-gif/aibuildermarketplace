Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

One page, two prices

The pricing page contradicts itself, and you should know both numbers before you sign up. The plan card says $17 a month billed yearly or $22 billed monthly for Pro, with unlimited usage and integrations. The FAQ on that same page says Sunsama costs $25 a month per person, or $20 on yearly plans, and works through an example of $50 a month for two people. Enterprise is quote-only (2026-08-30, re-read on their own pricing page from a US exit).

One of those is stale, and there is a third source that narrows it down: the machine-readable llms.txt on their own domain gives Pro as $17 a month billed yearly and $22 billed monthly, which is the plan card rather than the FAQ. That makes the FAQ the likelier stale half, but it does not settle what your card is charged — so budget on $25, the higher of the two, and check the number on the payment screen against the number that persuaded you.

There is an irony worth naming here rather than scoring points with. The stance is genuine and it is the kind of thing that tells you something real about a company. It also sits oddly beside two different prices on one page — and the second thing is the one that affects your card. The structural point is simpler: this is per person, with no team discount mentioned anywhere. Two people is two subscriptions, which their own FAQ confirms with the $50 example.

For a tool whose premise is daily planning and calendar integration, that is worth modelling across the whole team before anyone else asks for it. The free trial needs no card, which is the right way in. What you are testing is not features but whether the daily ritual — planning each day deliberately, in a tool, every morning — is one you keep doing after two weeks. That is a question about you rather than about the software, and no tier changes the answer.

How to actually use Sunsama

Check the price on the payment screen against the price that persuaded you, because the pricing page gives two. The plan card says $17 a month billed yearly and $22 monthly; the FAQ on the same page says $20 yearly and $25 monthly, with a worked example of $50 for two people. Budget on $25 until you have seen which one your account is actually on. Then start the free trial — no card is needed — and treat it as a test of a habit rather than of a feature list.

The premise here is that you plan each day deliberately, every morning. What you need to know after two weeks is whether you still did it in week two, because that is the entire value and no tier affects it. Connect your real calendars and task sources on day one rather than trying it clean. Sunsama's argument is that it pulls everything into one daily plan, and that argument only becomes testable when everything is actually in there — including the sources that make your day messy.

If you are buying for a team, multiply properly: this is per person with no team discount mentioned, so their own example of $50 for two is the shape of it.

Frequently asked questions

What should I weigh before buying Operations & Workflow software?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.
