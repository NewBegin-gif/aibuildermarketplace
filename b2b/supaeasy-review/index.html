A free plan plus four paid monthly tiers. The catch is in the free plan: it works only on a development store, not a live one. Starter is $29 a month ($278 a year), Premium is $49 a month ($470 a year) and Advanced is $99 a month ($950 a year), each with a 7-day trial on the App Store listing, which bills in USD; Ultimate is $399 a month ($3,830 a year) on the vendor's own pricing page and is not on the listing.

Starter is new since our 16 October check: it covers the flow interface for conditional discounts, payment-gateway and delivery-rate rules, validation functions and import/export between stores. The headline feature, the AI Functions Generator, which writes a function from a plain-language description, does not appear until Advanced at $99, alongside the Scripts Editor and the Functions Wizard, and the App Store listing prices AI credits for it separately: $25 per 50, $40 per 100 or $149 per 500.

So using SupaEasy the way it markets itself, on a live store, realistically starts at $99 a month. Plans change, so check the live price on their site before you buy.

What they praise
  • • The clearest recurring job is migrating off Shopify's deprecated Scripts Editor: one merchant describes re-creating 'unique and complicated' discounting logic that had lived in Scripts, and names that migration as the reason to install.
  • • Reviewers who need conditional logic get specific about it: reading cart attributes, checkout validations, and custom pricing rules that Shopify's native discount tools cannot express.
  • • Several reviews describe the vendor building the function for the merchant rather than the merchant building it: one store needed pricing logic for a bundle app it had built itself, was told the app could do it, and had the function written for them; another got a walk-through call until they understood how it worked.
  • • Tenure on the reviews is unusually long for an app store: multiple reviewers write at 'about 1 year', 'almost 2 years' and 'about 2 years' with the app still in place.
  • • One reviewer credits the help docs and setup videos, the only praise in the sample aimed at self-service material rather than at staff.
What they complain about
  • • The sharpest criticism sits inside a five-star review: a US merchant writes plainly that they do not find creating functions 'supaeasy', and credits the support team for making it possible anyway.
  • • Getting one Black Friday discount script right took a ninety-message email chain, by the reviewer's own count: offered as praise for the support person who stayed with it, but it is also a measure of the effort a complex rule can take.
  • • 205 reviews and not one below five stars, with an identical one-line vendor reply under each. A record with no dissent in it cannot tell you what the failure modes are; read it as evidence of an attentive team, not as evidence of an app that never disappoints.

Where the ratings disagree. Across both pages the object of the praise is almost always a named person (Andrea, Fabio, Yuvraj, Abhi) rather than the software. Taken as a whole the reviews describe a done-for-you function-building service with an app attached, which matters if you are buying it expecting to configure discounts yourself.

What the money actually buys

SupaEasy's tiers are really a ladder of where the automation may run and how much of it is AI-assisted. The free plan is a sandbox: a development store, not your real one. Starter at $29 (added by 22 October 2026) opens live use of the flow interface for conditional discounts, payment and delivery rules and validations; Premium at $49 adds templates plus checkout and validation customisations; Advanced at $99 is where the app's actual pitch lives, adding the AI Functions Generator, the Scripts Editor, the Functions Wizard and the Scripts Migrator, with AI credits sold on top at $25 per 50, $40 per 100 or $149 per 500; Ultimate at $399 is for the heaviest use.

The honest frame is that the value proposition ('describe the function in plain language and we'll build it') is an Advanced-tier feature, so the interesting version of SupaEasy starts at $99 a month, not the $29 the tier list now leads with. What you buy at that price is help with the move off Shopify Scripts, which Shopify stopped supporting on 30 June 2026, and the ability to ship checkout and discount logic without a developer.

For a store that has Scripts to move or recurring custom-logic needs, that can pay for itself against a freelance developer; for a one-off simple discount, it is an expensive way in, and a focused discount app does the job for less. What no tier buys is an automatic conversion: SupaEasy's own FAQ says there is no bulk import or automatic transformer that takes a legacy script and creates a function, so each Script's logic is rebuilt in the app by hand (read 29 September 2026).

How to actually use SupaEasy

SupaEasy installs from the Shopify App Store (apps.shopify.com/supaeasy) and works inside the Shopify Admin, so there is no separate account to create. The natural first move depends on why you are there. If you are migrating away from Shopify Scripts, the app's core use case now that Scripts is deprecated, you rebuild each Script's logic as a Shopify Function inside the app: the vendor's FAQ says there is no bulk import or automatic transformer that takes a legacy script as input, and the Scripts Migrator sits on the Advanced plan.

If you are building fresh, you can start from a preset for a common case (a discount, a payment-method or shipping rule, a checkout validation) or, on the Advanced plan, describe what you want in plain language and let the AI Functions Generator write it. The practical gate is the plan: the free plan only runs on a development store, so testing on your live shop and using the AI generator both require a paid tier ($99 for the AI).

Budget the first real session around whichever paid plan includes the feature you actually came for. Read on apps.shopify.com/supaeasy and supaeasy.com/pricing, 28 August 2026, and re-read 29 September 2026.

Frequently asked questions

What does SupaEasy cost?

A free plan (development stores only), then Starter $29, Premium $49 and Advanced $99 a month on the App Store listing, with Ultimate at $399 a month on the vendor's own pricing page; Starter, Premium and Advanced carry a 7-day trial (re-read 22 October 2026, when Starter had been added). The AI Functions Generator that headlines the app is an Advanced-tier feature, so live AI use starts at $99 a month, with AI credits sold separately from $25 per 50.

Is the free plan actually usable?

Only for testing: it runs on a development store, not a live shop. Any production use is paid, so treat the free plan as a sandbox rather than a real free tier.

Which plan has the AI generator?

Advanced, at $99 a month, not Premium. Advanced also adds the Scripts Editor and Functions Wizard. If the plain-language AI builder is why you want SupaEasy, $99 is the real entry price, plus AI credits bought separately: $25 per 50, $40 per 100 or $149 per 500 on the App Store listing (29 September 2026).

Why use it instead of coding Functions myself?

To skip the developer. SupaEasy generates Shopify Functions from the Admin without code, which matters most if you are moving off deprecated Shopify Scripts, though each Script's logic is rebuilt in the app rather than imported automatically. With engineering time, native Shopify Functions cost nothing extra; the app is paying not to write them.
