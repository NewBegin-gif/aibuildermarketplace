What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The meter that actually forces an upgrade is documents: 120 to 360 optimizations depending on tier, which a working content team passes faster than it expects. And the AI-visibility tracking that the product now leads with is capped by prompt count per tier, so watching more of your market costs a step up rather than an add-on.

What Surfer (SurferSEO) does

Content optimization against live SERP data, and since 2026 a tracker for how often a brand is cited in AI answers like ChatGPT and Google AI Overviews. Billed yearly at four tiers.

What they praise
  • • The content editor's live score while you write, and the SERP analysis behind it that shows what pages already ranking actually contain.
  • • NLP keyword suggestions are repeatedly credited with taking the guesswork out of on-page optimisation.
  • • Google Docs and WordPress integrations make handing work to freelance writers straightforward.

What they complain about

  • • The AI frequently ignores reference material it was given, forcing rewrites that undo the time saved.
  • • Humanising the output tends to undo its optimisation, so the two things the tool sells pull against each other.
  • • One reviewer reports statistics and references in generated content that were inaccurate or fabricated, meaning everything has to be checked by hand.

When the paid plan is worth it

Every rate on this page is a monthly figure quoted on annual billing, and annual here means the whole year charged at once: the pricing FAQ answers “do I have to pay upfront for the entire year?” with “Yes.”

The upgrade most teams expect to fix a volume problem is Standard to Pro, and it does not fix it. Both plans carry the same 360 documents. What $99 to $182 buys is prompt tracking — 25 refreshed weekly becomes 50 refreshed daily — along with five brand workspaces, one-click internal linking, the cannibalization report, MCP access and a set of content-planning tools. The document ceiling moves only at Peace of Mind, $299, and the “unlimited” there is qualified in writing: Surfer's Fair Usage Policy puts that plan at 500 documents a month, or 6,000 a year on annual billing. That policy also shows how the billing types differ — an annual limit is twelve times the monthly one, granted in a single batch — and Surfer's plan guide confirms monthly limits reset each month without rolling over. Either way, changing plan resets your credits.

How to actually use Surfer (SurferSEO)

Surfer's own step-by-step collection begins outside the app: Step 0 is setting up Google Search Console, Step 1 is connecting it to Surfer, and only then do Sites and Content Audit have anything to work with. The prerequisite is firm — the Sites walkthrough asks for “at least 30 days of Google Search Console (GSC) data” — so a domain you connect this week will not give you a useful audit this week.

Count your workspaces before you pick a tier rather than after. A single Surfer workspace holds one Site project, and how many workspaces you get depends on the plan, so an agency with four clients is counting projects, not seats. New accounts get a seven-day Pro trial with full access, which is the only way to see the upper tiers before committing to a year. After setup the documented rhythm is once and then weekly: build it once, then review the refresh recommendations at least once a week.

Frequently asked questions

What does Surfer cost?

Read on surferseo.com/pricing on 19 September 2026 from a Netherlands connection, the list is in euros and billed yearly: Discovery €49 a month, Standard €99, Pro €182 and Peace of Mind €299, with a separate AI Search Analytics plan from €82 and Enterprise from €999. Those are the same figures we read as dollars from a US exit on 26 August, so the page swaps the currency label without converting the number, and the one your own checkout shows is the one that counts. The line that looked like a discrepancy — $158 in August against €82 today — is not one: AI Search Analytics is sold by prompt tier, and the page's own pricing data puts 50 prompts at 82, 100 at 158 and 200 at 308 a month on the annual commitment. The card simply opens on a different rung than it did in August, when 100 prompts was the default. Every green save badge on the cards, from €120 to €720 a year, is the yearly-billing discount — up to 17 percent by their own FAQ — and the monthly rates behind the billing toggle (€59, €119, €219, €359 and €95) are what paying month to month actually costs.

If I pay for a year, do my credits roll over each month?

The cards put a number on documents — 120 on Discovery, 360 on Standard and Pro, unlimited on Peace of Mind — without saying over what period, and the unlimited carries a fair-usage asterisk on the page itself. Their FAQ answers it only for annual plans: credits reset yearly, or when you change plan. So on a yearly plan the document allowance reads as one pot for the whole year rather than a monthly refill, and an upgrade or downgrade resets it. Ask support to confirm the monthly-plan behaviour before you buy on that basis; the page does not state it.

Which plan do I need if I only care about AI-visibility tracking?

The cheapest tracking is also the most tracking, as long as you read the prompt tier. The AI Search Analytics plan starts at €82 a month for 50 AI prompts refreshed daily across five brand spaces with MCP access, and steps up to €158 for 100 prompts and €308 for 200 — nothing on that card creates or optimises content at any tier. Standard, at €99, tracks only 25 prompts and refreshes them weekly; to get 50 prompts refreshed daily alongside the writing tools you are on Pro at €182. So €100 more than the entry tracking plan buys you the editor while halving your prompt coverage and slowing it to weekly, and if it is prompts you want rather than an editor, the €158 tracking tier gives you twice Pro's coverage for less than Pro. Decide which of the two jobs you are buying before you look at the prices.

How do I cancel, and what can I pay with?

Plans auto-renew by default and you switch that off yourself on the in-app Billing page; their FAQ is explicit that this neither closes nor deletes the account and that you keep access until the end of the period you already paid for. Payment is credit or debit card on monthly and yearly plans, bank transfer on yearly only, and PayPal is not supported. An annual purchase is charged for the full twelve months upfront, so the discount and the commitment arrive together.
