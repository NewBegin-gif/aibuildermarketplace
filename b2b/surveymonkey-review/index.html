Every Team seat costs the same, the first one included: in the US table Team Advantage Annual is $360 a user a year and Team Premier Annual $1,104, the $30 and $92 a month the SurveyMonkey partner team quoted by email on 24 September 2026, so three seats come to $1,080 or $3,312 a year.

We carried $0.15 per additional response, then "corrected" it to €0.10 and noted that $0.15 appeared in none of our readings. Both figures are correct: SurveyMonkey's pricing.md prices the overage per storefront — $0.15 in dollar countries, €0.10 in euro ones, £0.15 in sterling — so the two were never in conflict, and that earlier correction is withdrawn (read 2026-09-08). Correction on 1 October 2026: the dearer 'core seat' we reported from the 8 September reading ($468 plus $360 a seat on Team Advantage, $1,668 plus $1,104 on Team Premier, €432 plus €360 and €1,188 plus €900 in euros) is not in the current pricing.md. Against our 8 September reading, two things did move: the Canada-only monthly Team variants are gone from the plan list, and Basic's response limit is now shown as 10 rather than 25 outside Canada. The overage rates are unchanged.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Response caps on the self-serve plans trigger $0.15-per-response overages (US rate; Enterprise contracts set their own limits) that become a hidden cost on big studies. Branding and customisation are limited, and a lot of the good analytics and logic sit behind higher tiers.

What SurveyMonkey does

Survey and feedback platform: build surveys, collect responses at scale and analyze results for market research, CX and employee feedback. A native Salesforce integration pushes responses straight into Salesforce, triggers workflows off the answers themselves (churn-risk follow-up or an NPS detractor landing on a rep's desk, for instance) and ties feedback back to accounts and pipeline, so sales and CS read it inside the tool they already work in rather than in a separate reporting dashboard.

The response pool decides the bill

A Team plan buys seats and a response pool, and the pool is the part that decides the bill. Team plans are annual-only and start at three users, with every seat priced the same: in the US, $360 a user a year on Team Advantage and $1,104 on Team Premier, so the three-seat minimum is $1,080 or $3,312 a year (pricing.md dated 22 September 2026, read 1 October). Euro storefronts charge €360 and €900 a seat.

What the step from Team Advantage to Team Premier buys is mostly analysis and presentation (white-label branding, advanced logic, block randomisation, multilingual surveys, crosstabs and phone support) plus a bigger pool: 100,000 responses a year instead of 50,000, as the pricing page states on the euro storefront. That pool is counted across all surveys and all users, so a colleague's large study draws down the allowance everyone else planned to use.

What upgrading does not solve is the overage. Above the pool, every response costs $0.15 in dollar countries, €0.10 in euro ones and £0.15 in sterling, on every self-serve tier. Premier doubles the allowance at two and a half times the per-seat price in euros: for a three-person team the step costs €1,620 a year for 50,000 extra responses, and the same money covers 16,200 overage responses at €0.10. If volume is your only reason to move up, staying on Advantage and paying overage is cheaper until you exceed the pool by more than that.

No tier includes respondents either: SurveyMonkey Audience is sold separately, so without your own list the research cost is a third line item. Below the Team plans, the free Basic tier allows 10 questions per survey and, in the 22 September pricing.md, shows only 10 responses outside Canada (25 in Canada). That is a demo of the editor, not a research instrument. A correction: earlier versions of this review said the first Team seat costs more than the rest. It does not; those figures were the individual-plan column of the same table.

How to actually use SurveyMonkey

Ask two questions before you buy: how many responses a year your tier includes, and whether that pool is shared across every user and survey on the account. The pricing page answers both for Team plans (50,000 a year on Team Advantage and 100,000 on Team Premier on the euro storefront, counted across all surveys and users), but confirm the figure for your own storefront at checkout, because the overage beyond it ($0.15, €0.10 or £0.15 a response) is what turns a cheap plan into an expensive study.

Budget from three seats: Team plans need a minimum of three users and are billed annually, so the entry price is $1,080 a year in the US ($360 a seat) or €1,080 in euro countries. If you work alone, compare the individual plans instead: in the US table, Standard Annual $300 a year, Advantage Annual $468 and Premier Annual $1,668, with Standard Monthly the only monthly option. Use the free Basic tier only to check question types and logic: 10 questions and, outside Canada, 10 visible responses per survey cannot test anything.

If you do not have your own list, price SurveyMonkey Audience separately before you commit. And if you later turn off auto-renew, responses above the Basic limit that are older than 365 days are deleted when the account drops to the free plan, so export first.

Frequently asked questions

What does SurveyMonkey cost for a team?

$360 a user a year on Team Advantage and $1,104 on Team Premier in the US, with a three-user minimum and annual billing only, so $1,080 or $3,312 a year to start; the first seat costs the same as the rest (pricing.md dated 22 September 2026, read 1 October). Euro storefronts charge €360 and €900 a seat a year, shown on the page as €30 and €75 a month. An earlier version of this answer added a dearer 'core seat'; that was our misreading of the individual-plan column and is withdrawn.

What happens if I go over my response limit?

On the self-serve plans you are charged per response above your plan's included limit, at a rate that depends on your storefront: $0.15 in dollar countries, €0.10 in euro ones, £0.15 in sterling. Two things make that bigger than it looks: on paid plans the limit is counted across all surveys and all users rather than per survey, so a colleague's popular survey can consume the whole team's allowance, and SurveyMonkey Audience responses are sold separately on top. Enterprise plans work differently: custom response limits and contract pricing. The pricing page states the included limit for Team plans (50,000 responses a year on Team Advantage and 100,000 on Team Premier, read on the euro storefront), while pricing.md still marks responses "Unlimited" on every paid plan, so ask for your own storefront's number in writing.

Is the free plan enough to test the product?

For the interface, yes; for a real survey, no. Basic allows ten questions per survey, and the 22 September 2026 edition of pricing.md shows its response limit as ten outside Canada (Canadian Basic users keep twenty-five). It keeps collecting, but responses past the limit stay hidden until you upgrade. For a genuine pilot, the only monthly plan is the individual Standard Monthly: $149 a month in the US table of pricing.md, although the partner team quoted $99 by email on 24 September 2026, so check the price at checkout. Team plans are billed annually from three users.

What should I weigh before buying Operations & Workflow software?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.
