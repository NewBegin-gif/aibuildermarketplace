What Survicate does

Customer feedback platform for continuous surveys: NPS, CSAT and in-product micro-surveys across web, in-app, email and link — with templates, audience targeting and native integrations (Segment and RudderStack among them) that pipe feedback straight into your CRM and analytics.

What buyers report about Survicate

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Survicate ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Survey creation, onboarding and multichannel delivery are all described as easy, and support is responsive — 4.6 across 147 reviewers.
  • • Once it is set up and integrated, reviewers report no ongoing friction.
What they complain about
  • • You pay per response, and reviewers dislike that specifically. Growth is $114/month for 250 responses; Pro starts at $349 and Enterprise $569.

The per-response price falls by two thirds

Pro is $349 with a custom yearly response pool, advanced survey logic and 10 dashboards, and Enterprise is $569 (read 2026-09-08 in the plan data the pricing page embeds; the page's own meta description says pricing starts from $56). Divide each rung by its allowance and the unit price falls hard: 56 cents a response at the bottom, 45.6 cents at $114, 33.8 at $169, 22.9 at $229 and 19.3 at $289.

The rung you land on therefore matters more than the plan name — 1,500 responses cost about a third per response of what 100 cost. It is still a lot for a unit of feedback; general-purpose survey tools charge single-digit cents for an extra response. What you are buying at that price is the in-product targeting and the integrations rather than the questionnaire, and that is a legitimate premium; it is just worth knowing which side of it you are paying for.

The single most useful difference between Growth and Pro is not on the feature list. Growth's 250 responses are a monthly cap; Pro's pool is annual. Feedback almost never arrives evenly — a launch, a price change or a support incident produces a spike, and a monthly cap turns exactly the month you most want data into the month you run out. If your feedback is campaign-shaped rather than steady, Pro's yearly pooling may be worth more than the ten dashboards it also brings.

One thing to unpick before you compare against anything older. We previously carried $89 as the entry price and then recorded a rise to $114, calling it a 28% increase. Neither figure was the floor and there was no increase: $89 is Starter's 100-response rung billed monthly, $114 is Growth's 250-response rung billed annually, and the actual Growth floor is $56. The billing asymmetry behind that confusion is real and worth knowing — Starter is monthly-only while Growth and above expect an annual commitment, so the plan that lets you leave cheaply is the one you probably do not want.

Do not plan to live on the free tier if NPS is why you are here. Survicate's own FAQ puts it at 25 responses a month, one active survey at a time, three collaborators, basic CRM integrations, a 300-response cap per survey and 1,000 per organisation, and 30 days of data retention before deletion. NPS is a trend measurement — a number with no previous quarter beside it cannot tell you anything — so the retention window alone rules it out.

The free plan can show you the product works; it structurally cannot do the job the product exists for. The 10-day trial of all features needs no card, which is the better way to look.

How to actually use Survicate

Estimate your monthly response volume before you look at plans, because that is the meter and the rung you land on sets the price. Survicate publishes the conversion it sees, which makes the sum doable: roughly 10% on web surveys, 16% in-product, 30% on mobile, and for email about a 20% open rate times a 20% click rate. On its own arithmetic, 250 responses means reaching 3,125 people on the web, 1,563 in-app, 833 on mobile, or sending 6,250 emails.

Work out your peak month rather than your average one, and note that a response counts per respondent — partial or complete, it is one. If your feedback is spiky, ask specifically about the response pool. Growth's allowance resets monthly; Pro's is an annual pool. A launch or an incident is precisely when you want the data and precisely when a monthly cap stops you, so for campaign-shaped feedback the pooling can matter more than any feature difference.

Use the 10-day trial rather than the free plan to evaluate. The free tier keeps only 30 days of history before deleting, which makes it useless for NPS or anything else measured as a trend — the trial gives you every feature and needs no card. And check which rung you are being quoted rather than which plan: Growth runs from $56 for 100 responses to $289 for 1,500, so a price named without its allowance tells you nothing.

Since 4 September 2026 there is a remote MCP server at https://mcp.survicate.com, which is worth setting up on day one if your team already works in an AI assistant. Connected, it will list your surveys and questions, pull survey and per-question results — response and completion counts, answer distributions, NPS and CSAT — over a date range you name, search the free-text responses, read and start AI research projects, and draft a survey that lands in your panel for review rather than going out.

Connection is one-click OAuth with no API keys, and every call runs as the connected user, so workspace role, folder access and Research Hub role apply exactly as they do in the panel — check that a shared assistant is connected under an account whose reach you are happy with. One gap to note: Survicate's own documentation does not say which Survicate plan the connector requires, and the only subscription it mentions is your AI assistant's, so ask before you assume it is included in the tier you are pricing.

Frequently asked questions

What should I weigh before buying Operations & Workflow software?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.
