How the migration actually works

These steps are summarised from SignNow’s own public documentation — we have not invented or embellished a single one. Step 1. Download your templates out of DocuSign

In DocuSign, open the Templates tab, select a template and click Use › Download. The template downloads to your device as a ZIP.

Step 2. Import them into signNow

In signNow, hover the Upload or Create button (top right) and choose Migrate Template from DocuSign. Click Select File and upload. You can upload an unlimited number of templates at once, as long as each file stays under 50 MB.

Step 3. Check that fields and roles carried over

Templates imported from DocuSign keep their fillable fields and signer role settings, so they should not need reconfiguring. Open two or three of your most complex templates and confirm before you trust the rest.

Step 4. Move your users

In the DocuSign Admin app, open Users and download the user list. Convert that CSV to .xls or .xlsx — signNow will not take the raw CSV. The file needs an Email column; First Name and Last Name are optional. Then use Invite Users From .XLS › Choose File.

What the docs quietly admit

Every migration has friction. These are the documented ones — the things worth knowing before you start, not after:

  • The user list export needs converting from .csv to .xls/.xlsx before signNow will accept it. It is a small step that stops a lot of migrations dead because the error is not obvious.
  • Templates over 50 MB have to go one at a time or be slimmed down first.
  • Completed envelopes are a separate question from templates. If you have retention or audit-trail obligations on historic agreements, resolve where those live before you close the old account.

When not to switch

The most useful thing we can tell you is when to stay put. Skip this migration if you need the deepest enterprise integrations and workflows — that's where the incumbent still earns its premium. Beyond that: if your current setup works, your list is healthy and nobody on your team is complaining, a migration buys you a week of admin and a fresh set of unknowns. Switch because something specific is broken or expensive — a deliverability problem you can name, a bill that has outgrown the value, a feature you genuinely cannot get — not because a comparison page told you the grass was greener.
