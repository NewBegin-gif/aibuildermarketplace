Seasonal beats annual unless you stream all year

Studio is $45 a month on annual billing and $65 month-to-month; Suite is $79 annually and $99 month-to-month (read 26 September 2026 on switcherstudio.com/pricing). Both Suite rates carry a promotion: $71 a month in the first year ($95 off, renewing at $948 a year) or $79 for the first month, then $99. Fourteen-day trial, no free plan. A correction first. On 8 September we built this section on a Studio Seasonal tier - $55 a month on a three-month commitment - and argued it beat the annual plan for anyone streaming fewer than about ten months a year.

That tier is gone from the page. What replaced it is a Studio 7-Day Pass: $35 once, all Studio features, for a single event. That changes the maths for occasional users. Studio annually is $540 a year; fifteen separate events on 7-Day Passes cost $525, sixteen cost $560. So the pass wins up to fifteen events a year, and a weekly stream belongs on the annual plan. Within a single month, two passes ($70) already cost more than a month of Studio at $65.

Month-to-month is otherwise steep: $65 against $45 is a 44% surcharge on Studio, and $99 against the $79 renewal rate is 25% on Suite. Payments for a past period are not refunded, and cancelling has to happen at least 24 hours before renewal. What Suite adds is distribution and selling, not production: 20 multistream destinations instead of 2, a library of up to 100 videos, embeddable players and web pages, ticketed streams, pay-to-watch and subscriptions at $0.99 a transaction with no revenue share, and 1,000 cloud assets instead of 250.

If selling access is not part of your plan, none of that is a reason to pay $34 a month more. What no plan fixes: the app switches only on Mac and iOS - Android phones can join as cameras, Windows has no route in - and wireless multi-cam, up to nine cameras, is exactly as reliable as the venue's Wi-Fi. Every extra angle is another device to buy, and the rehearsal that matters is on site with every camera connected.

How to actually use Switcher Studio

Test on the venue's Wi-Fi, not on yours. Wireless multi-cam is only as good as the network it runs on, and a hall full of guests is a different network from an empty one — so the rehearsal that matters is on site, with every camera connected, at something close to real conditions. This is the failure mode that ruins live events and it is not a software problem. Count the devices you already own before you count anything else.

The app runs on Mac and iOS, and Android phones can join as cameras, so the rig is less locked to Apple than it was — but each angle is still another device, and that hardware cost will be larger than the subscription. Windows is the one platform with no route in. Then pick the billing shape from how often you will actually stream. A single event is a $35 Studio 7-Day Pass; up to fifteen separate events a year, passes cost less than Studio annually at $540; a regular weekly stream belongs on the annual plan, since month-to-month carries a 44% surcharge. Cancel at least 24 hours before renewal, because past periods are not refunded.

Frequently asked questions

Can I pay for Switcher for just one event?

Yes. The pricing page (read 26 September 2026) sells a Studio 7-Day Pass for $35 one-time, with all Studio features, aimed at a single event you need to stream. Against Studio's annual plan at $540 a year, passes stay cheaper up to fifteen separate events; within one month, two passes cost more than a month of Studio at $65. There is also a 14-day free trial to rehearse before the event.

Can I get a refund if I cancel Switcher?

Not for time already paid. The FAQ (read 26 September 2026) says subscriptions renew automatically unless you cancel at least 24 hours before the end of the billing cycle, that payments for a previous period are nonrefundable, and that you keep access until the cycle ends. If you subscribed inside the app, the purchase went through the Apple App Store and has to be cancelled there; Switcher's support cannot do it for you.

Does Switcher take a cut of ticket or pay-per-view sales?

No revenue share, but a per-transaction fee: the pricing page (read 26 September 2026) says you pay just $0.99 per transaction. Selling tickets to live streams, pay-to-watch content and subscriptions are listed only on Suite, at $79 a month on annual billing or $99 monthly, so a Studio subscriber cannot sell access at all. On a $5 ticket the fee is about a fifth of the price; on a $30 ticket it is closer to 3%.

What should I weigh before buying Operations & Workflow software?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.
