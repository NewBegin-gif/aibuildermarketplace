Synthesia Pricing: The Complete Guide for B2B Founders (2025)

Synthesia promises to collapse that entire pipeline into a browser tab — type a script, pick an AI avatar, and export a broadcast-quality video in under 10 minutes. No cameras, no crew, no editing software.

Overview: What Synthesia Actually Does

Synthesia is an AI video generation platform that turns text scripts into videos featuring realistic human avatars speaking in 140+ languages. It's built primarily for corporate use cases — training modules, internal comms, sales enablement, and marketing localization — rather than viral social content. The platform is used by teams at companies like Zoom, Reebok, and Xerox, which tells you where it's positioned: enterprise L&D and internal comms, not TikTok creators.

Unlike consumer-facing tools like HeyGen or D-ID, Synthesia leans hard into compliance, brand control, and workflow features (templates, screen recording, PowerPoint import) rather than flashy avatar customization. That focus shows up directly in its pricing tiers.

Key Features

  • 230+ stock AI avatars across diverse ethnicities, ages, and professional styles, plus the ability to create a custom avatar of yourself or an employee (Enterprise-only on most plans).
  • 140+ languages and accents with AI-generated voiceovers, making this genuinely useful for localizing training content without hiring voice actors in every market.
  • Script-to-video generation — type or paste text, and the avatar lip-syncs and delivers it with natural pacing.
  • Screen recording and PowerPoint/PDF import to convert existing slide decks into narrated videos.
  • Templates library for common formats: onboarding, product demos, sales pitches, compliance training.
  • Team collaboration tools — shared brand kits, workspace permissions, and content approval workflows on higher tiers.
  • API access (Enterprise) for programmatic video generation — useful if you want to auto-generate personalized videos at scale (e.g., one video per lead or per employee).
  • SSO, SCIM provisioning, and custom data retention policies for enterprise security teams.

The API access point deserves its own callout: this is where Synthesia stops being "just a video tool" and becomes infrastructure. Teams building personalized video campaigns (think: a mortgage company generating a custom explainer video per applicant) are doing it through this API, not the web UI.

Pricing Breakdown

Synthesia's public pricing (as of 2025) is structured in four tiers. Note that Synthesia frequently adjusts exact numbers and minute allowances, so always confirm current rates on their site before budgeting — but here's the structure and real math you need to plan around:

  • Free plan — $0/month. You get roughly 3 minutes of video generation total (not monthly), a handful of stock avatars, and a visible watermark. This is a proof-of-concept tier, not something you'd run a business on. Good for testing avatar quality before committing.
  • Starter plan — approximately $18–29/month when billed annually (monthly billing runs higher, closer to $30–40/month). This tier includes around 120 minutes of video per year (roughly 10 minutes/month), 1 seat, access to the stock avatar library, and standard export resolution. No custom avatars, no API.
  • Creator plan — around $64–89/month billed annually. This bumps you to roughly 360–420 minutes/year (about 30 minutes/month), adds more avatar variety, higher resolution exports, and some collaboration features. Still capped at 1–2 seats depending on the exact package Synthesia is running that quarter.
  • Enterprise plan — custom pricing, typically starting around $1,000–$3,000+/month depending on seat count, minute volume, custom avatar creation, and API access. This is where you get SSO, dedicated account management, custom avatars of real employees, and volume-based minute pricing that can bring the per-minute cost down significantly if you're producing hundreds of videos a month.

The critical detail most reviews skip: minutes, not videos, are the billing unit. A 3-minute onboarding video and a 30-second product teaser both consume minutes from your pool. If your team is producing longer-form training content (10+ minutes per video), the Starter plan's ~10 minutes/month evaporates after a single video. Budget accordingly — most B2B teams doing serious content volume land on Creator or Enterprise within the first quarter.

Also worth noting: custom avatars (a digital clone of your CEO, a specific salesperson, or a brand spokesperson) are gated behind Enterprise pricing and typically involve a separate one-time production fee on top of the subscription, often in the $1,000–$5,000 range depending on avatar quality tier.

  • Best-in-class avatar realism for corporate/professional use cases compared to most competitors at this price point.

ROI for B2B Founders: Does the Math Work?

Here's where Synthesia earns its keep — or doesn't, depending on your use case. Let's run three realistic scenarios.

Scenario 1: SaaS onboarding videos. A 40-person SaaS company needs 12 onboarding videos (3 minutes each) translated into 5 languages for international customers. Traditional route: hire a videographer + voice actors per language = roughly $800–$1,500 per video per language, so 12 videos × 5 languages × $1,000 average = $60,000, plus 6–8 weeks of production time. On Synthesia's Creator plan ($89/month, ~420 minutes/year), the same 12 videos across 5 languages consume roughly 180 minutes (36 minutes × 5 languages), well within the annual allowance. Total cost: ~$1,068/year. That's a 98% cost reduction and a production timeline measured in days, not months.

Scenario 2: Sales enablement at scale. A B2B startup wants personalized outreach videos for its top 200 enterprise leads — a common tactic that boosts reply rates 2–3x compared to plain email. Manually recording 200 personalized videos is a non-starter for a small sales team. Using Synthesia's API on an Enterprise plan (~$2,000/month), a founder can template a script with dynamic fields (name, company, use case) and batch-generate 200 videos programmatically in an afternoon. If this campaign lifts close rate by even 2 additional enterprise deals worth $40,000 ACV each, that's $80,000 in new revenue against a $24,000 annual platform cost — a 3.3x return before counting sales team time saved.

Scenario 3: Internal compliance training. A 150-employee company needs quarterly compliance refreshers (5 minutes, English + Spanish). Outsourcing this to an agency typically runs $3,000–$5,000 per video cycle. On Synthesia's Starter plan ($29/month), four quarterly videos across two languages consume about 40 minutes/year — comfortably inside even the entry tier. Annual cost: ~$350 versus $12,000–$20,000 via agency. That's not a marginal saving; it's a rounding error compared to the alternative.

The pattern across all three scenarios: Synthesia's ROI is strongest when you have repeatable, script-driven video needs — onboarding, training, localization, personalized outreach at scale. It's weakest when you need a single, highly polished brand hero video, where a real production team or a tool like Colossyan's more cinematic templates might still edge it out on production value.

Best Alternatives

Synthesia isn't the only player here, and depending on your priorities, one of these might fit better:

  • HeyGen — Generally cheaper at the entry tier (~$24/month for similar minute allowances) and faster iteration on avatar realism updates. Better suited for marketing/social content creators; slightly less enterprise tooling (weaker SSO/compliance story) than Synthesia.
  • Colossyan — Strong direct competitor for corporate training specifically, with more built-in interactivity (quizzes, branching scenarios) baked into videos. Pricing is comparable to Synthesia's Creator/Enterprise tiers, but the interactive layer is a genuine differentiator for L&D teams.
  • D-ID — More developer-friendly and API-first from day one, with lower per-minute costs at scale. Better choice if you're building video generation into your own product rather than using it as an internal tool. Avatar realism is slightly behind Synthesia's flagship options.
  • Elai.io — Budget option, roughly 30–40% cheaper across comparable tiers, with a smaller but growing avatar library. Reasonable choice for early-stage startups testing whether AI video fits their workflow before committing to a pricier platform.
  • Hourone — Niche strength in fully automated "AI presenter" content (news-style formats, recurring branded shows). Less flexible for one-off corporate videos but interesting if you want an always-on AI host format.

If your primary need is polished, high-volume corporate communication with strong compliance requirements, Synthesia's combination of avatar quality and enterprise features still leads the pack. If you're cost-sensitive and doing lighter-weight social or marketing content, HeyGen or Elai.io will likely stretch your budget further.

Verdict

But for B2B teams with recurring, script-based video needs (onboarding, training, localization, personalized outreach), the ROI math is rarely close: you're typically looking at 80–98% cost reduction versus traditional production, with turnaround times measured in hours instead of weeks.

The founders who get the most value are the ones treating this as infrastructure, not a one-off marketing tool — plugging the API into CRM workflows, batch-generating localized training content, or automating personalized sales videos at scale. If your use case is a single polished brand film, spend the money on a real production house instead. But if you're producing video content repeatedly across languages, teams, or personalized segments, Synthesia's pricing — once you get past the Starter tier's tight minute caps — pays for itself within the first campaign.
