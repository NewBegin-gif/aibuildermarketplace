Overview: What Synthesia Actually Does

Synthesia is an AI video generation platform that turns text scripts into presenter-led videos using digital avatars and synthetic voices. Instead of hiring a camera crew, renting studio time, or booking voice talent, teams type a script, pick an avatar and voice, and export a finished video in minutes. It has become a go-to tool for corporate training, product explainers, sales enablement, onboarding content, and localized marketing videos.

For B2B founders and operators evaluating video tools, the appeal is obvious: video production is traditionally expensive, slow, and hard to scale across languages and regions. Synthesia positions itself as the solution to that bottleneck. But before committing budget, it's worth understanding exactly what you get at each pricing tier, what's genuinely useful versus marketing gloss, and how the cost structure compares to alternatives.

This guide breaks down Synthesia's published pricing, core features, and trade-offs so you can make an informed buying decision. We have not personally tested Synthesia; everything below is based on publicly available information from Synthesia's website and pricing page, with explicit notes wherever figures or claims are not disclosed by the vendor.

Key Features

Synthesia's feature set is built around the core workflow of "script in, video out," with several layers added for enterprise use cases:

  • AI avatars: Synthesia offers a library of stock digital presenters, plus the ability to create custom avatars modeled on real people (a feature typically gated to higher or enterprise tiers, according to Synthesia's own plan descriptions).
  • Text-to-speech voiceovers: Scripts are converted into synthetic speech in the avatar's voice, removing the need for voice actors or recording equipment.
  • Multi-language support: Synthesia publishes support for a large number of languages and accents on its site, positioned as a way to localize training and marketing content without re-shooting video. The exact current language count is listed on Synthesia's website and can change as the product evolves, so we won't restate a specific figure here beyond what's published live on their site.
  • Templates and screen recording: The platform includes pre-built templates for common use cases (onboarding, training, product demos) and a screen recorder for combining avatar narration with software walkthroughs.
  • Collaboration tools: Higher-tier plans add team workspaces, brand kits, and approval workflows, which matter for companies producing video at scale rather than one-off clips.
  • API access: Synthesia offers API access for programmatic video generation, aimed at companies that want to embed video creation into their own product or automate video output at volume. According to Synthesia, API access is generally tied to Enterprise plans rather than self-serve tiers.
  • Custom avatars and voice cloning: Enterprise customers can request personalized avatars (e.g., an executive or brand ambassador) and custom voice clones, per Synthesia's plan descriptions — though the exact process, turnaround time, and cost for this service are not publicly itemized on the pricing page and would need to be confirmed directly with sales.

Pricing Breakdown (Published Prices Only)

Synthesia uses a tiered subscription model, generally structured around plan names such as Free, Starter, Creator, and Enterprise. Below is a breakdown based strictly on what Synthesia discloses publicly on its pricing page. Because SaaS pricing pages change frequently and this article does not include a live pricing check, we recommend confirming exact current dollar amounts directly on Synthesia's official pricing page before budgeting.

  • Free plan: Synthesia publishes a free tier intended for evaluation, typically limited by a capped number of video minutes per month and a smaller selection of avatars and templates. The exact minute allowance and feature restrictions are listed on Synthesia's pricing page and should be checked there, as free-tier limits are among the details most likely to change over time.
  • Starter plan: A paid entry-level plan aimed at individuals or small teams producing a modest volume of video content monthly. Synthesia lists a monthly subscription price and a monthly minutes/credits allowance for this tier on its pricing page. We are not restating a specific dollar figure here to avoid citing a number that may no longer be current; Synthesia's pricing page is the authoritative source.
  • Creator plan: A mid-tier plan generally aimed at content teams needing more video minutes, more avatars, and additional customization options (such as custom avatars in some historical versions of the pricing page). Again, Synthesia publishes the exact monthly cost and included minute allowance on its site, and this is the figure founders should reference when budgeting rather than a number reproduced secondhand.
  • Enterprise plan: Synthesia does not publish a fixed price for Enterprise; it is explicitly quote-based, requiring prospective customers to contact sales. Enterprise typically bundles higher usage limits, custom avatars, API access, dedicated support, and security/compliance features (such as SSO), according to Synthesia's plan comparison. The exact enterprise price is not publicly disclosed by Synthesia — this is a case where the vendor deliberately keeps pricing opaque and gates it behind a sales conversation, which is common for enterprise SaaS but worth flagging clearly.

Two structural points matter for budgeting purposes, both consistent with how Synthesia describes its plans:

  • Pricing is generally usage-based on video minutes rather than flat unlimited usage — meaning your monthly cost is tied to how much video you actually produce, not just seat count. This is an important distinction from many SaaS tools that charge per user regardless of usage.
  • Synthesia typically offers a discount for annual billing versus monthly billing, a common SaaS practice; the exact percentage discount is listed on the pricing page itself and is not something we're restating without direct confirmation.

Bottom line on pricing: Synthesia is transparent about the existence and general structure of its Free, Starter, and Creator tiers, but exact dollar amounts should always be pulled from Synthesia's live pricing page since they are subject to change. Enterprise pricing is explicitly not public and requires a custom quote.

Pros & Cons

Pros

  • Removes the need for cameras, studios, actors, and video editors for many common corporate video use cases.
  • Faster turnaround than traditional video production — scripts can become finished videos without scheduling shoots or edits.
  • Multi-language support (as published on Synthesia's site) makes localization dramatically cheaper than re-filming content in every target market.
  • Clear tiered structure with a free option to test before committing budget.
  • Enterprise features like API access and custom avatars support scaling video production as a repeatable, programmatic process rather than a one-off creative project.

Cons

  • Usage-based pricing on video minutes means costs can scale up quickly for teams producing high volumes of long-form content — founders should model this against actual production needs, not just headline plan prices.
  • Enterprise pricing is not public, which makes upfront budgeting harder and requires a sales conversation before you know the real cost at scale.
  • Custom avatars and voice cloning appear to be gated behind higher tiers, so smaller teams may not access the most differentiated features without upgrading.
  • AI avatars, however polished, still carry a recognizable "synthetic" quality that may not suit every brand or audience — this is a qualitative consideration, not something Synthesia itself claims to have solved with published metrics.
  • No independently published performance benchmarks (e.g., viewer engagement lift, completion rates) are available from Synthesia to substantiate ROI claims; any such claims should be treated as directional, not proven.

Cost Considerations for B2B Founders

For founders evaluating Synthesia as a line item, the decision isn't just "which plan is cheapest" — it's whether the usage-based model matches your actual content cadence. A few practical considerations:

  • Estimate your real monthly minute volume before picking a tier. Because Synthesia's plans are gated by included video minutes (per its published pricing structure), founders should map out realistic monthly output — onboarding videos, sales demos, training modules — before committing to annual billing. Overestimating usage locks you into a higher tier unnecessarily; underestimating leads to overage costs or forced upgrades mid-cycle.
  • Factor in team growth, not just current headcount. If you're a five-person startup today planning to scale a customer success or L&D function, the Starter or Creator tier that fits now may not fit in twelve months. Since Enterprise pricing is quote-based and not published, it's worth having a rough sense from Synthesia's sales team of what the jump to Enterprise looks like cost-wise before you're forced into that conversation reactively.
  • Compare against the true cost of the alternative. The relevant comparison isn't "Synthesia's price vs. zero" — it's Synthesia's subscription cost vs. what you'd otherwise spend on freelance video editors, voice actors, translators for localization, and studio time. For many B2B use cases (internal training, repetitive product explainers), the traditional production cost is likely higher, but founders should run this comparison with their own real quotes rather than assuming it.
  • Watch for hidden scaling costs in custom features. Custom avatars, voice cloning, and API access are typically enterprise-gated. If your roadmap depends on any of these (e.g., embedding video generation into your own SaaS product via Synthesia's API), get Enterprise pricing clarity early rather than building a roadmap around a self-serve tier that may not support it.
  • Annual vs. monthly billing trade-off. Synthesia, like most SaaS vendors, appears to reward annual commitments with better rates. For an early-stage company still validating whether AI video fits its workflow, it may be worth paying the monthly premium initially rather than locking into annual billing before confirming adoption internally.

Best Alternatives

Synthesia isn't the only AI avatar/video generation platform, and founders should weigh it against comparable tools before committing:

  • HeyGen: A direct competitor offering AI avatars and text-to-video generation, often compared to Synthesia on avatar realism and multi-language support. HeyGen publishes its own tiered pricing separately; founders should compare its published plans directly against Synthesia's rather than assuming feature parity.
  • Colossyan: Positioned specifically at corporate training and L&D use cases, similar to Synthesia's core market. Colossyan's pricing and feature tiers are published on its own site and worth a side-by-side comparison for training-heavy use cases.
  • D-ID: Known for avatar and talking-head video generation with a stronger developer/API-first angle in some of its offerings, useful for founders who want to embed avatar video generation into their own product from an early stage.
  • Traditional freelance/agency video production: For companies with lower volume but higher production-value needs (brand films, investor videos), traditional production may still outperform AI avatar tools on creative quality, even if it costs more per video and takes longer.
  • In-house recording with basic editing tools: For very early-stage startups, simply recording a founder or team member on a webcam and editing with a lightweight tool may be more authentic and cheaper than any AI avatar subscription — a real alternative worth considering before adding another SaaS line item.

None of these alternatives are being ranked here with performance claims, since none of the vendors involved (including Synthesia) publish comparative benchmarks that would support a definitive ranking. The right choice depends on your specific volume, budget, and whether avatar realism or API flexibility matters more to your use case.

Verdict

Synthesia is a credible, well-established player in the AI avatar video space, with a tiered pricing model that scales from a free evaluation tier up to a custom-quoted Enterprise plan. Its core value proposition — turning scripts into finished, localized video without traditional production overhead — is well suited to B2B use cases like training, onboarding, and internal communications, where polish matters less than speed and repeatability.
