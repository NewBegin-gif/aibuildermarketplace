Overview: What Synthesia Actually Does

Synthesia is an AI video generation platform that turns text scripts into professional-looking videos featuring realistic AI avatars. No cameras, no actors, no editing suites — you type a script, pick an avatar and voice, and you get a finished video in minutes. It's positioned primarily for corporate training, sales enablement, product demos, and internal communications, which makes it a genuinely interesting tool for B2B founders who need to produce video content at scale without hiring a production team.

The honest bottom line on Synthesia. Worth it for teams making talking-head training, explainer and onboarding videos without cameras, actors or editing. Skip it once you need lots of video or cinematic, human-shot production — the minute caps and avatar style won't fit. Full review →

Founded in 2017 and now valued at over $2.1 billion after its 2023 Series D, Synthesia has moved well past "novelty AI tool" territory. It's used by companies like Zoom, Reuters, and Nike for internal training and communications. But the question every founder actually cares about is simple: what does it cost, and is it worth it? This guide breaks down the real pricing, the hidden constraints, and whether the ROI math actually works for a lean B2B team.

Key Features That Matter for B2B Teams

  • 230+ AI avatars across diverse ethnicities, ages, and professional styles, plus custom avatar creation on higher tiers where you can clone yourself or an employee into a reusable digital presenter.
  • 140+ languages and accents with AI-generated voiceovers, which is genuinely useful if you're localizing training content or product explainer videos for international markets without hiring voice actors per language.
  • Screen recording and template library for quickly building onboarding docs, SOPs, and sales enablement videos without starting from scratch every time.
  • Brand kit controls — logo, fonts, and color palettes — so videos stay on-brand across a distributed team producing content.
  • Team collaboration workspace with shared assets, brand templates, and permission controls, which matters once more than one person is producing content.
  • API access on enterprise plans for teams that want to programmatically generate video at scale — think personalized onboarding videos generated per customer or automated training refreshes triggered by content updates.
  • PowerPoint and document import that converts existing slide decks into narrated videos, which is a real time-saver if you already have training material sitting in slide format.

The feature set is genuinely strong for repetitive, structured video content — think onboarding, compliance training, and internal comms — but it is not a substitute for creative marketing video or anything requiring nuanced emotional delivery. The avatars are good, not perfect, and viewers can usually tell within the first ten seconds that they're watching an AI presenter.

Synthesia Pricing Breakdown (2024)

Synthesia restructured its pricing in 2024, moving away from a strict "minutes per month" model toward a credit-based system with plan tiers. Here's the real breakdown as of this writing:

  • Free plan: $0/month. Gives you 3 minutes of video generation total (not monthly — a one-time allowance), access to a limited avatar set, and Synthesia watermarking. This is a proof-of-concept tier, not something you can build a workflow on.
  • Starter plan: $18/month (billed annually) or $29/month billed monthly. Includes 120 credits/month, roughly translating to about 10 minutes of finished video, access to 50+ stock avatars, and 60+ languages. No custom avatar creation.
  • Creator plan: $64/month (billed annually) or $89/month billed monthly. This is where most solo founders and small teams land. It includes 360 credits/month (~30 minutes of video), access to the full avatar library, custom avatar creation for one personal avatar, brand kit tools, and priority rendering.
  • Enterprise plan: Custom pricing, typically starting around $1,000–$3,000/month depending on seat count and usage volume, based on publicly discussed customer figures and reseller quotes. Includes API access, SSO, dedicated account management, custom avatar production (studio-quality personal avatars), advanced security/compliance (SOC 2, GDPR data processing agreements), and volume-based credit packages negotiated per contract.

A few pricing realities founders should know before committing:

  • Credits, not raw minutes, are the real currency. A one-minute video with a single avatar and no screen recording costs fewer credits than a multi-scene video with scene transitions, multiple avatars, or embedded footage. Budget generously if you're planning complex video formats.
  • Custom avatars — the "clone yourself" feature — are gated behind Creator tier and above, and enterprise-grade personal avatars (studio-shot, higher fidelity) require a separate production fee often in the $1,000–$5,000 range depending on package.
  • Annual billing saves roughly 30-35% versus month-to-month across every tier, which matters if you're testing the tool — you'll want to commit to at least a quarter of usage before locking into annual pricing.
  • Overage credits, when you exceed your monthly allowance, are billed at a premium rate rather than a linear extension of your plan price, so heavy users often find it cheaper to upgrade tiers than to buy overage credit packs repeatedly.

Pros & Cons

Pros:

  • Dramatically faster video production — a 3-minute training video that would take a videographer 2-3 days (scripting, filming, editing) can be produced in under an hour.
  • No ongoing production costs once a script is written — no re-booking actors, studios, or editors for revisions.
  • Multi-language output from a single script eliminates the need for separate voiceover talent per market.
  • Consistent brand presenter across all content, which is valuable for companies scaling training or onboarding content across departments.
  • Enterprise security posture (SOC 2 Type II, GDPR compliance) is solid enough for regulated industries like fintech and healthcare adjacent B2B companies.

Cons:

  • AI avatars still read as artificial to most viewers — lip sync and micro-expressions have improved but aren't at "indistinguishable from human" level yet, which limits use in customer-facing marketing where authenticity matters more.
  • Credit-based pricing is confusing at first and makes it hard to predict costs precisely if your content mix varies month to month.
  • Custom avatar quality varies — self-service webcam-based avatars look noticeably worse than the studio-produced enterprise versions, creating a real quality gap between tiers.
  • No free trial beyond the very limited 3-minute lifetime allowance, making it hard to fully evaluate output quality before paying.
  • Rendering queue times can spike during high-demand periods on lower tiers, which is frustrating when you're on a content deadline.

ROI for B2B Founders: Real Numbers

The ROI case for Synthesia depends entirely on your content volume and what you're replacing. Here are three concrete scenarios:

Scenario 1 — Replacing a freelance videographer for onboarding content. A typical B2B SaaS company hiring a freelancer to produce 10 short onboarding/training videos per quarter might pay $300-$600 per video (scripting, filming, editing), totaling $3,000-$6,000 per quarter, or roughly $12,000-$24,000 annually. On Synthesia's Creator plan at $768/year (annual billing), producing the same volume of content — assuming each video is 2-3 minutes — falls comfortably within the 360 monthly credit allowance. That's a potential savings of $11,000-$23,000 per year, even accounting for the time a founder or ops person spends writing scripts and managing the tool.

Scenario 2 — Sales enablement video localization. A B2B company selling into 5 international markets (say, US, UK, Germany, France, and Japan) traditionally needs separate voiceover talent per language for product demo videos — typically $200-$400 per language per video. For 12 product update videos per year across 5 languages, that's $12,000-$24,000 annually in voiceover costs alone, not counting video editing. Synthesia's built-in multi-language voice generation, on the Creator or Enterprise plan, eliminates this cost almost entirely, since one script generates all language versions from the same base video.

Scenario 3 — Internal training at scale. A 150-person B2B company running quarterly compliance training might otherwise pay $5,000-$10,000 per training module produced by a corporate video agency (scripting, filming, professional narration, editing). At 4 modules per year, that's $20,000-$40,000 annually. On Synthesia's Enterprise plan (estimated $18,000-$36,000/year depending on seat count and volume), the company can produce significantly more content — including per-department customization and rapid updates when compliance rules change — for comparable or lower total cost, with the added benefit of instant re-recording when content needs updating (no re-booking a studio).

The pattern across all three scenarios: Synthesia's ROI is strongest when you have recurring, structured video needs — not one-off hero content. If you need one polished brand video per year, hiring a production company is still probably better value. If you need dozens of training, onboarding, or localized product videos per quarter, the math flips hard in Synthesia's favor.

Best Alternatives to Consider

Synthesia isn't the only AI avatar video platform, and depending on your use case, an alternative might fit better:

  • HeyGen — Often considered Synthesia's closest competitor, with arguably more natural-looking avatars and a lower entry price point (plans starting around $24/month for the Creator tier billed annually). HeyGen tends to be favored by marketers and content creators over pure enterprise training teams, and its avatar library, while smaller, is often cited as more expressive.
  • Colossyan — More explicitly positioned for corporate learning and development teams, with strong interactive video and branching scenario features (useful for compliance training with decision points). Pricing is competitive with Synthesia's Creator tier, starting around $27-31/month.
  • D-ID — A more developer-friendly, API-first platform, often chosen by companies wanting to build avatar video generation directly into their own products rather than using a standalone editor. Pricing is usage-based and can be more cost-effective for high-volume programmatic use cases.
  • Elai.io — A budget-friendly alternative aimed at smaller teams, with plans starting around $23/month, though avatar realism and language support lag behind Synthesia and HeyGen.
  • Traditional production (freelancer/agency) — Still the better choice for brand hero videos, testimonial content, or anything requiring authentic human emotion and storytelling nuance that AI avatars can't yet replicate convincingly.

If your primary need is polished, high-volume internal and training content with strong compliance and security features, Synthesia's enterprise tier remains the most mature option on the market. If you're a smaller team optimizing purely for cost or avatar naturalism, HeyGen or Colossyan are worth a serious side-by-side trial before committing.

Verdict

Synthesia earns its price tag for a specific type of B2B company: one with recurring, structured video needs — training, onboarding, internal comms, multi-language product explainers — where consistency and speed matter more than cinematic polish. For that use case, the Creator plan at roughly $64/month (annual billing) is a legitimately strong value proposition against freelance or agency alternatives that would cost 10-20x more for equivalent output.

Where Synthesia falls short is customer-facing marketing content that depends on emotional authenticity — a founder story video, a customer testimonial, a brand hero piece. AI avatars still can't fully replace a real human telling a real story, and trying to force that use case onto Synthesia will produce content that feels hollow to viewers.

The honest recommendation: if you're a B2B founder producing more than 5-10 internal or training videos per quarter, run the numbers from Scenario 1 or 3 above against your current production costs — the savings case is usually undeniable. If your video needs are occasional and marketing-facing, skip it and hire a freelancer instead.
