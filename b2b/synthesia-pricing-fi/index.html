Synthesia Pricing: The No-Fluff Breakdown B2B Founders Actually Need

If you've spent any time on LinkedIn in the past two years, you've seen AI avatars presenting product demos, onboarding videos, and training content that looks suspiciously polished for something built without a camera crew. Chances are, that content came from Synthesia — the AI video generation platform that has quietly become the default tool for B2B teams who need professional video content without hiring a production agency.

But here's the thing nobody tells you upfront: Synthesia's pricing page is deceptively simple until you actually try to scale usage across a team. Then the real cost structure — API access, seat limits, avatar customization tiers — starts to matter a lot. This guide breaks down exactly what you'll pay, what you get, and whether it makes financial sense for your business.

Overview: What Synthesia Actually Does

Synthesia is an AI video generation platform that turns text scripts into videos featuring realistic AI avatars speaking in over 140 languages. Founded in 2017 and based in London, the company has raised over $150M in funding and serves more than 55,000 companies, including large enterprises like Zoom, Reddit, and Xerox.

The core value proposition is simple: instead of scheduling a video shoot, hiring a presenter, renting equipment, and paying for editing, you type a script, pick an AI avatar (or clone your own), and get a finished video in under 15 minutes. For B2B companies producing training material, product explainers, sales enablement videos, or localized marketing content at scale, this collapses a process that used to take days into an afternoon task.

This isn't a toy. Companies use Synthesia for compliance training across 40+ languages, onboarding videos for distributed sales teams, and even personalized outreach videos at scale. The question isn't whether the tool works — it clearly does — it's whether the pricing model fits your actual usage pattern.

Key Features That Justify the Price Tag

  • 230+ AI avatars across diverse demographics, plus custom avatar creation from your own footage (available on higher tiers) — this matters enormously if brand consistency is a requirement, not a nice-to-have.
  • 140+ languages and accents, which is the single biggest cost-saver for companies doing international training or localized marketing. Dubbing a 10-minute training video into 15 languages with human voice actors can run $3,000-$8,000; Synthesia does it in minutes for a fraction of that.
  • Screen recording and template library with 60+ pre-built templates for common B2B use cases like onboarding, sales pitches, and how-to guides.
  • API access for programmatic video generation — this is where things get interesting for founders building products that need auto-generated video at scale (think: personalized video emails, dynamic training content).
  • Custom avatar builder ("Personal Avatar" and "Expressive Avatar") that lets you or an employee become a repeatable AI presenter, useful for founders who want to "appear" in every piece of content without being physically present.
  • PowerPoint and script-to-video import, which reduces the barrier for non-technical teams to produce content without learning a new workflow.
  • Brand kit and team collaboration tools on higher tiers, allowing shared asset libraries and consistent brand voice across a growing team.

The feature set is genuinely broad, but not every feature is available at every price point — and that's exactly where founders get caught off guard.

Pricing Breakdown: What You'll Actually Pay

Synthesia's pricing has shifted over time (it moved away from a strict "minutes of video per month" cap toward a credit-based system), so treat the numbers below as directionally accurate and always confirm current rates on their site before budgeting.

  • Free Plan — $0/month: Limited to roughly 3 minutes of video per month, watermark included, access to a small subset of stock avatars. Fine for testing the tool, useless for actual business production.
  • Starter Plan — approximately $18-29/month (billed annually): Roughly 120 minutes of video per year, access to 9 avatars, no watermark, single seat. This tier suits a solo founder producing occasional explainer videos, but it will feel restrictive fast if you're producing weekly content.
  • Creator Plan — approximately $64-89/month (billed annually): Around 360 minutes/year, access to more avatars (60+), custom avatar upload option, and higher resolution export. This is the realistic entry point for a B2B startup that wants to run a consistent content cadence — say, 2-4 videos per week for sales enablement or customer education.
  • Enterprise Plan — custom pricing, typically starting around $1,000-$3,000+/month depending on seats and volume: Unlimited or high-volume video minutes, full API access, custom avatar creation, SSO, dedicated account management, and SLA guarantees. This is where most scaling B2B companies actually land once they move past pilot usage.

A critical detail founders miss: minutes of video does not mean minutes of finished footage you can use freely. Each script draft, re-render, and language variant consumes minutes from your allowance. If you're localizing one 5-minute training video into 10 languages, that's 50 minutes of your quota gone in a single project — not 5.

Additional costs to budget for:

  • Custom avatar creation is often a one-time setup fee separate from the subscription, sometimes $1,000+ depending on the complexity and whether it's an on-demand enterprise build.
  • API usage on Enterprise plans is typically priced per generated video or per minute consumed programmatically, and can scale unpredictably if you're building a product feature around it — test volume assumptions before committing contractually.
  • Additional seats beyond the base plan allowance are billed incrementally, which matters if you have a marketing team of 5+ people who all need direct access rather than funneling requests through one person.

Pros & Cons

Pros:

  • Genuinely fast production cycle — a finished video in 10-15 minutes versus days for traditional video production.
  • Multilingual output is best-in-class for the price; no other tool matches the combination of quality and language breadth at this cost.
  • No video editing skills required — anyone on a marketing or sales team can produce content independently.
  • Enterprise-grade security and compliance certifications (SOC 2, GDPR compliance), which matters if you're selling into regulated industries.
  • Consistent brand presenter without scheduling conflicts, sick days, or turnover — the avatar is always available.

Cons:

  • AI avatars still have a "not quite human" quality in longer-form content — great for 2-3 minute explainers, less convincing for 15-minute deep dives where viewers notice the uncanny valley.
  • The credit/minutes system is genuinely confusing to budget against until you've run a few projects and understand your actual burn rate.
  • Custom avatar creation and Enterprise features push pricing well beyond what many early-stage startups can justify.
  • Limited emotional range and gesture variety compared to a real human presenter — this shows up in feedback from viewers on more casual or persuasive content types.
  • API pricing at scale can become unpredictable if you're building a product feature that generates video on-demand for end users.

ROI for B2B Founders: Does the Math Work?

Let's run actual numbers rather than vague claims about "saving time."

Scenario 1: Sales enablement content. A B2B SaaS company with a 10-person sales team needs monthly product update videos plus onboarding content for new reps. Traditional production (freelance videographer, editor, voice talent) for 4 videos/month at professional quality typically runs $800-$1,500 per video, or $3,200-$6,000/month. On Synthesia's Creator plan (~$89/month) or a mid-tier Enterprise contract (~$1,500/month for higher volume), the company produces the same or greater volume at 60-95% lower cost, with the added benefit of same-day turnaround instead of a 1-2 week production cycle.

Scenario 2: Multilingual training compliance. A company with offices in 8 countries needs annual compliance training refreshed and translated. Human dubbing and re-recording across 8 languages for a 20-minute training module typically costs $15,000-$25,000 when you factor in translation, voice talent, and studio time. Using Synthesia's multilingual avatar dubbing, the same output costs roughly $2,000-$4,000 in subscription and setup fees — an estimated 80-85% cost reduction, plus the ability to update content instantly when regulations change rather than re-booking studio time.

Scenario 3: Solo founder building a course or SaaS demo library. A founder producing a 12-video onboarding course would pay a freelance video producer roughly $300-$600 per finished video for scripting, filming, and editing — $3,600-$7,200 total. On Synthesia's Creator plan, the same output costs under $100/month in subscription fees, assuming the founder writes their own scripts (which most technical founders can do faster than they can schedule and coordinate a shoot).

The pattern across all three scenarios: Synthesia's ROI is strongest when you have repeatable, scriptable video needs — training, onboarding, product updates, localized content. It's weakest when your video needs are one-off, highly emotional (testimonials, brand storytelling), or require genuine human nuance that audiences will notice is missing.

Best Alternatives to Consider

Synthesia isn't the only option, and depending on your specific use case, a competitor might fit better.

  • HeyGen — Often cheaper at comparable tiers (~$24-$120/month range) with strong avatar quality and faster rendering. A solid choice if budget is tighter and you don't need Synthesia's enterprise compliance certifications.
  • Colossyan — Positioned specifically for corporate training and L&D teams, with strong interactive video features (branching scenarios, quizzes embedded in video). Worth evaluating if your primary use case is employee training rather than external marketing.
  • D-ID — More developer-friendly with granular API pricing, better suited to founders building a product feature around avatar video generation rather than producing marketing content directly.
  • Elai.io — A budget-conscious alternative with plans starting lower than Synthesia's, reasonable avatar quality, but a smaller language library and less polished enterprise features.
  • Traditional freelance video production (Fiverr, Upwork, or agency retainers) — Still the better choice for high-stakes brand videos, testimonials, or anything requiring genuine emotional connection with viewers. Don't force AI avatars into use cases where authenticity is the entire point.

The honest comparison point: Synthesia charges a premium over HeyGen and Elai.io, but that premium buys you enterprise security compliance, a broader avatar and language library, and more polished output quality. If you're a two-person startup testing video content for the first time, starting cheaper with HeyGen or Elai.io and upgrading later is a reasonable strategy.

Verdict: Who Should Actually Pay for Synthesia

Synthesia earns its price tag for B2B companies with recurring, scriptable video needs at meaningful volume — sales teams producing regular update videos, HR and L&D teams running multilingual training, or product teams maintaining a library of demo and onboarding content that needs frequent updates. In these cases, the cost comparison against traditional production isn't close; Synthesia wins on speed, cost, and scalability every time.

It's a weaker fit for companies whose video needs are occasional, emotionally driven (founder story videos, customer testimonials), or so low-volume that the subscription cost isn't justified by usage. If you're producing one video a quarter, a freelancer or even your own smartphone will outperform a $700+/year subscription on a cost-per-video basis.

The pricing structure itself is reasonable but not simple — budget time to actually model your minutes/credits usage against a real content calendar before committing to an annual plan, and negotiate Enterprise pricing directly rather than accepting the first quote if you're bringing meaningful volume to the table.

If your B2B team produces recurring video content and you're tired of production bottlenecks eating into your go-to-market speed, Synthesia is worth a serious trial. Start with the free plan to test avatar quality and script workflow, then map your actual usage before upgrading. You can create your account and test the platform directly through the official Synthesia signup page — run the numbers against your own content calendar before locking into an annual contract, and you'll know within your first two or three produced videos whether the ROI case holds for your specific business.
