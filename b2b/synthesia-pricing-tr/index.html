Synthesia Pricing: The Complete Guide for Technical Founders

If you've spent any time on LinkedIn or YouTube in the past two years, you've seen Synthesia videos without realizing it — those polished AI avatars delivering product demos, onboarding tutorials, and sales pitches in a dozen languages. What used to require a camera crew, a studio, and a week of editing now takes an afternoon and a text prompt. But before you commit budget to it, you need to know exactly what you're paying for, where the hidden costs live, and whether it actually makes financial sense for a B2B team. This guide breaks down Synthesia's pricing structure line by line, compares it honestly against alternatives, and shows real ROI math so you can make a decision based on numbers, not marketing copy.

The honest bottom line on Synthesia. Worth it for teams making talking-head training, explainer and onboarding videos without cameras, actors or editing. Skip it once you need lots of video or cinematic, human-shot production. Full review →

Overview: What Synthesia Actually Is

Synthesia is an AI video generation platform that turns written scripts into videos featuring realistic AI avatars speaking in over 140 languages. It's not a video editor with AI features bolted on — it's built from the ground up around the idea that you type a script, pick or create an avatar, and get a finished, lip-synced video in minutes. The company has raised over $150 million from investors including Kleiner Perkins and Nvidia's venture arm, and it's used heavily by enterprise L&D teams, sales enablement functions, and marketing departments that need to produce video at a volume traditional production simply can't match.

For B2B founders specifically, the appeal is obvious: you can produce localized product walkthroughs, customer onboarding sequences, investor updates, and internal training content without hiring a videographer or waiting on an agency's turnaround time. The question isn't whether the tech works — it does, convincingly — it's whether the pricing model fits your actual usage pattern.

Key Features

  • 230+ stock AI avatars spanning different ethnicities, ages, and speaking styles, plus the ability to create a custom avatar of yourself or an employee on higher-tier plans.
  • Text-to-speech in 140+ languages with natural intonation, meaning a single script can be localized into a dozen markets without hiring voice actors.
  • Screen recording and template library for turning existing slide decks or Loom-style recordings into polished avatar-led videos.
  • Brand kits that let teams lock in fonts, colors, and logos so every video stays on-brand without manual design work per video.
  • API access (on Enterprise plans) for teams that want to programmatically generate videos at scale — useful if you're building personalized video into a SaaS onboarding flow.
  • Collaboration tools including shared workspaces, script review, and approval workflows for teams producing content at volume.
  • Interactive video features like clickable elements and branching paths for training and product demo use cases.

The feature set is genuinely deep for a tool in this category, but most of the differentiation between plans isn't features — it's video minutes and seats, which is where the real budgeting decisions happen.

Pricing Breakdown

Synthesia uses a tiered subscription model based primarily on video minutes generated per month, number of seats, and feature access. Here's how the plans typically shake out (pricing is billed annually for the best rate; monthly billing costs more):

  • Starter — around $29/month (billed annually): This tier gets you roughly 120 video minutes per year, access to the stock avatar library, and basic editing tools. It's aimed at solo founders or small teams testing the waters — think one explainer video a month, not a full content engine.
  • Creator — around $89/month (billed annually): This is the plan most growing B2B teams actually land on. It typically includes more monthly minutes, access to a broader set of avatars, higher video resolution, and priority rendering. If you're producing weekly sales or marketing videos, this is realistically your floor.
  • Enterprise — custom pricing, typically starting in the $1,000–$3,000+/month range depending on seat count and usage: This unlocks custom avatars (including a digital twin of an actual employee or founder), API access, SSO, dedicated account management, and usage well beyond what self-serve plans allow. Enterprise pricing is negotiated, and Synthesia's sales team will ask about your intended use case, seat count, and expected video volume before quoting.

A few things founders consistently get wrong when budgeting for Synthesia:

  • Minutes, not videos, are the real constraint. A 90-second onboarding video and a 5-minute training module consume very different amounts of your monthly allowance. If your team produces long-form content (product deep dives, multi-module training), you'll burn through the Creator tier's allowance faster than expected.
  • Custom avatars are an Enterprise-tier feature, and Synthesia typically charges an additional setup fee (historically in the $1,000–$10,000 range depending on complexity) to create a photorealistic avatar of a real person. If your go-to-market plan hinges on "founder-led video at scale," budget for this separately.
  • Annual billing saves roughly 20–40% compared to month-to-month, but it also locks you into a usage estimate you might not hit in month one. Early-stage teams often overestimate how much video content they'll actually ship.
  • Seats matter more than most people assume. If your marketing, sales, and CS teams all want independent access rather than routing requests through one person, seat costs on Enterprise plans stack up quickly.

Pros & Cons

Pros:

  • Production speed is genuinely transformative — a video that would take a production agency 5-10 business days can be live in under an hour.
  • Localization into 140+ languages at essentially zero marginal cost is a real competitive advantage for B2B teams selling internationally.
  • Avatar quality is noticeably better than most competitors, with realistic lip-sync and natural pacing that doesn't trigger the "uncanny valley" reaction as strongly as cheaper tools.
  • No production overhead: no camera, no studio, no editor, no re-shoots when the script changes.
  • Brand consistency is easier to enforce across a growing content library since every video runs through the same brand kit.

Cons:

  • Pricing scales fast once you need custom avatars, API access, or higher minute allowances — self-serve pricing looks cheap until you actually need Enterprise features.
  • Video minutes as a billing unit is an awkward constraint for teams that don't yet know their content cadence; it's easy to either overpay for unused capacity or hit a wall mid-month.
  • Avatars, while impressive, still read as "AI-generated" to a trained eye — this matters for brand-sensitive use cases like investor communications or high-touch sales.
  • Enterprise pricing isn't published, which means every negotiation starts from a position of asymmetric information — you don't know what a "fair" quote looks like until you've gotten several.
  • Limited customization on avatar wardrobe, background, and gestures on lower tiers compared to what's possible with real video production.

ROI for B2B Founders

The financial case for Synthesia isn't abstract — it's a direct substitution for production costs you're either already paying or avoiding entirely by not producing video at all. Here's how the math typically works out:

Scenario 1: Replacing freelance video production. A B2B SaaS company producing one product demo video per month via a freelance videographer typically pays $800–$2,500 per video depending on complexity (scripting, filming, editing, revisions). At 12 videos a year, that's $9,600–$30,000 annually. The Creator plan at roughly $89/month ($1,068/year) covers a comparable monthly cadence for a fraction of the cost — even accounting for the learning curve and internal time spent on scripting.

Scenario 2: Localizing onboarding content for international expansion. Say your customer onboarding video is 8 minutes long, and you're expanding into five new markets (Spanish, German, French, Portuguese, Japanese). Hiring voice talent and re-editing for each language typically runs $1,500–$4,000 per language, or $7,500–$20,000 total. With Synthesia, the same script can be regenerated in each language for effectively the cost of your existing subscription plus render time. That's a five-figure savings realized in a single onboarding sequence.

Scenario 3: Sales enablement at scale. If your sales team currently spends time recording personalized Loom videos for prospects (call it 15 minutes per rep per prospect, across a team of 5 reps sending 20 personalized videos a week), that's roughly 25 hours/week of rep time — time not spent selling. Templated Synthesia videos with light personalization can cut that production time by 70-80%, freeing up the equivalent of half an FTE's worth of selling time per week.

The honest caveat: ROI collapses if your team doesn't have a clear, recurring use case for video. Buying Synthesia "because AI video is the future" without a concrete content calendar is how teams end up paying for unused minutes. The ROI math above only works if you're already producing video content (or clearly should be) and are replacing a more expensive, slower process — not if you're speculatively adding video to your marketing mix.

Best Alternatives

Synthesia isn't the only AI avatar video tool, and depending on your use case, a competitor might fit better:

  • HeyGen — Often the closest direct competitor, with similarly strong avatar quality and generally more aggressive pricing on lower tiers. Better suited for teams that want faster iteration and don't need Synthesia's enterprise compliance features (SOC 2, SSO). A good fit for solo founders and small marketing teams optimizing for cost.
  • Colossyan — Positions itself more directly at corporate training and L&D use cases, with stronger interactive video and quiz-branching features baked in at lower price points than Synthesia's Enterprise tier. Worth a look if your primary use case is internal training rather than external marketing.
  • D-ID — More developer-friendly with a strong API-first approach, making it a better fit if you want to build AI avatar generation directly into your own product rather than using a standalone content tool. Pricing is more usage-based (per-minute API calls), which suits unpredictable or high-volume programmatic use.
  • Elai.io — A budget-friendlier option with a smaller avatar library and less polish, but meaningfully cheaper for teams that need "good enough" video at high volume rather than premium production value.
  • Traditional production (freelancers/agencies) — Still the right call for high-stakes brand videos (funding announcements, major campaign launches) where AI avatar limitations — subtle stiffness, imperfect gesture timing — would undermine credibility. Don't use AI avatars for everything just because you can.

The honest comparison point: Synthesia wins on avatar realism and enterprise trust signals (compliance, support, API maturity at the top tier). HeyGen and Elai win on price-to-value for smaller teams. D-ID wins if you're building avatar generation into your own product rather than consuming it as a content tool.

Verdict

Synthesia earns its premium positioning through avatar quality and language coverage that's genuinely difficult to match, and for B2B teams with a recurring need for demo, training, or localized content, the ROI math is straightforward and favorable — you're trading a few hundred dollars a month for what would otherwise cost thousands in freelance production or localization fees. Where it gets expensive is the jump to Enterprise: custom avatars and API access are the features most technical founders actually want, and that's exactly where pricing becomes opaque and negotiation-dependent.

The practical recommendation: start on the Creator plan, ship a real content calendar for 60-90 days, and track your actual minute usage before deciding whether Enterprise pricing (and the custom avatar setup fee that comes with it) is justified by your volume. Don't buy the top tier speculatively — buy it once you've proven the use case on a cheaper plan and hit its ceiling.

If you're ready to test whether AI-generated video fits your content and sales workflow, sign up for Synthesia here and start on a lower tier to validate your usage pattern before committing to an annual Enterprise contract.
