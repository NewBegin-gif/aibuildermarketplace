What Synthesia does

Create studio-quality AI videos in minutes. 150+ realistic avatars, 120+ languages, no camera or studio needed. Trusted by half the Fortune 100.

Pricing (2026)

Basic is free (1,200 credits/mo, about 10 video-minutes/month, 25 assets); Starter $18/mo billed yearly ($29 monthly; 120 video-minutes/year), Creator $64/mo billed yearly ($89 monthly; 360 minutes/year), plus custom Enterprise (unlimited minutes). Priced by video-minute credits (re-verified 2026-07-19). Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The video-minute caps are tight — 10 min/mo on Starter goes fast when you iterate, and re-generating edits burns minutes. Fantastic for avatar-led training and explainers without a studio, but not for high-volume or cinematic production.

What buyers report about Synthesia

Recurring themes from G2 and practitioner reviews, read in August 2026. We have not run Synthesia ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Avatars and languages at a quality that makes internal training video practical without a studio — that is the job and it does it.
  • • New annual rates from $18/month, and the free plan gives full AI Playground access.
What they complain about
  • • The minute allowance is the trap. Basic is 36 minutes a year, Starter 120. A single five-minute training module with dubbing and multiple scenes eats a large share of a month's budget, and corporate buyers report hitting the ceiling in their first session.
  • • The plan most people actually need is Creator at $64/month or Enterprise on a quote — not the tier the pricing page leads with.
  • • Reviewers report being misled about credits rolling over, and confusion between annual and monthly billing.
  • • The avatars lack warmth. For anything emotional or creative, reviewers say it shows.

When the paid plan is worth it

The free plan is a demo, not a tier. Read on their own pricing page on 2 August 2026: Basic is $0 with no card, 10 minutes of video a month, one editor, 9 stock avatars — and no download. Videos keep Synthesia branding and stay on their platform. If you need a file to hand to a client, you are paying. Starter is $29 a month, or $18 a month billed yearly, and that is where downloads, the AI Video Assistant, AI Dubbing and logo removal start. It gives 1 editor plus 3 guests, 125+ stock avatars, 3 personal avatars, and 10 minutes a month monthly or 120 minutes a year on annual. Creator is $89 a month, or $64 annual, for 30 minutes a month (360 a year), 5 personal avatars, 180+ stock avatars, API access, multiple avatars per scene and interactive video. The step is worth it for volume, not features: at $64 versus $18 you are paying roughly 3.5× for 3× the minutes. Count the minutes you will actually publish per month — most people overestimate, and unused annual minutes are the most common way to overpay here.

How to actually use Synthesia

Decide monthly versus annual before anything else, because on Synthesia the two are not the same product. Annual Starter gives 120 minutes for the year; monthly Starter gives 10 minutes each month and they do not stack. Bursty work — a product launch, an onboarding library — suits annual. Steady weekly output suits monthly. Make your first video on the free plan before you pay, but know what you are testing: the free tier has 9 avatars against 125+ on Starter, and you cannot download the result. Use it to judge whether the avatar and voice quality clears your bar, not to judge the product. Personal avatars are the feature people buy this for and the one with a real limit: 3 on Starter, 5 on Creator, unlimited only on Enterprise. Frequently asked questions

Does Synthesia replace my current setup or sit on top of it?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.

Who should not use Synthesia?

Skip it if you need lots of video or cinematic, human-shot production — the minute caps and avatar style won't fit. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
