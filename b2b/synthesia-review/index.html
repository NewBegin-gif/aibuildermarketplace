What buyers report about Synthesia

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Synthesia ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Avatars and languages at a quality that makes internal training video practical without a studio — that is the job and it does it.
  • • New annual rates from $18/month, and the free plan gives full AI Playground access.
What they complain about
  • • The minute allowance is the trap. Basic is 36 minutes a year, Starter 120. A single five-minute training module with dubbing and multiple scenes eats a large share of a month's budget, and corporate buyers report hitting the ceiling in their first session.
  • • The plan most people actually need is Creator at $64/month or Enterprise on a quote — not the tier the pricing page leads with.
  • • Reviewers report being misled about credits rolling over, and confusion between annual and monthly billing.
  • • The avatars lack warmth. For anything emotional or creative, reviewers say it shows.
  • • SourceForge holds a single Synthesia review and it is from April 2023, so treat it as one dated voice rather than a trend. Two of its points are still worth checking against your own use: a cap on video length (the reviewer puts it at 10 minutes per video) and limited control over the speed, pitch and emotion of the generated voice.

When the paid plan is worth it

Free videos keep Synthesia branding and stay on their platform. If you need a file to hand to a client, you are paying. Starter is €19 a month, or €12 a month billed yearly when read from a Dutch address, and that is where downloads, the AI Video Assistant, AI Dubbing and logo removal start. It gives 1 editor plus 3 guests and 14,500 credits a year, about 120 minutes of video.

Creator at €79 a month, or €49 billed yearly, roughly triples that to 44,000 credits and about 360 minutes, and adds 5 personal avatars, API access, branded video pages, interactive videos and 1 editor plus 5 guests. The step to take seriously is not the money but the minutes: at 120 minutes a year Starter is about ten minutes a month, so a team publishing weekly will cross into Creator on volume long before it needs the features. Work out your minutes first and let that pick the plan.

How to actually use Synthesia

Decide monthly versus annual before anything else, because on Synthesia the two are not the same product. Annual Starter gives 120 minutes for the year; monthly Starter gives 10 minutes each month and they do not stack. Bursty work — a product launch, an onboarding library — suits annual. Steady weekly output suits monthly. Make your first video on the free plan before you pay, but know what you are testing: the free tier has 9 avatars against 125+ on Starter, and you cannot download the result.

Use it to judge whether the avatar and voice quality clears your bar, not to judge the product. Personal avatars are the feature people buy this for and the one with a real limit: 3 on Starter, 5 on Creator, unlimited only on Enterprise. If you plan to put several colleagues on camera, count heads before you pick the tier.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Synthesia cost?

Read on 17 August 2026: Basic free, Starter $29 a month or $18 a month billed yearly, Creator $89 or $64 yearly, Enterprise on quote. Be aware the FAQ block on the same page quotes annual totals of $264 and $804, which come out at $22 and $67 a month rather than $18 and $64. Take the figure from your checkout, not from either block.

Which plan do I actually need?

Count minutes, not features. The plans are metered in video-minute credits and reviewers consistently report the allowance, not the price, as what forces an upgrade — a single five-minute module reshot a few times with different voices eats a surprising share of a year's allowance. Work out your annual minutes first, then read across to the plan.

What are the alternatives worth looking at?

That is the most-searched thing about Synthesia on our pages, and it depends which half of the job you need. If you need a presenter on screen, the comparison is with other avatar-video tools. If you already have visuals and only need the voice, a text-to-speech tool like Murf covers that half for a fraction of the price — people search for exactly that comparison. Where reviewers say Synthesia is hard to replace is multilingual training video at consistent quality.

What should I weigh before buying Operations & Workflow software?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.
