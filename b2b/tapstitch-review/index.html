What Tapstitch does

Fashion-first print-on-demand for brand-builders: premium heavyweight blanks (streetwear tees, oversized hoodies), bigger print areas and made-to-order apparel with no inventory — integrates with Shopify.

The honest knock

Printify is cheaper, higher-margin and has a far bigger catalogue — if you're optimising for profit per sale or product breadth, that's the real trade-off. Tapstitch earns its place when garment quality and brand feel matter more than squeezing the last point of margin.

What you actually pay, per item

There is no plan to upgrade to and no threshold to cross: Tapstitch charges no subscription and no monthly fee at all. You pay per item — production plus shipping — which changes the whole shape of the decision. A subscription punishes you for a slow month; a per-item model does not, so holding a dormant Tapstitch store through a quiet quarter costs nothing but the time. For anyone testing a design or running seasonal drops, that is a genuine structural advantage over platforms with a monthly floor. What replaces the tier question is margin per sale, and that is where the real trade-off sits. Our monthly captures of the catalogue put blank item prices roughly between $2.99 and $19.99 depending on the garment, with the premium heavyweight stock — around 250 GSM tees and 350-380 GSM hoodies — landing at roughly $35-50 for a finished tee at the retail end. Printify's lower product and shipping costs mean noticeably higher profit per sale on comparable items, and a far bigger catalogue besides. So price the finished garment against YOUR retail price, not against another platform's platform fee, because there is no platform fee on either side of that comparison. The cost that does not appear on any price list is time. The vendor's shipping page puts print-on-demand production at 1-6 working days and international delivery at 5-15 working days, and Tapstitch defaults new stores to International Fulfillment — cheaper per item, slower to arrive — unless you change it under Account, then Store, then Settings, then Order Settings. On a per-item model, a refund driven by delivery time costs you the full production cost, so that default is worth a deliberate decision rather than an inherited one. Because there is no minimum order quantity, the sensible way to settle all of this is a single sample order before you publish anything. One garment tells you what the GSM claim actually feels like and how long it really took to arrive — for the price of one blank rather than the price of a month.

How to actually use Tapstitch

The fastest route to something real is a free account plus one sample order. After signing up from the Get Started button and verifying the email address, the documented sequence runs Catalog, then the in-browser Designer, then Push to Store, where a platform and the product title and price are filled in before publishing. Tapstitch documents no minimum order quantity, so a single garment can be ordered first to judge fabric and print. Budget genuine waiting time: the vendor's shipping page puts print-on-demand production at 1-6 working days and international delivery at 5-15 working days. Two settings cause most of the friction. Fulfillment preferences sit under Account, then Store, then Settings, then the Order Settings tab; left untouched, Tapstitch defaults to International Fulfillment, which is cheaper but slower than the US option. Published products also cannot be edited, so changed artwork means republishing as a new product and hiding the old listing. Store orders that never appear are pulled in manually with New Orders under My Orders. Frequently asked questions

What does Tapstitch cost to use?

Nothing as a subscription — there is no monthly fee. You pay per item produced plus shipping, with catalogue prices in our readings running from about $2.99 to $19.99 depending on the blank. Your real cost is the finished garment, which for their heavyweight tees lands nearer $35-50 at retail grade.

How does it compare with Printify on margin?

Printify generally wins on margin, and that is the honest summary: lower product and shipping costs mean more profit per sale. What Tapstitch offers instead is the blank itself — around 250 GSM tees and 350-380 GSM hoodies rather than lightweight stock. If your brand competes on garment quality the trade may be worth it; if it competes on price it is not.

Is there any fixed cost at all?

Not from Tapstitch as a platform fee. Your fixed costs are elsewhere — your storefront, your marketing and any sampling you do before launch. Order samples of the specific blanks before committing, since the whole argument for this supplier is the physical product.

Is Tapstitch worth it for a small team?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Small teams get the most out of this category when one clear problem is already costing real hours or revenue; buying ahead of that just adds cost and another login. Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.

Who should not use Tapstitch?

Skip it if you want the cheapest option or the widest catalogue — for pure margin and product breadth, traditional POD like Printify wins. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
