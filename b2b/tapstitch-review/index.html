What Tapstitch does

Fashion-first print-on-demand for brand-builders: premium heavyweight blanks (streetwear tees, oversized hoodies), bigger print areas and made-to-order apparel with no inventory — integrates with Shopify.

Pricing (2026)

No monthly fee — you pay per item (production + shipping). The honest catch is margin: Printify's lower product and shipping costs mean noticeably higher profit per sale, while Tapstitch's premium heavyweight blanks (~250 GSM tees, 350–380 GSM hoodies) sit at retail-grade (~$35–50 a tee). Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

Correction after re-checking: No subscription tier: Tapstitch states the platform is free to use and that you pay only once an order comes in, so the real cost is garment plus customization plus shipping, with shipping calculated at checkout. Catalogue base costs are far below finished retail: the 240 gsm Essential Sorona Boxy T-Shirt lists at $9.99 per item on international fulfillment, a women's Essential Standard Shoulder T-Shirt starts from $6.99, and heavyweight cotton hoodies start from $19.99. The recurring bill most sellers pay is the storefront rather than Tapstitch: its own Shopify FAQ notes Shopify plans start at 29 USD per month. (re-verified 2026-08-04)

The honest knock

Printify is cheaper, higher-margin and has a far bigger catalogue — if you're optimising for profit per sale or product breadth, that's the real trade-off. Tapstitch earns its place when garment quality and brand feel matter more than squeezing the last point of margin.

What buyers report about Tapstitch

Recurring themes from the Shopify App Store, Trustpilot and print-on-demand comparisons, read in August 2026. We have not run Tapstitch ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • The garments are the point — 250-400+ GSM blanks, and reviewers consistently use the word retail-quality about fabric weight and print. 4.7 across 80+ Shopify App Store reviews.
  • • No subscription, no platform fee and no minimum order. You pay cost of goods, which is why sellers can price at $35-50 a tee.
What they complain about
  • • Sizing runs about one size small against standard US sizing. Multiple reviewers report ordering XL and receiving what felt like L. Put that on your product page or you will eat the returns.
  • • Misprint resolution is reported taking a month, and orders cancelled without explanation.
  • • International shipping is slow, and the catalogue is apparel only.

How to actually use Tapstitch

The fastest route to something real is a free account plus one sample order. After signing up from the Get Started button and verifying the email address, the documented sequence runs Catalog, then the in-browser Designer, then Push to Store, where a platform and the product title and price are filled in before publishing. Tapstitch documents no minimum order quantity, so a single garment can be ordered first to judge fabric and print. Budget genuine waiting time: the vendor's shipping page puts print-on-demand production at 1-6 working days and international delivery at 5-15 working days. Two settings cause most of the friction. Fulfillment preferences sit under Account, then Store, then Settings, then the Order Settings tab; left untouched, Tapstitch defaults to International Fulfillment, which is cheaper but slower than the US option. Published products also cannot be edited, so changed artwork means republishing as a new product and hiding the old listing. Store orders that never appear are pulled in manually with New Orders under My Orders. Distilled from Tapstitch help centre articles, shipping policy, catalogue product pages and Shopify integration page, read 4 August 2026.

Frequently asked questions

Is Tapstitch worth it for a small team?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Small teams get the most out of this category when one clear problem is already costing real hours or revenue; buying ahead of that just adds cost and another login. Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.

Who should not use Tapstitch?

Skip it if you want the cheapest option or the widest catalogue — for pure margin and product breadth, traditional POD like Printify wins. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
