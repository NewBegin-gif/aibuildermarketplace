Do the free TIN matching before you file

The structure matters more than any single figure here, because the figures are the part we cannot fully verify. Filing is pay-as-you-go per form and the rate falls as your count rises — our dated reading puts it as low as about $0.68 a form at high volume. There is a free Essential account for creating payers and recipients and running TIN matching, and an eFile Plus subscription around $249 a year for teams that file often. Those bands are not published in a form our capture can read, so verify the current per-form tiers on their own page before you budget a season (re-verified 2026-08-17, with that caveat).

What follows from the structure is that there is no plan decision, only a volume one. Because price per form falls with count, your effective cost depends entirely on how many forms you file — and the eFile Plus subscription is worth it only where the per-form savings it unlocks exceed $249 a year. At the high-volume rate, $249 is about 366 forms' worth of filing outright, which gives you the order of magnitude: this is a subscription for a payroll bureau or a bookkeeping practice, not for a company sending out thirty 1099s in January.

The sequencing advice is where the real money is, and it costs nothing. TIN matching is free on the Essential account. A mismatched TIN is not a small problem — it produces a B-notice from the IRS and a correction, and corrections are a billable event here, on top of the original filing you have already paid for. So run every recipient through the free TIN match before you file anything. It is the single highest-return five minutes in this product, and it is available without spending a cent.

Then tally the meters rather than the headline. TIN matching, W-9 requests, state filing and corrections can each carry their own charge, and state filing is the one that multiplies: a business with contractors in six states is filing six times over, not once. Add those up before you compare the per-form rate to anything.

And be clear what this is. It is a filing tool, not a bookkeeping system, and it assumes your payee data is already clean. If your contractor records live in three spreadsheets with inconsistent names, the work that makes January painless happens before you open this at all.

How to actually use Tax1099

Open the free Essential account first and run TIN matching on every recipient before you file a single form. It costs nothing there, and a mismatched TIN produces an IRS B-notice and a correction — and corrections are billable, on top of the filing you already paid for. This is the cheapest step in the whole process and the one most often skipped.

Clean the payee data while you are in there. This is a filing tool, not a bookkeeping system: it takes what you give it. Names that disagree with the W-9, missing addresses and stale entity types are the things that turn a routine season into a fortnight of corrections.

Then tally the whole bill rather than the per-form rate. TIN matching, W-9 requests, state filing and corrections can each carry a charge, and state filing multiplies with the number of states your contractors sit in. Check the current per-form volume bands on their own page too — the rate falls with count and our reading of those bands is dated, so confirm before you budget a season.

Best for: businesses and accountants who need to e-file 1099s, W-2s and related forms accurately and cheaply, especially seasonal or higher-volume filers.
Who should skip it: if you only file one or two forms a year (the IRS's own free options may suffice) or you want full bookkeeping rather than just the filing step.

Frequently asked questions

What does Tax1099 cost per form?

It is volume-banded rather than flat: our dated reading puts the high-volume rate near $0.68 a form, with the price per form higher at low counts. There is a free Essential account for setting up payers and recipients and running TIN matching, and an eFile Plus subscription around $249 a year. Verify the current bands before a filing season — we could not read them from the page on 17 August 2026.

Do I need the subscription?

Only if you file often. eFile Plus is aimed at teams filing regularly through the year rather than at a business doing one January run. For a single season, pay-as-you-go per form is usually the cheaper structure — work it out on your actual form count.

What is free?

The Essential account: creating payers and recipients and running TIN matching cost nothing, which matters because TIN mismatches are what generate IRS notices later. Doing the matching before you file is the free part worth using even if you file elsewhere.

Is Tax1099 safe to run my finances through?

The bar here is higher than features. Check who actually holds the money and under what licence, whether client funds are segregated, what audit trail and permission controls you get, and how support behaves when something goes wrong mid-transaction. Anything touching payments should be able to answer those in writing; if it cannot, that is your answer.

Who should not use Tax1099?

Skip it if you only file one or two forms a year (the IRS's own free options may suffice) or you want full bookkeeping rather than just the filing step. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
