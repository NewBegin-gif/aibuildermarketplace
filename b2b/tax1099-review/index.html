Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 8 Sep 2026

Filing is graduated volume pricing, not a flat rate that falls: each band is billed at its own rate and the bill is the sum across bands. The page works the example itself for 600 forms — the first 20 at $2.99, forms 21-150 at $2.30, forms 151-500 at $1.31 and the last 100 at $0.68, totalling $885.30, an average of $1.48 a form.

Three eFiling plans: Essential is free, Teams (formerly eFile Plus) is $249 a year and adds multi-user access and 250 free TIN matches, and Scale (formerly Enterprise) is $349 a year and adds workflow, user and action management, API access and Notice Management (otherwise $199 a unit). TIN matching is not free on Essential: real-time is $1.00 a unit, within 24 hours $0.37, bulk $150 per 10,000 records — free on first use up to 10,000 records under one payer for Scale customers only.

Other add-ons: eDelivery $0.25 a unit, print and mail $1.90 a form (plus a $1 rush fee in the last five days of the season through 2 February), foreign mail $4.99 a form, state reconciliation $4.99 a payer, and W-9/W-8 requests at $1.00 for one, $7.00 for 25, $45.00 for 200 and $95.00 for 1,000. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

The cheap per-form rate is a marginal rate

Two corrections to what we told you before, and both change the budget. We said the per-form bands were not published in a form we could read; they are, on the pricing page, along with a worked example. And we reported $0.68 a form as the high-volume rate, which made it sound like a price you could reach. It is a marginal rate. Tax1099 bills each band separately and adds them up, so the vendor's own example of 600 forms runs 20 at $2.99, then 130 at $2.30, then 350 at $1.31, then 100 at $0.68 — $885.30 in total, an average of $1.48 a form.

You never pay $0.68 for your six hundredth form's worth of filing; you pay it only for the forms past the five hundredth. There is a plan decision after all, and it is not the one we described. eFile Plus has been renamed Teams at $249 a year, and there is now a third tier, Scale, at $349 a year for workflow management, user management, action tracking, API access and Notice Management that otherwise costs $199 a unit.

What Teams actually buys you is multi-user access and 250 TIN matches — at the $1.00 real-time rate those alone are worth $250, so the subscription roughly pays for itself if you would have run 250 real-time checks anyway. Below that it is a subscription for a payroll bureau or a bookkeeping practice, not for a company sending out thirty 1099s in January. The sequencing advice is still where the real money is, but it is no longer free and we should not have said it was.

TIN matching is an add-on: $1.00 a unit in real time, $0.37 a unit if you can wait 24 hours, or $150 per 10,000 records in bulk. The 250 free matches belong to the Teams plan, not to the free Essential account. Pay it anyway. A mismatched TIN produces a B-notice from the IRS and a correction, and corrections are billable here on top of the filing you already paid for — so 37 cents a recipient in the 24-hour queue is cheap insurance against a filing you have to buy twice.

Run every recipient through it before you file anything. Then tally the meters rather than the headline, and they are all published: eDelivery $0.25 a unit, print and mail $1.90 a form with a $1 rush fee in the last five days of the season, foreign mail $4.99 a form, state reconciliation $4.99 a payer, and W-9/W-8 requests at $1.00 for one, $7.00 for 25, $45.00 for 200 and $95.00 for 1,000.

State filing is the one that multiplies: a business with contractors in six states is filing six times over, not once. Add those up before you compare the per-form rate to anything. And be clear what this is. If your contractor records live in three spreadsheets with inconsistent names, the work that makes January painless happens before you open this at all.

How to actually use Tax1099

Run TIN matching on every recipient before you file a single form, and budget for it: it is an add-on at $1.00 a unit in real time or $0.37 within 24 hours, not a free feature of the Essential account as we previously said. Pay the 37 cents. A mismatched TIN produces an IRS B-notice and a correction, and corrections are billable on top of the filing you already paid for, so it is the cheapest insurance in the whole process and the step most often skipped.

Clean the payee data while you are in there. This is a filing tool, not a bookkeeping system: it takes what you give it. Names that disagree with the W-9, missing addresses and stale entity types are the things that turn a routine season into a fortnight of corrections. Then tally the whole bill rather than the per-form rate, and do not budget from the lowest band. Filing is graduated: each band bills at its own rate and they are summed, so the vendor's own 600-form example works out at $1.48 a form on average even though the top band is $0.68.

Add the add-ons — TIN matching, W-9 requests, eDelivery, mailing, corrections — and remember state filing multiplies with the number of states your contractors sit in.

Frequently asked questions

What does Tax1099 cost per form?

It is volume-banded rather than flat: our dated reading puts the high-volume rate near $0.68 a form, with the price per form higher at low counts. There is a free Essential account for setting up payers and recipients and running TIN matching, and an eFile Plus subscription around $249 a year. Verify the current bands before a filing season — we could not read them from the page on 17 August 2026.

Do I need the subscription?

Only if you file often. eFile Plus is aimed at teams filing regularly through the year rather than at a business doing one January run. For a single season, pay-as-you-go per form is usually the cheaper structure — work it out on your actual form count.

What is free?

The Essential account: creating payers and recipients and running TIN matching cost nothing, which matters because TIN mismatches are what generate IRS notices later. Doing the matching before you file is the free part worth using even if you file elsewhere.

What should I weigh before buying Financial Operations software?

The bar here is higher than features. Check who actually holds the money and under what licence, whether client funds are segregated, what audit trail and permission controls you get, and how support behaves when something goes wrong mid-transaction. Anything touching payments should be able to answer those in writing; if it cannot, that is your answer.
