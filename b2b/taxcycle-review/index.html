Price it per return, not per year

The prices are public (read 27 September 2026 on taxcycle.com, Canadian dollars before tax, one user): Tax Basics is $1,655 a year, the Complete Tax Suite $2,700 and the suite plus TaxFolder $2,852, with extra users at $540, $756 and $944. Those are still sale prices, $25 to $250 under the regular $1,680, $2,800 and $3,102, in a sale the page dates to 18 December; the deeper September sale ($1,600, $2,625 and $2,752 on 9 September) ended on the 24th.

Add your province's GST or HST on top. The number that makes sense of a range that wide is cost per return. At the regular $1,680, a practice preparing 200 T1s pays about $8.40 a return in software; the same figure across 50 returns is $33.60 each. That is why this is priced for firms — below a certain book of clients the software is a larger line item than the work justifies.

Do that division with your own return count first. You do not have to ask what your own mix costs, because every module is priced on the same page. TaxCycle T1 on its own is $618 for one user with extra seats at $238; T2 is $1,102, T3 $532, T5013 $516, T2202 $558, and the slip modules — T4/T4A, T5, T5018, NR4, RL — are $268 each with extra seats at $27.

TaxFolder is $375, DoxCycle $280 and Québec TP1 a $198 site licence. A preparer who only files personal returns is looking at $618, not $1,655 — $3.09 a return across two hundred T1s. Build your own line first, then check what the suite saves you. Three things to get in writing: what renewal costs in year two, whether seats are concurrent or named, and what support looks like during the filing peak.

Diarise the sale deadline too — the twelve-month payment plan runs only while the discount does, now to 18 December. What you get for it is the reason firms pay: CRA integration and the smart checks that catch errors before they leave your office. It is also Canada-only, so a practice with cross-border work will be buying something else for the other side.

Frequently asked questions

Can I get my money back if TaxCycle does not suit my practice?

Once, in your first year. The refund policy (read 27 September 2026) covers first-time purchases only, refunds the full price of the current-year product, and applies once per product; prior-year software is not refundable and neither are changes forced by government filing requirements. After 28 February of the licence year the refund may be prorated or refused. So decide during the free trial and before the filing season, not in March.

Is TaxCycle a subscription?

No, a yearly licence. The pricing page (read 27 September 2026) says all software is licensed per calendar year, not as an ongoing subscription, so each tax year is a new purchase; TaxFolder is the exception, licensed to the end of 2027. The 12-month payment plan spreads that yearly cost but commits you to the full amount, and it is only offered during a sale, currently to 18 December.

Who pays if TaxCycle miscalculates a client's return?

TaxCycle, for interest, up to a limit. Its T1/TP1 Accuracy Guarantee (read 27 September 2026) covers interest a client is charged because of a software miscalculation, up to CAD 2,500, if the return was filed on the latest version, the source data was correct, no calculation was overridden and you report it within three months of the notice. The guarantee is named for T1 and Québec TP1 returns only; the page does not extend it to T2 or T3.
