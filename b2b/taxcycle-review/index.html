Price it per return, not per year

The prices are public (read 9 September 2026 on taxcycle.com, Canadian dollars before tax, one user): Tax Basics is $1,600 a year, the Complete Tax Suite $2,625 and the suite plus TaxFolder $2,752, with extra users at $540, $756 and $944. Those are sale prices — the page says the sale ends 24 September and the regular figures are $1,680, $2,800 and $3,102. Add your province's GST or HST on top.

The number that makes sense of a range that wide is cost per return. At the regular $1,680, a practice preparing 200 T1s pays about $8.40 a return in software; the same figure across 50 returns is $33.60 each. That is why this is priced for firms — below a certain book of clients the software is a larger line item than the work justifies. Do that division with your own return count first.

You do not have to ask what your own mix costs, because every module is priced on the same page. TaxCycle T1 on its own is $618 for one user with extra seats at $238; T2 is $1,102, T3 $532, T5013 $516, T2202 $558, and the slip modules — T4/T4A, T5, T5018, NR4, RL — are $268 each with extra seats at $27. TaxFolder is $375, DoxCycle $280 and Québec TP1 a $198 site licence.

A preparer who only files personal returns is looking at $618, not $1,600 — $3.09 a return across two hundred T1s. Build your own line first, then check what the suite saves you. Three things to get in writing: what renewal costs in year two, whether seats are concurrent or named, and what support looks like during the filing peak. Diarise the sale deadline too — the twelve-month payment plan runs only while the discount does.

What you get for it is the reason firms pay: CRA integration and the smart checks that catch errors before they leave your office. It is also Canada-only, so a practice with cross-border work will be buying something else for the other side.
