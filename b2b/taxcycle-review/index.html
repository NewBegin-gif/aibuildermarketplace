Price it per return, not per year

There is no public price. TaxCycle is quote-based, priced by module and firm size, in Canadian dollars before tax, with reported figures ranging from roughly CAD $1,680 up into five figures for full suites. Add your province's GST or HST on top of whatever you are quoted. The number that makes sense of a range that wide is cost per return. At the low end, a practice preparing 200 T1s pays about $8.40 a return in software; the same $1,680 across 50 returns is $33.60 each.

That is why this is priced for firms — the per-return cost collapses with volume, and below a certain book of clients the software is a larger line item than the work justifies. Do that division with your own quote and your own return count before you compare it to anything, because a headline annual figure tells you nothing on its own. Because pricing is modular, the quote you should ask for is not for TaxCycle, it is for your specific mix.

Which returns do you actually prepare — T1, T2, T3, T4/T5 slips, forms — and how many seats need them at once during the season? Ask for the full suite priced as well as your current mix, because the module you add next year is the one that makes a cheap quote expensive, and the time to discover that price is now rather than mid-season. Four things to get in writing alongside the number: what the renewal costs in year two, whether seats are concurrent or named, what support looks like during the filing peak when you actually need it, and whether the quote is per year or per season.

What you get for it is the reason firms pay: CRA integration and the smart checks that catch errors before they leave your office. It is also Canada-only, so if your practice handles cross-border work, this covers one side of it and you will be buying something else for the other.
