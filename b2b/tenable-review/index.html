What Tenable does

Exposure and vulnerability management (makers of Nessus): scan, prioritize and remediate security weaknesses across IT, cloud and OT — for security teams of any size.

Recurring themes from G2 and PeerSpot, read in August 2026. We have not run Tenable ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Accuracy and depth of vulnerability identification is what security teams rate — 4.5 on G2 across 300 reviews, 8.4 out of 10 on PeerSpot.
  • • Easier to run than its depth suggests, and the detail in the findings is what reviewers act on.
What they complain about
  • • Cost is the dominant complaint. Nessus Pro runs around $3,390 for a one-year licence, and reviewers say maintaining it is what limits who can use it.
  • • Reporting is the weak spot, particularly at larger organisations — expect to build your own layer on top for board-level output.

When the paid plan is worth it

Nessus Essentials is free, but read the license before you plan around it: a 30-day license, 5 IPs, strictly non-commercial use, plugin updates delayed 30 days, and no reporting, data export, compliance checks or technical support. When it expires Tenable does not retain your scan data, so you re-download the free version and start over, or pay. Essentials Plus at $199/year adds 20 IPs, real-time plugin updates, basic PDF/HTML reporting and year-long data retention, still non-commercial, and free for verified students and instructors.

Professional at $4,790/year is the first tier licensed for commercial use: unlimited IPs, more than 450 pre-configured templates, and compliance and configuration checks with audit templates for internal PCI network, policy compliance, SCAP and OVAL. Two- and three-year terms cost $9,330.95 and $13,637.54. Budget support separately: 24x365 phone, email, community and chat is a $400/year add-on. Tenable positions Professional for point-in-time assessments, not continuous monitoring.

How to actually use Tenable

Tenable's documentation lays out five steps: Prepare, Install and Configure Tenable Nessus, Create and Configure Scans, View and Analyze Scan Results, and Refine Tenable Nessus Settings. You need an activation code, then you run a host discovery scan to identify assets and build a first scan from a Tenable-provided template. That first unauthenticated scan is surface-level; the work is in credentialed scanning. One person's published Windows lab walkthrough, run against a local Windows 10 virtual machine, had to set the Remote Registry service to start automatically, turn on network discovery and file/printer sharing, add a registry key so the remote account could connect, disable the domain, private and public firewall profiles, and reboot — after which the same host showed significantly more vulnerabilities.

Those last steps strip a machine's defences and belong on an isolated test host only; do not copy them onto a production system. Note which tier you start on: compliance checks are Professional-only, so an Essentials or Essentials Plus install covers vulnerability scanning alone.

Frequently asked questions

Is Tenable secure and reliable enough to depend on?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.

Who should not use Tenable?

Skip it if you have no one to triage and fix findings — a managed security service or a lighter scanner fits a small team better. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
