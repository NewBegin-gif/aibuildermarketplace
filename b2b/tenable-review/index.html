What they praise
  • • Accuracy and depth of vulnerability identification is what security teams rate — 4.5 on G2 across 300 reviews, 8.4 out of 10 on PeerSpot.
  • • Easier to run than its depth suggests, and the detail in the findings is what reviewers act on.
What they complain about
  • • Cost is the dominant complaint. Nessus Pro runs around $3,390 for a one-year licence, and reviewers say maintaining it is what limits who can use it.
  • • Reporting is the weak spot, particularly at larger organisations — expect to build your own layer on top for board-level output.

When the paid plan is worth it

Nessus Essentials is free, but read the license before you plan around it: a 30-day license, 5 IPs, strictly non-commercial use, plugin updates delayed 30 days, and no reporting, data export, compliance checks or technical support. When it expires Tenable does not retain your scan data, so you re-download the free version and start over, or pay. Essentials Plus at $199/year adds 20 IPs, real-time plugin updates, basic PDF/HTML reporting and year-long data retention, still non-commercial, and free for verified students and instructors.

Professional at $4,790/year is the first tier licensed for commercial use: unlimited IPs, more than 450 pre-configured templates, and compliance and configuration checks with audit templates for internal PCI network, policy compliance, SCAP and OVAL. Two- and three-year terms cost $9,330.95 and $13,637.54. Budget support separately: 24x365 phone, email, community and chat is a $400/year add-on. Tenable positions Professional for point-in-time assessments, not continuous monitoring.

How to actually use Tenable

Tenable's documentation lays out five steps: Prepare, Install and Configure Tenable Nessus, Create and Configure Scans, View and Analyze Scan Results, and Refine Tenable Nessus Settings. You need an activation code, then you run a host discovery scan to identify assets and build a first scan from a Tenable-provided template. That first unauthenticated scan is surface-level; the work is in credentialed scanning. One person's published Windows lab walkthrough, run against a local Windows 10 virtual machine, needed a string of changes first.

Set the Remote Registry service to start automatically, turn on network discovery and file/printer sharing, and add a registry key so the remote account could connect. Then disable the domain, private and public firewall profiles and reboot — after which the same host showed significantly more vulnerabilities. Those last steps strip a machine's defences and belong on an isolated test host only; do not copy them onto a production system.

Note which tier you start on: compliance checks are Professional-only, so an Essentials or Essentials Plus install covers vulnerability scanning alone.

Distilled from Tenable's official Nessus getting-started documentation and one person's published GitHub Windows credentialed-scanning lab walkthrough; tenable.com/buy and the Nessus Essentials page re-read 4 October 2026 (served HTML): every price unchanged, Essentials still a 30-day non-commercial licence for 5 IPs with scan data not retained at expiry.

The natural comparison is Qualys or Rapid7 — the other enterprise vulnerability-management platforms.

Frequently asked questions

What does Tenable Vulnerability Management cost?

$3,500 for a year covering 100 assets, with two- and three-year terms at $6,825 and $9,975. A second online entry point buys up to 250 assets for $3,700 a year — the page's own structured data labels that offer “Tenable One Vulnerability Management (1-Year, up to 250 assets)”. Past 250 assets you are into a quote and a sales conversation rather than a checkout, and the same is true of Tenable Security Center and the wider Tenable One exposure products. Read on tenable.com/buy on 16 September and again on 4 October 2026, in dollars without a region switch; nothing has moved since 30 August 2026.

Is that the same thing as Nessus?

No, and the mix-up costs money in both directions. Nessus is the scanner, licensed per installation: Nessus Professional is $4,790 a year ($9,330.95 for two, $13,637.54 for three) and Nessus Expert $6,790 ($13,208.13 and $19,304.19), with 24x365 phone, email, community and chat support a separate $400-a-year add-on. Vulnerability Management is the cloud subscription, priced per asset. Tenable positions Professional for point-in-time assessment rather than continuous monitoring, so the two answer different questions. Note what that does to the price comparison at small scale: at 100 assets the continuous cloud product ($3,500) costs less than the point-in-time scanner ($4,790). Read 16 September and 4 October 2026 on tenable.com/buy.

Can I use the free Nessus Essentials at work?

No. Read on 16 September and again on 4 October 2026 on Tenable's Nessus Essentials page, which calls it a 30-day free licence for up to 5 IPs and says it is “strictly for non-commercial use and is not engineered for enterprise or production environments”, suited to short evaluations, proof-of-concept testing or a very small home network. Essentials Plus is the step up for hobbyists — real-time plugin updates, a 20-IP scan limit, basic PDF/HTML reporting and year-long data retention — and is still non-commercial, though verified students and instructors can get it free for a year through the Nessus for Education programme. The first tier licensed for commercial use is Nessus Professional, at $4,790 a year.
