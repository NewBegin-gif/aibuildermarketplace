What the pricing page doesn’t say

That matters more here than in most categories, because hiring is lumpy: a company that hires in two bursts a year is paying for ten quiet months. Both paid tiers are a year up front

The free tier is $0 with 10 credits a month, no card needed, and includes preset custom questions and one full-access seat. Core is $142 a month on an annual commitment — $1,704 billed annually — with the full test library, analytics and two full-access seats. Plus starts from $400 a month with an annual commitment starting at $4,800 (2026-08-01, read on their own pricing page).

Core is not $142 a month in any meaningful sense. It is $1,704 up front, and there is no monthly option at all — which matters more here than in most categories, because hiring is lumpy. A company that recruits in two bursts a year is paying for ten quiet months, and unlike a CRM or a helpdesk there is nothing happening in between to justify it.

So price it per hire rather than per month. At $1,704 a year, ten hires costs $170 each and fifty costs $34 each — and against the cost of one bad hire, either figure is small. That is the honest case for this category and it is a strong one; it just depends entirely on volume, and the annual-only billing means you commit before you know.

The free tier is credit-metered rather than feature-limited, which makes it a genuine trial rather than a demo: ten credits a month is enough to run a real shortlist through a real assessment. Use it on an actual vacancy before you commit to a year, and pay attention to candidate drop-off as well as scores — the most common failure of assessment tools is good candidates abandoning a test that felt too long. How to actually use TestGorilla

Run a real vacancy through the free tier before you commit to anything, because both paid tiers are annual-only. Ten credits a month is enough to assess a genuine shortlist, and it is credit-metered rather than feature-limited, so what you test is what you would buy.

Watch candidate drop-off as closely as you watch scores. The usual way assessment tools fail is not bad measurement but good candidates abandoning a test that felt too long or too generic — so count how many people you invited against how many finished, and read the assessment yourself as a candidate would.

Then price it per hire rather than per month, since $1,704 a year is the real unit. Ten hires makes it $170 each and fifty makes it $34 — and if you hire in two bursts a year, be honest that you are paying for the quiet months too.

If you are being pointed at Plus, ask what moves the price before accepting a quote. It 'starts from' $400 a month with an annual commitment from $4,800, and the page does not say whether the variable is seats, candidates or tests.

Best for: companies hiring steadily enough that a year of testing is cheaper than one bad hire, and who will actually use the library rather than writing their own questions.

Frequently asked questions

Is TestGorilla compliant with local employment rules?

Compliance is country by country, so the honest answer is: only where the vendor says it is, and only for the entity type you actually have. Ask which countries are covered by its own entities versus a local partner, who carries the liability if something is filed wrong, and how changes in local law reach you. Get those answers in the contract rather than the sales deck.

Who should not use TestGorilla?

Skip it if you hire a few people a year — the annual commitment is the whole problem, and the free tier plus a well-designed take-home task covers occasional hiring. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
