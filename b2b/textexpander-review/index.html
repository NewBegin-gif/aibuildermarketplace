What TextExpander does

Text-expansion tool: you type a short abbreviation and it expands into a stored snippet system-wide on macOS and Windows and in Chrome, with fill-in fields, nested snippets, date maths and scripting. The real product is the team layer — a shared snippet library with permissions, sign-up by email domain, auto-subscribe for new hires, usage statistics and SSO — aimed at support, clinical-admin, recruiting and sales teams that need everyone sending the same approved wording. The vendor self-attests SOC 2/SOC 3 and HIPAA compliance with a BAA available, which is the clearest argument for paying rather than using a free expander. From $3.33 per user/month billed annually; no free plan, 30-day trial without a credit card.

Pricing (2026)

(vendor pricing page, read 21 Jul 2026; USD per user/month billed annually, excluding tax) Individual $3.33 ($39.96/yr), Business $8.33 ($99.96/yr), Growth $10.83 ($129.96/yr), Enterprise quoted. There is no free tier — the only free route is a 30-day trial with no credit card. Individual is a genuine single-user plan; the organisation plans start at a minimum of two seats (stated on the vendor's own upgrade documentation). Two things the tier table does not advertise loudly: snippet activity history — which is what lets you track changes and restore deleted snippets — is capped at 7 days on Individual, Business AND Growth, and is unlimited only on Enterprise; and the vendor's own pricing note says buyers in VAT/GST countries including the EU, UK and Australia may see an estimated 20% increase on the listed USD price. We are not quoting monthly per-user prices: the live page publishes the annual figures, and the monthly numbers circulating on comparison sites could not be confirmed at source. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

On a Mac the operating system already does the basic job free — Apple's built-in Text Replacement syncs static snippets across your Apple devices, though Apple's own documentation notes it is not available in every app — and Espanso covers the power-user case free and open source on Mac, Windows and Linux. On Windows there is no built-in equivalent (contrary to a widely repeated claim, PowerToys has no text-expansion utility), so the case for paying is genuinely stronger there. Do not buy it for mobile: the vendor's own mobile page is an early-access signup form for Android and iOS betas, not a shipping product, even though the surrounding copy is written in the present tense. Add the 7-day activity history that applies even on the $10.83 Growth tier — where SSO is the only published addition over Business — and the estimated 20% uplift for EU, UK and Australian buyers, and for one person with a few dozen fixed snippets the honest answer is usually don't.

What buyers report about TextExpander

Recurring themes from G2, Capterra and Software Advice, read in August 2026. We have not run TextExpander ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • 4.8 on G2 across 425 reviews, over a billion expansions a year, with Uber and Shopify among the customers. Cross-platform reliability is what reviewers name.
  • • For a support or agency team repeating the same messages daily, the time saved is straightforward to calculate.
What they complain about
  • • The subscription is the complaint in nearly every review. It moved from a one-time Mac purchase to SaaS and long-time users have not forgiven it.
  • • $3.33/month individual on annual billing, $8.33 per user for Business, with no free tier — only a 30-day trial.
  • • Solo users are pointed at free alternatives like Espanso or Raycast snippets, and reviewers say so themselves.
  • • Occasional glitches and no dark mode.
Best for: support, clinical-admin and recruiting teams that need everyone sending identical approved wording — the shared library, permissions, email-domain sign-up, auto-subscribe and SSO are what the paid tiers actually buy, and the SOC 2 and HIPAA-with-BAA posture the vendor advertises is the one thing no free expander offers a compliance-bound team.
Who should skip it: if you are one person on a Mac with a couple of dozen fixed snippets — macOS Text Replacement is built in and free, Espanso is free and open source across all three desktop platforms, and a subscription only starts earning its keep when you need fill-in fields, scripting, or a second person editing the same library.

The natural comparison is Text Blaze — Text Blaze has a genuine free tier and undercuts it at every paid rung (Pro $2.99 and Business $6.99 per user/month billed annually), so TextExpander has to win on OS-level expansion outside the browser and on the SSO, SCIM and HIPAA-BAA paperwork a procurement team asks for — not on price. Decide by which one fits the job above, not by the louder brand.
