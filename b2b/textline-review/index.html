Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 31 Aug 2026

Amounts unchanged since 2026-08-01. Add-on message credits $0.03 each, bought in advance; backup message credits $0.04 each, Textline's automatic fallback when you run out mid-conversation. A $15 monthly 10DLC campaign fee applies if you use a 10DLC long code; the same page states that toll-free numbers do not carry a monthly registration fee. NEW since the last reading: the page now names three tiers with their inclusions but still no prices — Essentials (600 minimum included message credits a month, three agents, three-year conversation history), Pro (2,000 minimum credits, five agents, unlimited history, Salesforce, custom roles) and Enterprise (quote).

HIPAA-compliant plans are charged an additional fee. Note for future readers: the per-feature tooltips on this page are unfilled placeholder copy, so the feature names carry no explanation. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The credit system has two rates: $0.03 for credits you buy deliberately and $0.04 for backup credits that fire automatically when you run out — a 33% premium for not noticing. And a $15 monthly 10DLC campaign fee appears on your bill if you use a 10DLC long code, charged by the carrier registry rather than by Textline, so no plan removes it; the same page notes that toll-free numbers carry no monthly registration fee, so the number type is the lever, not the tier.

Plan prices are still not published — the page names Essentials, Pro and Enterprise and what they include, but no dollar figures.

What buyers report about Textline

What they praise

What they complain about
  • • Message fees are separate from the monthly plan, and incoming messages are not free either. The $20-$50 per agent is the floor, not the total.
  • • The interface is dated enough that reviewers say it slows onboarding.
  • • There is no fully functional native mobile app.

Backup credits cost a third more

The page still documents the meters rather than the prices, but it does now name three plans, and that changes what you should ask for. Essentials carries a 600-message-credit monthly minimum, three agents and three years of conversation history; Pro raises that to a 2,000-credit minimum, five agents and unlimited history; Enterprise is a conversation. No dollar figure appears against any of them, and HIPAA-compliant plans cost extra on top of whatever you agree.

Two meters sit alongside the plan. Message credits bought in advance are $0.03 each; backup credits, the fallback that fires automatically when you run out mid-conversation, are $0.04 — a 33% premium for not noticing, charged at the exact moment you are mid-sentence with a customer. The $15 monthly 10DLC campaign fee is the line people budget wrong, and upgrading does not touch it: the carrier registry charges it, not Textline, so no plan removes it.

The number type does. The same page states that toll-free numbers carry no monthly registration fee, which makes this a routing decision rather than a billing one.

How to actually use Textline

Set your credit top-up threshold on day one, high enough that backup credits never trigger. Deliberate credits are $0.03 and automatic backup credits are $0.04, so this single setting is worth 33% on every message it prevents, and it takes a minute. Decide the number type before you register anything, because the $15 monthly campaign fee follows that choice and not your plan. A 10DLC long code carries the fee; the pricing page states toll-free numbers have no monthly registration fee.

Read Textline's own explainers on number types and on The Campaign Registry first, since the choice also affects how registration works. You can now compare the tiers yourself on what is included — 600 versus 2,000 minimum message credits a month, three versus five agents — so ask sales only for the two dollar figures and for what an overage costs. Settle compliance before the first campaign: registration, documented opt-in and a working opt-out stay your responsibility whatever the platform does.
