What TheTop does

An AI 'chief of staff' that pulls Gmail, Google Calendar, Outlook, Microsoft 365 and RSS feeds into one ranked morning brief, then drafts the replies and quick actions the brief calls for. Essential is $15/month for two connected sources; the enrichment, smart reply and five-source cap that let it act rather than summarise sit on Pro at $30.

Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 25 Aug 2026

(read 2026-08-25 on thetop.com/pricing) Two paid plans, both month to month with no contract. Essential is $15/month and covers the full morning brief, two connected sources, AI-powered summaries and insights, basic conversation memory and a daily email digest. Pro is $30/month and adds adaptive memory and learning, five brief refreshes a day, automatic data enrichment, quick actions and smart reply, and five connected data sources.

Switching the billing toggle to yearly drops the effective rates to $12 on Essential and $25 on Pro. Both plans open with a seven-day free trial of Pro. The pricing page shows no free tier, although its own FAQ answers a question about starting on Free and upgrading later. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Out-of-the-box integrations stop at Gmail, Google Calendar, Outlook, Microsoft 365, and RSS or news feeds, so anything else in your stack sits outside the brief. The tier caps bite before the feature list does: two connected sources on Essential, five on Pro, and Gmail plus Calendar alone uses up Essential. Automatic enrichment, quick actions and smart reply — the parts that do something rather than summarise something — are Pro only, which makes the realistic price the $30 tier rather than the advertised $15.

The pricing page also shows no free plan while its own FAQ answers a question about starting on Free, so ask before you count on one.

Everything that makes it a chief of staff sits on the upper tier

Essential at $15 buys you the brief. Pro at $30 buys you the acting. Put the two feature lists side by side and the split is clean: Essential gets the morning brief, the summaries, basic memory and a daily digest, which is a very good digest. Adaptive memory and learning, automatic data enrichment, quick actions and smart reply all live on Pro. The product sells itself as a chief of staff that acts where it can, and acting is a Pro feature.

The source cap decides it before the feature list does. Essential connects two sources, Pro connects five, and the integration list out of the box is Gmail, Google Calendar, Outlook, Microsoft 365, and RSS or news feeds. Run Gmail and Google Calendar and you have spent both Essential slots on the two most obvious ones. Any feed, any second mailbox, any Outlook account puts you on Pro. For most people the honest entry price of this product is the $30 tier, or $25 with a year committed.

That makes the trial the thing to use carefully. Both plans open with seven days of Pro, so the week you get is the expensive tier, and the question to answer inside it is not whether the brief is accurate. It is whether quick actions and smart reply saved you anything you would otherwise have done by hand. If the answer is no, Essential is a fair price for a good digest and you should stay there. If the answer is yes, you were always going to be a Pro customer, and the yearly rate is the cheaper way to be one.

One thing the pricing page does not settle. It shows two cards and no free plan, while its own FAQ answers the question "Can I start on Free and upgrade later without losing anything?" Whether a free tier still exists, and what it includes, is worth asking before you assume you can sit on it.

How to actually use TheTop

Connect your two heaviest sources first and then leave it alone for a week. The whole promise is that you open one view instead of five, and the only honest test of that is whether you actually stopped opening the other five. Count the mornings you acted on the brief rather than read past it.

Before you pay, write down the tools your day actually runs through and check them against what TheTop connects to out of the box: Gmail, Google Calendar, Outlook, Microsoft 365, and RSS or news feeds. If your work lives in Slack, a CRM, a ticket queue or a project tracker, the brief is built from a partial picture of your day, and no amount of AI ranking repairs a missing source.

Spend the seven-day Pro trial on the things you cannot get on the cheaper plan — quick actions, smart reply, automatic enrichment — rather than on the brief itself, which both tiers give you. The plan is month to month with no contract, so the yearly rate is a bet you can place later, once a month of real use has told you whether this became a habit or stayed a novelty.

Best for: professionals and small-business owners who lose the first hour of the day triaging Gmail and a calendar, and want one ranked brief instead of five open tabs.
Who should skip it: if your day runs through Slack, a CRM, a ticket queue or a project tracker — the integration list does not reach them, and a brief built from email and calendar alone will miss what actually moved.

Frequently asked questions

Is TheTop secure and reliable enough to depend on?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.
