Every advertised rate is the largest package

Everything here is a meter — per GB, per IP, per 1,000 responses — and every rate the site advertises is the price of the largest package on that ladder, not an entry price. Residential proxies are $0.65 a GB only at 5,000 GB, which is $3,250 up front; at 1 GB the same product is $2.00, at 50 GB $1.50 and at 350 GB $0.80. The pattern repeats everywhere: the SERP API is $0.70 per thousand at a million responses against $1.20 at fifteen thousand, and the Scraping Browser $2.50 a GB at 500 GB against $5.00 at one.

Budget at your own volume, then, which on a first project is roughly three times the advertised rate. Like for like, the anti-bot premium is smaller than the headlines suggest too: at 200,000 responses the unblocker (listed as Webpage since September) is $1.00 per thousand against the Web Scraper API's $0.83 at 150,000 — a fifth more, not double. What buying a bigger package does not fix: it will not tell you your bandwidth per page, and that is the number that sets your bill.

Measure it on the free API trials, on your own targets. Nor does it settle sourcing — those residential IPs come from consumer devices, so how the network was built and what your targets' terms say are questions for the start of the project.

How to actually use Thordata

Measure your bandwidth per page before you compare any rate. Use the free API trials on your own targets and record how many megabytes a typical page costs — that figure times the per-GB rate is your actual bill, and without it no proxy vendor's pricing can be compared to another's. Then price at your own volume rather than at the headline. Every advertised "from" rate is the largest package on its ladder: $0.65 a GB means buying 5,000 GB.

Find the row that matches what you will actually buy and quote the project there. Route by target rather than by habit. Send only the sites that genuinely need anti-bot handling through the unblocker the page now lists as Webpage, and only the ones that need JavaScript rendering through the Scraping Browser — at comparable purchase sizes that path costs about three times the residential rate per gigabyte and burns more gigabytes per page.

Before any of that, check the sourcing. Residential IPs come from consumer devices, so ask how the network was built and read your target sites' terms.

Frequently asked questions

To get $0.65 a GB I have to buy 5,000 GB. Can I get my money back if it does not work on my targets?

Barely. Read 23 September 2026, Thordata's refund policy allows a refund only within 7 days of your first purchase of that product, only if you have used no more than 30% of the package and no more than 2 GB, and not if you paid in crypto. On a 5,000 GB package the 2 GB cap is what bites: the window closes after 0.04% of what you bought. The same policy also lists, among the cases it will not refund, a bundle bought through the dashboard that has started transmitting data, which reads as closing even that small window once traffic flows. So do not test at the headline size. The 1 GB step is $2.00 and 10 GB is $18.00; measure your bandwidth per page there, then buy the package your real volume needs.

The residential prices are shown as a sale. Is the discount real?

Read the unit price and ignore the strike-through. Read 23 September 2026, the residential ladder shows struck 'original' prices of $2.85 at 1 GB, $2.75 at 10 GB, $1.95 at 50 GB, $1.50 at 150 GB and $1.70 at 350 GB, against selling prices of $2.00, $1.80, $1.50, $1.00 and $0.80. The struck price at 350 GB is higher than the one at 150 GB, so that column is not a price list anyone could have bought from in order. The selling prices are identical to what we read on 1 September, so nothing has moved in three weeks either way. The high-volume rows from 1,000 GB up carry no struck price at all: $0.73, $0.70 and $0.65.

What are the High Bandwidth Proxies, and are they cheaper than paying per GB?

There is no way to tell from the page. Read 23 September 2026, they are sold by time rather than volume: 1 day for $280, 7 days for $900, 30 days for $2,370 and 60 days for $4,140, which falls from $280 a day to $69 a day at the longest term. The menu places them under solutions for large-scale video data collection. What the pricing page does not state is how much bandwidth, how many IPs or which proxy type a day buys, so you cannot set them against the residential rate of $0.65 to $2.00 a GB. Ask for the throughput in writing before comparing, and check it against the refund terms, which are written around gigabytes used.

What should I weigh before buying Developer Tools software?

Read the documentation before you commit, because it is the most honest preview of the product. Look for a quickstart you can actually finish, error responses that are documented rather than guessed at, published rate limits and a clear statement of what happens when you hit them, and a changelog showing the API is still maintained. Vague limits usually mean you will discover them in production.
