What Thordata does

Proxy infrastructure and scraping stack — residential, mobile, ISP and datacenter proxies plus SERP/web-scraper APIs, priced per GB, IP or 1K requests.

Pricing (2026)

(2026) Usage-metered, with free trials on the APIs. At research time a ~50%-off sale ran: residential proxies from $0.65/GB (list $1.05), mobile $2.20/GB, static ISP and datacenter proxies from $0.75/IP (list $1.50); SERP API from $0.70 per 1K responses, Web Scraper API $0.50/1K, Web Unlocker $1.00/1K, Scraping Browser $2.50/GB. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Everything is a meter — per GB, per IP, per 1K responses — so the bill scales with usage, and the headline rates were SALE prices at research time (roughly half of list): budget on list price, not the promo. And the category-wide caveat we’d give any residential-proxy vendor: those ‘100M+ real residential IPs’ come from consumer devices, so check sourcing ethics and your target sites’ terms before building on it.

What buyers report about Thordata

Recurring themes from Trustpilot, G2 and proxy-comparison sites, read in August 2026. We have not run Thordata ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Residential traffic is genuinely cheap — around $2.00/GB at 1GB falling to $0.65-$0.73 above 1,000GB, with a 1GB free trial on request.
  • • For high-volume scraping the per-GB rate undercuts the established providers substantially.
What they complain about
  • • Trustpilot made Thordata's rating unavailable after removing fake reviews and flagging a guidelines breach. That is the single most important thing on this page — it means the public review signal for this vendor cannot be trusted at face value.
  • • Reviewers on Trustpilot and G2 report data expiring prematurely, unhelpful support, refused refunds and false advertising. Billing surprises are named specifically.
  • • Third-party listings put the residential network at roughly 60M IPs against the advertised 100M+.
  • • Static residential IPs and SOCKS5 support are not clearly documented, and network performance is reported as unstable.

Frequently asked questions

How good are Thordata's docs and rate limits?

Read the documentation before you commit, because it is the most honest preview of the product. Look for a quickstart you can actually finish, error responses that are documented rather than guessed at, published rate limits and a clear statement of what happens when you hit them, and a changelog showing the API is still maintained. Vague limits usually mean you will discover them in production.

Who should not use Thordata?

Skip it if you need predictable flat pricing, or your use case skirts site terms — residential proxying carries compliance questions no discount fixes. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
