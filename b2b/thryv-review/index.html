Four times the price, with nothing in between

Two published tiers: Starter at $85 a month, and Signature at $340 a month billed $4,068 annually. Annual billing is stated to save 15%, which puts the month-to-month Signature rate at around $400. Higher tiers and add-on services are not priced on the page, and there is a free trial (2026-08-01, read on their own pricing page). The gap is the thing to plan around: $85 to $340 is four times, with nothing in between.

So the question is not which plan suits you but whether the whole jump is worth taking in one step — and it is worth noticing that Signature is billed annually at $4,068. That is a year committed up front; the $340 monthly figure is a division rather than a payment option. The way to answer it is to list the bundled parts and mark the ones you would otherwise buy separately, with their prices.

Thryv's whole case rests on replacing four or five tools with one — CRM, scheduling, invoicing, reviews, marketing — and that case is either obviously true for your business or obviously not. If you would use two of the five, the arithmetic almost always favours keeping the two tools you already know; if you would use all five and you are currently paying for each, the bundle wins comfortably. Ask about the unpriced parts before the call ends.

Higher tiers and add-on services exist and are not on the page, and in a bundled product the add-ons are where the quote drifts away from the advertised number. Use the free trial on the module you are least sure about rather than the one that sold you. In an all-in-one, the weakest component is what determines whether you actually retire the tool it was supposed to replace — and a bundle you only half-adopt is the expensive outcome.

Frequently asked questions

What should I weigh before buying Business Operations software?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.
