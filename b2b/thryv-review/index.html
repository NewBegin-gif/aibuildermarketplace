Four times the price, with nothing in between

Two published tiers: Starter at $99 a month, Signature at $399, or $85 and $340 if you take the year, which the page states saves 15%. The third tier is Custom, quote-only, and the four Boosts services carry no public price at all. The gap is the thing to plan around: $85 to $340 is four times, with nothing in between. So the question is not which plan suits you but whether the whole jump is worth taking in one step.

The way to answer it is to list the bundled parts and mark the ones you would otherwise buy separately, with their prices. Thryv's case rests on replacing four or five tools with one — CRM, scheduling, invoicing, reviews, marketing — and that case is either obviously true for your business or obviously not. What the upgrade does not settle is the bill. The page prints no yearly total and will not say whether the annual term is charged up front, and the Boosts are unpriced and barred from Starter, so the two figures that decide a real quote are both off the page.

How to actually use Thryv

List the bundled parts and mark the ones you would otherwise pay for separately, with their actual prices beside them. Thryv's case is replacing four or five tools with one, so that list settles it: use two of the five and keeping your existing tools is usually cheaper; use all five and the bundle wins comfortably. Then price the commitment honestly. The annual toggle takes Starter from $99 to $85 and Signature from $399 to $340, a stated saving of 15 per cent — but both annual figures are printed per month, and the page does not say whether the year is charged in one payment or spread across twelve.

Ask, because $340 a month and roughly $4,080 up front are different commitments to a business with a quarter to get through. The gap from Starter's $85 to Signature's $340 is four times with nothing in between, so this is a single large step rather than a gradual upgrade. Ask what is not on the page before you finish the call. Higher tiers and add-on services exist and are unpriced publicly, and in a bundled product that is exactly where a quote drifts from the advertised figure.

Use the free trial on the module you are least confident about — the scheduling, or the invoicing, whichever you would find hardest to give up your current tool for. In an all-in-one it is the weakest component that decides whether you actually retire anything, and a bundle you only half-adopt is the expensive outcome.

Frequently asked questions

Is the $99 on the card a monthly price or an annual one?

Monthly — and that is worth checking rather than assuming, because most pricing pages default the other way. Read on thryv.com/pricing on 19 September 2026: the toggle opens with Monthly selected, showing Starter $99 and Signature $399. Click Annual, marked 'Save 15%', and the same cards read $85 and $340, still printed per month. So the headline here is the more expensive of the two, not the cheaper one.

What will a year of Signature actually cost me?

The page will not tell you, and neither will we. It prints $340 a month on the Annual tab, prints no yearly total anywhere, and says nothing about whether the year is charged in one payment or spread over twelve — checked again on 19 September 2026. Twelve times $340 is $4,080, but that is our arithmetic, not their price, and an earlier version of this review quoted $4,068 as though it were published. It was not. Ask for the annual total and the payment schedule in writing before the demo ends; $340 a month and roughly $4,080 up front are different commitments.

Can I add the Boosts marketing services to the Starter plan?

No. The Boosts — Ads, Brand, Search and Social — are done-for-you marketing services, and the FAQ on the pricing page states they can be added to Signature and Custom plans only. Since Starter is $85 a month and Signature $340, wanting any Boost makes the real entry price the higher tier plus an unpriced service on top. None of the four carries a published figure, so that part of the bill only appears in a quote.
