Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 26 Sep 2026

No subscription and no contract: pay-as-you-sell $0.85 a ticket, or prepaid credits from $0.70 down to $0.34 depending on volume — 100 credits (the minimum) cost $70, 500 cost $300, 1,000 cost $520 and 20,000 cost $6,800. Credits carry no time limit. Free events are free up to 5,000 tickets a year, the first five paid tickets cost nothing, and charities, B Corps, PTAs and not-for-profits get 50% off, as do very low-priced tickets.

Seated tickets cost two credits rather than one. Prices switch across GBP, EUR, USD, AUD and CAD. Stripe, PayPal or Square fees apply separately and Ticket Tailor never touches the payment. Note one contradiction in their own FAQ: one answer puts the free-ticket allowance at 5,000 a year, another at 2,000. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

The flat fee wins as ticket prices rise

No subscription and no contract: $0.85 a ticket as you sell, or prepaid credits that get cheaper the more you buy. These are the US figures. The credit question our earlier reading left open is answered on the page, twice: credits have no time limit and can be used over an unlimited period. Prepaying is a discount, not a bet. We stepped their calculator through its whole range to get the ladder: 100 credits cost $0.70 each and 100 is the minimum purchase, 500 cost $0.60, 1,000 cost $0.52, 5,000 cost $0.43, and the slider bottoms out at 20,000 for $0.34 — so the '$0.30' on the card is below anything the public calculator will sell you.

Against pay-as-you-sell, 500 credits saves $125. Two things change that arithmetic. A ticket sold through a seating chart costs two credits rather than one, so a seated venue should budget double. And when your balance reaches zero you revert to $0.85 automatically, which is why they offer top-ups at 25 credits. The flat fee is the whole argument: at $0.60 you pay 1.2% on a $50 ticket and 12% on a $5 one, so the advantage over percentage platforms is largest at the top and gone at the bottom.

The trade is that you bring your own audience — there is no discovery marketplace.

How to actually use TicketTailor

Work out where your sales came from last time before you switch. Ticket Tailor has no discovery marketplace, so every sale has to come from your own list, your own social channels or your own posters. If a meaningful share of your last event was filled by people browsing a platform, that is the traffic you are giving up and no fee saving replaces it. Then compare fees on your actual ticket price, not in the abstract.

A flat $0.85 is 1.7% of a $50 ticket and 17% of a $5 one — divide your per-ticket fee by your current platform's percentage to find the price at which they cost the same. And if you run a seated venue, budget two units per ticket: seat reservations cost an extra credit each. Start on pay-as-you-sell, then prepay once you know your volume. Credits have no time limit, so five hundred at $300, which saves $125 against the $0.85 rate, still pay off for an organiser who runs three events a year; the smallest purchase is 100 credits.

Check too whether you qualify for the 50% charity, B Corp, PTA or not-for-profit rate, and connect your own Stripe, PayPal or Square account so the money reaches you on your processor's schedule.

Frequently asked questions

Do Ticket Tailor credits expire?

No. The pricing FAQ (read 26 September 2026) says prepaid credits can be used over an unlimited time period. When the balance reaches zero you drop back to pay-as-you-sell at $0.85 a ticket, which is why you can set credits to top up automatically, monthly or when 25 or fewer are left. The minimum purchase is 100 credits at $0.70 each, $70; a seated ticket uses two credits.

When do I get the money from Ticket Tailor ticket sales?

As you sell, not after the event. Ticket Tailor never holds the money: you connect your own Stripe, PayPal or Square account, and the FAQ (read 26 September 2026) says revenue appears there almost immediately, though it may take a week or so to reach your bank. On pay-as-you-sell with Stripe the fee is deducted automatically; with PayPal or Square, Ticket Tailor invoices the previous month's fees once a month.

Are free events on Ticket Tailor really free?

Mostly. The pricing card (read 26 September 2026) says issuing up to 5,000 free tickets a year costs nothing; above that, support quotes a small per-ticket fee. One FAQ answer puts the threshold at 2,000 tickets instead, so confirm it if you are near either number. Free tickets sold through a seating chart are not free: each uses one credit.

Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.
