What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Read on 18 September 2026 it is 4.6 across 93 reviews, and the shape behind it is 84 five-star, 3 four-star, nothing at three or two, and 6 one-star. A month earlier it was 4.1 across 78, with 7 at the bottom. Nothing sits in the middle on either reading. That is not a mediocre product; it is a binary one — it works immediately, or it installs cleanly and then tracks nothing at all.

The one-star reviews describe the same failure in the same words: the pixel is set up by the instructions, no page views or add-to-carts ever arrive, and support does not reply. We cannot verify what happened in any individual account, but the consistency of the complaint is itself information. What is new since August is that the developer now answers them in public. A reply dated 2 September 2026 attributes the empty data to manual pixel configuration, where one mismatched ID or event setting is enough to stop events being recorded at all, and says that step is no longer needed: TiXel now integrates officially with Meta and TikTok and links the pixel to your ad account automatically.

That is the vendor's account of its own worst reviews, so treat it as a claim to test rather than a fix to trust. It is at least a specific and checkable one, and the trial has gone from three days to seven, which is enough room to check it.

How to actually use TiXel

Install it on the first day of the trial, not the last. Seven days is the window as of September 2026, where it was three in August, and the failure this app is criticised for is not a crash but a silence — the pixel appears installed and no events arrive. You need traffic through the store to tell those apart. So verify rather than assume. Fire a test view, an add-to-cart and a checkout yourself, then open Meta Events Manager or TikTok Events and confirm all three arrived with the right event names.

Do it in the first two days, while uninstalling is still an option rather than an argument about a refund. Take the official route in while you are there: the developer says TiXel now connects straight to your Meta and TikTok ad accounts and links the pixel for you, which removes the manual step its worst reviews describe. And if server-side tracking is why you came, test it separately.

Frequently asked questions

How long is the free trial, and is it enough?

Seven days as of 18 September 2026, which is a real change — the listing said three in August and this review was written against that. Seven is enough if you spend it deliberately. The thing you are testing is not whether the app installs but whether events arrive, and that needs traffic: put a genuine view, add-to-cart and checkout through the store on day one, then check Meta Events Manager or TikTok Events for all three by name. If they are not there by day two you still have five days to uninstall, which is the difference the longer trial actually buys. Do not spend the week configuring and leave the verification to the end, because the reported failure mode is silent and looks exactly like a correct installation until you go and look at the other side.

Why pay $19.99 when Shopify's Meta and TikTok channels install a pixel free?

For most stores you should not, and that is the honest answer. The free channel apps handle the ordinary case: one pixel, one brand, standard events. What this charges for is the cases they do not cover — unlimited pixels on a single store, which matters if you run several brands or agencies through one shop, pixels scoped to specific products, and server-side Events API pixels alongside the browser one. That last is the strongest reason to pay: browser pixels lose events to ad-blockers and to Safari's tracking prevention, and the Events API is the standard answer to it. If none of those three describe you, the free channels are not a compromise. At $19.99 a month or $119.94 a year the decision is small either way, but prove the pixel fires before you buy twelve months of it.

How do I check the server-side tracking is really sending?

Check it as its own test, because a working browser pixel proves nothing about it. In Meta Events Manager the two arrive as different connection methods — browser and server — and an event can show up under one while the other sends nothing at all; a rising total event count is not evidence. Fire a purchase or a checkout yourself, then look at the event's source rather than its tally, and check the deduplication is working so one order is not counted twice. Do this inside the trial. The app's plan text promises server-side Events API pixels and automatic event triggers with no manual configuration, so if the server source is empty after real traffic, that is the feature you are paying for not working, and it is a support question while you still owe nothing.
