Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 17 Aug 2026

Free covers a limited number of users, Starter is $9 per licence a month and Premium $14. But read the asterisk: that $14 applies to your first year of Premium annual, and then renews at $18 per licence a month. Enterprise is quoted. The renewal step is the thing to budget: a ten-person team on Premium goes from $1,680 in year one to $2,160 in year two, and it is stated on the page rather than hidden. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What Toggl does

Time tracking for freelancers, consultants and agencies, with budgets, rates and profitability per project. Free up to five users; Starter about $9/user/month on annual billing. The paid line is drawn at whether you bill clients — track time for personal discipline and the free tier covers it.

What buyers report about Toggl

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Toggl ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Starting a timer is effortless, which is the whole battle with time tracking — 4.6 on G2 across 1,500+ reviews, 4.7 on Capterra.
  • • The free plan covers up to 5 users and genuinely does what a solo or small team needs day to day.
What they complain about
  • • Syncing is the most common complaint: a timer started on mobile does not always stop on desktop, and entries can take seconds to a minute to appear on another device.
  • • Bulk-editing several entries in the calendar view is awkward compared with the rest of the tool.
  • • Support is slow, including for long-standing customers.
  • • Starter is $9 and Premium $18 per user on annual billing — reviewers on small teams call that steep for time tracking.

One question decides this: do you bill clients?

Free covers a limited number of users. Starter is $9 per licence a month. Premium is $14 — but read the asterisk, because that applies to your first year of Premium annual and renews at $18. Enterprise is quoted. Budget the renewal rather than the introduction. A ten-person team on Premium pays $1,680 in year one and $2,160 in year two, a 29% step. What is unusual, and worth crediting, is that Toggl prints this on the page instead of leaving you to find it on an invoice.

The real Premium price is $18, which is exactly twice Starter; the $14 is an introductory rate. The more useful thing about this pricing is where the paid line falls, because it is unusually clean. The free tier is capable enough that a great many teams never need to pay at all, and what sits behind the paywall is the client-billing layer: budgets, billable rates, profitability. So the question is binary rather than gradual.

If you invoice by time, Starter pays for itself almost immediately — at $108 a licence a year, it costs less than an hour and a half of billable time at a $75 rate, and the first unbilled hour it catches has already covered it. If you track time for your own discipline rather than to invoice anyone, resist the upgrade; the free plan is genuinely the product. Premium's step above Starter is the team-management layer, and at $18 renewal it is double the price — so it needs to earn itself on something specific rather than on 'more features'.

Ask what you would use that Starter lacks, in one sentence, before you take it. The cost nobody puts in the spreadsheet is adoption. Toggl is about as frictionless as time tracking gets, but the habit still has to be built, and a team that stops logging after three weeks has bought nothing at any tier. If you are rolling this out to other people, budget the fortnight of nagging alongside the licences — it is the part that actually determines whether the data is worth having.

How to actually use Toggl

Answer one question before you look at plans: do you bill anyone by the hour? If yes, Starter is the plan — budgets, rates and profitability are exactly what sits behind the paywall, and one recovered unbilled hour covers the year. If no, stay free; the free tier is a real product rather than a trial and a great many teams never need more. Start it on one person or one project rather than the whole team.

The thing that fails in time tracking is never the software, it is the habit — so find out whether the logging survives a busy week before you buy licences for everyone. If you take Premium, put the renewal in your calendar now. The $14 is a first-year rate and it renews at $18, which for ten people is $1,680 becoming $2,160. It is stated on the page rather than hidden, but it is still the number that will surprise you a year from now.

The natural comparison is Harvest or Clockify — the classic trio — Clockify undercuts on price, Harvest bundles invoicing; Toggl wins on tracking UX and reporting depth. Weigh it against Harvest on the job you actually need done.

Frequently asked questions

What does Toggl Track cost?

Starter is $9 per licence a month and Premium $14, with a free tier for a limited number of users. The catch worth knowing before you commit: the $14 Premium rate is a first-year price on annual billing, and it renews at $18. Read on 17 August 2026.

What is the real cost in year two?

About 29% more on Premium: $18 per licence a month instead of $14. For a ten-person team that is $2,160 a year rather than $1,680. Toggl states this on the pricing page under the plan, so it is a disclosed introductory rate rather than a surprise — but it is exactly the kind of line that gets dropped from comparison tables.

Is the free plan enough for a small team?

It covers a limited number of users with time tracking, which is genuinely enough for a freelancer or a pair. What it does not include is the budgeting and profitability reporting that make it a business tool — those start on Starter and Premium respectively. If you bill clients from the data, you are on a paid plan.

What should I weigh before buying Operations & Workflow software?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.
