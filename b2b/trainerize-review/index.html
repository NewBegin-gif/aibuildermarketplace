The add-ons are flat, so they hurt small coaches most

The client ladder looks gentle: free for one client, Grow around $9 for two, Pro scaling by client count from about $23 at five clients to roughly $275 at two hundred, and Studio Plus around $248 per location. Per client that is $4.60 at the bottom and $1.38 at the top, so the ladder itself is fair and gets better as you grow (2026). The add-ons are where the real bill lives, and their shape is the thing to understand.

Nutrition is $20 to $45 a month, payments $10, video coaching $10, and a branded app is about $169 one-time plus $99 a year. Crucially, those are flat: they cost the same whether you coach five people or two hundred. A working trainer switches on nutrition, payments and video, which is $40 to $65 a month regardless of tier. Run that through the ladder and the distortion is severe at the bottom and mild at the top.

At five clients your $23 plan becomes $63 to $88 — the add-ons are two to three times the subscription. At fifty clients, roughly $120 becomes $160 to $185, about a third to a half more. At two hundred, $275 becomes $315 to $340, only 15 to 25% more. The sticker price is most misleading for exactly the coach with the least money, which is worth saying plainly. The version that makes it decidable is software cost as a share of what you bill.

A coach charging $100 a month per client bills $500 at five clients and pays $63 to $88 for the platform — 13 to 18% of revenue, which is a lot. The same coach at fifty clients bills $5,000 and pays around $165, or 3.3%. So this tool is expensive while you are building and cheap once you have built, and the sensible move early on is to run without the add-ons you are not yet charging for.

Against that, the client experience and the wider ABC ecosystem are genuinely mature, and that matters more than the arithmetic suggests: churn-prone coaches lose far more revenue to a clunky client app than to a fair subscription. Price the stack you will actually coach with rather than the tier, and add the branded app only when you have enough clients that your brand is worth $169 plus $99 a year.

How to actually use Trainerize

Price the stack, not the tier. The plan scales with client count, but nutrition ($20-45), payments ($10) and video coaching ($10) are flat add-ons that cost the same at five clients as at two hundred — so at five clients they are two to three times your subscription. Work out your real monthly total before you commit. Turn on only what you charge for. If nutrition planning is not part of your package yet, do not subscribe to it; add each module the month it starts appearing on a client invoice.

That single discipline is the difference between 13% of revenue and 3%. Use the free single-client tier or Grow to test the part that actually keeps clients: the app they see. Put one real client through a full week — programme, check-in, message, progress photo — and watch it from their phone rather than your dashboard. Leave the branded app until you have the client numbers to justify $169 up front and $99 a year.

The natural comparison is TrueCoach or Everfit — the coaching-platform peer set — all use client-tiers plus add-ons; compare your fully-dressed stack price, not entry rows.
