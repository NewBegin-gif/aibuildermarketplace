What Trainual does

Documents SOPs and runs new-hire onboarding, with AI that drafts and updates training docs from your own account context. Not publicly priced: third-party sources put Core near $249/month for up to 10 seats billed annually, plus a reported one-time implementation fee around $1,000.

Pricing (2026)

Not publicly listed / quote-based; third-party sources put Core around $249/mo for up to 10 seats (billed annually, extra seats ~$3-5 each), higher tiers ~$319-399/mo, with a one-time implementation fee reported near $1,000 (2026). Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Priced in seat bands with a setup fee, so it's a real commitment for a small team — the value only lands once you have enough repeatable processes and hires to justify systematizing them. Under a handful of staff, a shared doc may do.

When the paid plan is worth it

Trainual's pricing page lists no free tier and publishes no rates, routing buyers to a demo instead, so the real decision is which paid tier rather than free versus paid. Core is positioned for teams that mainly want documentation and search, and its caps are the ones to check first: video storage of 2 GB and version history of 30 days. Pro earns its place when training has to be personalised and provable, adding individual training paths, an org chart, video storage of 15 GB, version history of 90 days and e-signatures at 300/yr. Premium is a compliance and brand purchase: unlimited video storage, unlimited version history, custom domain and SSO. What upgrading demonstrably does not fix is the content itself. No tier writes your processes for you, and the vendor charges a separate one-time implementation fee of $1,000 for the guided build. Higher tiers also buy more tracking, not more completion, so a team that ignores assignments on Core will keep ignoring them on Premium.

How to actually use Trainual

The fastest route to something usable is one published subject, not a full playbook. From the left-hand menu open the Content page, click "Create subject" in the top right and fill out the prompts; the subject lands as a draft, which you build out with topics and steps, then set the content status dropdown to "Published" and choose either "Publish as is" or "Finish all and publish". Starting from the library of 500+ customizable templates avoids the blank page. Only after that add people, via Account, then "Manage users", then the "Add teammate" dropdown, entering full name, email address and permission level. That order matters because the vendor's own guidance is to add people when you are ready to create content and again when you are ready to assign it. The usual stall is inviting the whole company first: seats become usable immediately and are prorated, while seat reductions only apply at the start of the next billing cycle. Teams buying guided implementation should plan around a 4-6 week engagement. Frequently asked questions

Does Trainual replace my current setup or sit on top of it?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.

Who should not use Trainual?

Skip it if you're a team of two or three — a well-organized Notion or Google Doc captures your processes without the seat-band pricing. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
