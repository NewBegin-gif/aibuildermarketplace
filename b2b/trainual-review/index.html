Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 18 Sep 2026

No prices at all. The page lists four plans — Core, Pro, Premium and Enterprise, grouped under a Training, an Operations and a Performance suite — and every one of them carries a “Get a demo” button where a rate would be. The only figure the page publishes is a one-time implementation fee of $1,000, stated in its own FAQ alongside a 4–6 week engagement with an implementation specialist.

The FAQ also answers the sizing question directly: “Trainual is best suited for companies with 25 - 1000 employees”, and says pricing varies with team size and the features you need. When the paid plan is worth it

Trainual's pricing page lists no free tier and publishes no rates, routing buyers to a demo instead, so the real decision is which paid tier rather than free versus paid. Core is positioned for teams that mainly want documentation and search, and its caps are the ones to check first: video storage of 2 GB and version history of 30 days. Pro earns its place when training has to be personalised and provable, adding individual training paths, an org chart, video storage of 15 GB, version history of 90 days and e-signatures at 300/yr.

Premium is a compliance and brand purchase: unlimited video storage, unlimited version history, custom domain and SSO. What upgrading demonstrably does not fix is the content itself. No tier writes your processes for you, and the vendor charges a separate one-time implementation fee of $1,000 for the guided build. Higher tiers also buy more tracking, not more completion, so a team that ignores assignments on Core will keep ignoring them on Premium.

How to actually use Trainual

The fastest route to something usable is one published subject, not a full playbook. From the left-hand menu open the Content page, click "Create subject" in the top right and fill out the prompts. The subject lands as a draft, which you build out with topics and steps. Then set the content status dropdown to "Published" and choose either "Publish as is" or "Finish all and publish". Starting from the library of 500+ customizable templates avoids the blank page.

Only after that add people, via Account, then "Manage users", then the "Add teammate" dropdown, entering full name, email address and permission level. That order matters because the vendor's own guidance is to add people when you are ready to create content and again when you are ready to assign it. The usual stall is inviting the whole company first: seats become usable immediately and are prorated, while seat reductions only apply at the start of the next billing cycle. Teams buying guided implementation should plan around a 4-6 week engagement.

Frequently asked questions

What does Trainual cost?

Nobody outside a sales call knows, and that is the honest answer. All four plans — Core, Pro, Premium and Enterprise — sit behind a “Get a demo” button on trainual.com/pricing, read on 18 September 2026, and the page publishes no seat rate, no floor and no range. The single figure it does publish is a one-time implementation fee of $1,000. The FAQ says pricing varies with team size and the features you need, which tells you the shape of the quote without telling you the number.

Can I trial it before committing?

No. Trainual's pricing FAQ asks “Can I try Trainual before committing to a subscription?” and answers it by inviting you to book a demo so the team can walk through your needs and decision timeline (read 18 September 2026). There is no free tier and no self-serve trial on the page. For a tool whose value depends entirely on whether your team actually uses it, that means the first real test of adoption happens after you have signed and paid the $1,000 implementation fee.

Am I too small for it?

Possibly, and Trainual says so itself rather than making you guess. Its FAQ states the product is best suited for companies with 25 to 1000 employees, while noting some companies outside that band use it too (read 18 September 2026). Put that next to the mandatory $1,000 implementation fee and the 4–6 week specialist engagement it buys, and a ten-person team is being told twice that it is not the intended buyer — once by the sizing note, once by the onboarding cost.

What should I weigh before buying Operations & Workflow software?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.
