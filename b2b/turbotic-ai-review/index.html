Turbotic AI Review: How I Talk to My Computer to Buy Back My Free Time

I use Turbotic to make all my different websites and tools talk to each other. Instead of clicking around in complicated settings for hours, I just type a normal sentence — and, as we say in Dutch, the tool "regelt het": it simply sorts it out for me. It is the ultimate bridge that lets me close my laptop early.

The problem with too many websites

When you run a couple of websites like I do with aibuildermarketplace.com and theweeklyaiedge.com, you quickly notice that you spend half your day just moving stuff around. For example, when someone leaves a message on one site, I want a note in my inbox, a row added to my spreadsheet, and a message sent to my sales tool, Close CRM.

Normally, connecting all those different apps is a giant headache. You get stuck in tech settings, links break, and you end up staring at an error screen for hours. Turbotic completely fixes this. Its whole goal is: you think of something, you type it in normal language, and the tool builds it for you.

1. No tech skills required — just talk to it

With older tools, you had to be a programmer or click through messy, complicated menus. With Turbotic, I just open a chat box and type exactly what I want, like I'm texting a friend.

For instance, I can just type: "When a new partner link comes into HubSpot, tell my sales team in Slack and put it neatly in a Google Sheet."

Turbotic reads that, understands what I mean, and connects the apps in a few seconds. It handles all the logins and tricky background connections itself. It's perfect for me because it opens up the power of automation without needing to stare at code all day.

2. It fixes its own mistakes

The thing I used to hate most about automating things is that links break all the time. A software company changes a small button, and suddenly your whole setup crashes. You wake up to a broken system and have to spend your morning troubleshooting.

Turbotic is much smarter. It never stops watching over my tasks. If a connection drops or an app behaves weirdly, it doesn't just crash. It automatically tries again, figures out a different way around the problem, or gently lets me know. This means I rarely have to log in to fix things, which gives me massive peace of mind.

3. Connecting everything with my AI agent 'Victor'

Because Turbotic doesn't have silly limits on which apps it can connect, I can link it to my custom setup. On my Hostinger VPS server, I have my own AI assistant running, named Victor. Victor translates pages into 35 different languages in the background while I am out with the family.

Using Turbotic, I can connect Victor directly to all my other tools. If Victor finishes a new translation, Turbotic instantly grabs it, posts it on the right website, and updates my logs. It makes my whole digital network feel like a real company with multiple employees, even though it's just me and my laptop.

Honest pros and cons

As always, both sides of the story — here's where Turbotic genuinely changed my week, and where you should keep your eyes open.

What I love

  • You just type what you want. No flow-charts, no menus — a plain sentence becomes a working automation in seconds.
  • It watches and heals itself. Broken connections get retried or rerouted before they become my morning problem.
  • No artificial app limits. It happily talks to my own custom AI agent on a VPS, not just the big-name apps.
  • It removes the "human bridge" role. I'm no longer the person manually copying names from a form into a spreadsheet.

Where to be a little careful

  • You hand over the keys. Turbotic manages logins between your apps — take a moment to think about which accounts you connect.
  • Trust, but verify at the start. I let every new automation run a few times while keeping an eye on the results before I fully forget about it.
  • Plain language still needs clear thinking. A vague sentence gets you a vague automation — know what you actually want to happen.
