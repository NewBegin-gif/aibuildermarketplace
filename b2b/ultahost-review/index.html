$4.13 on the banner, $3.44 on the card

Everything here turns on which term the price on screen assumes, and the page picks one for you: it opens on the two-year term, where the cut runs deepest at the bottom of the range. Pay monthly instead and Starter returns to its $6.89 list, twice what the card in front of you shows. Two numbers on the same page disagree, and the cheaper one is the real one. The banner advertises 'Starting at $4.13/mo', which is $6.89 less the stated 40% two-year discount.

The Starter card at that same term shows $3.44, which is 50% off. The discount is not uniform across the range either: Basic is 43.6% off, Business 39.2% and Pro 38.8%. So 'Save 40%' is an average rather than a rate, the entry plan is cut hardest, and the number to trust is the one on the card you are ordering. The renewal question is now answered on the card, and the answer is the good one.

Each plan reads 'Billed for 24 month term. Renews at $3.44/mo for 2 years. Cancel anytime.' Four years of Starter is therefore $165.12 at a flat $3.44 a month — not the cheap first term and dear second that budget hosting usually means. Our August reading recorded renewals at $4.39 and $6.89; the page no longer says that, so that warning is withdrawn. Treat the visit ceilings as the real constraint rather than the price.

Starter's ~10,000 visits a month is a number one post that travels will pass, and stepping to Basic's ~15,000 costs 51% more.

Which term should I pick?

The discount ladder rewards length steeply (30%, 40%, 50% for one, two and three years), so the arithmetic favours the longest term you are confident about. The counterweight is that hosting is easy to outgrow or regret, and three years is a long time to be wrong — the two-year rate is where most of the discount already sits.

What should I weigh before buying IT & Productivity software?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.
