UniTel Voice Review (2026)Extra Users Cost $9.99

Communication & VoiceResearched assessment · by Daniel HaketContent updated 2026-09-25Prices checked 2026-09-09View UniTel Voice in the Atlas →

UniTel Voice is a virtual phone layer, not a full unified-comms suite — you won't find the deep team messaging, video meetings and contact-center features pricier platforms bundle.

Best for: solopreneurs and small teams that want a professional toll-free or local number, auto-attendant and call routing on their existing phones — no hardware.
Who should skip it: if you need integrated video meetings, team chat and a full contact center, or you have no need for a separate business line at all.
Price: Solo $9.99/mo (1 user, unlimited minutes, one phone number); annual billing $6.99/mo · full pricing as we read it on 9 Sep 2026 ↓

Pricing (2026)

Annual billing takes 30% off, making them $6.99, $13.99 and $20.99 a month. Solo is one user, Team carries unlimited users, and Office includes three with additional users at $9.99 each a month. The plan set we recorded in August — Start-Up, Unlimited at $24.99 and Office-Max at $69.99 — is no longer offered on the page.

Frequently asked questions

What does UniTel Voice cost?

$9.99 a month for Solo, $19.99 for Team and $29.99 for Office, or $6.99, $13.99 and $20.99 on annual billing, which takes 30% off. Every plan carries one phone number and unlimited minutes, with a 30-day money-back guarantee and US-based support. Read on their own pricing page on 9 September 2026 — the Start-Up, Unlimited and Office-Max plans our August capture recorded are no longer offered.

What does it cost for a team rather than one person?

The plan price is not the team price. Additional users on the Office plan are $9.99 per user per month on top of the plan fee, and you can add them at any time after signing up. For a five-person office that is the plan plus four extra seats, so work from the seat count rather than the headline.

Are the minutes really unlimited?

The three plan cards all say "Unlimited Minutes", and that is what you are buying. Their pricing FAQ muddies it: asked what happens if you go over the minutes on your plan, it answers that you can "simply pay 3.9¢ per minute for additional minutes on any plan". That same FAQ still refers to an Office-Max plan the page no longer sells, so it looks like text left over from their older metered plans — but they have not cleaned it up, so ask support to confirm before you prepay for a year.

Is there really no contract?

That is what the page states, alongside a 30-day money-back guarantee — if you are not happy within thirty days they refund without questions. Annual billing is optional and is what buys the 30%-plus discount, so the trade is the usual one: commit for a year to pay less, or stay monthly and keep the exit.

What should I weigh before buying Communication & Voice software?

Generally yes, since this category is built for it, but the details that bite are timezone handling, how per-seat cost scales as the team spreads, and whether numbers, routing or coverage exist in the countries your people actually sit in. Availability varies far more by country than the marketing pages suggest, so check your specific regions before committing.
