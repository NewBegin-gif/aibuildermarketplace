At five users, two plans cost the same

Four plans and no contracts: Start-Up $9.99 a month, Unlimited $24.99, Office $29.99 and Office-Max $69.99, with a stated saving of over 30% on annual billing and a 30-day money-back guarantee. Support is US-based and included. The line to read before you size a team is the one about extra people: additional users on the Office plans are $9.99 per user a month on top of the plan, addable at any time after signup.

Work that through and you find a clean crossover. Office with five users is $29.99 plus four extras at $9.99, which is $69.95 — five cents under Office-Max at $69.99. Below five people, Office plus add-on users is the cheaper route; at five they are identical; above five it depends entirely on what Office-Max includes, which is the question to ask. If Office-Max carries a larger or unlimited user allowance, everything past five people belongs there and the add-on route stops making sense.

The gap between Unlimited at $24.99 and Office at $29.99 is only five dollars, and the names suggest they differ on different axes — minutes versus users and features. Our dossier does not record what separates them, so ask; a five-dollar difference between two middle-tier plans is usually a decision about your calling pattern rather than your budget. Annual billing takes over 30% off, which on Office is roughly $21 a month or about $252 a year, and there are no contracts either way — so the annual discount is a genuine saving rather than a lock-in.

What decides whether any of this is the right category is scope. This is a virtual phone layer, not a unified-communications suite: you will not find the deep team messaging, video meetings and contact-centre features that pricier platforms bundle. For a solo founder or a small team that needs a professional number, an auto-attendant and sensible call routing, that focus is exactly the point and the price reflects it. For a larger organisation hoping to run everything on one platform, it will feel thin no matter which tier you buy.

How to actually use UniTel Voice

Count your users before you pick a plan, because the crossover is sharp. Additional users on the Office plans are $9.99 each on top, so five people on Office is $69.95 — effectively identical to Office-Max at $69.99. Under five, build up from Office; at five or more, ask what Office-Max includes before you keep adding seats. Ask what separates Unlimited ($24.99) from Office ($29.99) too. Five dollars between two middle tiers usually reflects a difference in minutes versus users and features, and which one suits you depends on your calling pattern rather than your budget.

Use the 30-day money-back window to test the thing that actually matters: call quality and the auto-attendant, on the devices and networks your team really uses. There are no contracts, so annual billing at over 30% off is a straightforward saving once you know the calls hold up — take it in month two rather than month one.

The natural comparison is Grasshopper or Google Voice — Grasshopper is a close, pricier equivalent; Google Voice is cheaper but barer — UniTel's Unlimited tier is the value play.

Frequently asked questions

What does UniTel Voice cost?

$9.99 a month for Start-Up, $24.99 for Unlimited, $29.99 for Office and $69.99 for Office-Max, with over 30% off if you pay annually and a 30-day money-back guarantee. Read on their own pricing page on 17 August 2026, unchanged from our July and August captures.

What does it cost for a team rather than one person?

The plan price is not the team price. Additional users on the Office plan are $9.99 per user per month on top of the plan fee, and you can add them at any time after signing up. For a five-person office that is the plan plus four extra seats, so work from the seat count rather than the headline.

Is there really no contract?

That is what the page states, alongside a 30-day money-back guarantee — if you are not happy within thirty days they refund without questions. Annual billing is optional and is what buys the 30%-plus discount, so the trade is the usual one: commit for a year to pay less, or stay monthly and keep the exit.
