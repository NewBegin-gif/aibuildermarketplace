What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Growth covers you to 2,000 orders a month for $9.99. The next rung is Scale at $199. There is nothing in between, so the store that grows from 1,900 orders to 2,100 does not pay a little more — it pays twenty times more, for a feature set the vendor says is identical on every plan. What Scale actually buys at that price is priority chat, guided onboarding and earlier access to features.

The page also argues with itself in two places: the Scale card says billed monthly while the comparison table one screen below says Scale is annual only, and the Growth card is headed FREE above a volume list running to 2,000 orders while the Shopify listing charges $9.99 from the 51st. And checkout upsells are still Shopify Plus only, so on a standard Shopify plan the checkout stage of the funnel you are buying stays shut.

What Upsell.com does

Shopify upsell app, formerly ReConvert: AI-driven cart, checkout and post-purchase upsell and cross-sell funnels, frequently-bought-together and product page recommendations, A/B testing and analytics with segmentation. The free plan is development stores only, and the three published tiers — $4.99, $9.99 and $19.99 a month — stop at 200 orders or $200 of upsell revenue; above that the listing only says charges rise.

Where the ratings disagree. The praise and the complaint are about different things and both are worth reading. Merchants describe the funnel builder and the support warmly and in detail; the sharpest complaint is not about the product at all but about a price the app page does not publish. That is the same gap the pricing section flags — the listed tiers stop at 200 orders — showing up in a merchant's invoice rather than in a table.

When the paid plan is worth it

The three listed rungs are the same product at three volumes, and the arithmetic comes out flat: $4.99 for 50 orders, $9.99 for 100 and $19.99 for 200 all work out at about ten cents an order, whichever of the two meters — orders or upsell revenue — you reach first. On the published part of the ladder there is therefore nothing to weigh: buy the rung your volume needs and move on.

Two things more money will not fix. Checkout upsells are gated on Shopify Plus, which is a Shopify subscription and not an app tier, so no amount paid here opens that stage of the funnel for a standard plan. And the ladder stops being published above Business: the listing says charges rise with volume and points at a table it does not show, so the tier you will actually sit on once the app is working is the one whose price you cannot look up before you install it.

How to actually use Upsell.com

Install on a development store first — that is the only place the free plan applies, and it is enough to learn the drag-and-drop editor without a bill. Then build one funnel rather than the whole journey. The app spans the product page, the cart, checkout and post-purchase, and switching all of it on at once leaves you with a conversion number nobody can attribute. Leave checkout for last whatever you do: the listing marks that stage Shopify Plus only, so on a standard plan it is not yours to switch on.

Set the A/B test when you set the offer, not afterwards, because retro-fitting a control costs you the first weeks of data. Then watch both meters, orders and upsell revenue, since whichever climbs faster is the one that decides your bill. The vendor runs a help centre at help.upsell.com, which is organised as Upsell.com for Shopify, Upsell.com for Wix and Integrations.

Frequently asked questions

What does it cost me once I pass 2,000 orders a month?

$199 a month, and there is no step between. Growth is $9.99 up to 2,000 monthly store orders or $2,000 of upsell revenue, whichever is hit first; the next tier, Scale, is $199 and runs to 10,000. So the bill can go up twentyfold for the order that crosses the line, and the vendor's own comparison says every feature is on every plan, so nothing new arrives with it — Scale adds priority chat, guided onboarding and earlier feature rollouts. If your volume sits anywhere near the boundary, price the year on Scale before you install, not on Growth.

Is the metering on orders or on the revenue the upsells make?

Both, and the one that trips first wins. Every tier is described as a ceiling on "monthly store orders or upsell revenue (higher of the two)", so a store doing 400 orders a month that earns $2,400 from upsells is charged on the $2,400. That detail matters most at the bottom: the free tier stops at 50 orders or $50 of upsell revenue, which a single successful offer can exhaust in a week. Read the ceiling as whichever number your shop hits first, not as an order count.

Is Scale billed monthly or annually?

The page says both, on the same screen, and we could not resolve it from the page. The Scale card reads "starting at $199/mo, Billed monthly"; toggle the page to Annual and it reads "$149/mo, Billed annually, $199 monthly", which is a clean 25% off. But the comparison table below the cards lists Scale's billing as "Annual only" while giving Growth "Monthly or annual". The Shopify listing adds its own version, "$199/month or $149/year and save 94%", which is neither. Ask which of the three applies before you commit to a year.

Can I run the checkout upsells on a standard Shopify plan?

No. Checkout upsells need Shopify Plus, which the app says plainly in its listing and the pricing page repeats twice — the checkout placement is marked "Checkout (Shopify Plus)", and Elite lists "Checkout upsells *for Plus stores" as one of the things it adds. Everything else in the funnel does work on a standard plan: product page, cart page and drawer, post-purchase one-click, thank-you page and order status page. So the gap is one stage of six, but it is the stage with the highest intent, and no tier of this app opens it for you.

What should I weigh before buying Growth & Revenue software?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Small teams get the most out of this category when one clear problem is already costing real hours or revenue; buying ahead of that just adds cost and another login. Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.
