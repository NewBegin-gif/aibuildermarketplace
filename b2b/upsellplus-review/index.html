Four tiers, billed in USD, with a 14-day free trial on the paid ones. Free runs one offer, opens all twelve upsell types plus analytics, and allows up to $500 in upsell revenue. Launch is $49 a month or $500 a year (the listing says that saves 15%) and also allows up to $500 of app-generated revenue a month, adding cart, pop-up and drawer upsells, product page and post-purchase upsells, multivariate A/B testing, progress bars and free gifts.

The Shopify Plus tier is $119 a month or $1,149 a year (save 20%), raises the ceiling to $2,000 a month and adds checkout customization, checkout upsells, thank-you page customization and AI recommendations. Scale is $199 a month or $1,899 a year (save 20%) for up to $5,000 of upsell revenue a month, live support and multivariate A/B testing. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The $49 buys offers and features, not headroom — headroom starts at $119. The listing also never says what happens when a store passes Scale's $5,000 ceiling, which is precisely the moment the app is working, so ask before you build a checkout flow around it. And read the feature list closely: checkout upsells are marked Plus-only, meaning your store has to be on Shopify Plus, so on a standard Shopify plan the checkout half of an app called Checkout Upsell is not what you are buying.

What UpsellPlus does

Upsells, cross-sells and free gifts across Shopify product pages, cart, checkout, post-purchase, customer accounts and POS, with AI recommendations and A/B testing in 23 storefront languages. Free plan runs one offer; Launch is $49/month, the Shopify Plus tier $119 and Scale $199 — each capped by how much upsell revenue it may generate ($500, $2,000 and $5,000 a month).

What the money actually buys

The ladder is priced by how much revenue the app is allowed to generate for you: $500 a month on Free and on Launch at $49, $2,000 on the $119 tier, $5,000 on Scale at $199. Read that column before the feature lists, because the ceilings differ far more than the features do. What $49 adds over free is offers and tooling — cart, pop-up and drawer upsells, product page and post-purchase offers, multivariate A/B testing, progress bars and free gifts, with a 14-day trial.

What $119 adds is the checkout: checkout customization, checkout upsells, thank-you page customization and AI recommendations, plus four times the revenue ceiling. Scale at $199 adds live support and a $5,000 ceiling. Annual billing takes 15% off Launch ($500 a year) and 20% off the two upper tiers ($1,149 and $1,899). What the listing does not say is what happens when you outgrow $5,000 a month — worth asking in writing before you build a checkout flow on top of it.

How to actually use UpsellPlus

Start on the free plan and treat it as a real trial rather than a demo: it runs one offer, opens all twelve upsell types and the analytics, and allows up to $500 of upsell revenue. That is enough to learn whether your customers take an offer at all, and where they take it — product page, cart drawer, post-purchase or thank-you page. Before you pay, check two things about your own store.

First, are you on Shopify Plus? The listing marks checkout upsells as Plus-only, and they are the reason most people look at an app with Checkout Upsell in its name. Second, how much upsell revenue do you expect in a month? The Launch plan at $49 carries the same $500 ceiling as the free plan, so the tier that actually buys you headroom is $119 or $199, not $49.
