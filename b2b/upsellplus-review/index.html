Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 19 Sep 2026

(listing and vendor pricing page re-verified 2026-09-19 on apps.shopify.com/upsellpluscheckout, USD; the Scale ladder confirmed in writing by UpsellPlus support on 2026-09-21) Fixed monthly tiers based on the revenue generated through UpsellPlus offers, with no percentage commission. The listing shows four: Free runs one offer, opens all twelve upsell types plus analytics, and allows up to $500 in upsell revenue. Launch is $49 a month or $500 a year (saves 15%) and allows the same $500 of app-generated revenue a month, adding cart, pop-up and drawer upsells, product page and post-purchase upsells, multivariate A/B testing, progress bars and free gifts.

The tier named Shopify Plus is $119 a month or $1,149 a year (save 20%), raises the ceiling to $2,000 a month and adds checkout customization, checkout upsells, thank-you page customization and AI recommendations. Scale is $199 a month or $1,899 a year (save 20%) for up to $5,000 a month, live support and multivariate A/B testing. 14-day free trial on the paid tiers; charges billed every 30 days.

The vendor's own page adds two things the listing does not carry: a fifth Enterprise tier with no published price, sold through a demo, and a Managed Service at an extra $499 a month. That page contradicts itself on Scale - the plan card and the listing both say up to $5,000 a month, while its comparison table puts Scale under Unlimited - so we asked the vendor.

UpsellPlus support answered on 21 September 2026 that Scale is a ladder rather than a single price: $199 up to $5,000 of upsell revenue a month, $299 up to $10,000, $399 up to $15,000, $499 up to $20,000, $699 up to $30,000, $799 up to $40,000, and an Unlimited plan at $999 with no ceiling. A store that goes past $5,000 is moved automatically to the $299 tier, which covers up to $10,000.

So the plan card is the accurate one and the comparison table is wrong, and the amounts above $199 appear nowhere on the published pricing page (figures from UpsellPlus support, 21 September 2026). Hitting a ceiling is not a stop: their FAQ says you automatically upgrade to the next tier so upsells are not interrupted, so the bill rises by itself. Their FAQ also states Shopify Plus is NOT required except for checkout customization and checkout upsells, which is a Shopify restriction; cart, product page, cart drawer, thank-you and post-purchase offers work without it. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The $49 buys offers and features, not headroom — headroom starts at $119. And crossing a ceiling is not something you decide: their FAQ says you are automatically upgraded to the next tier so the offers keep running, so a good week moves the invoice on its own. Read the feature list closely too, because the tier named Shopify Plus is not the same thing as the requirement — their FAQ says Plus is needed only for checkout customization and checkout upsells, so on a standard Shopify store the checkout half of a checkout-upsell app is not what you are buying.

The last thing to know is at the top, and it is not on their pricing page. The plan card says Scale stops at $5,000 a month while the comparison table on that same page says Unlimited; we asked, and UpsellPlus support confirmed on 21 September 2026 that Scale is a revenue ladder - $199 up to $5,000 of upsell revenue a month, $299 up to $10,000, $399 up to $15,000, $499 up to $20,000, $699 up to $30,000, $799 up to $40,000, and an Unlimited plan at $999 with no ceiling - and that passing $5,000 moves a store to the $299 tier on its own.

That is better news than the Enterprise tier above it suggests, but it means the number you are quoted at signup is the floor of a ladder whose higher rungs are not published anywhere.

What the money actually buys

The ladder is priced by how much revenue the app is allowed to generate for you: $500 a month on Free and on Launch at $49, $2,000 on the $119 tier, $5,000 on Scale at $199. Read that column before the feature lists, because the ceilings differ far more than the features do. What $49 adds over free is offers and tooling — cart, pop-up and drawer upsells, product page and post-purchase offers, multivariate A/B testing, progress bars and free gifts, with a 14-day trial.

What $119 adds is the checkout: checkout customization, checkout upsells, thank-you page customization and AI recommendations, plus four times the revenue ceiling. Scale at $199 adds live support and a $5,000 ceiling. Annual billing takes 15% off Launch ($500 a year) and 20% off the two upper tiers ($1,149 and $1,899). What happens when you outgrow $5,000 a month is not on the listing or the pricing page, so we asked.

UpsellPlus support answered on 21 September 2026 that Scale keeps climbing in published steps - $199 up to $5,000 of upsell revenue a month, $299 up to $10,000, $399 up to $15,000, $499 up to $20,000, $699 up to $30,000, $799 up to $40,000, and an Unlimited plan at $999 with no ceiling - and that crossing $5,000 moves you to $299 automatically. Budget against that ladder rather than against the $199 on the card, because the app decides when you step up, not you.

How to actually use UpsellPlus

Start on the free plan and treat it as a real trial rather than a demo: it runs one offer, opens all twelve upsell types and the analytics, and allows up to $500 of upsell revenue. That is enough to learn whether your customers take an offer at all, and where they take it — product page, cart drawer, post-purchase or thank-you page. Before you pay, check two things about your own store.

First, are you on Shopify Plus? The listing marks checkout upsells as Plus-only, and they are the reason most people look at an app with Checkout Upsell in its name. Second, how much upsell revenue do you expect in a month? The Launch plan at $49 carries the same $500 ceiling as the free plan, so the tier that actually buys you headroom is $119 or $199, not $49. Work out that number first and the choice of plan makes itself. Read on apps.shopify.com/upsellpluscheckout, 31 August 2026.

Frequently asked questions

What happens when I pass the revenue ceiling on my plan?

You are moved up a tier and the bill follows, without you doing anything. Their FAQ states it plainly: hit the upsell revenue limit and you automatically upgrade to the next tier so that your upsells are not interrupted. Read alongside the ladder, that is the whole pricing model in one sentence — $500 a month of upsell revenue on Free and on the $49 Launch plan, $2,000 on the $119 tier, $5,000 on Scale at $199. Crossing $500 in a good week therefore moves you from free to $49 or from $49 to $119 on its own. It is a defensible design, since the alternative is switching your offers off mid-month, but it means the invoice is driven by your own success and not by a decision you make.

Is Scale capped at $5,000 a month or is it unlimited?

Their own pricing page says both, and we could not resolve it on 19 September 2026. The Scale plan card says up to $5,000 a month in upsell revenue, and the App Store listing agrees. The comparison table further down that same page puts Scale in the 'Unlimited' column for the upsell revenue limit. One of the two is wrong, and the difference decides what happens to a store doing $8,000 of upsell revenue a month: either it sits on Scale at $199 or it lands in Enterprise pricing. Given that the tier below auto-upgrades when its ceiling is crossed, ask which it is in writing before you build around the $199 number.

Does the App Store listing show the whole price ladder?

No — it stops one tier short. The listing shows Free, Launch at $49 a month or $500 a year, the tier named Shopify Plus at $119 or $1,149, and Scale at $199 or $1,899. The vendor's own pricing page carries a fifth option the listing never mentions: Enterprise, with no published price, sold through a demo and described as a custom plan with an audit, a dedicated customer success manager and monthly performance reviews. The same page also prices a Managed Service at an extra $499 a month on top of Scale. If you are sizing this against a competitor on listing prices alone, you are comparing against a ladder that is missing its top and its add-on.

The $119 tier is called Shopify Plus. Do I need to be on Shopify Plus to use this?

Not for most of it, and their FAQ is unusually direct about why. It says Shopify Plus is not required except when you want to customize your checkout with recommendations and customizations, that this is a Shopify restriction they cannot get around, and that offers on the cart page, product page, cart drawer, thank-you page and post-purchase page all work without it. So a standard Shopify store can buy the $119 tier for its higher revenue ceiling and its AI recommendations, and simply not get the checkout half. The Scale tier at $199 does state Shopify Plus is required. The tier name is the confusing part, not the rule.

What should I weigh before buying Growth & Revenue software?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Small teams get the most out of this category when one clear problem is already costing real hours or revenue; buying ahead of that just adds cost and another login. Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.
