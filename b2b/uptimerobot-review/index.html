What UptimeRobot does

Uptime monitoring with a generous free tier — website, port, keyword and API checks down to 60-second intervals, status pages, integrations and mobile apps.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The famous free tier is real — but the paid ladder has quiet extras: extra login seats cost $15-19 per month EACH beyond the included ones, and SMS or voice-call alerts are pay-per-credit on every plan. The Solo tier includes no team seats at all.

When the paid plan is worth it

UptimeRobot's free tier is unusually complete, which changes the upgrade question. The vendor's help centre states the free plan gives 50 monitors, 5-minute checks, status pages and the core monitor types, and explicitly permits business, commercial and revenue-generating use, so cost alone rarely forces a move. Paid tiers earn their keep when detection speed or reporting depth matters. The pricing page lists a 60-second interval on Solo, 30 seconds on Team and 15 seconds on Scale.

History runs longer too: 3 months of retention on free against 12 months on Solo, and 24 months on Team and Scale. That makes the upgrade case strongest for sites where a few minutes of undetected downtime costs money, and weakest for hobby projects and internal tools where a five-minute gap is tolerable. Anyone comparing plans should price them on the vendor's own page rather than from a summary, since the plan cards carry monitor-tier choices that move the headline figure.

Upgrading also does not change what the product is. It runs external checks, so it reports that an endpoint failed rather than why it failed, and a shorter interval surfaces more transient blips, not fewer.

How to actually use UptimeRobot

The documented path to a first working check is short. After logging in, click "+ Add New Monitor" and choose a type; the help centre lists HTTP(s), Keyword, Ping, Port, Heartbeat, DNS, API and UDP. For a standard website check, enter the URL you wish to monitor, which auto-populates the Friendly Name field, though that can be adjusted. Review the alert contact settings and decide which ones should receive notifications, set the monitoring interval, choose the region to monitor from, and click "Create monitor".

The stall points sit around that alert step rather than the monitor itself. A monitor created without reviewing its contacts watches quietly and tells nobody, and on the free plan the 5-minute interval means a brief outage can pass entirely between consecutive checks. Keyword monitors need the keyword specified alongside the URL, so the check is only as reliable as the text chosen. Heartbeat monitors behave differently again: the vendor notes the unique URL is generated only after the monitor is created, so the cron job or job runner has to be edited afterwards.

Frequently asked questions

What does UptimeRobot cost?

Read from their own machine-readable pricing page on 17 August 2026, in euros: Free €0 for 50 monitors at 5-minute checks; Solo from €9 a month billed annually; Team from €35; Scale from €65 for 200 monitors and from €149 for 500. Note the currency — the same numerals circulate as dollars elsewhere, including in our own earlier write-up.

Is the free plan actually usable?

Unusually so: 50 monitors at 5-minute checks with no credit card, and platform API access is included even on Free. What you give up is the check interval and the incident detail — five minutes rather than sixty seconds, and no root-cause commentary on incidents. For a personal project or a handful of sites that is a real plan, not a demo.

What decides which paid tier I need?

Two numbers, and neither is a feature list: how many monitors you run and how often you want them checked. Solo covers 10 or 50 monitors at 60 seconds, Team 100 at 30 seconds with 24-month retention, Scale 200 or 500 at 15 seconds. If your alerting requirement is 'within a minute', that is what you are buying.

How good are UptimeRobot's docs and rate limits?

Read the documentation before you commit, because it is the most honest preview of the product. Look for a quickstart you can actually finish, error responses that are documented rather than guessed at, published rate limits and a clear statement of what happens when you hit them, and a changelog showing the API is still maintained. Vague limits usually mean you will discover them in production.

Who should not use UptimeRobot?

Skip it if you need many teammates in the dashboard (seats are a paid add-on) or heavy SMS alerting — credits stack on top of the subscription. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
