Thirty cents a minute is eighteen dollars an hour

This is quote-oriented and priced for resellers. Agency plans start around $750 a month — the starter tier carries 5 agents and about 2,500 AI voice minutes — with growth tiers advertising unlimited agents and minutes, and platform or service-provider plans from roughly $2,500 a month. Verify current tiers with sales (2026). Divide the entry tier by its allowance and the number that makes this comparable to anything else appears: $750 for 2,500 minutes is $0.30 a minute, or $18 per hour of actual conversation.

That is not cheap automation. It sits close to the cost of the labour it replaces, which is precisely why the product is sold to agencies who spread that $750 across five clients at $150 each, rather than to one business wanting a receptionist bot. If that is you, the entry price buys far more platform than you need and the per-minute arithmetic will not work in your favour. The allowance is also smaller than it sounds.

At a three-minute average call, 2,500 minutes is about 833 calls a month across five agents — roughly 167 calls per agent. For a busy front desk that is a pilot budget, not a production one, so establish what minutes cost beyond the included block before you commit anyone's phone line to it. The growth tiers advertise unlimited minutes; what they cost is not published, and that is the number that decides whether this scales for you.

Then the universal truth of voice AI, which no pricing analysis replaces: demo calls are rehearsed and your callers are not. A demo has a clear line, a cooperative speaker and a predictable request. Your calls have accents, background noise, interruptions, people who change their mind mid-sentence and the caller who wants something the script never anticipated. So pilot it on your real call types and measure containment — the share of calls fully handled without a human — on the mix you actually receive rather than the ones that demo well.

Measure it separately for your hardest category, because that is where a front desk earns its keep and where a failed handover costs you a customer. Get the containment number before you bet the front desk on it, and get the per-minute rate beyond the block before you get the containment number.

How to actually use Vida AI

Work out the per-minute cost before anything else. The $750 starter tier includes about 2,500 minutes, which is $0.30 a minute or $18 an hour of conversation — close enough to the labour it replaces that the business case is not automatic. And ask what minutes cost beyond the block, because 2,500 is roughly 833 three-minute calls across five agents, which is a pilot allowance rather than a production one.

Then pilot on your hardest calls, not your simplest. Demo calls are rehearsed; yours will have accents, background noise, interruptions and people who change their mind halfway through a sentence. Route a real slice of your incoming calls to it and leave the easy category out of the sample deliberately. Measure containment rate — the share of calls fully handled without a human — and measure it per call type rather than as one average.

One good number hiding one terrible one is how front desks get automated badly. And if you are a single business wanting one receptionist bot rather than an agency reselling to clients, price this against smaller tools first: the entry tier here is built to be split across five clients.

The natural comparison is Synthflow or Retell AI — builder-oriented voice-AI platforms — Vida's edge is the reseller/white-label model; compare per-minute economics at your projected volume.
