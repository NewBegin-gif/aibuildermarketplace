What VidPal does

Autonomous content engine for faceless and topic-based channels: it discovers trending stories in your niche, auto-generates short-form videos and carousels with AI voiceovers, and auto-publishes them across Instagram, TikTok, YouTube and more.

What buyers report about VidPal

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run VidPal ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • It runs the whole loop unattended: finds trending stories in your niche, writes the script, generates voiceover and visuals, burns in captions, renders 9:16 and publishes to Instagram, YouTube, TikTok and Facebook on schedule.
  • • Approval is optional rather than a gate, which is the difference between an engine and an editor.
What they complain about
  • • We found no independent user reviews, so there is nothing outside the vendor's own material to judge the product on. In August 2026 we also found no published pricing detail; that has since changed, and VidPal now publishes its plans and credit allowances (see the pricing section above).
  • • Publishing without review is the feature and the risk. Anything the engine gets wrong about a trending story goes out under your name — decide whether you actually want approval off.

Pro's autopilot runs out on day 20

Vidpal's free plan is the trial: 4 lifetime credits, standard-quality exports and a "Made with Vidpal.ai" watermark, no card. On annual billing the paid tiers are Starter $12 a month ($144 a year, 15 credits), Pro $23 ($276, 40 credits), Business $41 ($492, 100 credits) and Enterprise on quote; month to month it is $19, $39 and $69, as VidPal confirmed in writing on 7 October 2026 (the plan cards still show $24, $46 and $82 struck through).

Auto-publish and scheduling start on Pro, so Starter will not post for you. But Pro's own arithmetic undercuts its pitch. One credit buys a Reel, a carousel, an editing upload or ten minutes of clip source, and twice-daily autopilot spends its 40 credits in twenty days. Business is the first tier whose allowance survives a month: 100 credits against three slots a day is 33 days. Credits do not roll over on monthly billing; annual plans receive the year's credits as one pool, and top-up packs expire after 60 days.

What upgrading does not fix: the free plan cannot auto-publish at all, so hands-free posting — the thing you are actually buying — is the one thing you cannot test before you pay for it.

How to actually use VidPal

Vidpal's homepage promises setup in under 5 minutes; its own cross-posting guide budgets about 15 minutes once several platforms are involved, so plan for the longer figure. You pick niche topics, set a brand voice and connect a social account. The free plan's 4 credits need no card, so you can see output before paying — though free renders carry the "Made with Vidpal.ai" watermark. Check what your plan allows before you build a workflow around posting.

Manual sharing to Instagram, YouTube and TikTok starts at Starter; auto-publish and scheduling only at Pro.

Frequently asked questions

The page shows two different monthly prices — which one is real?

The lower one. On 7 October 2026 VidPal confirmed to us in writing that month-to-month billing is $19 for Starter, $39 for Pro and $69 for Business, and that annual billing is $12, $23 and $41 a month ($144, $276 and $492 a year). The pricing page, re-read the same day, still shows both: the plan cards strike through $24, $46 and $82 next to the annual prices, labelled "save 37%" on Starter and "save 41%" on Pro and Business, while the FAQ lower down gives the $19, $39 and $69 that VidPal confirmed. Those save percentages also work out against $19, $39 and $69, not against the struck-through figures. Annual billing is still the cheaper route: on Pro it is $276 a year against $468 for twelve monthly payments.

If I pay for the year, do I get all the credits up front?

Yes, and that is the main practical reason to choose annual. The plan table read 20 September 2026 states the annual allowance as 12 × 15, 12 × 40 and 12 × 100 videos — 180, 480 and 1,200 credits — and the FAQ confirms annual plans are billed up front with the credits delivered as a yearly pool you can spend unevenly across the twelve months. Monthly billing has no such flexibility: credits reset at the start of each cycle and do not carry over. Two expiry rules sit outside that pool. Credit packs bought on top of a plan are valid for 60 days from purchase. And the refund, per the same FAQ, covers the unused portion of your credits within 30 days of payment, by email to support@vidpal.ai — so an unspent annual pool is not a year of refundable balance. Upgrades take effect immediately with prorated billing; downgrades wait until the end of the period.

Which plan do I need for the export quality I want?

Export quality is a hard tier line, not a setting. Read on 20 September 2026, the comparison table runs Free at standard quality with a "Made with Vidpal" watermark, Starter at 1080p with the watermark gone, Pro at 2K (1440p) and Business at 4K (2160p), with the 2K and 4K limits stated specifically for AI Clips and AI Editing. So if you need 4K masters you are on the $41-a-month annual tier regardless of how few videos you make, and its 100 credits come along whether you use them or not. Two other things move with the same line: auto-publish and scheduling to Instagram, YouTube and TikTok start at Pro, and AI Avatar and long-form video are locked on Free entirely, at 5 credits each once unlocked. Payment runs through Dodo Payments as merchant of record rather than through Vidpal directly.

What should I weigh before buying Growth & Revenue software?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Small teams get the most out of this category when one clear problem is already costing real hours or revenue; buying ahead of that just adds cost and another login. Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.
