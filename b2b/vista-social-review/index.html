Vista Social undercuts the big suites convincingly. Throughout this review we weigh it against Hootsuite.

Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 25 Sep 2026

(2026) Professional $99/mo ($950/yr, shown as $80/mo on yearly billing) — 15 profiles, 2 users, 10,000 DM contacts, 1,000 AI credits/mo, Ask Vista + MCP; Advanced $199/mo ($1,910/yr, $160/mo) — 30 profiles, 4 users, 25,000 contacts, 2,000 credits, Zapier/Make integrations; Scale $449/mo ($4,310/yr, $360/mo) — 70 profiles, 8 users, 100,000 contacts, 3,500 credits, white label and client connect; Enterprise custom with unlimited profiles and users.

Yearly billing is 20% off on all three priced plans. Add-ons on every tier: listening across social, web and news from $75/mo, X (Twitter) $29/mo, Employee Advocacy free for 3 advocates then from $199/mo for 25. 14-day trial, no card. Extra profiles (in increments of 10) and extra users are sold only on Scale and Enterprise; the comparison table leaves those rows empty for Professional and Advanced.

Annual billing is one upfront payment for the term. Nonprofit pricing exists on Professional and Advanced for US nonprofits. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The honest check is coverage depth: platform APIs change constantly, so verify the specific networks and features YOUR clients need (particularly newer TikTok/threads capabilities and approval workflows) inside the trial rather than trusting the comparison table. Agencies stepping up from Professional hit the Scale tier for white-labeling — price that step into client contracts from day one.

What Vista Social does

Unified social media management and publishing. Schedule posts, manage conversations, and track analytics across all your brand accounts.

What buyers report about Vista Social

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Vista Social ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • 4.8 on G2 across 1,071 reviews, ranked number one Easiest to Use in 2026, and 4.1 on Trustpilot — an unusually consistent picture.
  • • Social listening is included where competitors charge premium rates for it, and one inbox genuinely covers Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, Threads and LinkedIn.
What they complain about
  • • User seats are capped per plan (3, 6, 10), so growing the team means upgrading rather than adding a seat. Three paid tiers, and the step up between them is steep.
  • • Heavy AI users run out of credits, and the AI suggests hashtags reviewers describe as unrelated to the content.
  • • The mobile app reformats content and is harder to check things in.
  • • There is so much in it that features like social listening, approval workflows and review management are hard to find if you do not know they exist.

White-labelling costs 4.5 times the entry plan

Professional is $99 a month, or $950 a year on yearly billing, for 15 profiles and two users; Advanced $199 ($1,910) for 30 profiles and four; and Scale $449 ($4,310) for 70 profiles, eight users, white-labelling and client connect. Enterprise is custom, with unlimited profiles and users. The 14-day trial needs no card (price re-verified 2026-09-25 against the vendor's own pricing page; every list price rose after our 23 September reading).

Three things the tier price does not include, on any tier. Listening is free only on your own profiles; listening across social, web and news starts at $75 a month. X (Twitter) is $29 a month. Employee advocacy is free for three advocates and $199 a month for 25. A Professional account that needs listening and X is $203 a month on monthly billing, not $99. AI is a meter rather than an assistant you own: 1,000 credits a month on Professional, 2,000 on Advanced, 3,500 on Scale, unlimited only on Enterprise.

The yearly discount now matches the badge on every tier: $950, $1,910 and $4,310 are each 20% off twelve monthly payments, so Scale costs 4.5 times Professional on either billing. Until 23 September 2026 Scale's yearly price was only 13% off, so an older quote may show a smaller Scale discount. What no upgrade fixes: whether the networks and post formats your clients use are supported this month. Only the trial answers that.

How to actually use Vista Social

Connect your clients' real accounts on day one of the 14-day trial, not sample ones. Platform APIs change constantly, so publish an actual post in each format you use, on each network you use, and run your real approval chain through it. That is the only test that means anything. Count users before profiles. Professional includes two users and Advanced four, so a three-person agency hits the user ceiling long before it fills 15 profiles — the boundary that bites first is not the one the plan is named after.

Add the add-ons to your quote before you compare anything. Listening beyond your own profiles is $75 a month, X is $29, and employee advocacy is $199 for 25 people, on every tier. If you are an agency, price the Scale tier into your retainers before your first client. White-labelling only exists there at $449 a month, and it is triggered by a client wanting your brand on the dashboard rather than by your volume.

Yearly billing takes the same 20% off Scale as the other tiers: $4,310 instead of $5,388.

Frequently asked questions

How many users does Vista Social Professional include - two or three?

Two, if you go by the plan card and the comparison table, which is what you are billed against. Read 25 September 2026, both list 2 users on Professional, 4 on Advanced and 8 on Scale. The FAQ lower on the same page still says Professional comes with 3 users and Advanced with 6, and the expanded Scale feature list says 'invite up to 10 users' while the Scale card says 8. Three different answers on one page means the FAQ and the feature list are stale, not that you get the bigger number. Plan on the card figures and ask sales to confirm in writing if your team size sits exactly on the line, because the table leaves the 'extra users' row empty for Professional and Advanced: on those two tiers the fix for one more person is the next plan, not an add-on seat.

We manage 18 client profiles. Can we stay on Professional and buy three more?

Not according to the comparison table. Read 25 September 2026, Professional includes 15 profiles and Advanced 30, and the 'extra profiles' and 'extra users' rows are marked customizable only for Scale and Enterprise, where extra profiles are sold in increments of 10. So profile 16 on Professional means moving to Advanced: $199 a month instead of $99, or $1,910 a year instead of $950. That step also doubles you to four users and 2,000 AI credits a month, so check whether you would have needed it for seats anyway. What it does not add is listening beyond your own profiles ($75 a month) or X ($29 a month); those stay separate charges on every tier.

Is yearly billing really 20% off on every Vista Social plan?

Yes, since the price change we read on 25 September 2026. The toggle says 'save 20%', and it is now exact on all three priced plans: Professional is $99 a month or $950 a year (against $1,188 on monthly billing), Advanced $199 or $1,910 (against $2,388) and Scale $449 or $4,310 (against $5,388). Two days earlier the same page listed Scale at $349 a month or $3,638 a year, only 13% off, so an older quote or screenshot can show a smaller Scale discount than the page does today. The FAQ adds that annual billing is one upfront payment for the whole term, and says you can switch between plans. US nonprofits can ask for separate pricing on Professional and Advanced, which the page links to but does not print.

What should I weigh before buying Growth & Revenue software?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Small teams get the most out of this category when one clear problem is already costing real hours or revenue; buying ahead of that just adds cost and another login. Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.
