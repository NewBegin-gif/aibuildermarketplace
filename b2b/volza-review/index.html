Your countries decide the price, not the plan

From about $1,500 a year for more than three billion shipment records across 200-plus countries, bundling verified buyer and supplier contacts. The comparison that sells it is real: Panjiva and ImportGenius have historically run $15,000 to $50,000 a year and more, so this is somewhere between ten and thirty times cheaper (2026). Then read the meter, because it changes that comparison depending on where you trade. Downloads run on a point system and the cost per record varies by country: US or India shipments are one point, while thinner markets cost up to ten.

So the effective price of a record differs tenfold across the catalogue, and the headline assumes you are working the cheap lanes. If your trade is US-India, the ten-to-thirty-fold saving against the incumbents holds comfortably. If your lanes are thin markets where every download costs ten points, your effective cost per record is ten times higher and the gap narrows sharply. Which makes one number the whole purchase, and our dossier does not carry it: how many points a year does your plan include?

Fifteen hundred dollars for three billion records is not a price until you know how many of those records you are allowed to pull, and at what point cost for your specific countries. Ask for both — the annual point allowance, and the per-country point cost for the markets you actually work — before you compare this to anything. The second catch is freshness. The update cycle is slow and some datasets take up to two months to refresh, which does not matter equally for all uses.

If your question is structural — who are the top fifty suppliers in this category, which countries dominate this HS code — a two-month lag is irrelevant. If your question is reactive — has my competitor just switched supplier, who started importing this last month — then a two-month lag means you are reading history rather than watching a market, and the tool is unfit for the job regardless of price.

There is a learning curve too, and it rewards analysts rather than casual users. That is worth taking seriously in a purchase this size: a trade-data subscription nobody in the building can operate is $1,500 a year for a login.

How to actually use Volza

Ask two questions before you sign, because the headline price does not answer either. How many points does the plan include a year, and what does a shipment record cost in points for the specific countries you trade with? US and India are one point; thinner markets run up to ten, so the effective cost of your data varies tenfold depending on your lanes rather than on your plan. Then decide whether the refresh cycle fits your question.

Some datasets take up to two months to update. For structural work — mapping the top suppliers in a category, sizing a country's share of an HS code — that is fine. For watching a competitor switch supplier this quarter, it is not, and no plan fixes it. Name the person who will actually use it before you buy. This rewards analysts and has a genuine learning curve, so a subscription bought for the team in general tends to become a login nobody opens. Give one person the first month and a specific question to answer with it.

The natural comparison is ImportGenius or Panjiva — the US-focused faster-updating one and the pricey enterprise incumbent. Weigh it against ImportGenius on the job you actually need done.
