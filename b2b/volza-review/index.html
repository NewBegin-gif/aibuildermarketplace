Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 1 Sep 2026

Three paid annual plans and a free trial. Startup is $1,500 a year: 182 countries (44 export and 50 import detailed databases, 160 export and 158 import mirror databases, 7 export and 7 import one-side databases, premium countries excluded), data from 2014 onward, 6,000 searches, full shipment view, 360,000 full-shipment download credits and 2 users. SME is $4,500 a year: 200 countries, adds the statistical databases (46 export, 48 import), unlimited searches under fair use, 1,040,000 download credits and 10 users.

Corporate is $9,600 a year: the same 200-country coverage, unlimited searches under fair use, 2,400,000 download credits and 15 users. The free trial runs 7 days with no card, is advertised as "$50 On sign up + Unlimited Volza credits", shows the last three years in full view with a six-month preview, and carries no full-shipment download credits. An annual and a 2-year value plan are offered as billing terms.

The page also states a refund promise: if you find no profitable opportunity, Volza will help you find one or refund in full. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Three honest catches. Downloads run on a credit allowance — 360,000 a year on Startup — and an earlier reading of the catalogue put the cost per record at one credit for US or India shipments and up to ten in thinner markets; that per-country rate is not stated on the current pricing page and we could not re-verify it on 1 September 2026, so treat it as a question for sales rather than a fact.

And the update cycle is slow, with some datasets taking up to two months to refresh, which suits structural questions far better than reactive ones. There is a learning curve too; it rewards analysts, not casual users.

What Volza does

Export-import trade intelligence: search customs-level shipment records to find verified buyers and suppliers. Startup is $1,500 a year for 182 countries and 2 users; the 200-country coverage and 10 users start at $4,500 (read 2026-09-01). Downloads run on a published credit allowance, from 360,000 full-shipment credits a year.

What buyers report about Volza

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Volza ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Actual shipment records across 209 countries — you find real buyers and suppliers rather than a directory listing. India data refreshes weekly, US daily.
  • • 30 pre-set dashboards and pivot tables mean you are analysing rather than building reports, and support is described as responsive.
What they complain about
  • • Pricing is points-based on an annual subscription from around $1,500 a year, and reviewers warn the points burn faster than expected. Model your real search volume before committing.
  • • One reviewer specifically values downloading to Excel without point deduction — which tells you how much the point economy shapes daily use.
  • • It is an annual commitment; there is no light monthly option to test at low volume.

The $1,500 plan is not the 200-country plan

The headline and the coverage do not belong to the same plan. Startup is $1,500 a year and it buys 182 countries — the detailed and mirror databases, with the premium countries carved out. The 200-country figure that sells this whole category starts one rung up, at SME, $4,500 a year. So the honest entry price depends entirely on whether the markets you actually trade in sit inside the cheaper set, and that is the question to settle before you compare this with anything else.

Seats are the second trip-wire and they are unusually tight at the bottom. Startup covers two users; SME covers ten. Trade data is normally read by more people than it is searched by, so a two-seat plan tends to become one person exporting on behalf of colleagues — which spends the download allowance faster than the plan assumes, and hides who the tool is really for. Those allowances are published, and they are the number to plan against rather than the price: 360,000 full-shipment download credits a year on Startup, 1,040,000 on SME, 2,400,000 on Corporate.

Searches are capped at 6,000 on Startup and run unlimited under fair use above it.

How to actually use Volza

Ask two questions before you sign, because the headline price does not answer either. How many points does the plan include a year, and what does a shipment record cost in points for the specific countries you trade with? US and India are one point; thinner markets run up to ten, so the effective cost of your data varies tenfold depending on your lanes rather than on your plan. Then decide whether the refresh cycle fits your question.

Some datasets take up to two months to update. For structural work — mapping the top suppliers in a category, sizing a country's share of an HS code — that is fine. For watching a competitor switch supplier this quarter, it is not, and no plan fixes it. Name the person who will actually use it before you buy. This rewards analysts and has a genuine learning curve, so a subscription bought for the team in general tends to become a login nobody opens. Give one person the first month and a specific question to answer with it.
