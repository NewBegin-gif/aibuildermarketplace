Ask what happens when the pool runs dry abroad

There are no published rates. Voye sells usage-based data pooling with no prepaid bundles — you pay for what your team actually consumes, with pooled billing and spend controls, and enterprise tiers quoted. Their own materials cite roaming savings of 50 to 70% and more (2026).

The model is sound and worth understanding, because it explains where the saving comes from. Classic roaming prices each traveller separately, so everyone carries their own allowance and everyone's unused data is wasted. A pool means the colleague who barely opened a laptop in Lisbon covers the one who ran a week of video calls from Singapore. The saving is therefore largest where usage is uneven across a team — which it always is. That part is structural rather than promotional.

What is promotional is the percentage. Treat 50 to 70% as a claim until your own invoice confirms it, and make the comparison properly: take your last three months of actual roaming charges, list the trips and the countries, and ask Voye to price those exact trips. A saving calculated on someone else's travel pattern tells you nothing about yours.

The homework that decides this is coverage and speed on your own corridors. eSIM quality varies by country and by which local carrier the provider has partnered with, and a network that is excellent in Germany can be mediocre in Indonesia. Pilot with two or three of your heaviest travellers on your actual routes before migrating anyone else — not with a test SIM in the office.

And ask the question the pooled model creates, which no pricing page will raise: what happens when the pool empties mid-trip? Specifically, are the spend controls hard caps or alerts? A hard cap that cuts data off while someone is navigating a foreign city is considerably worse than an overage charge, and the answer determines how you configure this rather than whether you buy it. Get it in writing alongside the rate.

How to actually use Voye

Pull your last three months of roaming charges before you talk to anyone. List the trips, the countries and the actual data used, and ask Voye to price those exact journeys. That comparison is the only one that means anything — a vendor's 50-to-70% saving is calculated on somebody else's travel pattern.

Pilot on your real corridors with your heaviest travellers, two or three of them, before migrating the team. eSIM quality varies by country and by local carrier partner, so the question is not whether the service works but whether it works in the specific places your people go. A test in the office proves nothing.

Then settle one thing in writing: what happens when the shared pool runs out mid-trip, and whether the spend controls are hard caps or alerts. A cap that cuts someone's data off in a foreign city is worse than an overage, and knowing which it is determines how you configure the account rather than whether you sign.

The natural comparison is Airalo for Business or Truphone — the business-eSIM field — compare per-GB rates on your actual destination list, not the global average. Decide by which one fits the job above, not by the louder brand.
