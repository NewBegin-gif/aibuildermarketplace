Why a year up front costs more per gigabyte

Voye publishes its rates, and the page rewards a calculator (read 6 September 2026). Monthly plans run $9.99 for 5 GB, $19.99 for 12 GB, $39.99 for 30 GB, $69.99 for 55 GB and $119.99 for 100 GB, each valid 30 days. The yearly plans are a single payment valid 365 days: $500 for 200 GB, $1,000 for 450 GB, $2,000 for 950 GB and $3,500 for 1,800 GB. Divide, and the ladder inverts.

The monthly plans fall from $2.00 a gigabyte to $1.20 as they grow. The yearly plans run $2.50, $2.22, $2.11 and $1.94. Buying a year up front costs more per gigabyte, not less — and the two plans that share the name Small Businesses make the point sharply: twelve months of the $39.99 monthly plan is $479.88 for 360 GB, against $500 for 200 GB on the yearly one, which also includes ten eSIM seats where the monthly includes twelve.

What the yearly plans actually buy is expiry. A monthly allowance resets every 30 days and unused data goes with it; the yearly pool sits there for a year. If your travel is seasonal — three heavy months and nine quiet ones — that is the whole argument, and it is a real one. If it is steady, the monthly ladder is cheaper on every measure. What upgrading does not fix is coverage.

Every tier rides the same carrier partners, so a country where the network is mediocre stays mediocre on the $3,500 plan. Paying more buys gigabytes and eSIM seats, never a better signal. Pilot your own corridors before you migrate anyone.

How to actually use Voye

Work out your gigabytes before you look at the plans, because gigabytes are the only thing being priced. Pull your last three months of roaming charges, list the trips and the data actually used, and you will know which of the five monthly tiers you belong in. Then decide expiry before you decide size. Monthly allowances die after 30 days; the yearly plans hold their data for 365. Steady travel is cheaper on the monthly ladder — twelve months of the $39.99 plan gives you 360 GB for less money than the $500 yearly plan's 200 GB.

Seasonal travel is the case where paying more per gigabyte to stop the clock is worth it. Then pilot on your real corridors with two or three of your heaviest travellers before migrating the team. eSIM quality varies by country and by local carrier partner, and no tier changes that. And settle one thing in writing: whether the spend controls are hard caps or alerts. A cap that cuts someone's data off in a foreign city is worse than an overage charge.
