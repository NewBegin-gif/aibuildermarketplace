Your winning variants are rendered by VWO, not by you

Published from about $314 a month on annual billing for up to 10,000 monthly tracked users, with higher tiers adding Insights (heatmaps and recordings) and Personalize. Pricing is usage-based and scales with traffic (2026). At the entry tier that is $3,768 a year, or roughly three cents per tracked user per month. The thing that costs people money here is not on the price list at all, and it is the most important paragraph on this page.

VWO applies page changes through its SmartCode, and their own documentation describes the asynchronous version as one that 'loads page changes asynchronously when the web page loads'. In other words, a variant you left running in the editor is being rendered by VWO on every page load — it was never built into your site. That is fine while you are paying and gone the moment you stop. Follow that through a few years of testing and the consequence is uncomfortable.

Every winning test you never handed to your developers becomes a permanent subscription dependency, and a testing tool quietly becomes the renderer of parts of your actual website. The discipline that avoids it is simple and worth writing into your process: when a test wins, implement the change in your own codebase and switch the VWO variant off. Treat the visual editor as a laboratory, not a deployment system. Their server-side testing does not have this problem, because those changes live in your own stack — if that is an option for you, it changes the arithmetic considerably.

Asynchronous application has a second, smaller cost worth checking during a trial rather than after launch: because the original renders before the change is applied, visitors can see the unmodified page flash first. Load a page with a live variant on a slow connection and watch for it — it is a real conversion cost on the exact pages you are trying to optimise. On the tiers themselves, the jump from Growth to Pro is steep enough that smaller businesses are effectively priced out of multivariate testing and complex targeting, and the interface is dense with options.

Some users report the reporting layer breaking more often than in pricier tools — worth knowing that this is a dashboard complaint rather than a data one; it does not affect the tests themselves. Price this from your monthly tracked users, budget the developer time to bake in your winners, and the tool earns its keep.

How to actually use VWO

Write the hand-off rule before you run your first test: when a variant wins, implement it in your own codebase and turn the VWO version off. VWO's SmartCode renders page changes on load rather than building them into your site, so a winner left running in the editor is a permanent subscription dependency — fine while you pay, gone when you stop. If server-side testing is an option for you, it avoids this entirely because those changes live in your stack.

Check for flicker during the trial, not after. Because changes are applied asynchronously, the original page can render before the variant replaces it. Load a live variant on a throttled connection and watch — a visible flash costs conversions on precisely the pages you are trying to improve. Size the plan from your monthly tracked users rather than your ambitions. The entry tier covers 10,000 MTUs at about $314 a month, and the step to Pro is steep enough that multivariate testing and complex targeting are effectively out of reach below it.

So if MVT is why you are here, price Pro before you start rather than discovering the gap mid-programme.
