Wati Review (2026)

Communication & VoiceResearched assessment · by Daniel HaketUpdated 2026-07-23

Communication tools win on reliability and coverage — every missed call or message is a customer who quietly moves on. So where does Wati actually fit?

The honest bottom line. Worth it for businesses that want to run WhatsApp support and broadcasts at scale with a shared team inbox and automation. Skip it once your customers aren't on WhatsApp or your volume is tiny.

What Wati does

WhatsApp Business API platform for sales and support: shared team inbox, bulk broadcasts and no-code chatbots — plus Astra, its AI agent builder for agents that actually resolve conversations (orders, refunds, bookings, lead qualification) across WhatsApp, web and voice. Its omnichannel inbox now also carries TikTok DMs natively (all plans, no extra cost) alongside Instagram and RCS — with ad-to-chat context that shows exactly which TikTok ad triggered a conversation.

Pricing (2026)

(re-verified 2026-07-23) Growth $39/mo, Pro $79/mo and Business $199/mo when billed annually; paying monthly costs $49, $99 and $249. Users are capped per tier: Growth includes 3 and lets you add none at all, Pro and Business include 5 with extra seats at $24 and $59 per user per month. Add-ons are separate again — 1,000 automation triggers $25/mo, 1,000 AI co-pilot credits $20/mo, Shopify $4.99/mo, each extra WhatsApp number $29/mo. On top of all of that sit Meta's own per-conversation message fees, which Wati bills through at its own rate card. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The plan price is the smallest part of the bill. Growth is capped at three users and you cannot buy a fourth at any price, so growing past three people forces the jump to Pro; from there extra seats are $24 each, and $59 each on Business. Automation triggers, AI credits, the Shopify link and every additional WhatsApp number are all separately metered, and Meta's per-conversation fees land on top of the lot. Model your real message volume and seat count before committing — that is where the money actually goes.

When the paid plan is worth it

Growth ($39/mo annual) pays for itself once WhatsApp is a channel you answer every day and three seats genuinely cover the team — but Growth sells no extra seats at any price, so if you are already three people, price Pro ($79/mo plus $24 per extra seat) from day one and save yourself the forced mid-quarter jump. Business ($199/mo) only earns its premium once you concretely need multiple WhatsApp numbers or the advanced AI agents. And know what upgrading does not fix: Meta's per-conversation fees are identical on every tier, so the metered part of the bill never shrinks by paying Wati more.

How to actually use Wati

Setup is three chains, not one: a verified Meta Business Manager account (have real business documents ready), a phone number that is not already tied to any WhatsApp account, and then Wati's own connect flow — which is genuinely quick once the first two exist. The comments under every tutorial tell the same story: people rarely get stuck in Wati itself, they get stuck at Meta's business verification and at numbers that will not release from an existing WhatsApp. Budget days, not minutes, for Meta's checks; then start small with one approved template broadcast and the no-code chatbot builder before layering automations. Distilled from Wati's own setup tutorials and independent walkthroughs on YouTube, plus their help-center (Getting Started, Connect Your Number) — including the comments, which is where real users say where they got stuck.

Best for: businesses that want to run WhatsApp support and broadcasts at scale with a shared team inbox and automation.
