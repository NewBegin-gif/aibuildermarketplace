Work out how many recovered appointments it needs

Weave's pricing page now names a floor: plans start from $199 per location a month (read 5 September 2026). It remains a per-location subscription with add-ons sold on a quote above that floor, and practice owners commonly report core-bundle costs around $400 to $700 per location per month, plus a one-time setup fee of roughly $750 and further fees for form uploads. Take the middle of that range and a single-location practice is looking at about $550 a month — $6,600 a year, or $20,550 across three years once setup is included.

A three-location group at the same rate is $19,800 a year. Because the meter is locations rather than users or patients, that figure scales in steps rather than smoothly, and adding a fourth surgery adds a fourth full subscription. The way to make that decidable is not to compare it to other software but to convert it into appointments. Divide the monthly cost by your average appointment value: at $550 a month and a $200 appointment, the software has to produce fewer than three recovered bookings a month to pay for itself.

Put your own two numbers in — that is the entire business case, and for a practice with a meaningful no-show rate or a lapsed-recall list it is usually a low bar. Which is why practices that use the recall and reminder features properly tend to be happy, and practices that install it and change nothing tend not to be. Two cautions on the commercial side. The pricing is opaque and quote-driven, and the all-in cost is commonly well above what the first sales conversation implies — the base bundle is a starting point, with add-ons, setup and per-location fees landing on top.

Get every line in writing, including what a second location costs, what happens to the rate at renewal, and what the contract term is. And check the fit before the price. This is deeply tuned for healthcare and dental front desks — the phone, texting, payments and reviews bundle is built around how those practices actually run their day. For a busy practice that specificity is exactly the value. Outside those verticals, a substantial share of what you are paying for will not apply to you, and a more general tool will cost less and fit better.

How to actually use Weave

Convert the quote into appointments before you accept it. Divide the monthly per-location cost by your average appointment value: that tells you how many recovered no-shows or reactivated recalls the software has to produce each month to break even. At around $550 a month and a $200 appointment it is under three, which is a low bar — but only if you actually switch the recall and reminder features on.

Get every line in writing, not just the bundle. Ask for the per-location monthly rate, the one-time setup fee, form-upload fees, each add-on you are being shown, the contract term, and what the rate becomes at renewal. The consistent complaint here is that the all-in cost sits well above what the first conversation implied. Price your second location now even if you only have one. The meter is per location, so growth adds a full subscription rather than a marginal seat, and that number belongs in your plan before you commit rather than after.
