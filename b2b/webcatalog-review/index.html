Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 5 Sep 2026

Basic is free and no card is required: 2 desktop apps, 2 profiles, 1 space, 1 page and 5 links. Pro is $5 per user per month billed annually, plus local taxes, and lifts those caps to unlimited apps, profiles, spaces and pages with cloud backup and sync. Business is $8 per user per month billed annually and adds shared spaces, team management, centralised billing and an activity log, with SAML single sign-on as a paid add-on.

Both paid plans carry a 7-day trial, and a legacy lifetime licence still exists without cloud sync. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The honest question is whether you need the paid tier at all. The free plan's 2-app limit is tight, but browser profiles and PWAs solve part of the same problem for free. Pro earns its fee when multi-account juggling (several Gmail/Slack/WhatsApp identities) is daily reality.

What WebCatalog does

Turn web apps into tidy desktop apps and manage multiple accounts side by side. Less tab chaos, more focus — your whole stack organized in one place.

What buyers report about WebCatalog

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run WebCatalog ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Pro at $5 a month is by far the cheapest in the workspace-management category, and the one-click catalogue makes setup fast.
  • • Turning scattered web apps into separate desktop apps with isolated multi-account profiles genuinely solves the several-logins problem. Basic is free for 2 apps.
What they complain about
  • • Each app runs its own Chromium process. On a machine with 8 GB of RAM that is a real bottleneck, and it is the most frequently cited complaint.
  • • Support is slow and the community forum is thin next to rivals.
  • • Team management needs Business at $8 per user.

Count how often you switch accounts

Free Basic covers 2 apps and 2 spaces. Pro is about $5 per user a month on annual billing (around $6 monthly) with unlimited apps, profiles, sync and blocking. Business is roughly $8 to $10 and adds team management. A legacy lifetime licence still exists without cloud sync, and there are 7-day trials (2026). The honest question is whether you need to pay at all, because part of this problem is already solved for free.

Browser profiles give you separate identities, and progressive web apps give you app-shaped windows for most of the services people install this for. If you use one Gmail account, one Slack and one WhatsApp, those free mechanisms cover you and the two-app free tier will feel tight for no reason. Here is the test that settles it: count how many times in a normal day you sign out of one account to sign into another of the same service.

If the answer is zero, do not buy this. If the answer is five — three Gmail identities, two Slack workspaces, a personal and a work WhatsApp — then Pro at $60 a year is buying back a genuinely irritating few minutes every day, and multi-account juggling is precisely what it is built for. Ask about the legacy lifetime licence before you take the subscription. It exists, it lacks cloud sync, and cloud sync is worth nothing to someone who works on one machine.

If that is you, a one-off purchase against $60 a year is worth a direct question to support rather than an assumption. Business at $8 to $10 adds team management, which is a genuinely different purchase: it is for standardising which apps a team has and blocking the rest, not for the individual convenience Pro sells. Do not buy it for a team unless someone actually intends to manage that configuration.

How to actually use WebCatalog

Try the free approaches first, for a week. Set up browser profiles for your separate identities and install two or three services as progressive web apps. For a lot of people that is the whole problem solved at no cost, and the two-app free tier here will feel artificially tight by comparison. If it is not enough, count your account switches over a normal day: how many times do you sign out of one identity to reach another on the same service?

That number is what Pro is worth to you. Zero means do not buy; five means $60 a year is buying back several minutes daily. Ask support about the legacy lifetime licence before subscribing. It still exists and it lacks cloud sync — which is worth nothing if you work on a single machine, and everything if you do not. And keep Business in reserve: it adds team management rather than personal convenience, so it only makes sense if someone will actually administer which apps the team has.

The natural comparison is Rambox or browser PWAs — Rambox is the direct competitor with similar pricing; PWAs are the free baseline — pay only if profile isolation is your real pain.
