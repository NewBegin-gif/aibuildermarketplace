Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 25 Sep 2026

Basic is free and no card is required: 2 desktop apps, 2 profiles and 1 space, with cloud sync, to-dos, focus mode and curated wallpapers. Pro is $5 per user per month billed annually or $6 billed monthly, plus local taxes, and lifts the caps to unlimited apps, profiles and spaces, with an ad-free experience, pages, theme colours, an ads and tracker blocker, an app lock, location services and priority support.

Business is $8 per user per month billed annually or $10 monthly and adds shared spaces, shared pages, team management, centralised billing, an activity log and SAML single sign-on. Both paid plans carry a 7-day trial. The page advertises a 20% saving on yearly billing and prices in USD, EUR or JPY. A legacy lifetime licence is not mentioned on the pricing page. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

Count how often you switch accounts

Free Basic covers 2 desktop apps, 2 profiles and 1 space. Pro is $5 per user a month billed annually, plus local taxes, with unlimited apps, profiles and spaces, an ad-free experience and tracker blocking; cloud sync is on the free plan as well (re-read 25 September 2026). Business is $8 and adds team management. Both paid plans carry a 7-day trial (read September 2026). The honest question is whether you need to pay at all, because part of this problem is already solved for free.

Browser profiles give you separate identities, and progressive web apps give you app-shaped windows for most of the services people install this for. If you use one Gmail account, one Slack and one WhatsApp, those free mechanisms cover you and the two-app free tier will feel tight for no reason. Here is the test that settles it: count how many times in a normal day you sign out of one account to sign into another of the same service.

If the answer is zero, do not buy this. If the answer is five — three Gmail identities, two Slack workspaces, a personal and a work WhatsApp — then Pro at $60 a year is buying back a genuinely irritating few minutes every day, and multi-account juggling is precisely what it is built for. Two things ride along with Pro that people forget to price: an ads and tracker blocker across every app window, and an app lock.

If you were paying for a blocker anyway, that changes the $60-a-year arithmetic. Both are on the plan comparison, not sold separately, and neither is free. Business at $8 to $10 adds team management, which is a genuinely different purchase: it is for standardising which apps a team has and blocking the rest, not for the individual convenience Pro sells. Do not buy it for a team unless someone actually intends to manage that configuration.

How to actually use WebCatalog

Try the free approaches first, for a week. Set up browser profiles for your separate identities and install two or three services as progressive web apps. For a lot of people that is the whole problem solved at no cost, and the two-app free tier here will feel artificially tight by comparison. If it is not enough, count your account switches over a normal day: how many times do you sign out of one identity to reach another on the same service?

That number is what Pro is worth to you. Zero means do not buy; five means $60 a year is buying back several minutes daily. Check the currency before you budget: the pricing page prices in USD, EUR or JPY and adds local taxes on top, so the figure you plan around is not always the figure you first saw. And keep Business in reserve — it adds shared spaces, team management and SAML single sign-on rather than personal convenience, so it only makes sense if someone will actually administer which apps the team has.

Frequently asked questions

How much more is WebCatalog Pro if I pay monthly?

One dollar a month, or $12 a year. Read 25 September 2026 in USD: Pro is $5 per user a month billed annually ($60 a year) or $6 billed monthly ($72), and Business is $8 or $10 ($96 against $120), with local taxes added to each. The page advertises a 20% saving for yearly billing; that is exact on Business and about 17% on Pro. Both paid plans start with a 7-day trial.

Does the free plan show ads?

Basic is not ad-free: the plan comparison (read 25 September 2026) marks 'Ad-free experience' No on Basic and Yes on Pro and Business. The ads and tracker blocker is a separate line in the same table and is also Pro-only. The free tier still includes cloud sync, to-dos, focus mode and curated wallpapers, so it is usable, just capped at 2 desktop apps, 2 profiles and 1 space.

Can I get my money back if I subscribe by mistake?

Within 14 days, but it is not promised. The pricing-page FAQ (read 25 September 2026) says a refund can be requested within 14 days of purchase if you signed up by mistake, and that refunds are not guaranteed and may be declined for repeated or abusive requests. Cancelling is simpler: you can cancel at any time and keep access until the end of the billing cycle, and one subscription covers every device where you sign in with the same account.

What should I weigh before buying IT & Productivity software?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.
