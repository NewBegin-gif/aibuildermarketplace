What Webflow does

Visual website builder that writes production code — design, CMS, hosting and ecommerce in one place.

Pricing (2026)

(re-verified 2026-08-04, read on their own pricing page with every plan accordion and billing tab open) Webflow bills in two places at once. Site plans, billed yearly: Starter free; $15/mo for a site that does not need a CMS (custom domain, 300 static pages, 10 GB bandwidth); $25/mo for the CMS tier, which the page now calls Premium. Ecommerce site plans are separate again: Standard $29/mo with 500 items and a 2% transaction fee, Plus $74/mo with 5,000 items and no transaction fee, Advanced $212/mo with 15,000 items. Workspace plans, also billed yearly: Starter free (2 staging sites, 1 full seat), Core $19/mo, Growth $49/mo, and on the freelancer-and-agency tab Freelancer $16/mo and Agency $35/mo. Seats are charged on top of the workspace plan: a full seat is $39/mo billed yearly, a limited seat $15/mo, a review-only seat $0. Platform plans start at Team $2,500/mo with an annual contract required, and Enterprise above it is quote-only. Add-ons: Optimize from $299/mo by page views, Analyze from $9/mo by sessions, Localize from $9/mo, 2,000 AI credits $20/mo. Webflow Cloud overages run at $2 per million requests and $2 per 5 hours of CPU usage, with web app bandwidth pooled into the site plan's bandwidth; storage is no longer priced as a separate line on that page. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

The honest knock

Three separate bills, and the pricing page shows them on separate tabs. A site plan is what puts your site online: $15 a month for a static site, $25 with the CMS. A workspace plan is what lets your team work on it: $19 or $49 a month. And seats are charged on top of the workspace plan at $39 a month each for anyone who can actually design. So a two-person team on a CMS site is not $25 a month — it is $25 for the site, $19 for the workspace and $39 for the second seat, which is $83, and that is before add-ons. Two more numbers to find before you commit. The entry ecommerce plan takes a 2% transaction fee on every order, which the $29 headline does not mention and which is the largest single cost for anyone selling real volume; removing it means Plus at $74. And Optimize, the conversion tooling Webflow leads with in its marketing, starts at $299 a month on top of everything else and is priced by page views. Price your team, your site and your add-ons together, because no single tab on that page shows you a total.

What buyers report about Webflow

Recurring themes from G2 and Capterra, read in August 2026. We have not run Webflow ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Design control without a developer, and the exported code is clean — that combination is what reviewers say nothing else matches.
  • • Hosting is fast and the CMS is genuinely enough to run a professional site. Around 4.4 on G2 across 975 reviews, 71% of them five stars.
What they complain about
  • • Pricing stacks. Site plan plus workspace seats plus CMS limits, and multi-site agencies still report bills over $1,000 a month even after the May 2026 simplification.
  • • CMS and template ceilings push upgrades you did not plan for.
  • • There is no live chat or phone support on any tier — async only. On a launch deadline that is the complaint reviewers remember.
  • • Beginners describe a steep learning curve; this is a design tool, not a site builder.

Where the ratings disagree. The headline 4.4 covers years of reviews. Recent sentiment is markedly cooler — a sample of 15 G2 and Capterra reviews averages 2.7, with 4 of 15 recommending it. Weigh the recent ones more heavily than the average.

Best for: designers and marketing teams who want to ship a real, fast site without a developer in the loop for every change, and who value that the CMS and hosting are the same product as the editor.
Who should skip it: if you are one person publishing a simple site — the free tier and a $15 site plan cover that, and the reason to be here is the team workflow you are not using; also skip the entry ecommerce plan if you sell in any volume, because 2% of every order will outrun the $45 a month that Plus costs extra.

The natural comparison is Framer or WordPress — the two directions people leave for — Framer for a simpler visual builder, WordPress for cheaper hosting and more plugins; compare the total of site plan, workspace and seats, not the entry sticker. Decide by which one fits the job above, not by the louder brand.

Frequently asked questions

Is Webflow secure and reliable enough to depend on?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.

Who should not use Webflow?

Skip it if you are one person publishing a simple site — the free tier and a $15 site plan cover that, and the reason to be here is the team workflow you are not using; also skip the entry ecommerce plan if you sell in any volume, because 2% of every order will outrun the $45 a month that Plus costs extra.
