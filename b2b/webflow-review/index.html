Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 29 Aug 2026

Webflow bills in two places at once. Site plans, billed yearly: Starter free; $15/mo for a site that does not need a CMS (custom domain, 300 static pages, 10 GB bandwidth); $25/mo for the CMS tier, which the page now calls Premium. Ecommerce site plans are separate again: Standard $29/mo with 500 items and a 2% transaction fee, Plus $74/mo with 5,000 items and no transaction fee, Advanced $212/mo with 15,000 items.

Workspace plans, also billed yearly: Starter free (2 staging sites, 1 full seat), Core $19/mo, Growth $49/mo, and on the freelancer-and-agency tab Freelancer $16/mo and Agency $35/mo. Seats are charged on top of the workspace plan: a full seat is $39/mo billed yearly, a limited seat $15/mo, a review-only seat $0. Platform plans start at Team $2,500/mo with an annual contract required, and Enterprise above it is quote-only.

Add-ons: Optimize from $299/mo by page views, Analyze from $9/mo by sessions, Localize Essential $9/mo and Localize Advanced $29/mo by number of locales, 2,000 AI credits $20/mo billed yearly, and a published bandwidth ladder running from +50 GB at $20/mo billed annually to +2.45 TB at $974/mo, with monthly billing about half again as expensive at every step. Webflow Cloud overages run at $2 per million requests and $2 per 5 hours of CPU usage, with web app bandwidth pooled into the site plan's bandwidth.

Storage is priced on that page again: on the Basic and CMS site plans the D1 and KV stores include 100 MB and then run at $2 per 500 MB, and R2 object storage at $2 per 5 GB; on the Team plan those same lines read $2 per GB. (Re-verified 2026-08-29 on the pricing page itself: every plan, seat and add-on price above was unchanged.) Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

A site plan is what puts your site online: $15 a month for a static site, $25 with the CMS. A workspace plan is what lets your team work on it: $19 or $49 a month. And seats are charged on top of the workspace plan at $39 a month each for anyone who can actually design. So a two-person team on a CMS site is not $25 a month — it is $25 for the site, $19 for the workspace and $39 for the second seat, which is $83, and that is before add-ons.

Two more numbers to find before you commit. The entry ecommerce plan takes a 2% transaction fee on every order, which the $29 headline does not mention and which is the largest single cost for anyone selling real volume; removing it means Plus at $74. And Optimize, the conversion tooling Webflow leads with in its marketing, starts at $299 a month on top of everything else and is priced by page views.

Price your team, your site and your add-ons together, because no single tab on that page shows you a total.

Three bills, and no tab shows you the total

Webflow charges in three places at once and its pricing page puts them on separate tabs, so the single most useful thing we can do here is add them up. A site plan puts your site online: free on Starter, $15 a month billed yearly for a static site with a custom domain, 300 pages and 10 GB of bandwidth, and $25 for the CMS tier the page now calls Premium.

A workspace plan lets your team work on it: free on Starter with two staging sites and one full seat, then Core $19 or Growth $49. And seats are charged on top of the workspace: a full seat is $39 a month billed yearly, a limited seat $15, a review-only seat free. So a two-person team on a CMS site is not $25 a month. It is $25 for the site, $19 for the workspace and $39 for the second full seat — $83, or 3.3 times the headline, before a single add-on.

Three people is $122 a month, $1,464 a year. That seat structure is also where the easiest saving lives. Only people who actually design need a full seat at $39. Content editors belong on a limited seat at $15 and stakeholders who just leave comments belong on a review-only seat at nothing. Getting that wrong on a ten-person team costs about $312 a month in seats nobody needed. Ecommerce is a separate ladder again, and it contains one number worth doing properly.

Standard is $29 a month for 500 items with a 2% transaction fee; Plus is $74 for 5,000 items and no fee. The $45 difference equals 2% of $2,250, so the moment you sell more than $2,250 a month, Plus is the cheaper plan. That is a low bar — under $27,000 a year — which makes Standard a genuine starter plan rather than a small-business one, and makes the 2% the largest single cost for anyone selling real volume.

Advanced is $212 for 15,000 items. Then the add-ons, which is where the marketing and the invoice part company. Optimize, the conversion tooling Webflow leads with, starts at $299 a month priced by page views — three and a half times the entire two-person setup above. Analyze starts at $9 by sessions, Localize at $9, and 2,000 AI credits are $20. Webflow Cloud overages run $2 per million requests and $2 per five hours of CPU, with web app bandwidth pooled into the site plan's bandwidth.

Above all of it, Platform plans start at Team at $2,500 a month with an annual contract required, and Enterprise is quoted. Price your team, your site and your add-ons together before you compare Webflow with anything, because no single tab on that page will do it for you.

How to actually use Webflow

Add the three bills together before you decide anything. A site plan puts the site online ($15 static, $25 with CMS), a workspace plan lets your team work on it ($19 or $49), and seats are charged on top at $39 each for anyone who designs. A two-person CMS project is $83 a month, not $25 — and no tab on the pricing page shows you that total. Then assign seats deliberately, because this is the easiest money to waste.

Full seats at $39 are only for people who actually build; content editors go on limited seats at $15 and anyone who just reviews and comments goes on a free review-only seat. On a ten-person team, getting that wrong is about $312 a month. If you are selling, do the transaction-fee arithmetic first. Standard at $29 carries a 2% fee; Plus at $74 does not, and the $45 difference is 2% of $2,250 — so above $2,250 a month in sales, Plus is simply cheaper.

Below it, Standard is fine. Leave Optimize until last. It starts at $299 a month and is priced by page views, which is more than three times a working two-person setup — it is a conversion programme, not a feature you switch on while you are still building the site.

Distilled from Webflow's pricing page (webflow.com/pricing), read 29 August 2026 on yearly billing with the ecommerce and workspace accordions expanded — site plans, platform plans, add-ons, seat prices and the AI-credit line come from that page. Its FAQ confirms Webflow Cloud is metered on bandwidth, requests, CPU and storage but prints no overage rate; the per-request and per-CPU figures above come from Webflow's Cloud documentation read earlier and were not re-read on 29 August 2026.

The natural comparison is Framer or WordPress — the two directions people leave for — Framer for a simpler visual builder, WordPress for cheaper hosting and more plugins; compare the total of site plan, workspace and seats, not the entry sticker.

What should I weigh before buying Website Builders software?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives.
