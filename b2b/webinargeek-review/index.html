Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 14 Sep 2026

The plans are Basic, Premium and Enterprise; an earlier reading of ours called the first two Premium and Advanced, which was wrong. Basic is €69/mo, or €49 billed annually, and covers 50 viewers, 1 presenter and a 2-hour session. Premium is bought per viewer tier: €99/€69 at 125 viewers, €159/€99 at 250, €249/€139 at 500 and €399/€199 at 1,000 (monthly/annual). The annual discount widens as you climb, from 30% at 125 viewers to the advertised 50% at 1,000, so "save up to 50%" only holds on the top rung.

Enterprise is annual-only and quoted from €349 at 500 viewers, €449 at 1,000, €649 at 2,000 and €849 at 5,000. The page carries a currency switch across EUR, USD, GBP, SEK, NOK and DKK and serves by region, so both the number and the currency depend on where you buy; the figures render client-side and a plain fetch retrieves none of them.

Add-ons are billed annually on top of a Premium subscription: extra users from €10 per user per month, localisation €20, AI assistant €20, branding profiles €59 (which include an extra workspace and e-mail domain), HubSpot €49, Salesforce €49 and RTMP streaming €34.50, plus a one-off €495 for online onboarding. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What WebinarGeek does

European live and automated webinar platform — viewer-tier pricing, engagement tools, CRM integrations and GDPR-friendly hosting. The vendor states you can now generate a full webinar — including polls, quizzes and a registration page — from a plain-text description, with UTM tracking and post-webinar redirects on the registration page (vendor mail, August 2026).

What buyers report about WebinarGeek

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run WebinarGeek ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Support is the standout — reviewers describe it as fast, easy to reach and actually helpful, which is rare in this category.
  • • Live, automated, hybrid and on-demand formats all in one tool, with viewer-level analytics and solid branding controls.
What they complain about
  • • Basic caps at 50 viewers and shorter sessions, so the entry plan is not a smaller version of the product — check the cap against your list first.
  • • Expensive for anyone running the occasional webinar rather than a programme.
  • • The trial is short and reviewers find the pricing structure confusing.
  • • Tutorials are thin and user roles are not clearly separated.

When the paid plan is worth it

WebinarGeek has no free plan, only a 14-day trial that unlocks the Premium feature set, so the real question is which paid tier you land on rather than whether to pay at all. Basic, at €49 a month billed annually or €69 monthly, caps you at 50 viewers, 1 presenter and a 2-hour session. Premium is bought per viewer tier and the annual discount widens as you climb: €69 against €99 at 125 viewers, €99 against €159 at 250, €139 against €249 at 500 and €199 against €399 at 1,000.

The advertised "save up to 50%" therefore only holds on that top rung. What upgrading does not solve is demand, because capacity is bought per tier rather than per attendee: an over-bought tier is money sitting idle and an under-bought one fails on the day. Nor does it fold in the depth. The add-ons are sold as extensions of a Premium subscription, so localisation and the AI assistant at €20 each, branding profiles at €59, HubSpot and Salesforce at €49 each and RTMP at €34.50 are all out of reach from Basic and all billed on top of Premium — including the AI assistant, which the vendor says now runs on on-demand webinars as well as live ones (vendor mail, 8 September 2026). Only Enterprise, annual-only from €349 at 500 viewers to €849 at 5,000, includes them.

How to actually use WebinarGeek

The quickest route to something real is not a blank webinar but the demo webinars WebinarGeek puts in every new account, "My first webinar" and "My first on demand webinar", which can be opened, edited and even used live. The vendor's own guide then runs 11 steps, and the ones that actually gate a launch are Publish and the dry run. The documentation is explicit that publishing is the moment a registration link becomes active: select the webinar, go to "Publish" in the left sidebar and click Publish before promoting anything.

The dry run sits behind the "Dry run" button, is not recorded, and stops automatically after 5 hours of broadcasting. The docs also set connectivity floors: about 2 Mbps upload for presenters and 0.5 Mbps download for viewers, and viewers cannot enter the room until 5 minutes before the scheduled start.

Frequently asked questions

What does WebinarGeek cost?

Basic is €69 a month, or €49 billed annually, for up to 50 viewers. Premium is priced per viewer tier: €99/€69 at 125, €159/€99 at 250, €249/€139 at 500 and €399/€199 at 1,000, monthly against annual. Enterprise is annual-only, from €349 at 500 viewers up to €849 at 5,000. The page offers six currencies and prices by region, so confirm yours at checkout — the figures render client-side and do not appear in a plain fetch. Read on 14 September 2026.

What is not included in the plan price?

The add-ons, and each is billed annually on top of a Premium subscription: extra users from €10 per user per month, localisation €20, the AI assistant €20, branding profiles €59 (an extra workspace and e-mail domain included), HubSpot €49, Salesforce €49 and RTMP streaming €34.50, plus a one-off €495 for online onboarding. A team that wants CRM sync and a second brand profile is adding over €100 a month to the plan.

Which tier do I need for my audience size?

Viewer count is the meter: Basic covers 50 concurrent viewers, Premium steps through 125, 250, 500 and 1,000, and Enterprise runs 500 to 5,000. Mind the overlap — at 1,000 viewers Premium is €199 a month on annual billing and Enterprise €449, so that €250 gap buys the Enterprise feature set (custom domain, 12-hour sessions, RTMP, workspaces and the Salesforce, HubSpot and localisation add-ons included) rather than more capacity. Size on the largest webinar you realistically expect, because the cap is what fails on the day.
