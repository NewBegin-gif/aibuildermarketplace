What WebinarGeek does

European live and automated webinar platform — viewer-tier pricing, engagement tools, CRM integrations and GDPR-friendly hosting. The vendor states you can now generate a full webinar — including polls, quizzes and a registration page — from a plain-text description, with UTM tracking and post-webinar redirects on the registration page (vendor mail, August 2026).

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The friendly €49 headline is the annual rate for just 50 viewers, and the real cost lives in add-ons: localization, the AI assistant, CRM integrations and extra workspaces are each €20-59 a month on top. A serious webinar stack quickly doubles the base plan.

What buyers report about WebinarGeek

Recurring themes from Capterra, G2, GetApp and Software Advice, read in August 2026. We have not run WebinarGeek ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Support is the standout — reviewers describe it as fast, easy to reach and actually helpful, which is rare in this category.
  • • Live, automated, hybrid and on-demand formats all in one tool, with viewer-level analytics and solid branding controls.
What they complain about
  • • Basic caps at 50 viewers and shorter sessions, so the entry plan is not a smaller version of the product — check the cap against your list first.
  • • From €49/month it is expensive for anyone running the occasional webinar rather than a programme.
  • • The trial is short and reviewers find the pricing structure confusing.
  • • Tutorials are thin and user roles are not clearly separated.

When the paid plan is worth it

WebinarGeek has no free plan, only a 14-day trial that unlocks the Premium feature set, so the real question is which paid tier you land on rather than whether to pay at all. Basic, at EUR 49 per month billed annually or EUR 69 billed monthly, caps you at 50 viewers, 1 presenter and a 2-hour session, which suits a solo host running short demos and little else. Premium opens at EUR 69 annually or EUR 99 monthly for 125 viewers and climbs to EUR 199 or EUR 399 at 1,000 viewers, and the vendor markets its localisation, AI assistant, branding and CRM add-ons as extensions of a Premium subscription, so the localisation and AI assistant options at EUR 20 per month on annual billing, or the HubSpot connector at EUR 49, are out of reach from Basic, whose card lists only extra users as an expansion.

What upgrading does not solve is demand: viewer capacity is bought per tier, not per attendee, so an over-bought tier is money sitting idle. Enterprise, from EUR 349, is sold on annual billing only.

How to actually use WebinarGeek

The quickest route to something real is not a blank webinar but the demo webinars WebinarGeek puts in every new account, "My first webinar" and "My first on demand webinar", which can be opened, edited and even used live. The vendor's own guide then runs 11 steps, and the ones that actually gate a launch are Publish and the dry run.

The documentation is explicit that publishing is the moment a registration link becomes active: select the webinar, go to "Publish" in the left sidebar and click Publish before promoting anything. The dry run sits behind the "Dry run" button, is not recorded, and stops automatically after 5 hours of broadcasting. The docs also set connectivity floors: about 2 Mbps upload for presenters and 0.5 Mbps download for viewers, and viewers cannot enter the room until 5 minutes before the scheduled start.

Frequently asked questions

What does WebinarGeek cost?

Premium is €69 a month, or €49 billed annually, for up to 50 viewers; Advanced €99 or €69 annually and scales from 125 to 1,000 viewers; Enterprise starts at €349. The page offers six currencies and up to 50% off annual billing, so confirm yours at checkout — the figures do not render for a plain fetch.

What is not included in the plan price?

The add-ons, and they are each billed annually on top: localisation €20 a month, the AI assistant €20, an extra workspace €59, and the HubSpot and Salesforce integrations €49 each. A team that wants CRM sync and a second workspace is adding over €100 a month to the plan.

Which tier do I need for my audience size?

Viewer count is the meter: Premium covers 50 concurrent viewers, Advanced scales from 125 up to 1,000, and above that you are in Enterprise. Size on the largest webinar you realistically expect rather than the average, because the cap is what fails on the day.

Will WebinarGeek integrate with my existing stack?

Usually yes for the major platforms, but check yours specifically. Tools in this class typically ship native connectors for the big CMS, CRM and email platforms and fall back on Zapier, webhooks or an API for everything else. The practical question is whether your stack is on the native list or needs glue, because a missing native connector normally means either manual work or a paid middleware layer. Check the integrations page for your exact tools before you buy.

Who should not use WebinarGeek?

Skip it if you run occasional small webinars (your video-call tool may cover it), or you need the CRM and localization features that sit behind paid add-ons. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
