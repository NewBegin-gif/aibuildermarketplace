Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 8 Oct 2026

Three plans, each for one website. Free has the editor, 300+ templates, mobile optimisation, free icons and photos and the Evo AI assistant, with up to 3 pages per site, 100 MB storage and 100 MB bandwidth and no credit card; the Free card also carries a '60 days' label that the page does not explain. Starter is $11 a month billed annually ($132 a year) or $16 billed monthly ($192 a year): advanced SEO settings, lead capture tools, advanced design, integrations and custom code, up to 10 pages per site, 3 GB media storage, 5 GB bandwidth and no Weblium branding.

Business, marked most popular, is $17.50 a month annually ($210 a year) or $25 monthly ($300 a year) and adds the online store, the blog, multilingual sites in up to 5 languages, up to 500 pages, 10 GB storage and 20 GB bandwidth. Starter and Business have a 14-day free trial without a card. Each subscription covers one website; bundles for 5, 10 or 15 sites save 25-40%.

Annual plans include a free domain for the first year. You can upgrade any time, but to move to a lower plan you cancel auto-renewal and buy the new plan after the current one ends. If a renewal payment fails, paid features stay on for 5 days and the site then drops to Free. Weblium Studio builds sites for you from $300, and a white-label version for agencies starts at $48 a month. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Every subscription covers one website, and moving to a cheaper plan only works after the current term ends. The Free plan is limited to 3 pages, 100 MB storage and 100 MB bandwidth, and its card carries a '60 days' label the page does not explain. We have not used Weblium ourselves.

What Weblium does

Template-based website builder for small businesses, with an AI assistant, lead capture forms and integrations, and an online store, blog and multilingual pages on the Business plan.

Starter is a lead-generation site; selling or blogging means Business

The jump from Free to Starter is mostly about being taken seriously: your own branding off, SEO settings, forms and integrations, and room for 10 pages instead of 3. For a service business that wants a few pages and a contact form, $11 a month billed yearly is the whole bill for one site. The line that decides most purchases is between Starter and Business. The online store, the blog and multilingual pages are only on Business, and Starter stops at 10 pages and 5 GB of bandwidth.

If you plan to publish articles or sell anything, budget $17.50 a month yearly from the start rather than upgrading later. Monthly billing costs about 45% more on Starter ($16 against $11) and a downgrade only takes effect after the paid term ends, so test on the 14-day trial before choosing a yearly plan.

How to actually use Weblium

Build the first version on the Free plan or the 14-day trial; neither needs a card. Count the pages you need: Free stops at 3 and Starter at 10, while Business allows 500. Decide on Business before launch if you want a blog, a shop or a second language. Choose yearly billing only once the site is ready, because the yearly plan includes the first-year domain but cannot be downgraded until it ends.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Weblium cost?

Read on 8 October 2026: Free with 3 pages; Starter $11 a month billed yearly ($132) or $16 monthly; Business $17.50 a month yearly ($210) or $25 monthly. Prices are per website.

Can I sell products on Weblium Starter?

No. The online store, the blog and multilingual sites are part of the Business plan.

Can I downgrade a Weblium plan?

Not mid-term. The FAQ says you cancel auto-renewal and buy the lower plan after the current subscription ends; upgrades are possible any time.

Is a website builder secure and reliable enough to depend on?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.
