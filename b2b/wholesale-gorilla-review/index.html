Wholesale Gorilla is a feature ladder, not a volume ladder, and the features most people picture when they say wholesale sit high on it.

Best for: Shopify merchants who already have wholesale buyers and want them ordering from the same store as retail customers — custom price groups, a signup and approval flow, net terms and wholesale-only shipping rules — without moving up to Shopify Plus for native B2B..

What they complain about
  • • Two long-standing merchants (four and nearly seven years on the app) wrote in August and May 2026 that support has moved to an AI chatbot and got worse; one reports weekly issues with customers unable to check out and says they lost business.
  • • The theme integration is theme code their team edits: a merchant reported in 2023 that the wrong live theme was changed, outside the scope they had given, over a long weekend.

Where the ratings disagree. The praise and the complaints are about the same thing — support — and they split by tenure rather than by platform. Lite buys a price list, not a wholesale storefront

The three tiers gate features, not volume: pricing rules and customer price groups are unlimited on all of them. The only question is which features you need, and the two most people picture sit above the entry rung. Volume discounts are Premium at $149.95. Net payment terms, wholesale shipping rules, the quick-order form and discount codes start at Advanced at $69.95. The rung that decides the experience is theme integration, also Advanced.

By default you get the Wholesale Portal: a separate, app-hosted, login-only area. The Unified Storefront — buyers shopping your ordinary product pages at wholesale prices — is a theme integration their team performs on request. Their own documentation recommends it over the portal, calls the portal a workaround for app conflicts, and notes third-party apps do not function inside it. So $34.95 buys custom prices in a side entrance; the storefront the marketing shows is $69.95.

What no tier fixes is that the integration is theme code their team edits on your live store. Merchants have reported an integration applied to the wrong live theme, and uninstall instructions that leave code behind. There is no free plan either, so budget from the day the 21-day trial ends.

How to actually use Wholesale Gorilla

The vendor publishes a three-step quick start with its own time estimates: create a wholesale price rule (5 minutes), set up wholesale registration so accounts can be identified (10 minutes), then shop your own store with the test account you just made and confirm the prices apply (10 minutes). Do that third step properly — it is the only one that shows you what your buyers see, and the app's default is the separate portal, not your storefront.

The unified storefront is a fourth, optional step, and it is not self-service: open the Online Store page in the app, find the Unified Storefront card and click Activate, then wait while their team integrates the app into your theme and emails the merchant address on file. Their documentation puts that at one to two business days. Plan the 21-day trial around that wait, because the integration is the thing you are actually evaluating.

The natural comparison is Shopify Plus native B2B — Shopify's own B2B is included once you are on Plus, and it puts company accounts, price lists and payment terms in the platform itself rather than in an app that sits on top of your theme. Wholesale Gorilla exists for the shops below that line: it brings the same shape of wholesale to a standard Shopify plan for $34.95 to $149.95 a month, which is a fraction of the step up to Plus. The trade is where the logic lives. Native B2B cannot break when a theme updates; an app that integrates with your theme can, and the recurring complaint in this app's own reviews is exactly that — updates and theme changes that interrupt checkout. If you are already on Plus, paying again for this is hard to justify..

Frequently asked questions

Is the 21-day trial long enough to know?

Only if you start the part that needs their help on day one. Every tier carries the same 21-day free trial and there is no free plan underneath it, so the clock runs from install on all three. The catch is that the setup most merchants are actually testing — the Unified Storefront, where approved buyers shop your ordinary product pages at wholesale prices — is theme integration their team performs on request, not a switch you flip. Request it the day you install, not in week three, or you will spend most of the trial inside the app-hosted Wholesale Portal, which is a different product experience and the one their own documentation treats as the fallback. Spend the remaining days the way you would run a real order: one buyer, one price group, one net-terms invoice, one shipping rule, start to finish.

Will it work alongside my other apps?

On your storefront yes, inside the portal no, and that distinction decides which setup you should ask for. The listing's compatibility field names only Checkout, customer accounts and Shopify Admin — this is not an app with a long integration list — and the vendor's own knowledge base is explicit that third-party apps do not function inside the Wholesale Portal, describing the portal as the workaround for stores where the theme integration runs into conflicts. So if your wholesale flow depends on a bundling app, a shipping-rate app, a subscription app or a custom product-page block, those things live on the theme side and you need the Unified Storefront rather than the portal. Make a list of the apps a wholesale order has to pass through before you install, and ask their team whether the theme integration survives them.

Does the bill grow with my wholesale volume?

No, and in this category that is worth saying plainly. There is no usage meter anywhere on the plans: price rules and customer price groups are unlimited on all three tiers, and what the ladder gates is features — quick order form, net terms, shipping rules and theme integration at $69.95, volume discounts and payment terms at $149.95. A store doing ten wholesale orders a month and a store doing a thousand pay the same $34.95, $69.95 or $149.95. The standard Shopify footer line about recurring and usage-based charges appears here as it does on every listing, but nothing on this one is priced by use. That makes the sum simple: pick the rung by the feature you cannot do without, then divide its monthly price by the wholesale orders you expect, and compare that against the per-order cut a B2B channel would take.

What should I weigh before buying E-commerce software?

Check your platform and version specifically. Shopify support is close to universal in this category, while WooCommerce, BigCommerce, Magento and headless setups vary a lot, and 'supported' sometimes means a generic API rather than a maintained app. A listing in your own platform's app store, with recent reviews there, is a better signal than the vendor's logo wall.
