What WP Rocket does

The most powerful WordPress caching plugin. Boost site speed by 300%+ with one-click optimization, lazy loading and CDN integration.

Pricing (2026)

Annual license: Single about $59/yr (one site), Plus $119/yr (three sites), Multi $299/yr (many sites) as of 2026. All tiers are feature-identical — you pay for the number of sites — and it renews at the same rate. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

It's a subscription, not a lifetime license — you keep your settings if you stop paying but lose updates and support, and a technically-minded user can get most of the way there with a free caching plugin.

What buyers report about WP Rocket

Recurring themes from G2, Trustpilot and independent speed tests, read in August 2026. We have not run WP Rocket ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • It delivers real speed gains without asking you to understand caching, which is the whole reason non-developers buy it.
  • • Independent testers consistently measure improvement out of the box, with no configuration.
What they complain about
  • • Renewal is where the complaints are. Trustpilot reviewers report price rises announced close to renewal, new customers getting better rates than long-term subscribers, and double charges paired with a restrictive refund process.
  • • In August 2025 the unlimited-site licence was discontinued and existing customers were moved to a capped Multi 500 tier. If you manage many sites, check which tier you are actually on now.
  • • Sources disagree on whether there is a renewal discount at all — some report 30-50% off, others full price with no loyalty pricing. Confirm your own renewal figure rather than trusting either.
  • • Refunds close after 14 days.

Frequently asked questions

Is WP Rocket secure and reliable enough to depend on?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.

Who should not use WP Rocket?

Skip it if you're comfortable configuring a free caching plugin like LiteSpeed Cache or W3 Total Cache and enjoy the tinkering. Buying a tool to fix a problem you don't have yet just adds cost and another login to manage.
