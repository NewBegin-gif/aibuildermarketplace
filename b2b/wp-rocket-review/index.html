How to actually use WP Rocket

The licence renews automatically after a year and the page never states at what price — that unpublished renewal is where the complaints about this product cluster. Switching it off costs you nothing and turns next year into a decision rather than a charge. Measure before and after with your own numbers. There is no trial, only a 14-day refund, so run a proper speed test on your key pages before you install and again afterwards.

That evidence is what the refund window is actually for. Count your sites, because that is the only thing separating the tiers — they are otherwise feature-identical. Frequently asked questions

Can I try WP Rocket before buying?

No. Their pricing page is direct about it (read 12 August 2026): there is no trial version, and what you get instead is a 14-day refund policy. So the trial is a purchase you undo, and the window is short — install it on the site you actually care about in the first fortnight, not the staging copy.

How many sites does the Multi licence cover?

Not unlimited, and this is where the old advice is out of date. Read on 12 August 2026, Multi carries a site-count selector at 50, 100 or 500 websites, and above 500 the page sends you to a custom licence. Single covers one site, Plus covers three. The tiers are otherwise feature-identical — you are paying purely for the number of installs, so buy the band you are in rather than the one you hope for.

What will it cost me next year?

Their page does not say, and that is the honest answer. It confirms the licence auto-renews after one year and that you can switch that off in your account's Billing section, but it prints no renewal rate anywhere (read 12 August 2026). Trustpilot reviewers are where the complaints about this land: price rises announced close to renewal, and new customers offered better rates than long-standing ones. Check the figure on your own invoice rather than any published number, including ours.

What should I weigh before buying IT & Productivity software?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.
