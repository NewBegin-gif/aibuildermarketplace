What buyers report about Wrike

What they praise

What they complain about

  • • Ratings vary sharply by company size: 3.9 on Trustpilot against 4.7 on Gartner Peer Insights. Small teams rate it worst.

Business has a five-seat floor

There is a permanent free plan for up to five users, then Team at about $10 per user a month and Business at $25 on annual billing, with Pinnacle and Apex above them at custom rates in the region of $50 to $80 a user. Business carries a five-seat minimum (2026). That minimum is the number to catch. A three-person team that needs something from Business pays for five seats — $125 a month — which works out at $41.67 per actual user, 67% above the advertised $25.

The step from Team to Business is not two and a half times the rate for a small team; it is $125 against $30, more than four times the bill. Below five people, that is the real comparison. Above five it settles down: ten people on Business is $250 a month, $3,000 a year, at the advertised rate. The free plan is genuinely generous at five users, and it is also the right way to answer the question that actually matters here, which is not about price.

Wrike is powerful and scales to complex cross-team portfolios — dependencies, resource management, work across departments that do not share a process. That power carries a real learning curve, and small teams frequently find it heavier than they need beside a simpler board tool. So spend a fortnight on the free plan asking one question: are you using the depth? If your work fits comfortably in columns and cards, a simpler tool will be adopted faster and abandoned less, and no Wrike tier improves on that.

If you are already trying to track dependencies across three departments in a spreadsheet beside your board tool, that is exactly the gap Wrike fills and the per-seat cost is worth it. Whichever way that goes, decide it before you cross the five-seat line, because the pricing rewards being sure.

How to actually use Wrike

Run the free plan for a fortnight before you price anything. It covers five users permanently, which is enough to answer the only question that matters: are you using the depth? If your work fits in columns and cards, a simpler board tool will get adopted faster and Wrike's learning curve buys you nothing. If you do move up, check the seat count against the Business minimum. Business has a five-seat floor, so a three-person team pays $125 a month — $41.67 per actual user against an advertised $25, and more than four times what Team would cost.

Under five people, Team at about $10 a user is usually the honest answer. Pick one real cross-team project for the trial rather than a tidy one. Wrike earns its price on dependencies and work that crosses departments, so the test should be the messy project you currently track in a spreadsheet beside your board tool — that is the gap it either closes or does not.

Frequently asked questions

What should I weigh before buying Operations & Workflow software?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.
