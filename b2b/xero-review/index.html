Read the second number

Every headline price on Xero's US page is a promotion, and the page says so: 'Get 90% off your plan for your first 6 months when you buy by 30 September 2026'. Early is $2.50 a month for six months and then $25. Growing is $5.50 and then $55. Established is $9 and then $90. Inventory Plus is an optional paid add-on, and Xero Payroll powered by Gusto is another at $36 a month plus $6 per employee or contractor (2026-08-30, read on their own US pricing page).

So read the second number, not the first. It is not hidden — it is printed right there — but it is printed smaller, and the plan people choose on the strength of $2.50 a month is the one that becomes $25. The gap is a factor of ten. Work out what the offer is actually worth across a year, because '90% off' sounds larger than it is. Six months at 90% off is 5.4 months of free service, which across twelve months is a 45% discount on your first year and nothing thereafter.

In cash: Early costs $165 in year one against $300 at the standard rate, Growing $363 against $660, Established $594 against $1,080. Budget from the standard rate, treat the promotion as a one-off rebate, and note that it is dated — the offer is tied to buying by 30 September 2026. Two more things before you commit. Check which country's page you are looking at, because Xero prices and tiers differently by market and the US page is not the UK or Australian one — a comparison you read elsewhere may be describing a different product at a different price.

And be clear that the entry tier is a genuine floor rather than a taster: if you need multi-currency or project tracking, that is Established at $90, not Early at $25. The decision that actually matters with accounting software is not the monthly fee at all — it is that your books end up inside it, which is what makes leaving expensive a year later. Pick on whether your accountant works in it and whether it connects to your bank properly, and let the $25-versus-$55 question follow from that.

How to actually use Xero

Check which country's page you are on before you read a single price. Xero's tiers and rates differ by market, so a comparison you found elsewhere may describe a different product at a different price — and the US figures are not the UK or Australian ones. Then budget the standard rate rather than the promotional one. Every headline is 90% off for six months: $2.50 becomes $25, $5.50 becomes $55, $9 becomes $90.

Across a full year the offer is worth about 45%, not 90%, so plan from month seven — and the offer itself runs only until 30 September 2026. Pick the tier on the two features that force it rather than on price. Multi-currency and project tracking sit on Established at $90, and if you need either, the cheaper plans are not cheaper versions — they are different products. Inventory Plus is a separate paid add-on again.

Before any of that, ask your accountant or bookkeeper which package they work in, and connect your bank feed on day one. Accounting software is chosen once and lived in for years, and the two things that actually determine whether it works are whether your bank feed is reliable and whether the person doing your year-end is fluent in it. The monthly fee is the smallest part of this decision.

Frequently asked questions

The offer ends 30 September 2026 — what happens the next day?

Two things at once, and the page says both. The 90% offer expires at 11:59pm UTC on 30 September 2026, and a separate notice on the same page states that Xero subscription prices are increasing from 1 October 2026. The new rates are not printed. That means the $25, $55 and $90 you are budgeting against are themselves scheduled to change the day after the discount closes, so the honest planning number for month seven onwards is “$25 or more” rather than $25. If you are buying anyway, buying before the deadline locks the discount but not the standard rate that follows it.

Who actually qualifies for the 90% off?

Narrower than the banner suggests. The terms restrict it to new customers in the United States purchasing their first organisation on xero.com, using code DC90626308US, until 30 September 2026. It applies to the base Early, Growing and Established subscriptions only — not to add-ons, usage or payment fees — and the subscription auto-renews monthly at the regular price once the six months are up. It cannot be combined with or exchanged for another offer, and Xero reserves the right to change or cancel it. If you already have a Xero organisation, this is not your price.

Does the $25 Early plan let me invoice normally?

No, and this is the limit that decides the tier for most people. Early is capped at 20 invoices and 5 bills a month. The footnote is the part that catches people: the invoice limit counts both approving and sending, and transactions initiated by app partners may automatically contribute to it. So a connected store, POS or payments app can spend your monthly allowance without you opening Xero at all. Growing at $55 is the first tier the comparison table puts no number against — it simply reads “Included”, and the word unlimited does not appear anywhere on the page — which makes $55 the realistic entry price for anyone whose billing runs through connected apps.

What else lands on the bill besides the subscription?

Payroll powered by Gusto is $36 a month plus $6 per employee or contractor, and the promotional discount explicitly does not apply to it. Inventory Plus is optional on Growing and Established, excluded on Early, and carries no price on the page. Bill payments are included only for standard ACH in USD; fees apply to other methods, same-day ACH and international or cross-border payments, and those fees are not eligible for subscription discounts. All prices exclude tax, which is calculated from your billing address. One more to note before you pick a tier upward: a downgrade is only permitted one month after upgrading.

What should I weigh before buying Financial Operations software?

The bar here is higher than features. Check who actually holds the money and under what licence, whether client funds are segregated, what audit trail and permission controls you get, and how support behaves when something goes wrong mid-transaction. Anything touching payments should be able to answer those in writing; if it cannot, that is your answer.
