Four prices, none of which is a price

Zedonk is a modular fashion ERP billed annually, and every figure on the page is prefixed 'prices starting from'. PLM is £166 a month, ERP Essential £166, B2C £250 adding Shopify integration and barcodes, and B2B £332 adding the digital wholesale showroom. Those four words are doing more work than the numbers. There is no self-serve signup and no trial — every tier goes through a demo — so none of these is a quote.

£332 a month is £3,984 a year, and that is the floor of the wholesale option rather than its price. One thing the page does settle, and it is worth knowing before the call. B2C and B2B are both described as 'All ERP Essential Features PLUS' — Shopify and barcodes at £250, the digital showroom at £332 — so the wholesale tier already contains the ERP rather than stacking on it.

A wholesale brand is looking at £3,984 a year, not the £5,976 you would get by adding the two cards together. The FAQ then takes the tiers away again: the four displayed plans are 'merely examples', and your plan is assembled from modules and can change at any time. So ask for your stack written out line by line with a total at the bottom — not because the cards are ambiguous, but because yours will not be one of them.

The useful way to size whether any of it is worth having is per style rather than per month. A brand producing four collections a year at sixty styles each is running 240 styles through the system; at £3,984 that is £16.60 a style for tech packs, costings, production tracking and order management. Put your own two numbers in. Two things to establish before the annual commitment. What the renewal looks like, since this is billed yearly and quoted privately.

And what implementation costs, which is a separate question from the licence: every plan includes onboarding and training at no extra cost, but Zedonk's own FAQ puts customisation, data migration and integrations in a one-time figure you only get in a consultation. Get that one-time number in the same email as the monthly one.

How to actually use Zedonk

Go into the demo with your own numbers rather than questions. Styles per season, collections per year, whether you sell wholesale, retail or both, how many suppliers and factories, and how many people need access. Those five figures determine your quote, and having them written down turns a sales call into a pricing conversation. Ask for the stack line by line with a total. Every price says 'starting from', and the FAQ is explicit that the four displayed plans are examples rather than products — your plan is assembled from modules.

B2C and B2B do include ERP Essential ('All ERP Essential Features PLUS'), so £332 is the top of a standard wholesale setup rather than a component of one. Then ask what sits outside the licence. Onboarding and training are included in every plan, but Zedonk's own FAQ puts customisation, data migration and integrations in a separate one-time figure you only get in a consultation. And ask what the price becomes at renewal, since there is no trial and the billing is annual.

Frequently asked questions

What should I weigh before buying Operations & Workflow software?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.
