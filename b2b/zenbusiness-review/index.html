Pricing (2026)

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

A registered agent is a legal requirement in every state, and theirs is $199/year — so the realistic first-year cost of 'the $0 LLC' is the state fee plus $199, before compliance filing at another $199/year. Price the second year, not the first. The second thing to hold in view is that the state fee is the largest and most variable number in the whole exercise, and ZenBusiness cannot change it.

It is set by your state, ranges widely, and their own page sends you to a lookup rather than quoting it. Anyone comparing formation services on the advertised price alone is comparing the small half of the bill.

What ZenBusiness does

US business-formation service — LLC, S corp and C corp filings with no service fee beyond the state's own, and the money in the recurring stack around it: registered agent, compliance service, operating agreement and EIN, each priced individually rather than bundled into tiers.

When the paid plan is worth it

The upgrade question here is not a plan tier, it is which recurring line you keep. Worry-Free Compliance at $199/year buys the annual report and amendment filings being done for you. The same annual report filed individually is $100 per report, and an amendment is $100. So the maths only works if you expect more than one filing a year, or you value not tracking the deadline. Advanced Compliance Protection at $299/year adds unlimited amendment filings and a money-back guarantee on penalties for missed compliance events — that guarantee is the real difference between the two, so read what it covers before paying the extra $100.

Registered agent at $199/year is the one line most businesses genuinely cannot skip, unless you are willing to use your own address publicly and be available during business hours to receive legal documents.

How to actually use ZenBusiness

Look up your state's filing fee and processing time first — their own page has the lookup, and that number will usually be larger than anything ZenBusiness charges you. Decide the registered agent question before you file, because it is a required field on the filing itself: your own address is free but becomes public record and ties you to business hours, theirs is $199/year. Then file at $0 and add nothing else on day one.

The EIN at $99 is the add-on worth pausing on: it is required to open a business bank account or hire, and the IRS issues it directly at no charge, so you are paying to not deal with the IRS yourself. Standard filing is included; rush at $79 only matters if you have a launch date. If you are forming in New York, budget the $200 publication line separately — that state requires a newspaper announcement and it catches people out.

Frequently asked questions

Which of the two compliance subscriptions should I take, if either?

Price them against the a-la-carte filings rather than against each other. Read 22 September 2026, Worry-Free Compliance is $199 a year and Advanced Compliance Protection $299, and the page describes the higher one as adding 24/7 AI monitoring, advanced support, unlimited amendment filings and a money-back guarantee on any penalties for missed compliance events. Filed individually an annual report is $100 per report and an amendment is $100, so the $199 tier only breaks even if you expect more than one filing in a year, and the extra $100 for the top tier is paid back by a single amendment. That makes the decision unusually concrete: if you know you are changing a director, an address or your share structure this year, the $299 tier is arithmetic rather than insurance. If you expect one annual report and nothing else, neither subscription beats paying the $100 when it falls due -- what you would be buying is the deadline being someone else's problem. Read what the penalty guarantee actually covers before you treat it as the tiebreaker.

The website and email products are cheap. Is that a reason to buy them here?

Treat them as a separate purchase, because they are a separate catalogue that happens to share an invoice. Re-verified 22 September 2026: business website $129 a year, eCommerce website $279 a year, business email address $25 a year, domain $25 a year, domain privacy $10 a year, logo kit $60, business banking $5 a month and Money Pro with online payments $30 a month. None of these is discounted for being bought alongside the formation, and each competes with general-purpose providers on its own merits, so compare them one at a time rather than as a bundle. There is one structural point worth a thought: buying the domain here puts your registered agent, your public filing address and your DNS with the same vendor, which is convenient until you want to leave. The legal lines and the web lines are independent -- you can take the $199 registered agent and none of this.

What is genuinely unavoidable in the first year?

Two things, and only one of them is ZenBusiness's. The state's filing fee is the larger and more variable number in the whole exercise, it is set by your state rather than by ZenBusiness, and their page sends you to a lookup instead of quoting it -- so it is not visible until you pick a state. The other is the registered agent, which is a legal requirement in every state; theirs is $199 a year, and the alternative is using your own address, which becomes public record and ties you to being available during business hours. Everything else on the price list is optional, including the $99 EIN, the $99 operating agreement, the $79 rush filing and both compliance subscriptions. New York is the one jurisdiction with a third unavoidable line: $200 for the newspaper publication that state requires. Re-verified 22 September 2026.

What should I weigh before buying Operations & Workflow software?

Usually it sits on top rather than replacing everything. Tools in this class tend to become the layer where work is tracked and handed off, while your existing systems stay the source of truth for their own data. Before you buy, map which tools it would replace outright and which it would merely sync with. If the answer is that it syncs with all of them, you are adding a layer rather than removing one.
