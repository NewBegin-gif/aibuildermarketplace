Zendrop Review (2026)

E-commerceResearched assessment · by Daniel HaketUpdated 2026-09-15

The free plan is a catalogue browser rather than a working tier: it allows no linked products, so nothing can actually be sold through it.

Best for: Shopify merchants starting or scaling a dropshipping or print-on-demand store who want sourcing, automated fulfilment, tracking and custom branding in one app, and who can price the per-order product and shipping cost into their margins.
Who should skip it: if you already have supplier relationships worth keeping, or you need the cheapest possible international shipping — the vetted US supplier network is the selling point here and you pay for it.

Free to install, with three paid tiers: Beginner $29, Pro $49 and Plus $79 a month. What separates them is not features but how many products you may link to your store. The free tier allows none at all, Beginner covers 1-20, Pro 21-100 and Plus 101 or more. Product and shipping cost is charged per order on top of every plan, and the listing states that external charges may be billed by Zendrop separately from your Shopify invoice. That offer sits on Zendrop's own site rather than on the App Store listing, so which price you pay depends on where you sign up. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Past that you are paying for an allowance, not for capability — Beginner, Pro and Plus carry exactly the same feature list, and the only thing $79 buys over $29 is room for more than a hundred linked products. The subscription is also the smaller half of the bill: product and shipping cost lands per order on top, and Zendrop bills that separately from your Shopify invoice, so the figure you budget is not the figure you pay.

The 4.5 average covers a split verdict, with 83% five stars alongside 7% one star across 1,191 reviews.

What Zendrop does

AI dropshipping and print-on-demand for Shopify: browse 1M+ products from US and global suppliers, link them to your store and let fulfilment and tracking run automatically, with custom branding, POD and 3PL as you scale. Free to install, but linking products needs a paid tier — $29, $49 or $79 a month by product count, with per-order product and shipping cost on top.

What buyers report about Zendrop

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Zendrop ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Support resolution is overwhelmingly what the positive reviews describe, and reviewers name individual agents. Several say a refund they did not expect was issued within minutes or after an agent stayed with the case for weeks.
  • • Onboarding for beginners: reviewers repeatedly say the Shopify connection and product importing work without friction and that the interface is manageable for a first store.
  • • Fulfilment itself gets steady credit from longer-tenured merchants — a UK store ten months in says order processing has been smooth.
What they complain about
  • • Billing is the single biggest theme in the negative reviews, and it has a specific shape. Reviewers report that the advertised free trial charges immediately, that refunds are only available within five days of that charge, and that the cancellation path is hard to find — one June 2026 reviewer was charged for a renewal and could not locate a cancellation option at all.
  • • The plan scales with how many products you link (free up to ten, then paid tiers). A June 2026 reviewer says the downgrade is behind a toggle that defaults to on, so you keep paying for products you are not selling.
  • • Stock accuracy: the same reviewer built a store and ad campaign around products marked in stock, and found they flipped to out of stock at the moment of ordering a sample. Their advice — do not run ads on anything until you have successfully placed a real sample order — is the most actionable line in the negative reviews.
  • • Margins: an August 2026 Shopify reviewer claims the wholesale prices on many items run $5-$10 above what the same goods retail for on Amazon or Walmart, before your margin, shipping and the $29-$79 monthly fee.
  • • An August 2026 reviewer reports that installing the app published a Zendrop demo theme and deactivated their live theme on a store with paid traffic running. The vendor apologised publicly for the disruption.
  • • A July 2026 reviewer was locked out for over a week by a blank white screen at login, with no way to reach support without being logged in first.

Where the ratings disagree. Two platforms, two different pictures. Trustpilot shows 4.6 across roughly 20,700 reviews with 2% one-stars; the Shopify App Store shows 4.5 across 1,197 with 79 one-stars, about 6.6%. The gap is in who writes and about what. A large share of the Trustpilot five-stars are people thanking a named agent for resolving a charge or issuing a refund — which means the volume of praise is partly a measure of how many buyers hit a billing problem in the first place. The Shopify reviews come from merchants who installed the app into a live store and write about margins, stock accuracy and the theme override. Read Trustpilot for how Zendrop handles you when something goes wrong, and the Shopify listing for what goes wrong.

When the paid plan is worth it

Read what the free tier actually is before anything else: free to install means you may link zero products to your store. It is not a usable free plan, it is an empty account, so the real entry price is $29 a month. Above that the tiers are a product allowance and nothing else -- the feature list is identical on all three paid plans (trending products, Zendrop Academy, Zendrop AI access, custom branding).

Beginner at $29 covers 1 to 20 products, Pro at $49 covers 21 to 100, Plus at $79 covers 101 or more. Per product at each ceiling that is about $1.45, $0.49 and under $0.78, so the middle tier is the best value per line and the step to Plus only pays once you are genuinely past a hundred. Since no feature is gated, upgrading early buys you nothing at all.

Neither the plan nor the product allowance is the bill. So the App Store subscription is the smaller of the two lines you will pay. One thing that decides the price is not on the listing at all. That offer lives on their own site rather than in the App Store, so where you sign up changes what you pay.

How to actually use Zendrop

Install on the free tier first and treat it as research: you can browse the full catalogue there, but you cannot link a product, so nothing ships until you are on a paid plan. Pick the tier by counting the products you actually intend to list rather than the ones you want to try, because the ladder is priced on linked products (1-20, 21-100, 101 or more) and that count is the only thing you are choosing.

Before committing, put the per-order product and shipping cost of your three best candidates next to your intended retail price — that number decides whether the store works, and no plan changes it. Finally, the app bills outside your Shopify invoice, so put it in whatever you use to track subscriptions instead of expecting it to appear on the Shopify bill.
