The free plan does not cap your users

The free plan carries unlimited users, unlimited chats and built-in to-dos. Pro is about $3 per user a month for integrations and storage, Business+ around $8 for roles, audit trails and SLAs, and Enterprise is custom. That first sentence is the whole product decision. Team chat tools normally limit the free tier by people or by history — the two things a growing team needs most — and ZenZap limits it by integrations and storage instead. So a twenty-person team can run organised channels and shared to-dos indefinitely without paying, which is a genuinely complete landing spot rather than a countdown.

The paid line is drawn at integrations, and that makes the evaluation specific rather than general. Do not ask whether ZenZap has integrations; write down the five your team actually touches every day and check those five by name. Team chat lives or dies on the connections and the habit, and this app catalogue is a fraction of Slack's. A team wired into hundreds of integrations will feel the gap immediately; a team whose work arrives by message will not notice it at all.

Business+ at $8 is a different kind of purchase from Pro at $3. Roles, audit trails and SLAs are compliance features: you buy them because a regulator, a customer's security questionnaire or your own policy requires them, not because they make anyone's day easier. If nobody has asked you for an audit trail, do not pay 2.7 times more for one. A twenty-person team is $720 a year on Pro against $1,920 on Business+.

The comparison that actually applies here is often not Slack. This lands best with teams currently running their work inside WhatsApp — where there are no channels, no task list and no boundary between a delivery schedule and someone's family group. Against that, the free tier is a substantial upgrade at no cost. One thing to settle before you move: ask what happens to your existing chat history, because leaving it behind is a real cost that no pricing page will mention.

How to actually use ZenZap

List the five integrations your team touches every day and check them by name before you move anything. That is where the paid line falls and it is the only meaningful test — the app catalogue here is a fraction of Slack's, so 'does it integrate' is the wrong question and 'does it connect to these five' is the right one.

Then run the free plan properly, because it does not cap your users. Unlimited people, unlimited chats and built-in to-dos mean a whole team can work in it for months without paying — so migrate one project or one department and let habit decide rather than a trial clock.

Ask what happens to your existing history before you commit. Whether old conversations can be imported, or whether you are starting empty, is a real cost that appears on no pricing page. And leave Business+ alone unless somebody has actually asked you for roles, audit trails or an SLA — at $8 a user against Pro's $3, that tier is a compliance purchase, not a productivity one.
