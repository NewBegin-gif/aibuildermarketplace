1. Aspire — Lean, global teams that want finance in one app

All-in-one finance stack for global businesses: multi-currency accounts (30+ currencies), corporate cards with 1.2% cashback, expense management, bill pay, payments and payroll — with AI fraud detection and multi-admin approval workflows.

My take: My top pick: a multi-currency account, 1.2%-cashback corporate cards, expense automation and AI fraud-freeze — the closest thing to a finance department in one dashboard, with no minimum balance. Read our full Aspire review →

2. Payoneer — Getting paid by international clients & marketplaces

Local receiving accounts for freelancers and sellers paid by international clients and marketplaces. Free to open — the cost sits in the fees: 1% on non-local currency received, 1.2–4% to withdraw to a bank in another country, whether or not currency is converted, $1.50 flat to withdraw locally, and a $29.95 annual fee waived above $6,000 received.

My take: The veteran for cross-border receivables — if your income arrives from abroad, this is the simplest way to collect it in multiple currencies. Read our full Payoneer review →

Visit Payoneer →

3. Bill.com — Automating accounts payable & receivable

Accounts-payable and receivable automation for finance teams with real approval chains. Priced per user from about $45/month, with transaction fees stacked on top — roughly $0.49 ACH, $1.99 cheque, $9.99 international wire, 2.9% card.

My take: Best when invoices and approvals eat your week: strong AP/AR automation with accounting sync. Read our full Bill.com review →

Visit Bill.com →

4. Melio — Simple B2B bill payments for small teams

Bill payments for small businesses by bank transfer or card, syncing with your accounting tools. The free Go plan covers five ACH transfers a month, then $0.50 each; Core is about $25/month. Card payments carry a flat 2.9%, which only pays off when you are deliberately floating cash.

My take: Pay vendors by bank transfer or card even when they only take checks — low-friction and free to start. Read our full Melio review →
