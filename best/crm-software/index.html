CRM records, marketing journeys and calling are separate needs

A CRM can hold the customer record without being the right place to run every marketing campaign. Before choosing Keap, folk or Close, describe the work that starts a customer relationship and the work that keeps it moving. Who adds the person, what counts as an opportunity and who decides the next action? Then identify whether the purchase is mainly for a combined business workflow, relationship management or an inside-sales team working through calls and messages.

The three tools in this guide do not offer interchangeable versions of one marketing-automation system. Keap's dossier describes CRM, email, payments and automation together. folk's describes a lighter relationship-management approach. Close's describes a sales workflow with communication functions. A requirement for a particular marketing journey must be checked on the proposed plan. Do not infer equivalent automation because all three appear under a CRM heading.

Keap: map the whole business workflow before paying

Keap deserves evaluation when the team intends to use the combined CRM, email, payment and automation workflow described in the dossier. Draw the process with its trigger, decision, message and handoff to a person. Include the correction path: what happens if a contact is misclassified or a customer replies at the wrong stage? A workflow is not complete until someone can explain how it stops as well as how it begins.

Our dossier cautions about the learning work involved and the commercial commitment. Avoid buying the range of features as a substitute for defining who will configure them. Assign an owner and ask that person to build a representative journey during evaluation. Keep payment actions and live communications out of a casual demonstration. A successful internal test should show what a customer record looks like at each stage and what another colleague can safely change.

If the current requirement is only a simple pipeline or an occasional newsletter, compare that limited job with the cost and upkeep of the combined platform. The benefit of one system depends on genuinely using the connected work. It does not establish a revenue increase, and this guide has no comparative conversion measurements for Keap. Use the review for the recorded terms and unresolved purchasing questions rather than assuming that a more comprehensive package produces a better outcome.

folk: confirm the connection points you will need

folk's dossier is relevant to a smaller team seeking relationship records and a manageable setup. List the sources from which relationships enter the system and the actions a colleague must take next. If connecting another system is essential, verify the plan boundary before importing the full address book. Our dossier notes that the API and particular automation connections sit above the entry tier. That can change which plan belongs in the comparison.

The dated hands-on review describes the trial conditions and what our team actually checked. It does not establish that a connected mailbox, social account or live outreach workflow was tested when those accounts were not connected. Use the observed record work as evidence for that task, and treat untested integrations separately. During your own evaluation, rehearse the exact connection with safe records and confirm what a user with ordinary permissions can do.

A lightweight CRM should still have a convention for duplicate people, organisations and ownership. Agree that convention before migration. Ask a second user to find a relationship, understand its status and record the next action. If the information is only meaningful to the person who created it, an attractive interface has not solved the handoff. Decide which fields are mandatory and what will remain in another system.

Close: separate the seat from communication usage

Close's dossier centres on inside-sales work with calls, SMS and customer records in the same workflow. That makes it worth trialling when those communication tasks are frequent enough to justify the arrangement. Record the channels the team actually uses and the expected activity. Do not compare only the per-user subscription if the proposed workflow also needs numbers, calling or messaging usage.

Our team has a dated trial record, with explicit limits on what was connected and exercised. A record-management test does not establish call quality, SMS delivery or campaign results when no live calls or messages were sent. Keep those boundaries attached to the evidence. If calling is the reason to buy, make the supported destinations, number requirements and usage charges part of your own acceptance process before approving a full rollout.

Also distinguish the plan needed by one person from the plan needed by a team. A single-user entry tier can be a real offer without being the relevant purchase for several salespeople. Count paid users and the required capabilities together. The pricing panel below reads the central rules and preserves the billing term; it does not turn the lowest figure on a ladder into an organisation-wide quote.

Give every candidate the same record exercise

Use a small fixture with a person, an organisation, an opportunity and a next action. Include one duplicate and one correction. Import the fixture, change a stage and export the resulting records. Inspect the actual file instead of stopping at the success message. Check whether the information needed by the next system is present and whether someone can explain the difference between a contact record and an opportunity.

Test a handoff between two colleagues if the purchase is for a team. Have one create the record and the other find it without being guided through the interface. Try the permissions that matter to your process. These are proposed evaluation tasks, not measurements this guide claims to have run across all three tools. The aim is a comparable acceptance record, with unresolved issues written down rather than concealed inside an overall score.

Define automation in observable terms

An automation requirement should name the trigger, data used, resulting action and stop condition. Ask which plan covers every part. A reminder to a salesperson, a scheduled message and a multi-stage marketing journey are different capabilities. A generic automation label does not establish that the required connection, permission or channel exists on the selected tier. Where the dossier has no evidence, keep the requirement open and ask the supplier.

Use harmless internal records while testing. Keep a copy before enabling a rule and confirm how to turn it off. Review who can edit the rule and who notices an exception. An automation that saves an action but hides an error can create more work later. That is a process-design consideration, not a claim that any of these products has failed a particular scenario.

Compare the first usable configuration

Build the commercial comparison around the plan your team can actually use. Include users, the relevant communication usage and any separately priced service. Keep annual commitments distinct from monthly payments. If a central price rule is missing or older than the dossier's price reading, the table leaves it unconfirmed. Open the review for context and obtain a matching quote where necessary; an old entry amount is not a reliable substitute.

Choose Keap for a trial of the combined business process, folk for a trial of lighter relationship management with checked connection points, and Close for a trial of a communication-heavy sales workflow. Approve the purchase when the team can repeat the record journey, explain the bill and retrieve the information it needs. The product with the longest feature list is not automatically the product that completes that work.
