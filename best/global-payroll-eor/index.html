The best global payroll and EOR platforms (2026)

Hiring in one country only? read our honest Gusto review → — what US payroll costs once the per-employee fee stacks on the base.
Short answer: The best global payroll eor are Remote People for eOR without an entity: the cheapest published EOR rate we have on file — $199 a month, billed monthly, no minimum headcount; Deel for eOR or contractor management: the widest reach — contractors paid compliantly in 150+ countries, or full EOR where you need someone on the ground fast; Rippling for payroll where you have an entity: one employee record across HR, IT and Finance — a hire triggers payroll, a laptop and app access at once, and offboarding claws it all back. Below, what each is best for — and who should skip it.

Three different jobs share this page, and the price of choosing the wrong one is an order of magnitude. Path one: you have no entity in the country and want the person employed there — that is employer of record (Remote People, Deel, Oyster). Path two: you have an entity, usually in the US, and want payroll, benefits and filings run for you (Gusto, Rippling, ADP). Path three: you are paying contractors, not employees (Deel, Oyster at their contractor rates). Compare inside a path, never across. Employer of record is one product with wildly different price tags. Read on their own pricing pages: Remote People starts EOR at $199 per employee a month with no commitment and no minimum headcount (7 August 2026), Deel starts at $599 (17 August 2026), and Oyster at $699 (14 August 2026). Same job — they employ your hire in a country where you have no entity — and a three-and-a-half-fold spread. Then there are the numbers that never appear on a price card at all: Oyster holds a refundable deposit per EOR employee and takes a currency-conversion fee, and neither carries a figure anywhere on the page. Meanwhile the largest names publish nothing. ADP shows no rate for RUN in four places we checked — /pricing is a 404 and every route ends at a quote form (15 August 2026) — and Rippling publishes exactly one number, an $8 per user a month floor, beside two buttons that say get a quote (27 August 2026). So the first question is not who is cheapest. It is whether you need employment abroad at all, because contractor management does a different job at a tenth of the price.

How to choose

Start by naming what you actually need, because the price gap between the answers is enormous. Paying a contractor abroad runs $29 to $49 a month per person. Employing that same person through an EOR runs $199 to $699. Carrying the misclassification risk yourself sits in between, at $199 to $325 for a contractor of record. Those are three different products and the wrong one costs you an order of magnitude. Then count the people and the countries: EOR is priced per employee per month for as long as the person is employed, while your own entity costs incorporation, local payroll, accounting and a wind-down later; where the crossover sits depends on the country and the horizon, and we have not computed it here — ask each vendor for a 12-, 24- and 36-month comparison for your case and hold them to the answer. Finally, ask for the costs that are not on the price card. Oyster's refundable deposit and its FX fee are both real and both unpriced publicly; deposits are working capital out of your account before anyone is paid. And treat every 'starting at' as the floor of a range that sales will set.

Frequently asked questions

What does an employer of record actually cost?

Between $199 and $699 per employee per month on the published rates we read in August 2026 — Remote People at the low end, Deel at $599, Oyster at $699. Every one of those is a 'starting at' figure that firms up in a sales conversation, and two common costs sit outside it entirely: a refundable deposit per employee and a currency-conversion fee on payments made in another currency.

Do I need an EOR, or will contractor management do?

It depends on whether the person is genuinely an independent contractor under local law, and getting that wrong is the risk the whole category exists to sell you out of. The price difference is roughly tenfold: contractor management from $29 a month against EOR from $199. In between sits contractor of record, at $199 to $325, where the vendor carries the misclassification risk instead of you.

Why won't ADP and Rippling publish a price?

Both sell modular products where the bill depends on headcount, modules and country, so a single number would be misleading — that is the honest version of the argument. The effect on you is the same either way: you cannot compare them against anyone without first handing a salesperson your headcount. Rippling does publish one figure, $8 per user a month, but it is a floor for the HR-and-payroll entry rather than the price of the platform.

Is the cheapest EOR the right EOR?

Not automatically, and the monthly rate is the smaller half of the decision. What matters more is which countries the vendor actually covers, whether it employs people itself or through a local partner, how quickly it can onboard, and what happens when someone leaves. Price the monthly fee against those, and ask each vendor to put its country list and notice terms in writing.
