Start with the document journey

PDF editing, collecting signatures and sending a fax are different jobs. A business can need all three without needing every employee to buy all three. Follow a representative document from arrival to archive: who changes the content, who approves it, who signs it and who needs the finished copy? That sequence identifies the paid capability. If the only action is reading a finished document, start by asking whether an existing reader already covers the work.

This guide compares Foxit, SignNow and Alohi by those jobs. It does not rank them by a single overall score. Foxit's dossier centres on editing, OCR and document organisation. SignNow's centres on signature requests. Alohi covers separately subscribed faxing, signatures and scanning products. Comparing the cheapest advertised figure without identifying the relevant product can therefore compare a fax allowance with a PDF editor or a signature service, which tells a buyer very little.

Editing and signing should have separate owners

For the editing stage, collect examples with the awkward features you actually encounter: a scanned page, an existing form and a document with a layout someone must preserve. Define the change needed on each. Ask the trial user to complete it and explain how another colleague would repeat the operation. A demonstration with a clean, newly created PDF does not answer whether your incoming documents will be manageable.

For the signing stage, map the sender and every recipient. Decide whether signatures need to happen in sequence, what the sender must see while waiting and what record your business must retain afterwards. Use the actual process to ask about plan coverage. Do not infer that an editor's fill-and-sign feature includes a business workflow for requesting signatures from other people. Likewise, buying a signing tool does not establish that it can repair a difficult scanned PDF.

Foxit: check the commitment as well as the payment interval

The Foxit dossier records editing, conversion, OCR, forms and document security, and distinguishes subscription capabilities from a perpetual licence. That distinction is useful if a team wants a conventional desktop editor rather than a cloud workflow. Write down whether browser access, cloud storage, mobile use and AI features are requirements. Price the licence that actually covers those requirements instead of assuming that a one-time purchase contains every subscription feature.

There is a particularly important billing qualification in our review: the recorded PDF Editor toggle offers an annual plan paid in full and an annual plan billed monthly. Monthly instalments therefore do not establish a monthly cancellation right. Treat contract duration and payment frequency as separate columns in your purchasing notes. If you need the tool for a short project, resolve the commitment before paying. The dated review explains the options recorded in that reading; this guide does not turn an instalment amount into a cancellable monthly subscription.

When signatures are also needed, inspect the exact bundle and allowance in the review. Do not add a separate signing subscription before checking whether your proposed editor subscription already covers the relevant work. Conversely, do not assume that a bundled allowance is large enough for your annual sending volume. Count the documents and the envelope or invitation unit the supplier uses, then compare the expected workload with that unit.

SignNow: count invitations rather than colleagues

SignNow is worth evaluating when many people send or sign documents occasionally. Our dossier describes unlimited users on the relevant plan alongside a bounded annual invitation allowance. Those are compatible statements: access can be broad while the activity is metered. A team-size comparison alone would miss the limit most likely to determine the purchase. Record how many requests your business sends in a normal month and during its busiest period.

During a trial, walk through a complete request using harmless sample information. Check what the sender receives after completion and how your colleague will find that evidence later. Ask how retries, replacement documents and additional recipients affect the measured unit if those details matter to your process. Where the dossier does not answer a counting question, obtain clarification rather than guessing. A trial can reveal the workflow, but it does not change the contract or establish a refund right.

Keep the review open when comparing annual and monthly offers. Our price rules distinguish billing terms, and the currency recorded in the rules must remain attached to the amount. A number in euros cannot be relabelled as dollars because another part of an older page used a dollar symbol. Compare the same term and the same allowance. Alohi: choose a product before choosing a plan

Alohi's Fax.Plus, Sign.Plus and Scan.Plus are separate products in our dossier. Begin with the task your business needs and write that product name on the request for approval. A team needing scanning and signature requests may be looking at more than one subscription. A low fax entry price is not an entry price for that entire workflow. The linked review is the place to inspect the separate allowances and dated billing details.

For faxing, list the destinations and expected incoming and outgoing volume before asking for a quote or selecting a tier. For signatures, identify the signature level and supporting records your process requires. For regulated work, send the precise requirement to the supplier and your responsible adviser. A product name, security label or feature on another tier does not establish that the proposed configuration meets a particular obligation. This guide does not certify legal suitability.

Our dossier notes that some compliance-related fax capabilities depend on a higher tier. That is a reason to check the plan boundary early, before moving documents into a trial. Use anonymous examples while confirming the workflow. Ask who administers the account, which colleagues need access and how records are exported. If the answer requires an additional Alohi product, include that line explicitly in the cost comparison.

Compare a year of real activity

Build a small worksheet with the licence or account charge, billing term, expected activity and any allowance boundary relevant to the product. For editing, the number of people who need paid editing rights may matter. For signing, invitations or envelopes may be the useful unit. For faxing, volume and destinations may change the decision. Keep those units distinct instead of trying to find one universally cheapest subscription.

Include the time needed to establish templates, approvals and an archive convention. That time is an internal planning assumption, not a supplier price and not something we have measured for these tools. Assign an owner and a completion criterion. A tool that covers the documented functions still needs a repeatable process for correcting a document, withdrawing an incorrect request and finding the signed result after the original sender leaves.

A practical acceptance checklist

Approve the purchase only after someone has completed the editing or sending journey with representative sample documents. Check the result on the recipient's side, inspect the archived file and confirm who can retrieve it. Record unresolved issues alongside the proposed plan. Do not let a successful demonstration of one document stand in for every format or signature requirement your business handles.

Use the review links below for the underlying dossier and its reading dates. There are no comparative hands-on results for these three tools in this guide, so workflow suggestions here are things for you to test. The decision is straightforward once the jobs are separated: evaluate editing for the people who edit, signature activity for the people who send requests, and a separate fax or scanning product only when that job exists.

Keep the recipient's experience in the decision

Include a recipient who did not prepare the document in the acceptance exercise. Ask them to open the request, identify what they are approving and find the completed copy. Check the route for correcting a mistake before signature. The sender's dashboard can look complete while the recipient still needs help; both sides belong in the workflow you are buying.
