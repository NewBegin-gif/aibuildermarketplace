Should You Buy an AI Agent, or Build One? (Real Costs)

'AI agent' is 2026's most oversold phrase. Before you pay a monthly fee for someone else's agent, it's worth knowing how cheaply you can often build your own — even without a coding background.

Here's the honest build-vs-buy comparison, from someone who builds with AI rather than writing code.

When buying makes sense

If you need a polished, supported agent for a common job (support, scheduling, outreach) and don't want to maintain anything, a ready-made platform is worth it. Lindy → and MindStudio → let you stand one up fast.

When building wins

If your workflow is specific, you want full control, or you'd otherwise pay per-seat forever, building is often cheaper and more flexible. Dify → (open, low-code) and Replit → (build with AI) make it reachable for non-coders.

The real costs nobody mentions

Buying: predictable subscription, less control, possible lock-in. Building: cheaper to run, but you own maintenance and the learning curve. Model both over a year — the 'cheap' subscription often isn't.

Frequently asked questions

Can a non-coder build an AI agent?

Increasingly yes — low-code platforms like Dify and AI build tools like Replit let non-coders assemble agents from plain-English prompts. You still need to define the workflow clearly.

Is it cheaper to build or buy an AI agent?

It depends on usage. Ready-made agents are faster but charge ongoing fees; building costs more effort up front but is cheaper to run and more flexible. Model a full year of both.

Which AI agent platform should I start with?

For buy-and-go, try Lindy or MindStudio; for building with control, Dify or Replit. Start with the simplest one that fits your specific job.
