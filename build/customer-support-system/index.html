Step by step

  1. 1

    Map your repetitive questions

    Take a recent batch of tickets, group the questions that repeat, and note which ones your help content already answers well. Those are the candidates for automation.

  2. 2

    Add live chat

    Add chat where customers already ask, and answer from the same help content.

  3. 3

    Set up voice the smart way

    A cloud phone with routing and voicemail sends each call to the right person without desk hardware; decide who covers calls outside office hours.

  4. 4

    Deploy an AI agent for the basics

    Let an AI agent answer the repeat questions you mapped. Test it on past questions, including ones it should not answer, and check the handover to a person.

  5. 5

    Route everything through a help desk

    One place for tickets and workflows, so every request has an owner.

One request, end to end

Pick the channels first. If nobody phones you, leave out the phone system; if your tickets do not repeat, leave out the AI. Then wire each part to the help desk and check it before you add the next. Every link below is one the vendors document themselves.

  1. 1

    A chat becomes a ticket

    Connect Freshchat to Freshdesk. An agent then turns a conversation into a Freshdesk ticket with “Resolve and Create Ticket”, or an automation creates one, for example when no agent has replied. Chats are not converted on their own by default, so decide which of the two you want.

    Check: Send a test chat from a private window, resolve it into a ticket, and confirm the ticket sits in Freshdesk under the right contact.

  2. 2

    A call becomes a ticket

    Install the Aircall integration for Freshdesk. For every call and voicemail, Aircall creates a ticket in Freshdesk on the caller’s contact, and the agent sees that contact’s history when the phone rings. Aircall sells a minimum of three licences.

    Check: Ring your support number from a phone Freshdesk does not know and leave a voicemail. One new ticket should appear for that call, on a new contact.

  3. 3

    Every ticket gets an owner

    Decide who owns which kind of request before anything is automated: billing, orders and technical questions each go to a named person or group in Freshdesk.

    Check: Open three test tickets, one of each kind. Each must land with its owner without anyone moving it by hand.

  4. 4

    The AI answers what repeats

    Connect Typewise to Freshdesk; its integrations page says it reads, triages and resolves Freshdesk tickets end to end. Its meter is published: a ticket it closes counts as one resolution, a hand-off to a person as half, and a ticket it passes straight through or flags is free. Whether a reopened ticket counts again is not published.

    Check: Run the 14-day trial (100 resolution credits) on a batch of real past tickets, including ones it should not answer. Count how many it closed and how many it handed off, and read every closed answer.

  5. 5

    A person takes over

    Write down what must always reach a person, such as refund disputes, complaints and anything legal, and keep that list next to the AI settings.

    Check: Send one such request as a test. It must reach a person with the conversation attached, not an AI reply.

FAQ

How do I handle more support without hiring?

Find the questions that repeat, answer them well in your help content, and let chat and an AI agent serve those answers, with a clear handover to your team for everything else. How much that takes off your team depends on your own ticket mix; measure it on a recent batch.

Does AI support actually work?

For routine questions that your help content answers well, it can. Test it on real past questions, including ones it should hand over, and make escalation to a person easy for anything complex or sensitive.

Chat, voice or both?

Start with chat if most customers already write to you, and add phone once call volume justifies it. Many teams run both through one help desk.

What's the first thing to set up?

Live chat plus a help desk to capture and route tickets. Layer in AI deflection and voice as volume grows.
