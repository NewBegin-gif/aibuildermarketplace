The Hidden Cost Report 2026: where software pricing actually leaks

Original researchBy Daniel Haket647 tools researched · Data as of 2026-11-12

A pricing page is a marketing document. The real price lives in the footnotes: the seat that wasn't included, the credits that don't roll over, the feature that turns out to be an add-on. We hand-researched the pricing of 647 B2B and AI tools, reading the fine print, and mapped where the money actually leaks. Trap 1 — Usage metering: the sticker price assumes you'll stay small

The most common pattern in our corpus, found in 44% of the tools: a reasonable-looking subscription with a meter running underneath — credits, overage minutes, per-form fees, or a percentage of the money flowing through the tool. Metering isn't dishonest; it's misjudged. The sticker assumes usage limits that an actively used tool routinely exceeds, which means the price rises precisely when the tool is working.

“Both plans charge 10% of the ad spend Adwisely manages, with a monthly minimum that acts as a floor rather than a fee on top: $49 on Self-Serve and $249 on Concierge (managed).” — our Adwisely dossier
“The headline price is a floor, not a ceiling: the included minutes, numbers and SMS are modest, so an active campaign quickly hits metered overages that meaningfully lift the bill.” — our CallRail dossier

The defense is arithmetic, not suspicion: calculate the price at your real volume — this month's and the volume you're hoping for. A fee of 10% on managed ad spend is ten times a $49 minimum once that spend reaches $5,000 a month. That's not hidden; it's just not on the button.

Trap 2 — Per-seat pricing: the cost of growing

22% of the corpus scales its price with every user you add. Per-seat is the most honest-looking model — the number is right there — but it hides a compounding assumption: that everyone who touches the tool needs a paid login. Field crews, occasional viewers, the bookkeeper who checks once a month.

“On Freshdesk the annual rates are unchanged since 1 August 2026: Growth $19 per agent per month, Pro $55, Enterprise $89, billed annually.” — our Freshworks dossier

The question that saves the money: who needs a seat versus who needs a report? Many tools offer free guest, viewer or client roles; the difference between a 5-seat and a 12-seat bill is often just role hygiene.

Trap 3 — Add-on gating: the plan is the lobby, not the building

In 28% of researched tools (184 of 647), the feature you actually came for is a paid add-on on top of the advertised plan. This is the trap that most reliably breaks budgets, because the gap between sticker and real can be multiples:

“Paid add-ons on top of the seat price: Copilot $50 per agent/month (€50), Workforce Engagement bundle $50 (€50), Contact Center $83 (€82).” — our Zendesk dossier
“Add-ons: warmed-up mailbox $33 per mailbox setup, per-campaign managed service $149 per campaign, fully managed service $2,500 per month.” — our AISDR dossier

Rule: price the features you need, never the plan. Make a three-line list of must-haves before opening the pricing page, then find which tier — plus which add-ons — actually contains all three.

Trap 4 — Annual commitment: the best price assumes a year up front

22% of the tools (140 of 647) show their headline price only on annual billing, or ask for a yearly commitment before a plan is available at all. The monthly rate is higher, and a year paid up front is money you cannot get back if the tool doesn't fit.

“Annual billing is 20% less than twelve monthly payments: $96 per user for Pro and $288 per user for Team, with the full amount added as credits.” — our AI for Database dossier

Start monthly when that option exists, even at the higher rate, and switch to annual only once the tool has proven itself in your real workflow.

Trap 5 — Hidden pricing: quote-only pricing

1 in 20 tools in our corpus (33 of 647) publishes no usable price at all — a demo call stands between you and a number. Quote-based pricing isn't a scam; it usually means pricing genuinely varies by deployment. But it moves the negotiation to their home field.

Tools that publish an entry price and ask for a quote only above it are not counted here: the Quote-Only Club lists them separately.

“No published price: feedvisor.com/pricing returns a 404 and the plan comparison page routes every tier to Request Demo (read 3 Sep 2026)” — our Feedvisor dossier

The counter-move: demand the all-in figure in writing — base, seats, add-ons, implementation, and what happens at renewal. A vendor who won't write it down is telling you something.

Trap 6 — The renewal jump (rarer than you think)

The classic intro-price-then-triple trap appears in only 1 in 34 of our corpus (19 of 647), and it clusters in IT & Productivity and Financial Operations rather than mainstream B2B SaaS.

“The promotional rates end: Lite is $1 a month for the first year and $23 after that, and Plus and Premium are 80% off for three months only.” — our FreshBooks dossier

The defense takes two minutes: our free Before You Renew check finds your notice deadline and puts a calendar reminder in before the cancel window closes. Check the renewal rate when buying hosting or antivirus, but don't let renewal-paranoia distract from the everyday leaks above.

The good news: 35% has a real free tier

224 of the 647 researched tools offer a genuinely free plan or version — not a trial, a tier. That's an evaluation strategy: for many jobs you can assemble a zero-cost stack first, prove the workflow, and pay only where the free tier's ceiling actually constrains you. How to read a pricing page in five minutes

The five questions, in order:
1. What's metered? (credits, minutes, forms, % of spend — calculate at your real volume)
2. Who needs a paid seat vs a free role? (count logins, not people)
3. Which of my three must-have features are add-ons?
4. Is the headline price monthly or annual, and what does renewal cost — in writing?
5. Is there a free tier that covers month one? (35% of the time: yes)

Methodology — and why you can check every claim

The numbers come from our research dossiers on all 647 tools in the corpus: for each, we read the vendor's own pricing page, dated the reading, and wrote an assessment that includes who should skip the tool. Cost-trap flags are derived rule-based from that research text, and every flag carries the sentence it rests on. This page is recalculated from the same data as the Atlas and the open dataset whenever a dossier changes; the date above is the most recent price check in the corpus, not the date every tool was checked.

Price checks across the corpus: 312 of the 647 price notes were re-read in the last 30 days, 335 between 30 and 90 days ago.

What the corpus is, and is not. Tools enter it because readers ask about them, because they sit in the categories we cover, or because we joined their partner programme. It is not a random sample of the SaaS market, so every percentage on this page describes our 647 researched tools, not software pricing in general. A flag is counted when at least one sentence in the dossier supports it; the definitions are on the Atlas, and each flag in the open dataset carries its sentence. Frequently asked questions

What is the most common hidden cost in B2B software?

Usage metering: 44% of the 647 tools we researched (285 of 647). Calculate the price at your real volume and headcount before trusting a sticker price.

How common is quote-only pricing?

1 in 20 tools (33 of the 647) publishes no usable price. Ask for the all-in figure in writing: base, seats, add-ons, implementation, renewal.

Are renewal price jumps common in B2B SaaS?

Less common than people fear: 1 in 34 of our corpus (19 of 647), mostly in IT & Productivity and Financial Operations. The everyday leaks are meters, seats and add-ons.
