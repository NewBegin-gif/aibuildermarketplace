Diagnose the reason to move before exporting a list

A migration should solve a specific constraint: the billing unit, a missing journey, a publication workflow or the work needed to prepare campaigns. Write that reason down with the list size, typical sends and people who need access. Without those inputs, a low entry price can look persuasive while describing a tier your business cannot use. This guide compares ActiveCampaign, GetResponse, Brevo, Kit and Campaign Monitor against those different jobs.

We do not hold a verified Mailchimp price ladder for a like-for-like baseline here. Consequently this guide does not claim that every alternative is cheaper, and it does not price a fictional standard customer. Bring your current invoice and the functions you actually use. Compare each alternative at the matching contact or sending allowance, billing term and feature tier. A lower starting figure is only useful if the required workflow is covered.

ActiveCampaign: buy automation only when a journey needs it

ActiveCampaign's dossier centres on marketing automation and lifecycle email with CRM-style follow-up. Put a real journey on paper before trialling it: the entry condition, branching decision, message, waiting period and stopping condition. Ask a colleague to explain what would happen to a contact at each point. That exercise shows whether the depth serves an actual process or merely gives you more settings to maintain.

Our team has a dated hands-on record linked from the review. Use it for the observed interface and task limitations; it does not establish your campaign results or inbox placement. During your own trial, work with harmless test contacts and confirm how the journey is stopped or corrected. If all you need is a broadcast newsletter, the dossier's caution is relevant: do not buy automation depth that nobody will configure or maintain.

GetResponse: evaluate the combined workflow

GetResponse's dossier describes email alongside landing pages, automation and webinars. That combination is relevant when one team genuinely needs the connected jobs. List which components you will use, then inspect the plan boundary for each. A product having a webinar capability somewhere in its range does not mean that its entry email plan covers the event format or audience you intend to run.

For the trial, follow a subscriber from the landing page into the list and through the planned follow-up. If an event is part of the purchase, make that a separate acceptance exercise. Record any additional tool the process still needs. An all-in-one arrangement may simplify administration, but that benefit depends on your workflow; it is not proof of lower total cost or better campaign performance. The linked review supplies the recorded pricing and regional context.

Brevo: sends and stored contacts both matter

Brevo deserves attention when the list is large but campaigns are selective, because its dossier emphasises sending volume as a billing consideration. Do not reduce that description to unlimited contact storage on every inexpensive tier. The dossier also flags stored-contact limits on send tiers. Write down both the people you retain and the messages you expect to send before deciding that the model fits.

A daily free sending limit is also different from a monthly allowance you can spend whenever you choose. Check whether the planned campaign must reach everyone at once. Test account approval, sender setup and a small internal send while there is time to address a blocked step. The review contains a dated hands-on account; this guide does not imply that another account will receive the same approval outcome or deliverability result.

Kit: distinguish audience publishing from store operations

Kit's dossier describes an audience-first business: creators, writers and coaches using email, landing pages and related creator features. Begin with the publication and subscriber journey. Decide whether the central job is a recurring newsletter, a product launch or a set of behaviour-driven messages. Then verify the tier required for the actual journey. A large free subscriber allowance does not establish that every paid feature is included.

For a migration, inspect how subscribers are counted and what happens when the list grows beyond the chosen tier. Keep a record of the list size and billable status you used for the comparison. The dossier cautions that a large cold list deserves cleaning before paying per subscriber. That is a reason to review consent, suppression and engagement records before import, rather than using an attractive free threshold as a reason to move everything without inspection.

Campaign Monitor: check list structure as well as headcount

Campaign Monitor's dossier fits teams that value designed email and a manageable campaign workflow. Use a real campaign with the normal approval process in the trial. Ask the colleague who will run the next campaign to edit and send the internal test. The editor and the reporting screen both matter, but so does whether the team can repeat the workflow without relying on the person who set it up.

Our review includes questions about billing counts across lists and exporting them. That makes list architecture a buying consideration. Compare the billable total the platform will use, not just a deduplicated headcount in a spreadsheet. If the same person appears in several lists, resolve the counting treatment before migrating. Also rehearse the export you would need later; being able to inspect one list is not proof that every list and record leaves in one convenient file.

Compare a realistic month and a busy month

For each candidate, make two workload descriptions. The ordinary month should include active subscribers, stored contacts where relevant, messages and editors. The busy month should include the launch, seasonal campaign or event that changes the bill. Keep those descriptions separate from the supplier's units. Contacts, subscribers, active contacts and sends are not interchangeable names for the same amount of work.

Attach the feature tier to the cost comparison. If a required automation, webinar or permission sits above the entry plan, price that tier. Keep annual commitments distinct from monthly payments and keep currencies as recorded. The panel below uses evidenced central price rules when they remain current against the dossier. It leaves missing or stale rules unconfirmed. Open the review for the full reading rather than filling a missing cell with an old figure found elsewhere.

Migrate a small, inspectable slice first

Before a full import, preserve a copy of subscriber records, consent information and suppression lists. Choose a small test slice that contains the awkward cases: a corrected address, an unsubscribed contact and someone belonging to more than one segment. Confirm the intended treatment before enabling any automation. This is a proposed migration exercise, not a claim that these products handle every record in the same way.

Check forms, internal sends and unsubscribe behaviour before stopping the old system. Read the recorded cancellation terms before assuming a refund or free overlap.

Choose by the operating model

Trial ActiveCampaign when a defined automation journey justifies the configuration work. Trial GetResponse when the combined email, landing-page and event workflow is the attraction. Trial Brevo when selective sends and stored-contact limits fit the list. Trial Kit for an audience-first publishing process, and Campaign Monitor for a campaign workflow centred on designed email and list management. Each recommendation is conditional on the recorded job and plan coverage.

A successful switch leaves the team able to repeat a campaign, explain the bill and retrieve subscriber records. It does not merely reproduce the old contact count in a new dashboard. Keep your acceptance record, the plan you evaluated and the reading dates from the reviews. Revisit the purchase when the list or sending pattern changes, because the billing model that fits an occasional newsletter may fit a frequent sender differently.
