OUR METHODOLOGY

How we review

Our methodology — written plainly, so you know exactly what our verdicts are worth.

Most "review" sites are thin pages built to harvest affiliate clicks. We're trying to be the opposite. This page explains how our reviews are made, where their limits are, and how we make money — because a review is only useful if you can trust where it comes from.

Who writes these

Reviews are written by Daniel Haket, who spent two decades in international banking before building this. That background matters here: the job trained me to tell real value from hype, to read pricing and fine print carefully, and to be sceptical of a good pitch. Where I'm uncertain, I say so on the page.

Two kinds of assessment — and we label which is which

We're honest about depth, because pretending to have used every tool would be a lie:

  • Researched assessment (most reviews). Based on the live product, its official documentation and pricing, public security and feature materials, and how it sits against competitors in the market. It is not a multi-month hands-on test, and we never claim it is.
  • Hands-on tested (clearly badged). Tools I've actually used myself get a "✓ Hands-on tested" badge. That label only appears where it's true — a small set where it's true, not a blanket claim.

How we form a verdict

For each tool we look at what it actually does, who it's genuinely for, the real cost (including the parts vendors bury), the honest trade-offs, and the best alternative if it isn't the right fit. Every review states plainly who should not buy it — a review without a downside isn't a review, it's an ad. We don't publish star ratings at all anymore — the section below explains why we removed them.

Pricing

Software pricing changes constantly and varies by region. We describe how a tool's pricing is structured and where the hidden costs are, but we ask you to confirm the current numbers on the provider's own site before deciding. We do not publish fake or "exclusive" discounts.

The research dossiers

Behind the reviews sits a growing corpus of hand-researched dossiers — all 235 tools we partner with (completed July 2026). For each one we research the current pricing structure from multiple sources, write an honest “knock” (the trade-off the vendor won’t lead with), define who the tool is genuinely best for, who should skip it, and name the natural comparison. Those dossiers are the single source that feeds the review pages, the Honest Atlas and the Stack X-Ray — so a claim you read in one place is the same claim everywhere, and fixing it once fixes it everywhere. Tools we haven’t deep-researched yet are labelled “dossier pending” in the Atlas rather than dressed up as finished work.

Cost-trap flags — and why each one carries its evidence

The Atlas marks tools with flags like per-seat pricing, usage-metered, hidden pricing or renewal jump. These are not opinions we assign by feel: they are derived rule-based from the text of our own researched dossiers, and every flag carries the exact sentence that justifies it — hover a flag and you can read the evidence. Our build tooling refuses to publish a flag without an evidence sentence attached. The aggregated findings are public in The Hidden Cost Report, where every statistic can be traced back to the dossiers it was computed from.

The day we removed our own stars

In July 2026 we audited the star ratings that had accumulated across this site and didn’t like what we found: most were a stamped default, and even the varied ones couldn’t be sourced to our standard. Worse, showing “based on N reviews” without naming a source implies reviews we collected — and we hadn’t. So we removed every third-party star rating from over a thousand pages in one day, including the structured-data markup search engines read. What replaced them — evidence-backed flags and researched pricing notes — is less shiny and more true. We’d rather look plainer than borrow trust we can’t repay.

No invented numbers — including yours

You will not find a “companies waste 30% of their SaaS spend” statistic here, because we can’t verify it and neither can the people who publish it. Where our interactive tools calculate savings or costs — the AI Roadmap, the Stack X-Ray — they compute exclusively with numbers you enter, arithmetic you can check on a napkin, and they say so on the page. Amounts shown in the Atlas are the entry prices we found at research time, dated, with a standing instruction to verify live pricing before deciding.

How AI is used here

Honesty about our own stack: this site is built and maintained with heavy use of AI tooling — including an autonomous agent (Victor) that handles translations, page generation and housekeeping, which we’ve written about openly on the About page. AI drafts and assembles; the research direction, the verdicts, the dossiers and the editorial standards described on this page are human decisions, and the validation tooling (evidence-required flags, no-unsourced-numbers rules, build checks) exists precisely so that scale never outruns honesty. When automation and our standards conflict, the standards win — the star-rating removal above is what that looks like in practice.

How we make money

Many of our outbound links are affiliate links: if you sign up through one, we may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. That is the entire business model, and we're upfront about it. Crucially: commission never buys a better verdict, and rankings are never for sale. We'd rather lose a commission than recommend the wrong tool and lose your trust.

What we will never do
  • Claim we tested something hands-on when we didn't.
  • Invent ratings, review counts, testimonials or statistics.
  • Run fake "limited-time" or "exclusive" discounts or false urgency.
  • Sell rankings or let a commission change a verdict.
  • Hide that a link is an affiliate link.

Corrections

Tools improve and change. If something here is out of date or wrong, tell us via the About page and we'll fix it — getting it right matters more than being right the first time.
