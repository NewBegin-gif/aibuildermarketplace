What it means

Why this matters

A pricing page is written to sell. The number in large type is often the annual rate, the promotional rate, or the rate for a plan many buyers will outgrow — and the conditions attached to it can sit in smaller type, in a footnote, or on a different tab. That is not deception; it is how pricing pages are commonly built.

The risk is what happens when an AI engine answers “what does this cost” by reading that page back to you: the headline number may survive the summary while the conditions do not. This study does not measure that — it records where the answers point, not whether they keep the conditions. When 77% of the sources behind those answers are the vendor’s own site, the vendor’s framing is the most likely starting point; a citation of the vendor’s site is not, on its own, evidence of a poor answer.

Counting note: 77 citations is not 77 independent observations. They resolve to 76 distinct URLs on 37 domains, and a single vendor’s site plus its help centre can account for dozens of them. “Vendor’s own site” means the cited domain is the vendor’s registered domain in our database; a vendor help centre on a different domain is counted elsewhere unless we have checked it as the vendor’s. Domains that contain the tool’s name but are not its known domain are shown separately as “possibly the vendor”.

What we are not claiming

This is one run of 21 answers across 20 tools and two engines, in a US locale, on a single day. It is enough to show a pattern and not enough to quantify it precisely. We are publishing it because the direction is stark and the method is cheap to repeat — not because 21 answers settle anything.

We did not test whether the answers were correct. Measuring that at scale would mean re-verifying every quoted price by hand, which is the work our own corpus already does for 20 of these tools, but not on the engines’ answers.

And the result that is least flattering to us: this site was cited 0 times. We publish verified prices with reading dates, and none of that reached these answers. That is a finding about our own visibility, and it belongs in the report.

Method

Queries of the form [tool] pricing for 20 business tools we research ourselves, submitted asynchronously to the Cloro API against ChatGPT and Perplexity, US locale, on 2026-11-01. Every response was stored with its full source list. Counting rule: a citation counts as “the vendor’s own site” when its registrable domain equals the vendor’s domain in our database, so a help centre on a subdomain of that domain counts as the vendor. The full rule and domain lists are in the study package.

Raw responses, including the full answer text and source lists: in the study package below, per monthly run. The underlying pricing corpus is published as an open dataset at /data/ under CC BY 4.0.
