Hands-on test · run on 2026-09-28

One pitch deck. Four AI presentation tools. Here is what they got right and what they made up.

On the morning of 28 September 2026 we gave Gamma, Beautiful.ai, Prezi and Canva the same brief for a ten-slide investor deck: eleven facts about a made-up bakery chain and one rule, add no other numbers. Then we checked every deck against 15 tests we wrote down before the first one was generated, from invented numbers and chart accuracy to changing the ask in one prompt, exporting and sharing. This is one run per tool: a case study, not a ranking.

Told to use only eleven facts and add no other numbers, Canva's deck added nine, including an itemised list of expansion costs that ends at 250,000. Gamma, Beautiful.ai and Prezi added none, though Prezi made up a website for the company. The brief

Sent as the first message to each tool, word for word:

Create a 10-slide investor pitch deck for Kettle & Crumb, a fictional bakery-café chain in Rotterdam, the Netherlands. Use exactly these facts and do not add any other numbers:
- Founded in 2021 by Sanne de Vries (CEO) and Tomasz Nowak (head baker).
- 3 shops in Rotterdam and 38 employees.
- Revenue: €0.4 million in 2023, €0.8 million in 2024 and €1.2 million in 2025.
- Gross margin: 62% in 2025.
- Plan: open 2 new shops in Utrecht in 2027.
- Asking €400,000 for 12% of the company.
- Use of funds: 70% new shops, 20% central kitchen equipment, 10% marketing.

Slides: 1 title, 2 problem, 3 solution, 4 traction with a revenue chart for 2023-2025, 5 business model, 6 market, 7 team, 8 growth plan, 9 use of funds as a chart, 10 the ask.

Do not invent customer numbers, market sizes, quotes or testimonials. If a slide needs a number we did not give, put a clearly marked placeholder such as [MARKET SIZE] instead.

Small enough for a free plan, and built around the traps of AI decks: numbers that must be copied without additions, two charts with known values, a market slide that invites a made-up market size, and an amount that appears in more than one place when you change it later.

How we tested

  • Same start. New accounts, prompts sent between 08:37 and 08:59 Amsterdam time. Gamma Free, Prezi Basic and Canva Free; Beautiful.ai offered us only a 14-day Pro trial, so we used that. Where a tool showed an outline or plan first, we accepted it unchanged and kept its default style.
  • The facts. For every deck we compared each number on every slide with the brief (C02, C03) and measured the charts in pixels against their stated values (C05, C06).
  • One change prompt. After the first reading each tool got the same sentence through its own AI: “Change the ask to €450,000 for 12%”. We counted how many places still said €400,000.
  • By hand. We renamed slide 2 and swapped slides 8 and 9 in each editor, then exported to PowerPoint and PDF where the plan allowed, opened the files and checked whether text and charts could still be edited.
  • Sharing. We opened each tool's share link in a fresh browser that was not logged in and took the screenshots below from there. All links were closed again the same morning.

The evidence

Screenshots from each tool's public share page, opened without logging in (Gamma's from its own PDF export, because its share page sits behind a bot check).

What each tool did

Gamma

Asked us to pick a style, then stalled: the outline pane stayed empty until we wrote “Please draft the outline.” After that the outline marked both unknowns as [MARKET SIZE] and [COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE] and the deck kept to the brief, apart from leaving out the founding year.

The change prompt updated all three places on the ask slide and showed the old and new version side by side. Exports keep the text editable, but the two charts arrive in PowerPoint as pictures, and the free plan stamps each PDF page with a badge.

Beautiful.ai

Went from outline to finished deck in about two and a half minutes, with no branding on the slides or files, but you get there through a trial that needs a card. The outline had a [MARKET SIZE] placeholder; the finished market slide dropped it and named Amsterdam as a target city.

Its charts carry the right data but read oddly: a revenue axis labelled 0 and 1, and use of funds in euros rather than the percentages we gave. There is no AI to change the deck as a whole, and PowerPoint export defaults to flat images unless you choose editable.

Prezi

Built ten slides plus its zooming overview and kept the numbers, placeholder and charts right. It did add things: a company website, a gender for the CEO, and a sentence that presents last year's revenue as a forecast.

The change prompt fixed the ask slide but not the use-of-funds text. On Basic the AI editor's PDF, PowerPoint and SCORM downloads were locked to Plus (the public room page showed PDF and PowerPoint download buttons, to us and to logged-out viewers behind a sign-in, and Prezi's help centre documents a Download PPTX on the dashboard; we tested none of these), and a deck can only be Public: anyone can find it, with the account holder's name on the page. To take ours offline we moved it to the bin.

Canva

Rewrote our slide list into its own plan (two problem slides, funds and ask on one) and was the quickest to a finished deck, at about two minutes. It is also the one that broke the brief: nine numbers we never gave, including a costs list, and revenue bars that are not to scale.

Changing the deck with Canva AI needs a paid plan. Exports on the free plan work, without a watermark. Its share link starts on “anyone with the link can edit”, which is worth changing before you send it to an investor.

Choose for your situation

One run per tool on one day does not support a winner, so this is what the run does support.

  • The numbers have to be exactly yours. Gamma and Beautiful.ai added none in this test. Whatever you use, read every slide against your own figures: one of four decks added nine, and its chart looked right until we measured it.
  • You will change the deck after the first draft. In this test only Gamma's AI found every place an amount appeared. Elsewhere, search the deck yourself after each change, including chart data and body text.
  • You need a file to send or edit in PowerPoint. On Prezi's free plan the AI editor's PDF and PowerPoint downloads were locked to Plus, and we did not test its other download routes. The other three export text you can edit, but none of them exported a chart you can edit as a chart.
  • You share by link. Check the defaults before you send: one link opened on “can edit”, one public page shows the owner's email, and one free plan cannot make a deck private at all.

Check the evidence

  • Date and plans: 28 September 2026, prompts sent between 08:37 and 08:59 Amsterdam time; Gamma Free, Beautiful.ai 14-day Pro trial, Prezi Basic, Canva Free.
  • The data: every check with what we saw, the prompt times and the credit readings are in results.json.
  • The decks: the share links are closed again, so the screenshots above and the file checks in results.json are the record. The fictional company, its founders and its numbers were made up for this test.
  • What this is not: one run each, so a tool could do better or worse on another day or brief. Not a design review: we did not score how good the slides look. Not a test of paid plans, apart from the Beautiful.ai trial.
