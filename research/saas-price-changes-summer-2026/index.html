Nobody publishes a changelog for a pricing page. So since July 2026 we have been taking one ourselves: on the 2nd of every month we capture the pricing page of every tool we review, extract the price lines, and compare them with the month before. A script finds the candidates; a person reads both captures and decides what actually happened. This is what 4 rounds of that found.

Between July 2026 and November 2026, roughly one in 18 SaaS pricing pages changed a price in any given month: 62 documented changes across 1147 monthly page comparisons, 18 of them increases.AIBuilder Marketplace, Report 04, 2026-11-11. Free to quote with attribution.

Which way prices moved

Increase18
Decrease8
Rebuilt30
Withdrawn6

Rebuilds lead: 30 of the 62 changes were a ladder rebuilt rather than a number moved — new tiers, per-seat instead of flat, a metered add-on where a bundle used to be — against 18 plain increases. That matters because a rebuild is where the real price change tends to hide: the old plan and the new one no longer compare. The small moves are frequent too: Replit’s annual Core price read $20, $17 and $18 in three consecutive captures, and Sellfy’s August increase was gone again by September. And 6 vendors stopped publishing a price at all, a change no comparison of amounts can see.

Every change, with the lines we captured

Struck-through text is the earlier capture; the line below it is the later one. These are the literal strings from the vendor’s page, not our paraphrase. The full capture pairs, with all lines, are on Price Watch.

A capture “reads” when the pricing URL returns a page we can parse; it fails on bot walls, logged-in pricing and pages rendered entirely in the browser. “Readable amounts” means at least one currency figure survived extraction. A page counts as comparable when it carried readable amounts in the first month and read again in the second, so that an increase, a decrease, a rebuilt ladder or a withdrawn price would all be visible; pages with amounts in both months are a subset, shown per round. That is why the base for each round is smaller than the run. Corpus figures measured 2 September 2026 (July to September), 7 October 2026 (October) and 8 November 2026 (November).

What we are not claiming

This is not the SaaS market. The 628 tools are the ones in our own review database: business software for small and mid-sized companies, weighted to sales, marketing, support, HR, e-commerce and infrastructure. A corpus of enterprise platforms would move differently.

One capture is one render. A pricing page can show different figures by country, currency selector, billing toggle or A/B test. We capture from one location with one default state each month. Where a change turned out to be a toggle or a currency setting, it is in the discard list, not the count.

Amounts, not always plan names. The extractor keeps price lines, and on some pages the plan label sits in a different element. Where the pairing of a figure to a tier was uncertain, the entry says so or was held back until a third capture settled it.

A month is a coarse grid. A change that appears and disappears between two captures is invisible to us. The true rate of change is therefore at least what we report, not at most.

Method

On the 2nd of each month a script requests the pricing URL on file for every tool (falling back to /plans and the homepage), decodes the page, keeps the lines that carry a currency figure, and stores them with a hash of the page text. A comparison script pairs each month with the previous one and flags every tool whose set of amounts changed. Those flags are candidates, nothing more: text hashes change on most pages every month, and amounts appear in case studies, calculators and funding announcements. A person then reads the raw lines of both captures for every candidate and records what happened, in one of four kinds: increase, decrease, restructure, withdrawn. Every published entry carries the literal lines; the discard reasons are published too.

The register is append-only. An older round is never rewritten, because the sequence of rounds is the price history and a series adjusted after the fact is not a series. Rounds so far: July 2026 to August 2026 (150 pairs, 24 candidates, 9 verified, checked 2026-08-22); August 2026 to September 2026 (280 pairs, 30 candidates, 10 verified, checked 2026-09-02); September 2026 to October 2026 (323 pairs, 40 candidates, 18 verified, checked 2026-10-07); October 2026 to November 2026 (394 pairs, 119 candidates, 25 verified, checked 2026-11-08).
