1 in 30 SaaS Pricing Pages Changes Price Each Month (2026)

Nobody publishes a changelog for a pricing page. So since July 2026 we have been taking one ourselves: on the 2nd of every month we capture the pricing page of every tool we review, extract the price lines, and compare them with the month before. A script finds the candidates; a person reads both captures and decides what actually happened. This is what 2 rounds of that found.

Between July 2026 and September 2026, roughly one in 30 SaaS pricing pages changed a price in any given month: 19 documented changes across 567 monthly page comparisons, 8 of them increases.AIBuilder Marketplace, Report 04, 2026-09-02. Free to quote with attribution.

Which way prices moved

Increase8
Decrease3
Rebuilt7
Withdrawn1

Increases lead, but not by the margin the “SaaS inflation” story would predict. Nearly as many pages were rebuilt — new tiers, per-seat instead of flat, a metered add-on where a bundle used to be — and a rebuild is where the real price change tends to hide, because the old plan and the new one no longer compare. Two of the 3 decreases are worth a second look: Replit cut its Core price by 15% and shrank the credits that come with it by 20% in the same month, and Sellfy’s August increase was gone again by September.

What we threw out, and why

July 2026 → August 2026. Currency selectors (Rank Math $118 → €118, FullEnrich €720 → $720 — same digits, different setting on the page). Captures that failed (Freshservice returned four lines in July and nine in August, one of them raw Next.js JSON). Annual rates shown for the first time (Beehiiv, VidPal). A feature change with no price change (Leadpages went from 500 pages to unlimited; its '13-year track record' also became '14-year'). Promotional churn (Hostinger). Amounts that were never prices — Emergent's pricing page was announcing a $130M Series C. Too small to bother reporting (Notify Me, $9.99 → $9.90). And six where two captures weren't enough to tell what had happened (Flippa, Kartra, Aspire, Pangram, Trainerize, ZenZap). One more I held back at first: UENI clearly restructured, but my own notes on it were a month out of date, and publishing would have contradicted my own writeup. I re-read the page in a browser, corrected the notes from one subscription to four tiers, and it's in the list above.

August 2026 → September 2026. Sixty-two pages where amounts were only added or removed, not changed (new annual rates shown, a tier's card no longer captured). BuddyPunch: the capture shows two rate rows side by side and, without a browser, we cannot tell which row is annual and which is monthly, so a possible second increase stays unreported until we read the page live. Glide: the August capture caught only the add-on lines, so there is nothing to compare. Hostinger: promotional churn, all three renewal rates unchanged. Amounts identical with different formatting (Leadfeeder, Housecall Pro, Calilio, KrispCall, Compliancely, Heard, UENI). Amounts that were never prices (Printify's page shows an $813 order example; Vida AI's a $1,500 pilot). Too small to report (Notify Me).

A capture “reads” when the pricing URL returns a page we can parse; it fails on bot walls, logged-in pricing and pages rendered entirely in the browser. “Readable amounts” means at least one currency figure survived extraction. Only pages that read in both months and carried amounts in both are compared, which is why the base for each round is smaller than the run. Corpus figures measured 2026-09-02.

What we are not claiming

This is not the SaaS market. The 428 tools are the ones in our own review database: business software for small and mid-sized companies, weighted to sales, marketing, support, HR, e-commerce and infrastructure. A corpus of enterprise platforms would move differently.

One capture is one render. A pricing page can show different figures by country, currency selector, billing toggle or A/B test. We capture from one location with one default state each month. Where a change turned out to be a toggle or a currency setting, it is in the discard list, not the count.

Amounts, not always plan names. The extractor keeps price lines, and on some pages the plan label sits in a different element. Where the pairing of a figure to a tier was uncertain, the entry says so or was held back until a third capture settled it.

A month is a coarse grid. A change that appears and disappears between two captures is invisible to us. The true rate of change is therefore at least what we report, not at most.

Method

On the 2nd of each month a script requests the pricing URL on file for every tool (falling back to /plans and the homepage), decodes the page, keeps the lines that carry a currency figure, and stores them with a hash of the page text. A comparison script pairs each month with the previous one and flags every tool whose set of amounts changed. Those flags are candidates, nothing more: text hashes change on most pages every month, and amounts appear in case studies, calculators and funding announcements. A person then reads the raw lines of both captures for every candidate and records what happened, in one of four kinds: increase, decrease, restructure, withdrawn. Every published entry carries the literal lines; the discard reasons are published too.

The register is append-only. An older round is never rewritten, because the sequence of rounds is the price history and a series adjusted after the fact is not a series. Rounds so far: July 2026 to August 2026 (237 pairs, 24 candidates, 9 verified, checked 2026-08-22); August 2026 to September 2026 (330 pairs, 30 candidates, 10 verified, checked 2026-09-02).
