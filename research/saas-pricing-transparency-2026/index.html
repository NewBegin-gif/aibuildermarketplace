The sticker price is the whole story for only 23% of business software

We hand-researched the pricing of 597 business tools and wrote down, with evidence, every structure that moves the real cost away from the advertised one. The result: 457 of 597 tools (77%) carry at least one documented cost caveat — usage meters, per-seat scaling, paid add-ons, annual lock-ins, hidden price lists or renewal jumps. Every number on this page is computed from our open dataset, so you can check us.

🧮 Usage-metered: 270 of 597 tools (45%)

Cost moves with usage: credits, overages, volume tiers or percentage fees. Most common in Growth & Revenue. Documented examples from the dossiers:

“There are two meters, and the second one is easy to miss: every conversation that touches an AI block counts as an AI chat at €0.10 beyond the allowance, on top of regular chat overage sold in €25 packs.” Landbot review →
“(read 2026-10-25 on recruitcrm.io/pricing/ with both the Annually and the Monthly toggle clicked, from the Netherlands, where the page shows euros; and in the Recruit CRM help centre) Pro is €79 per user a month billed annually or €95 monthly, with 100 credits per user a month.” Recruit CRM review →

🪑 Per-seat pricing: 140 of 597 tools (23%)

Cost scales with every user you add. Most common in Operations & Workflow. Documented examples from the dossiers:

“(read 2026-10-27 on nexgenvirtualworkplace.net, SMB Market page; the Enterprise and Webex pages were read on 2026-10-20; dollar amounts, the page does not name the currency) NexGen prices its virtual workplace per user for companies with 1 to 100 employees, all with self implementation: $25 per user paid monthly (cancel anytime), $20 per user a month paid quarterly ($60 per quarter) or $15 per user a month paid yearly ($180 per year).” NexGen Virtual Office review →
“For teams, Business is €9.99 per user a month with 1 TB per user and Business Pro €14.98 per user a month with 2 TB per user (€44.94 a month for 3 users, marked down from €59.94, for new accounts only), both with a minimum of 3 users and pCloud Encryption included; Business has a 30-day free trial.” pCloud review →
“(read 2026-10-25 on recruitcrm.io/pricing/ with both the Annually and the Monthly toggle clicked, from the Netherlands, where the page shows euros; and in the Recruit CRM help centre) Pro is €79 per user a month billed annually or €95 monthly, with 100 credits per user a month.” Recruit CRM review →

🧩 Add-ons extra: 169 of 597 tools (28%)

Key features are paid add-ons on top of the plan. Most common in Growth & Revenue. Documented examples from the dossiers:

“An optional Backup add-on is listed as $6.99 to $59.99 a month; the listing does not break that range down.” EditEngine review →
“Active paid subscribers can buy add-on credits: 10,000 for $249, 25,000 for $549 or 60,000 for $1,199, valid for 12 months.” Lensmor review →
“For personal plans, client-side encryption is a separate add-on: €150 once (from €229).” pCloud review →

📅 Annual commit: 135 of 597 tools (23%)

Best price (or any price) requires an annual/quarterly commitment. Most common in Growth & Revenue. Documented examples from the dossiers:

“Basic is $499 a month or $4,990 a year, for 35,000 credits a month and a shared workspace of up to 5 seats; the credits reset every month, also on annual billing.” Lensmor review →
“(read 2026-10-26 on yaps.ai/pricing with annual billing selected; the monthly price was read on 2026-10-16; dollar amounts, the page does not name the currency) Yaps is free on Android, iPhone and iPad: voice typing in other apps, voice notes with local search and read-aloud with 4 voices, without an account or card.” Yaps review →
“Prices are before tax, and annual billing is a 12-month commitment.” Landbot review →

🔒 Hidden pricing: 28 of 597 tools (5%)

No public price list — quote or sales call needed. Most common in Operations & Workflow. Documented examples from the dossiers:

“No published price: samsara.com/pricing is a fleet-size form and samsara.com/resources/plans names seven packages, each with a Get pricing button and no amount (read 10 Oct 2026)” Samsara review →
“No published price: none of the three tiers (Platform, Scale, Managed) shows a figure anywhere on the site, including the self-serve entry tier (read 4 Sep 2026)” Mycroft review →
“No published price: Starter, Team and Business all route to a Talk to sales button (read 27 Aug 2026)” Pecan AI review →

📈 Renewal jump: 15 of 597 tools (3%)

Intro price rises substantially at renewal. Most common in IT & Productivity. Documented examples from the dossiers:

“The yearly services renew at a price: the registered office address £29 a year plus VAT, the director's service address £28, business mail forwarding £98, and the Premium filing service £128 a year excluding VAT, with the £50 Companies House confirmation statement fee paid separately.” Your Company Formations review →
“(read 2026-10-12 in a live browser on hostpapa.com/web-hosting-plan and hostpapa.com/renewal-pricing; US site, prices in US dollars excluding taxes) Shared web hosting comes in four cPanel plans, each shown at a promotional price that the page says applies to the first term only, on a 3-year discounted term, for new customers.” HostPapa review →
“Taking payments needs a paid bundle, and every bundle renews at twice the first-year price: Earth $398, Universe $598 and Infinity $1,598 a year.” LifterLMS review →

🆓 Free tier: 208 of 597 tools (35%)

A genuinely free plan or version exists. Most common in Growth & Revenue. Documented examples from the dossiers:

“(read 2026-10-26 on the EditEngine listing in the Shopify App Store; the listing says all charges are billed in USD, every 30 days) EditEngine has a free plan and three paid plans, and every plan allows unlimited bulk edit, import and export tasks.” EditEngine review →

“The first billing period is discounted: the Superdeal table shows the first 3 months of all three plans at $0, and the Promotion table shows Start at $0 for a first month or quarter or $112.50 for a first year, Business at $0, $112.50 or $225, and Pro at $0, $225 or $450.” Netart review →

Prices also move after you sign

Transparency is not only about today’s price list — it’s about whether the price you signed at survives. Our Price Watch registry documents the hikes, free-tier cuts and intro-to-renewal jumps we found, each with its evidence, and since July 2026 we snapshot all 597 pricing pages monthly so new moves get caught automatically.

Methodology — and its honest limits

Which edition you are reading. First published 9 July 2026 on 245 dossiers. This is edition 2026-11-01, computed over 597 dossiers. A new edition is computed at most once a calendar month, so the figures on this page do not move with every new dossier; the running corpus is the open dataset. Every earlier published state, from 245 tools on 9 July onward, is listed under editions.

Two distinctions the flags now make. “Hidden pricing” counts only vendors with no usable public price; 4 more publish every tier except an enterprise one and are not counted. “Annual commit” splits into 18 vendors where a yearly or quarterly contract is required and 117 where annual billing is an optional discount. A negated match (“no free tier”) never counts as the flag.

How the numbers are made. Every tool in the corpus has a hand-researched dossier. A flag only counts when the dossier contains an explicit evidence sentence for it — the exact classification rules are the same ones that power the Honest Software Atlas, and this page is rebuilt from the corpus, so the numbers cannot drift from the source.

What this corpus is not. It is not a random sample of the software market: it covers the 597 tools we research and review, which skews toward tools with affiliate programs. Percentages describe this corpus, not the entire industry. We publish the raw data so anyone can re-run the analysis: open dataset (CC BY 4.0) · MCP server · how we review.
