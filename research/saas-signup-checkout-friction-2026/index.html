We signed up for 309 business software tools between 2026-10-20 and 2026-11-19, ran a real task in each, and where we could, opened the checkout and cancelled again. This report counts what stood in the way: a card before the trial, a phone number, a connection you must make before you see anything, extras ticked for you at checkout, and a cancel button behind a row of offers.

In hands-on tests of 309 business software tools (2026-10-20 to 2026-11-19), AIBuilder Marketplace found that 23 of 259 tools where the sign-up could be checked (9%) put at least one hurdle between sign-up and first use: a required payment card, a required phone number or a required account connection. Of the 5 checkouts above the advertised price, 3 added VAT only at the checkout; the others added fees or extras. The base differs per line, because not every test reached every step: we only count a tool for a hurdle when our test record says what happened at that step and a screenshot from the same test shows it. Where we cancelled (22 tools), the median route took 4 screens and 2 retention offers; 1 asked for the account password at the end. Where we timed the first useful result (151 tools, usually one small task in a working account), the median was 2 minutes.

What we are not claiming

This is not the SaaS market. These are the tools we review and could test ourselves, mostly software for small and mid-sized businesses.

A hurdle is not always a flaw. A card check can stop abuse of a trial, and a domain check can protect your data. We count what a buyer meets on the way in and out, not whether the vendor had a good reason.

Refused sign-ups are undercounted. A refused account leaves no screenshots from inside the product, and we only count a finding that a published screenshot backs. Tools that turned us away are therefore mostly missing here.

One account, one date. Sign-up and checkout flows change often. Each finding carries its test date, and a vendor that changed its flow can send us the new screen; we publish corrections as a new edition.

Method

We created a separate account for each tool; our AI assistant then ran the test in our own browser and wrote down every step, with screenshots. Names, e-mail addresses and account details are masked in every screenshot before it is published. Where a trial asked for a card, we used our own card and cancel each trial before the first charge; nothing in this report depends on a payment going through.

For this report an AI model read each test record and coded nine features: card required, phone required, sign-up refused, required connection, pre-ticked extras, checkout total against the price page, the cancel route, time to the first useful result, and e-mails after sign-up. Two of them are not reported as a number: refused sign-ups (see above) and e-mails after sign-up, which too few tests counted. A feature was kept only if the model quoted the test record word for word and pointed to a published screenshot of the same test; a script checked both and dropped the feature otherwise. We then checked every finding that counts as a hurdle by hand against its quote and screenshot, and corrected or removed 11, mostly where the hurdle came from our own test setup or was not clearly the vendor's. For e-mails after sign-up, see Report 07.
