The invoice is a legal document pretending to be paperwork

An invoice feels like administrative filler, but it's the document that creates your right to get paid, anchors your bookkeeping, and answers to the tax office years later. Getting the basics right is not bureaucracy — it's the difference between money that arrives and money you chase. This generator builds a clean, complete invoice in your browser (nothing uploaded, which is exactly what you want for documents full of client data and bank details) and produces a professional PDF in a minute.

The sections below cover what actually belongs on it, the habits that make invoices get paid faster, and the honest point where a PDF generator stops being enough.

What every valid invoice must contain

Requirements vary by country, but the core set is near-universal: your business name and contact details (plus registration and VAT numbers where applicable); the client's name and address; a unique, sequential invoice number; the issue date; a clear description of what was delivered, with quantities and unit prices; any tax broken out at its rate; the total due; and the payment terms — when and how to pay, including your bank details.

Miss the tax breakdown or the sequential numbering and you create friction with exactly the two parties you never want friction with: your client's accounts department and your tax authority.

Invoice numbers: boring, sequential, sacred

The humble invoice number does more work than any other field. Sequential numbering (2026-001, 2026-002…) is what lets your bookkeeping prove completeness — a gap in the sequence is the first thing an auditor asks about, because a missing number could be a deleted sale. Pick one scheme and never improvise: a year prefix keeps things sorted, and per-year resets are fine as long as the pattern is consistent. Never reuse a number, even for a cancelled invoice — cancel with a credit note instead.

It's the cheapest professionalism there is: a tidy number series signals a tidy business to clients, accountants and auditors alike.

Payment terms that actually get you paid

"Payment within 30 days" is a convention, not a law of nature — and for small suppliers it's often a slow-motion cash-flow problem. You're allowed to set 14 days, or 7, especially for smaller amounts; most clients simply follow what the invoice says. State the due date as a date, not just a term — "due 16 July 2026" beats "net 14" for clarity. Make paying frictionless: full bank details, a payment reference (the invoice number), and where possible a payment link or QR code so the transfer is one tap instead of a typing exercise. Every step you remove from the payer's side statistically shortens your wait.

The polite art of chasing

Late payment is mostly not malice — it's queues and forgotten inboxes — which means a boring, consistent reminder process recovers most of it without drama. A working rhythm: a friendly note the day after the due date ("probably crossed in the mail"), a firmer one at +7 with the invoice reattached, and at +14 a phone call, which resolves more than any email. Keep every reminder factual and attached to the paperwork; the moment chasing feels personal, you've lost the frame.

And prevention beats chasing: invoices sent immediately on delivery, with correct details and easy payment, are the ones that don't need reminders in the first place.

VAT, reverse charge and cross-border basics

Tax is where invoice mistakes get expensive. Domestically, show the VAT rate and amount separately — clients need it for their own reclaim, and tax offices require it. Selling B2B across EU borders typically shifts VAT to the buyer under the reverse-charge mechanism: you invoice at 0% with both VAT numbers on the document and the phrase "VAT reverse-charged". Selling outside your tax system has its own rules again. The pattern to internalise: the invoice must make the tax treatment self-evident to a stranger reading it later.

When a situation is new — first foreign client, first exempt sale — ten minutes with your accountant beats improvising a format the tax office will question.

Look professional without a designer

Clients judge reliability from surfaces, and the invoice is a surface. The good news: professional means plain. One typeface, your logo if you have one, generous whitespace, amounts right-aligned in a clean table, and zero decorative clutter. Consistency matters more than beauty — the same layout, naming and numbering every month reads as an organised counterpart. Save every invoice as PDF (never an editable file — you don't want your prices quietly "adjusted"), name files predictably (2026-014-clientname.pdf), and keep copies in one place.

This generator's output follows exactly these rules, which is most of what "looking established" takes.

Keep copies like the tax office is watching — because it will

Every invoice you send is a record you're required to keep, typically for seven years or more depending on your country. The workable system is boring: one folder (or cloud drive) per year, files named by number and client, PDFs only, backed up like anything else that matters. Add a simple ledger — a spreadsheet with number, date, client, amount, paid-date — and you have receivables tracking and year-end preparation in one artifact.

Reconstructing a year of invoices from email attachments during a tax audit is a special kind of misery that fifteen minutes of filing discipline per month permanently prevents. Your future self, and your accountant's invoice, will both be smaller for it.

Frequently asked questions

Is this invoice generator really free?

Yes — fill in the fields, download the PDF, no signup or watermark. Everything runs in your browser, so your client and pricing details never leave your device.

Does it save my details?

Only if you tick “Remember my business details on this device”. Then your business name, address, payment details, logo, invoice style and the next invoice number are kept in this browser's local storage, so the next invoice starts pre-filled. Client details and line items are never saved. “Forget saved details” removes everything, and nothing is sent to us, so the saved details do not follow you to another browser or device.

Can I print it or save it as a PDF myself?

Yes. Download PDF makes the file directly, with page numbers when the invoice runs over one page. Print opens your browser's print dialog with only the invoice, where you can also choose Save as PDF.

Is a PDF invoice legally valid?

Yes, a PDF invoice with your details, the client's, a number, date, itemised charges and total is perfectly valid in most countries. Check local tax rules for what else (like a VAT/tax number) you must include.

Can I add tax or VAT?

Yes — enter a tax name and rate, and the invoice shows the taxable amount, the tax and the total separately. One rate applies to the whole invoice; for several rates per line or recurring billing, a dedicated invoicing tool is easier.

What if I send lots of invoices?

A generator is great for occasional invoices. If you're invoicing regularly and need to track who's paid and chase late payers, a payments platform like Melio handles sending, tracking and getting paid.
