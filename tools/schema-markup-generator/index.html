What structured data actually is

Schema markup — structured data — is a small block of code you add to a page that spells out, in a language search engines understand, what the page's content means. A human reads "Q: Do you offer refunds? A: Yes, within 30 days" and instantly gets it. A search engine sees text and has to infer. Schema removes the guesswork: it labels that block explicitly as a question and its answer, this text as a recipe's cook time, that number as a product's price.

This tool generates the FAQ version in JSON-LD, the format Google recommends, so you can paste it into a page and give machines an unambiguous map of your content.

Why it matters more every year

Structured data has quietly become one of the most important technical-SEO investments, for two reasons. First, rich results: when Google understands your content precisely, it can display it more richly — star ratings, prices, FAQ dropdowns, event dates — which makes your listing bigger and more clickable. Second, and increasingly, machine comprehension: as AI-driven search and answer engines pull from the web, the sites that have clearly labelled their content are the ones those systems can parse, trust and cite with confidence.

Schema is how you speak to machines in their own language instead of hoping they interpret yours correctly.

FAQ schema, specifically

FAQ schema marks up a list of questions and their answers. It's one of the easiest wins because so many pages already contain natural questions — a product page's objections, a service page's "how it works," a blog post's common follow-ups. Wrapping those in FAQPage markup tells Google exactly what they are. One honest caveat matters here: Google narrowed FAQ rich results in 2023, and the visible dropdowns now appear mainly for authoritative government and health sites for most queries.

So don't add FAQ schema expecting a guaranteed rich snippet. Add it because it helps every kind of search system understand your page — and because the landscape of who gets rich results keeps shifting, so clean markup keeps you eligible when it does.

The schema types worth knowing

FAQ is one of a family. Article and BlogPosting markup label your posts with author, date and headline. Product markup carries price, availability and reviews, and powers the rich shopping listings. Breadcrumb markup shows your site hierarchy in the results. LocalBusiness markup feeds your address, hours and phone number to local search. Organization markup establishes your brand as an entity with a logo and social profiles. HowTo, Recipe, Event and Review each unlock their own display features.

You don't need all of them — you need the ones that match what your page genuinely is. Marking up a page as something it isn't is a fast way to earn a manual penalty.

How Google actually uses it — eligibility, not a guarantee

The most important mental model is this: schema makes you eligible for enhanced treatment; it does not force it. Correct markup is a prerequisite for many rich results, but Google decides case by case whether to show them, based on quality, relevance and its own evolving rules. Adding schema is also not a direct ranking boost — it won't lift a page's position by itself. What it does is help search engines understand and, when they choose to, display your content better, which can lift click-through even at the same rank.

Approach it as removing ambiguity and unlocking possibilities, not as pressing a button that guarantees a fancier listing.

The rules and gotchas that get sites penalised

Structured data has one iron rule: the markup must match the content visible to users on the page. Marking up an FAQ that doesn't actually appear on the page, inventing review stars for products with no reviews, or labelling content that isn't there is against Google's guidelines and can trigger a manual action that strips your rich results entirely. Other common mistakes: incomplete required fields (Product markup missing a price), marking up the same thing in conflicting ways, and letting markup drift out of sync when you edit the visible page but forget the schema.

Honest, accurate, in-sync markup is the whole game — schema rewards sites that describe themselves truthfully and punishes those that don't.

Test everything before you trust it

Never assume schema works because you pasted it in. Google's Rich Results Test and the Schema.org validator both check your markup for errors and show you what, if anything, you're eligible for. Run every page through one before you rely on it, because a single malformed field can invalidate the whole block silently. After it's live, Search Console reports on your structured data at scale — which pages are valid, which have warnings, which have errors — so you can catch problems that only appear once the markup is deployed across many pages.

Generate, validate, publish, then monitor: skip the middle steps and you'll never know your markup was broken until the results never showed up.

Start with the schema your pages already justify

Don't try to mark up everything at once. The fastest, safest wins come from labelling what your pages genuinely already are: a blog gets Article and Breadcrumb markup, a shop gets Product markup, a local service gets LocalBusiness and Organization, and any page with real questions and answers gets FAQ. Match the type to the truth of the page and you stay firmly inside Google's guidelines while unlocking whatever enhanced treatment is available.

Adding schema you can't back up with visible content is worse than adding none at all, so let the page lead and the markup follow.

Frequently asked questions

What is FAQ schema?

Structured data (JSON-LD) that tells search engines your page has a list of questions and answers, making it eligible for FAQ rich results in Google.

Where do I paste the code?

Inside your page's HTML, ideally in the <head>. On WordPress, an SEO plugin like Rank Math can inject it for you automatically.

Will this guarantee rich results?

No — schema makes you eligible, but Google decides whether to show rich results. Make sure the marked-up Q&A is actually visible on the page.
