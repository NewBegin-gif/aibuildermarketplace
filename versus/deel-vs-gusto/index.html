Deel vs Gusto: it depends on where your people live

Part of your team is in the US and part is abroad, and you would like one tool to pay everyone. Maybe you pay contractors in several countries and worry a local authority will call them employees. Or you found the right hire in a country where you have no entity. You are comparing Deel and Gusto because one is built for US payroll and the other for hiring across borders.

Both tools are affiliate partners of ours: we may earn a commission if you sign up through a button on this page, at no extra cost to you. That is why we also say when to pick neither.

Short answerPick by where your people are. For an all-US team Gusto wins on a price you can see: Simple is $49 a month plus $6 per person (read 16 Oct 2026). Deel wins once people sit outside the US: contractor management is $49 per contractor a month and EOR starts at $599 per employee a month (read 9 Oct 2026). For a mostly US team with a few contractors abroad, Gusto for the US staff and Deel for the contractors can cost less than putting everyone on one of them.

Which one fits you

Pick Deel if

  • You pay contractors in several countries. Contractor management is $49 per contractor a month, or $325 if Deel becomes Contractor of Record and carries the misclassification risk (read 9 Oct 2026).
  • You need one person employed in a country where you have no entity. Deel's EOR starts at $599 per employee a month (read 9 Oct 2026). Buyers on Capterra and G2 report all-in costs 20-60% above the quoted fee, so get a quote per country.
  • Your contractors are spread widely. Deel pays contractors compliantly in 150+ countries, and on Capterra (about 4.9 across 4,200+ reviews) country coverage is what reviewers rate highest.

When to pick neither

You hire enough people in one foreign country that your own entity there beats paying EOR fees per employee forever. Gusto cannot employ them, and Deel's EOR keeps costing from $599 per employee a month.

EOR means renting an employer: it pays while you test a market and gets expensive once that country holds a real team, so a local entity with local payroll wins.

What it costs at a real size

A US team of 10 plus 2 contractors abroad. Gusto Simple for the US staff: $49 + 10 × $6 = $109 a month (read 16 Oct 2026). Deel contractor management for the two contractors: 2 × $49 = $98 a month (read 9 Oct 2026). Both together: $109 + $98 = $207 a month. The same 10 US staff on Deel's US PEO would start at 10 × $125 = $1,250 a month. Gusto can also pay global contractors with no monthly fee, but it charges a service and foreign-exchange fee per payment that our reading does not price.

Questions buyers ask

Can Gusto pay people abroad?

Partly. Global contractors carry no monthly per-contractor fee; Gusto charges a service and foreign-exchange fee per payment instead. Since 1 May 2026, new international employees are referred to Remote's employer-of-record service, from about $599 per employee a month on a separate contract.

What is the difference between Deel's $49 and $325 contractor options?

Who carries the misclassification risk. At $49 you manage the contractor yourself through Deel; at $325 Deel is the Contractor of Record. Its Contractor of Record page (read 9 Oct 2026) also asks a deposit of one month of the contractor's payment per contract.

Is Deel's $599 EOR price what I will pay?

It is a starting price. Buyers on Capterra and G2 (Aug 2026) report all-in costs 20-60% above the quoted fee once country-specific employer costs are added.

Does either lock me into a long contract?

Not on our reading. Gusto is month-to-month with no long-term contract, and Deel's pricing FAQ says pricing is month to month with no long-term commitment.
