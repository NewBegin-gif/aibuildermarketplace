Deel vs Papaya Global: HR-led speed or finance-led consolidation

You need people paid in countries where you may have no entity, and every vendor answers with a demo booking. Maybe you are a startup that found two hires abroad and needs them employed this month. Or you run finance for a company with entities in several countries, and month-end means stitching payroll reports together. You are comparing Deel and Papaya Global because both promise global payroll, but they are built for different buyers.

Both tools are affiliate partners of ours: we may earn a commission if you sign up through a button on this page, at no extra cost to you. That is why we also say when to pick neither.

Short answerIf the buyer sits in HR and you need someone hired abroad soon, Deel is the better fit: it is built for speed and contractors, priced month to month. If the buyer sits in Finance and you already run entities in several countries, Papaya Global is built to put that payroll and those payments on one platform. Papaya's published floors are lower (EOR from $499 against Deel's $599 per employee a month, read 1 Oct and 9 Oct 2026), but Business.com reports a typical two-year contract and reviewers report bills 30-50% over quote. The contract and the buyer decide this, not the floor price.

Which one fits you

Pick Deel if

  • You need two people employed abroad this month. Papaya's onboarding is slower than Deel's and its rollout is quote-led enterprise work; Deel's EOR starts at $599 per employee a month (read 9 Oct 2026).
  • You want to stay flexible. Deel's pricing FAQ says month to month with no long-term commitment, while Papaya's typical contract is two years with a rolling 90-day exit clause, per Business.com.
  • You pay contractors in many countries and worry about misclassification. Deel is $49 per contractor a month, or $325 as Contractor of Record (read 9 Oct 2026), and on Capterra (about 4.9 across 4,200+ reviews) reviewers rate its country coverage highest.

When to pick neither

Every employee works in one country. Deel prices each worker as a global hire, and Papaya is overkill and overpriced for domestic-only use.

Look at ADP instead. Our Deel research says a local payroll tool costs far less for an all-domestic team and names ADP for that case. Read our ADP review.

What it costs at a real size

Three EOR employees at the published floors. Deel: 3 × $599 = $1,797 a month (read 9 Oct 2026). Papaya Global: 3 × $499 = $1,497 a month (read 1 Oct 2026). Treat both as the start of a quote, not the bill: Deel buyers report all-in costs 20-60% above the quoted fee, and Papaya reviewers report bills 30-50% over quote, with setup, FX and filing fees added after signing.

Questions buyers ask

Is Papaya's $499 EOR price what I will pay?

Treat it as the floor of a negotiation. Third-party trackers put Papaya's EOR higher: Business.com from $599 (Feb 2026), eorHQ about $650 standard (Jun 2026). Reviewers also report bills 30-50% over quote. Ask for the fully landed monthly cost per employee, itemised, in the contract.

Which countries does Papaya run itself?

Papaya does not publish the split. A third-party estimate puts its owned entities near 40 countries, with in-country partners elsewhere. Ask by name for each country you hire in.

How easy is it to leave?

Deel's pricing FAQ says month to month with no long-term commitment. For Papaya, Business.com documents a typical two-year agreement with a rolling 90-day termination clause, so your notice date is three months before you want to leave.

Who carries the misclassification risk for contractors?

On Deel you do at $49 a month, and Deel takes it on at $325 as Contractor of Record. Papaya's Contractor of Record starts at $199 per contractor a month. We did not compare what each one covers in detail.
