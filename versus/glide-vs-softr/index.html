Glide vs Softr: a spreadsheet app or a client portal without code

Your team runs on a shared spreadsheet, or clients keep emailing for updates that already sit in one, and there is no developer to build a proper app. If you are comparing these two, you are probably an operations lead or a small agency owner who wants an internal tool or a client portal without code. Both turn your data into an app. They charge you for different kinds of growth.

Both tools are affiliate partners of ours: we may earn a commission if you sign up through a button on this page, at no extra cost to you. That is why we also say when to pick neither.

Short answerSoftr is the easier start for a client portal or internal tool with a known audience: its free plan allows unlimited apps, 3 builders and 5 app users, and Basic is $19 a month billed yearly (read 1 Oct 2026). Glide Classic suits teams whose operations already live in a spreadsheet, with Explorer at $19 a month billed yearly (read 3 Sep 2026), but its business plan jumps to $199. The deciding factor is what grows: on Softr each app user past the allowance costs extra, on Glide Classic each update past 5,000 does. Check which Glide you are buying, too: in our 7 Sep 2026 test the free signup button led to GlideOS, a different product.

Which one fits you

Pick Glide if

  • Your operations already live in a spreadsheet: buyers on G2 report that an idea becomes a working internal tool within an afternoon, with no code.
  • Many colleagues will use the app: Classic Business includes 30 users for $199 a month billed yearly, extra users are $5 each, and users count once per team and only in a month they are active (read 17 Oct 2026).
  • Your users mostly tap and type: on Classic, changes people make in the app to Glide Tables or Big Tables use no updates. Scheduled or automatic workflows do.

When to pick neither

You are building a public consumer app with open signup, heavy writes or custom logic. Softr's per-user pricing works against unpredictable user counts, and Glide's update metering punishes write-heavy apps.

Look at Lovable instead. Our Glide dossier points builders who need custom logic and an exact brand look to Lovable, because buyers report that Glide's own components box them in. Read our Lovable review.

What it costs at a real size

An internal app for 30 people. Glide Classic Business: 30 users are included. If each person causes ten writes a day that count as updates, over 21 working days that is 30 × 10 × 21 = 6,300 updates. The bill: $199 + (6,300 - 5,000) × $0.02 = $199 + $26 = $225 a month on yearly billing (read 17 Oct 2026). Softr: if all 30 share your company email domain they are Team users. Business includes 30 Team users for $329 a month billed yearly. Pro includes 10, so 20 more at from $1 each comes to $99 + $20 = from $119 a month (read 1 Oct 2026).

Questions buyers ask

Which Glide do I get when I sign up for free?

GlideOS, the newer AI builder, not the classic spreadsheet builder. In our test on 7 Sep 2026 both 'Sign up' and 'Get started' led there. The classic free tier sits behind glideapps.com/classic, with its own pricing page.

Who counts as a paid user?

On Glide Classic, people who use an app in that billing period, counted once per team. On Softr, builders and app users are counted apart, and app users split into Team users (your email domain) and Client users (everyone else). Basic includes 5 + 5, Pro 10 + 50 and Business 30 + 100.

Does a bigger Glide plan lift the 25k row limit?

Not for a spreadsheet-backed app. Glide's partner team said on 18 Aug 2026 that the 25k limit applies to synced spreadsheet sources whatever you pay. The 100k-plus figures belong to Glide's Big Table data source.

Do the trials turn into paid plans?

Glide's 14-day Business trial needs billing details and renews into a month of Business unless you cancel. Softr's 7-day Business trial drops back to Free on its own.
