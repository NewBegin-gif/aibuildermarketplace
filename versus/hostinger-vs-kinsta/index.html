Hostinger vs Kinsta: budget prepaid hosting or managed WordPress?

You need hosting for a WordPress site and the two price tags are more than ten times apart. If the site is a side project, a big hosting bill can outgrow anything it earns. If it takes orders, every slow or down hour costs sales, and a host that takes days to answer makes it worse. You are probably somewhere in between: a site that is starting to matter, and you want to know when paying more is worth it.

Both tools are affiliate partners of ours: we may earn a commission if you sign up through a button on this page, at no extra cost to you. That is why we also say when to pick neither.

Short answerFor a side project or brochure site you will keep for four years, Hostinger wins on cost, as long as you budget for the renewal: Premium goes from $2.99 to $10.99 a month after the 48-month term (read 9 Oct 2026). For a WordPress site that earns money, Kinsta is the safer pick: it is managed hosting with no multi-year prepayment, from $35 a month (read 3 Oct 2026), and buyers rate its performance and support 4.8 on Trustpilot and G2. The deciding factor is what an hour of downtime costs you compared with the hosting bill. We have not tested either host ourselves.

Which one fits you

Pick Hostinger if

  • Your site is a side project or brochure and you will keep it four years: Premium is $143.52 upfront for 48 months (read 9 Oct 2026), covering 3 websites, then $10.99 a month.
  • You might run more than three small sites: Unlimited is $3.99 a month for unlimited websites, 50 GB NVMe and daily backups, renewing at $16.99.
  • You want a cheap VPS you manage yourself: KVM 1 is $6.49 a month on a 24-month term (1 vCPU, 4 GB RAM, 50 GB NVMe) and renews at $11.99.

When to pick neither

Your WordPress site has outgrown budget shared hosting, but $35 a month for one site is more than it earns. Hostinger's shared tiers are not managed-grade, and Kinsta is priced as premium managed hosting.

Look at Cloudways instead. Our Kinsta dossier lists Cloudways among the cheaper managed-WordPress hosts. Read our Cloudways review.

What it costs at a real size

One WordPress site over four years. Hostinger Premium: $143.52 paid upfront for 48 months. Kinsta single site on annual billing: $350 x 4 = $1,400, or $35 x 48 = $1,680 paid monthly. Over those four years Hostinger is $1,400 - $143.52 = $1,256.48 cheaper than Kinsta annual. From year five Hostinger Premium renews at $10.99 a month, so $10.99 x 12 = $131.88 a year, against Kinsta's $350 a year. Kinsta's prices exclude tax.

Questions buyers ask

What does Hostinger cost once the intro term ends?

Read on 9 October 2026: Premium goes from $2.99 to $10.99 a month, Unlimited from $3.99 to $16.99, Cloud Startup from $7.99 to $25.99, and the KVM 1 VPS from $6.49 to $11.99. The renewal figures have held in every capture we hold since July 2026, even as tier names changed, so treat them as the real price.

Is there a Kinsta discount code?

No. Kinsta told us on 28 July 2026 that it gives no partner-only or affiliate-only discounts. The two months off on annual billing and the free first month are the public offers, and they were still on the page on 3 October 2026.

Can I get my money back from Hostinger?

Within 30 days, mostly. The refund policy (last revised 2 October 2026, read 9 October 2026) refunds products cancelled within 30 days, but the free domain is not refundable, and a VPS refund needs more than 180 days since your last VPS refund.

Is moving my site to Kinsta free?

Basic migrations are free and unlimited, limited only by the installs your plan allows. Kinsta's FAQ states a turnaround of up to 2 business days, Monday to Friday. Expedited migration costs $49 per site.
